It’s quite common after the market receives good news to see a significant jump in share prices across the board, and then after the initial glow fades and enthusiasm cools, to see a brief adjustment downward. This is especially true in the technology sector, where prices, information, and innovation can flip on a dime and make millionaires practically overnight.

This can be a golden opportunity to buy, as both moves often hit the market somewhat indiscriminately, and the lower price will only last for as long as it takes the market to make a more sober assessment. Once the stocks have been differentiated (and acted upon) into legitimate increases and happy accidents, it’s often too late to jump on the bandwagon.

Predicting when the whole market will boom is functionally impossible; but guessing which companies will experience long-term growth is only as difficult as assessing their quality. This may sound easy enough, but no matter how strong a hunch you have, betting real money on your gut instinct isn’t a wise financial decision.

This is where our deep learning AI (artificial intelligence) comes into play. Our AI analyzes the data for you to help guide your decision making, from affirming your gut instinct to realizing you dodged a bullet. This is possible via a four-point factor analysis and a final recommendation, which all comes together to paint a more detailed picture of which companies are best poised to see the growth you desire in your portfolio.

Top Communications Stocks

Verizon Communications Inc (VZ)

Verizon ticked up 0.14% on Friday to $56.53 per share on volume of 17.1 million. This is a slight drop compared to its 10-day price average of $57.10, though still an increase from the $55.93 22-day average. Though Verizon is down 7.4% for the year, they’ve made healthy gains on their initial pandemic market down. Our AI has rated this stock B in Technical, B in Growth, A in Momentum Volatility, and A in Quality Value.

As a company, Verizon has a long bet on managing the most extensive coverage in current networks. With 5G mounting on the horizon, they’ve moved quickly to prepare for the future, building the infrastructure before it’s even needed. After the recent improvement in the stock market, Verizon saw the same rise and subsequent small fall as markets adjusted. However, our AI believes that the company is significantly underpriced, and combined with an above-average report card, has rated Verizon a Top Buy.

AT&T Inc (T)

AT&T closed at $30.50 on Friday, a 1% increase for the day, though still locked in the throes of their 21.5% bear market down. They have experienced a general downward trend in recent weeks; Friday’s price comes in 3.7% beneath the 10-day price average and a 0.4% tick down from the 22-day price average. Our AI has graded this stock B in Technical, B in Growth, A in Momentum Volatility, and A in Quality Value.

Like its competitor, AT&T saw a large rise and small fall over the past week. It, too, is a strong competitor in its area of expertise. The company is also in talks to sell off its video game studio, which could net billions. With such a large stock charting likely growth ahead in tandem with a future infusion of revenue in the billions, it’s no surprise to hear it’s an AI Top Buy.

Motorola Solutions Inc (MSI)

Motorolaclosed up a healthy 3.3% on Friday to $140.60 per share on volume well over 2.4 million. They’ve experienced some fluctuation in recent weeks, as Friday’s price sits a bump over their 22-day price average of $130.84, though a decent 4.1% down from the 10-day price average. Overall, the stock is sitting pretty (comparatively) at a “mere” 13% down YTD. Our AI has rated Motorola B in Technical, C in Growth, A in Momentum Volatility, and B in Quality Value. 

Motorola is one of the great turnaround stories in business. Nearly touching bottom in early 2009, it has since embarked on a steady – and very impressive – growth pattern, with its share price reaching 15 times its 2009 low before coronavirus struck. Since then, it has fallen with the rest of the market – too far, our AI predicts. This stock has earned its rank as a Top Buy on our list.

Centurylink Inc (CTL)

Centurylink closed at $10.08 on Friday, up 3.4% for the day, though barely denting its bear market down of 22% YTD. The company has struggled to make up its gains since the pandemic crash, with Friday’s numbers closing a tick beneath both the 10- and 22-day prices averages ($10.53 and $10.09, respectively). This stock rates A in Technical, C in Growth, A in Momentum Volatility, and B in Quality Value.

Centurylink has followed much the same pattern as the rest of the market – with an added positive twist. Their focus on the backend of tech, such as in communication, networks, and security has seated them pretty for the COVID-19 crisis as demand has risen. The company has also been steadily and deliberately refinancing their debt to improve their balance sheet. Once more, due to savvy business and a decent report card, our AI has rated this stock a Top Buy.

US Cellular Corp (USM)

US Cellular ticked up 1.5% Friday to $31.16 per share on volume approaching 190,000. While the stock is down 14% for the year, it has been making steady gains on its pandemic market bottom of $25.31, despite minor fluctuations. Our AI has graded this stock average all-around, with B’s across the board from Technical to Quality Value.

When it comes to cellular providers, there are the big three that provide the bulk of paid services – and then a fourth quite a ways behind. That fourth is US Cellular. While having a large, extensive network is important for success, the flip side is that being fourth means being big enough to retain a loyal customer base with plenty of room to grow. A decent report card and recent downward adjustment have lent our AI to call US Cellular another Top Buy.

Top Software Stocks

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE)

HP closed up a robust 4.1% on Friday, ending the day at $10.15 a share on volume of 14.4 million. Though the stock is stuck down 37% for the year, prices have been holding fairly steady since the initial pandemic market crash, as seen in their 10- and 22-day price averages ($10.48; $10.02). With solid performance potential ahead, this stock has earned ratings of B in Technical, C in Growth, C in Momentum Volatility, and A in Quality Value.

When you combine a market rise with good news, you often see shares shoot up well beyond their value – and the same is true in reverse. Hewlett Packard Enterprise, the information technology spinoff of HP, has lost some ground to competitors lately. However, because the news came as the market dipped independently, share price has dropped well below its value, which is why our AI sees another opportunity for a Top Buy.

Microsoft Corp (MSFT)

Microsofton Friday closed up 0.8% to $187.74 per share on an impressive volume of 43.3 million shares. Unlike several of the stocks on this list, Microsoft has been reporting solid gains since the start of the pandemic market, with prices up almost 17% for the year. Friday’s closing numbers come in $3 over the 22-day price average of $184.74 – a mere 1.6% gain against such a high price, but a consistent increase, nonetheless. This stock rates C in Technical, B in Growth, A in Momentum Volatility, and C in Quality Value. 

If you were to follow tech news rather than the market, your list of giants probably wouldn’t include Microsoft. However, in the past two decades the company has transitioned from merely an OS company to so much more, with exponential growth to show for it. When you see the price dip on such a company, as it did this week, you should think what our AI does – that you’ve found another Top Buy for your portfolio.

Automatic Data Processing (ADP)

Automatic Data Processing closed at $147.01 on Friday, up 2.8% for the day, though still stuck well beneath a correction at 13.7% YTD. This stock has seen some big leaps in both directions since the market first crashed in March, with Friday falling beneath the 10-day average of $152.05, though still a healthy $3 increase over the 22-day average of $144.19. Our AI has graded this stock as average, for the most part, with ratings of C in Technical, B in Growth, B in Momentum Volatility, and C in Quality Value.

This stock is something of a quiet giant, providing human resource management and software and generating billions in revenue. When coronavirus struck, their steady increases reversed overnight, and stock prices dropped off a cliff. However, they’ve improved their position greatly since March. With a stronger drop in the adjustment of the week, they’re a great target for acquisition in a growing portfolio, rated as a Top Buy on our AI’s analysis.

Top Chip Stocks

Intel Corp (INTC)

Intel on Friday closed down 0.62% to $59.33 per share on volume over 30.4 million. This continues their month-long trend of small bumps up and down, with Friday a tick down from both the 10- and 22-day price averages. However, the stock is still holding strong at a mere 2.5% down YTD, with AI ratings of C in Technical, A in Growth, B in Momentum Volatility, and A in Quality Value.

In addition to the fluctuations in the rest of the market, Intel has recently announced the departure of a top chip design executive. While the move is more complex than seems at first blush, the market still responded by further dropping the stock. However, Intel is a giant for a reason, and its current lows are unlikely to hold according to our AI’s Top Buy declaration.

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)

Advanced Micro Devicesbumped up 1.3% on Friday to $53.50 per share at an astonishing volume of 71 million shares. The stock has experienced some minor rises and falls in the past month, but nothing major, as evidenced by the 22-day price average of $53.97 against Friday’s closing price. Overall, the stock is up 9% YTD, with an AI grading of B in Technical, A in Growth, B in Momentum Volatility, and C in Quality Value.  

Advanced Micro Devices has had a slightly better year than competitor Intel, as evidenced by positive stock gains for the year. Although they’ve handled the crisis well, comparing AMD to Intel is almost unfair, as the latter is simply so massive there’s less room for growth. On the other hand, the company’s rise over NVidia is purely a matter of excellent performance. While the battle continues, they’ve earned the coming rise predicted by our AI’s Top Buy status.
