Patent Filings Roundup: NPE District Court Filings Skyrocket, Target Automotive Industry; Another Fortress/Uniloc IPR Denied Under Director’s Discretion Due to Pending Trial

Jonathan Stroud

June 3, 2020, 04:23 PM   11

Share

Support IPWatchdogwith an individual sponsorship: Click here

This week’s 40 new Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) filings—39 inter partes reviews (IPRs) and one post grant review (PGR)—are up slightly from the average, but are dwarfed in comparison to this week’s explosion of 106 new district court complaints filed, including two notable wide-ranging automotive campaigns, dozens of filings by known aggregators, and the rare filing by licensor and innovator Dolby.

The PTAB filings included the first petition filed by RPX in years, challenges from the continuing fallout between battery companies LG Chem and SK Innovations, and a district court assertion by plenty of new and familiar LLC subsidiary assertion vehicles. And an IPR petition against Uniloc (Fortress IP) was denied due to a Western District of Texas trial date, again showing that the Section 314 discretionary doctrines—under which the PTAB/USPTO Director has discretion to deny or institute IPRs—have been and continue to be a boon to serial assertors, with that case, the VLSi denials, and other Uniloc denials making up the bulk of 314 denials so far this year.

Rogue’s Gallery: The usual suspects rounded out the lineup this week, with serial assertion entities like Endpoint IP, IP Investments Group, IP Edge, Equitable IP, Empire IP, and East Texas Patent Group filling out the lineup. There were even a handful of suits related to an ownership dispute/motions to dismiss in some old IV Nav suits, Zeroclick, here. These NPE/aggregators represented the vast majority of the 106 filings over the past week and show that NPE activity can spike during a downturn, though the spike likely has more to do with the availability of financing and major offloading of Intellectual Ventures assets. According to some license agreements that have been recorded with the USPTO, Intellectual Ventures (who appears to have sold themselves or the portfolio in part to Micron) has been entering into three-year escalating deals whereby another NPE pays pennies on the dollar upfront (say, $10,000) to monetize a portfolio, where they can keep a substantial portion of the profits from licensing activities, but have to pay IV back if they fail to successfully reap licensing revenues.

As long noted, “the company has played extensive games in an attempt to stifle the utility of the patent ownership recording system,” and had upward of 30,000 patents, according to Dennis Crouch circa 2011; in the years since, they have been winding down Fund III (as unsuccessful) and offloading assets, and recently offloaded thousands to Acacia, Dominion Harbor, IP Investments Group(some 2,507 assets transferred), Longhorn, and others; we are now seeing the fruits of those arrangements in the form of suits. Some of the divestitures have yet to mature into suits; for instance, Intellectual Venturesoffloaded another tranche of patents to Kioba Processing (see assignment records here) in November of 2019; or American Patents LLC (here) in December of 2019, or Liberty Patents LLC (This one has yet to be asserted, but one must assume this is the next campaign to be brought by the Antonelli, Harrington and Thompson law firm, as was American Patents. You have to love the names of these entities.). Most of these assignments are bare, like this, to Virtual Watchdog Solutions, LLC, and don’t include compensation, control, or all of the terms of the transfer. But occasionally, good assignment records slip through.

When IV does a deal with a publicly traded company, disclosure requirements mean that company must disclose the details of the transactions via a 10-K report. In 2018, Quest Research Patent Corporation had to disclose their various portfolio purchases from IV, here. In those disclosures, you see a privateering arrangement whereby the buyer/assertor would pay IV a pittance upfront, and then have escalating payments and owe IV 50% of the proceeds. Whether all of their deals, some, or just this one are structured this way, it’s clear that IV divestments are driving many of the assertions to date.
