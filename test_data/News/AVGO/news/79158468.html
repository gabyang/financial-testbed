Broadcom Trades at Record Levels on Bullish Outlook

Published: 2021-12-10 11:56:02 ET AVGO

By Maria Armental

Chip-maker Broadcom Inc. traded at record levels Friday, a day after reporting record revenue for the year and initiating a $10 billion share-repurchase authorization over the next year.

Shares, which Friday hit a record high of $644.75, recently traded at $627.91, up 7.6% for the day and on track for a record closing, according to FactSet.

The semiconductor powerhouse, which amassed more than $12 billion in cash in the year ended Oct. 31, had said it would decide by the end of the business year whether to direct a portion of that cash to dealmaking or repurchase its stock. On Thursday, it raised its dividend and said it planned to buy back up to $10 billion worth of stock through Dec. 31, 2022.

Broadcom Chief Executive Hock Tan, who built the company through a series of acquisitions, stressed on the earnings call that the $10 billion stock buyback authorization doesn't mean Broadcom won't be in the hunt for deal in 2022. Rather, he said, it is more a reflection of the level of cash generation thus far--and projections for 2022.

Mr. Tan pointed to strong overall demand, driven by "a very strong spending recovery, in enterprise particularly."

"We're pretty much booked all the way through '22 and even beyond '22 into '23," he said.

On Thursday, Broadcom reported fourth-quarter revenue ahead of Wall Street targets and the company's guidance and said it expects to generate about $7.6 billion in revenue this quarter.

It ended the year at a profit of $6.74 billion on $27.45 billion in revenue, compared with a profit of $2.96 billion and $23.89 billion in revenue a year earlier.

JPMorgan analysts raised their price target to $700, from $655, pointing to the company's outlook, backed by orders and backlog visibility across its networking, broadband, and server storage semiconductor businesses and driven by accelerating demand in enterprise along with sustained cloud hyperscalers/telco strength.

Similarly, BMO Capital Markets' Ambrish Srivastava raises his target to $675, from $625, saying fundamentals appear to be strengthening, with the moribund enterprise coming back strong.
