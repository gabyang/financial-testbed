Is The Corporate Perspective On Race Changing: Leaders Need To Consider This

As conversations around racial equality continue to dominate the headlines, it becomes increasingly apparent that there is much work to do to address the imbalance at the top of some of the world’s leading organisations.

As black lives matter protests continue unabated across the UK, it highlights the issues faced by black people and inclusion within the workplace, has not escaped its focus.

Business in the Community, the oldest and largest business led organisation in the UK, has recently shown, in a recently updated report, that despite much rhetoric around diversity and inclusion, businesses in the UK have failed to address representation at leadership levels, this despite the black population making up in excess of 3% of the UK total.

Top management roles across UK businesses, are held almost exclusively by white people. There can be no doubt that the current situation has to change. The Parker review gave businesses until the end of 2021 to appoint at least one non-white board director. It also set a similar target for the FTSE 250, with a deadline set for 2024. Yet still, there seems to be a defined lack of will and commitment on the part some of these companies to get it right.

Recently, bosses of Tesco, ITV and BT, who have all met the target, signed an open letter, calling for an end to the cycle of inaction of 'ethnic minority inclusion in business.'

Across the US, a similar picture is highlighted, with more than 80 percent of board seats filled by white executives.

Will Public Outcry Bring About Change?

Protests about the lack of diversity and the inclusion of black people, have highlighted racism, and structural inequalities, in a way that has caught the attention of the world.

With such public outcry, and support from people across racial divides, the eyes of the world are now on companies, willing them to act to develop committed plans for inclusion.

The CEO of Merck, Kenneth Frazier, has spoken about the black lives matter protests on CNBC's Squark Box. As one of the few black people, heading a US corporate company, Frazier said:

“Businesses have to use every instrument at their disposal to reduce these barriers that existed.”

He went on to state that:

“There are in fact barriers that are faced by African Americans even though we don’t have laws that separate people on the basis of race anymore. We still have customs. We still have beliefs. We still have policies. We have practices that lead to inequity.”

Public outcry has gained the attention of corporate businesses across the globe, with many giving corporate donations to support black communities, and others, including Lloyd's of London, publicly apologising for their role in the slave trade.

It appears that some kind of reality check is taking place in the mindset of corporations around the world, in relation to racial equality, but there is much to do and this is just the start.

The Mindset of the Movement

The Black Lives matter protests has gone global, and with it issues of racism and racial equality have gone viral. Whilst corporate organisations have begun to take small steps to discuss racism, and look at how leadership can intervene to make changes, should be recognised that this is just a start.

Black lives do indeed matter. In a world that is faced with many challenges, including health, economic, environmental and social, the outcry defined by the ongoing global protests, deliver a salient message.

Corporate racism will no longer be tolerated. The experiences of black employees around the world, must no longer defined by the colour of their skins. The covert, insidious microaggressions that impact the careers of black people must be resigned to the history books, along with the racism that has defined the existence of generations of black people.

As public and corporate consciousness begins to wake up to the reality of the black experience, and issues of systemic racism it remains to be seen how corporate companies will develop strategies to fix the problem of institutional, structurally based racism, over the long term.

Considerations for Leadership

The Black Lives Matter campaign has sparked the beginning of productive conversations around race. Reports aside, it is clear that leadership must enable internal discussions, inclusive recruitment and systemic refresh to get race equality right. Real change is the demand,not rhetoric.

Leadership must be ready to commit to real, authentic inclusion at every level of business to ensure that not only are they developing equity for black people, but they are involving black people in the process.

Diversity and inclusion is a must have, not a nice to have, and should be driven by data, both quantitative and qualitative, in order to develop solutions that impact positively on the lives of black employees, and communities.

Corporate culture must be redesigned to ensure that racism has no place to hide within any organisation. This is the only way that industry will turn racism into the pariah that it deserves to be.

This journey will not be a quick one. Centuries and decades of malignant practice and behaviours have been allowed to endure. Small, well thought out steps, that form part of a defined, overall strategy will work best, but the goal must be attained.

Calls for racial equality will not go away. Perhaps it is best to use the opportunity that has presented itself, to get this right once and for all.
