Stock Market Pares Losses As S&P 500 Makes Stand At Key Level

Licensing
  • JUAN CARLOS ARANCIBIA
  • 01:20 PM ET 01/07/2022

The stock market pared losses Friday afternoon after the December jobs report missed forecasts and caused some selling. Financials continued to outperform as Bank of America (BAC) topped a buy point.

↑ X

U.S. employers added 199,000 jobs in December, the Labor Department reported this morning. The number marks a sharp decline from the 249,000 jobs created in November. The December total is about half what economists polled by Econoday had expected.

The unemployment rate dropped to 3.9% from 4.2% the prior month. Economists had forecast 4.1%.

Anu Gaggar, global investment strategist for Commonwealth Financial Network, called the nonfarm payroll number a disappointment, and the impact of omicron wasn't even fully factored into the report.

"Overall, this print had mixed messaging — the payrolls growth number may look disappointing, but the underlying story is lack of availability of labor, which is manifesting itself in faster wage growth," Gaggar said in a note. "The combination of the decline in unemployment rate to below Fed's long-term equilibrium level and acceleration in wage growth brings the Fed's March meeting in play for the first rate-hike of this cycle."

Stock Market Falls On Jobs Report

The news threw cold water on a stock market that's trying to bounce back from three days of losses.

The S&P 500 fell 0.1% and is making a stand at its 50-day moving average. A bounce off this level would indeed be a positive sign for the market.

The Nasdaq composite dropped 0.5% after coming off session lows. The Nasdaq, which fell below its 50-day line earlier in the week, now tests its critical December lows.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.3%, as Honeywell (HON) jumped nearly 3%. UBS upgraded the stock to buy from neutral and placed a 237 price target on it. The diversified manufacturing company's shares are about 10% below prior highs.

Travelers (TRV) rose above the 163.39 buy point of a 19-week pattern. Johnson & Johnson (JNJ) topped the 173.61 buy point of a cup-with-handle base, though volume was weak.

The Russell 2000 small-cap index fell 0.4%. Volume fell on the NYSE and Nasdaq compared with the same time on Thursday.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury note climbed four basis points to 1.77%. That's the highest since January 2020.

Financial Select Sector SPDR ETF (XLF) climbed 1.1% to a new high as banks and other financials rallied on rising yields. The ETF broke out of a base with a 40.96 buy point. Communications services and transportation sectors also performed well.

Financials Lead Stock Market

Bank of America broke out of a cup-without-handle base after rising above the 48.79 buy point. Volume was heavy and the relative strength line is almost at new highs, two signals that bode well for the breakout.

UMB Financial (UMBF) climbed above the 110 buy point of a flat base. The stock is in a buy zone up to 115.50. Volume was weak, but the RS line is at new highs.

Dozens of regional banks continue to make new highs.Zions Bancorp (ZION) is in buy range from the 68.35 buy point of a flat base. First Bancorp Holding (FBP) is in buy range from a 14.92 entry.

Utilities, real estate and health care — sectors that are largely defensive — were down 0.2% to 0.6%. Health Care Select Sector SPDR (XLV) fell below its 50-day moving average.

T-Mobile U.S. (TMUS) slid 5.5% after the wireless carrier reported subscription data that missed estimates. The stock has lagged for months and is meeting resistance at an already depressed 50-day moving average.

Innovator IBD 50 ETF (FFTY) worsened in afternoon trading, down 1.8%. The ETF is threatening to undercut its December lows and is down nearly 8% in the first week of the new year.

HomebuilderGreen Brick Partners (GRBK) fell 4% and is spending another day below its 50-day line. The stock already gave up all gains from its 28.01 buy point.

Financials such as Customers Bancorp (CUBI), Jefferies Financial (JEF) and Fidelity National Financial (FNF) climbed 1% or more in the IBD 50.
