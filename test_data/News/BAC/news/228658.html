Relief over STIMULUS PACKAGE — GAVIN doubles down on closures — MAJOR BANKS, but not BofA, providing 90 days’ relief — JOHN COX tests positive, KATIE PORTER self-isolating — ICE wants more masks — CA RESTAURANTS pleading for help

By CARLA MARINUCCI, JEREMY B. WHITE, GRAPH MASSARA and AARON LEATHLEY 

03/26/2020 09:15 AM EDT

Presented by Phenomena Global

THE BUZZ — DOUBLING DOWN ON CLOSURES: Doesn’t get any clearer than this: “Don’t think for a second that we’re a day or two from lifting that order. We’re not. We’re not even a week or two.”

That was the word from Gov. Gavin Newsom on Wednesday in his Covid-19 briefing, where he put some very wide distance between himself and President Donald Trump’s increasingly insistent claims that it’s time to ease closures and “get back to work” — even as the numbers of coronvirus infections and deaths are booming.

Asked about the president’s assertions, Newsom reiterated:“We can’t let up on the good decision-making that we’ve seen. ... We know it’s had an impact ... so let’s not let up. Let us commit to this home isolation and physical distancing.’’ He added: “We don’t live under assumptions. We live under real data trend lines — and real application.”

THE QUESTION NOW: Who’s got the real leverage? Not Trump. Especially if Newsom, the governor of California — the world’s fifth-largest economy and the most populous state — and Andrew Cuomo of New York, the East Coast powerhouse, are demanding more medical equipment while saying they’re keeping their people home and their states’ businesses shut. The president cannot simply order the governors to shift gears, and if numbers of deaths and infections continue to rise, as most every major medical and scientific expert suggests they will — POTUS may have no choice to back away from his upbeat predictions of seeing “packed” churches by Easter.

As former Wilson and Schwarzengger alum Rob Stutzman puts it: “Trump is the outlier” on this issue. So this is one where the states — and the governors — will rule the day.

IMAGINARY CLOCK — “Governors reject Trump’s virus timeline,’’ via AP: “The pushback suggests Trump’s talk of an early reboot is unlikely to gain traction. In most cases, it’s state leaders — not the federal government — who are responsible for both imposing and lifting the stay-at-home orders and other restrictions intended to stop the contagion.”

RAMPING IT UP — TWO CALIFORNIA HOUSE MEMBERS have called for a full national lockdown, even as Trump repeatedly suggests he’s going the other way and may ease restrictions. Rep. Ro Khannaof the South Bay led the charge last week, and has repeated his calls again Wednesday, tweeting: “We don’t have any more time to debate.In order to preserve our country, we need an immediate national shelter-in-place.”

ANOTHER ROUTE: Now, Rep. Harley Rouda of Orange County also tweetedthat he has joined eight other House members in appealing to the National Governors Association to “consider immediately implementing stay-at-home orders, in all 50 states, to minimize long-term, devastating harm to our communities, families, and economy.”

MORE TAKEAWAYS FROM GAVIN’S BRIEFING:

BANK HOLIDAY — “Banks commit to 90-day foreclosure halt,’’ by POLITICO’s Jeremy B. White: “Major financial institutions have agreed to a 90-day freeze on evicting Californians who are behind on their mortgages, Gov. Gavin Newsom said Wednesday.

“The governor said he had received a ‘personal commitment‘ from the leaders of four banks — Wells Fargo, US Bank, Citibank and JPMorgan Chase — to offer a respite to Californians struggling to make mortgage payments as a coronavirus-spurred lockdown convulses the economy. Bank of America only agreed to 30 days, Newsom said.

“Bank of America spokesperson Bill Halldin said in a statement that the lender is ‘deferring payments on a monthly basis until the crisis is over.‘”

TESTING #s BOOSTED —The Governor said one important metric has improved: “Coronavirus testing more than doubles in California. It’s not enough,‘” by SFChronicle’s Catherine Ho.

BUENOS DIAS, good Thursday morning.Just in: Unemployment claims in the U.S. reached nearly 3.3 million last week — the most in American history.

QUOTE OF THE DAY: “Another thing is the media and politicians. I hope they’re gonna cool down because they were asking me about why President Trump is calling it ‘China Virus.‘ You know what? It started from China, so that is what he is calling it. … They asked me because I’m an Asian. That’s discrimination here, to start with.” Korean-American GOP House candidate Michelle Park Steel, who is challenging Democratic incumbent Rep. Harley Rouda in CA-48.She has declined until now to comment on Trump’s repeated use of “Chinese virus” to describe Covid-19 in a visit to the Calvary Church on Sunday.

TWEET OF THE DAY: Apple’s @tim_cook:“Proud to share we’ve been able to source 10M masks for the US and millions more for the hardest hit regions in Europe. Our ops teams are helping to find and purchase masks from our supply chain in coordination with governments around the world.”

TOP TALKERS

STRESSED, AND NOT SERVING — “California restaurants plead for insurance help during coronavirus closures,’’ by POLITICO’s Carla Marinucci, Katy Murphy: “SACRAMENTO — Ron Schur, owner of The Galley, a Santa Monica institution founded in 1934, said it was heartbreaking enough to abruptly close his doors last week at the direction of Gov. Gavin Newsom and lay off a staff of 25 — some who’d been with him 30 years. But the frustration wasn’t over.

“‘I tried to make a claim and the insurance person wouldn’t take it,’ said Schur, who’s known to many of his loyal customers as Captain Ron. ‘She said it would be rejected.’“

LOCK AND LOAD — “Sheriff suspends efforts to close L.A. gun stores amid coronavirus restrictions,’’ via LATimes’ Luke Money and Richard Winton.

‘CANCEL THE CLEANING‘— “‘Virus Leads Many to Cast Aside Household Help,” by NYT’s Miriam Jordan and Caitlin Dickerson: “Concerns about the safety of an outsider entering their homes coupled with financial instability have prompted even the well-heeled to dispense with their help, and severance payments are a rarity.”

THOSE TESTING VIPS — “A Silicon Valley firm said it could help investors get expedited coronavirus tests — but then said it misspoke,” by Recode’s Theodore Schleifer. “Not all Americans have equal access to testing for the coronavirus. And not all Americans even have equal information about what existing testing might be available amid widespread testing delays and gaps across the country. That’s part of the reason why an email the Silicon Valley venture capital firm DCVC sent to its investors on Tuesday — offering them access to expedited Covid-19 testing due to a “unique relationship” — caused a stir.”

Los Angeles Mayor Eric Garcetti warned Tuesday that residents should be prepared for more loss of life as the pandemic expands.

HERE AT LAST — “Senate approves massive $2 trillion coronavirus emergency package,” by POLITICO’s Sarah Ferris, Melanie Zanona and Heather Caygle: “The Senate late Wednesday unanimously approved a $2 trillion emergency package intended to stave off total economic collapse in the wake of the coronavirus crisis, bringing an end to more than five days of negotiations between senators and the Trump administration.“ Read the bill text here.

PRIORITIES? — “ICE requests 45,000 N95 masks despite dangerous shortage in U.S. hospitals,” by the SF Chronicle’s Tatiana Sanchez: “Agency officials said they “would like delivery within 30 days,” even though the masks have been deemed critical for health professionals who treat patients infected with coronavirus.”

UNEMPLOYMENT UPPED AGAIN — “Governors beg for cash as unemployment claims crush states,” by POLITICO’s Katherine Landergan and Katy Murphy: “California has averaged 106,000 unemployment claims daily — nearly 750,000 in a week, or 3.8 percent of the entire state workforce.”

CALIFORNIA AND THE CAPITOL CORRIDOR

LESSON PLAN — “School lessons on hold as California districts negotiate with teachers unions,’’ by POLITICO’s Mackenzie Mays: “Nearly two weeks after schools across California closed without warning due to the coronavirus, many districts are stuck in neutral until labor negotiations get hashed out.’

“Teachers unions across the state are demanding rules for distance learning obligations, holding up at-home lessons for students — especially in districts that already had fractured labor relationships. ‘These important emergency declarations have not suspended obligations to negotiate with unions,‘ California Teachers Association spokesperson Claudia Briggs said.“ (Pro link)

BIG CHECK via CA Finance Dept:“At Governor Newsom’s direction, Finance this afternoon transferred $1.3 billion from the Special Fund for Economic Uncertainties (SFEU) – the state’s traditional reserve account – to finance the immediate acquisition of equipment and supplies and support activities necessary for the state’s coordinated response to the COVID-19 pandemic... Purchases will be overseen and tracked by the Department of General Services.”

— “Bay Area’s largest housing development appears dead,” by the SF Chronicle’s J.K. Dineen: “The redevelopment of the Concord Naval Weapons Station, the Bay Area’s largest proposed housing project, appears to be dead after the Concord City Council declined to extend an agreement with Lennar, the project’s master developer.”

MAILING IT IN — “As Coronavirus threatens general election, California could be example for states expanding vote-by-mail,” by The Mercury News’ Casey Tolan: “While less than 20 percent of voters in the 1992 general election cast their ballot by mail, nearly two-thirds did during the 2018 election, according to state data. That’s likely to be even higher this year.”

BIG DIFFERENCE — “New York state has 10 times the COVID-19 cases California has. Why?” by the SF Chronicle’s Erin Allday: “Infectious-disease experts say early maneuvers in California, especially in the Bay Area — first discouraging people from gathering in crowds and then ordering them to shelter in place — may have had a dramatic impact, even if they came only a few days ahead of those in New York.”

— “How the coronavirus pandemic is crippling California’s efforts to prevent catastrophic wildfires,” by the SF Chronicle’s Kurtis Alexander: “From clearing out undergrowth in forests to training firefighters to tamp out flames, local, state and federal fire forces are trying to move forward within new social distancing guidelines, as well as with potentially sick employees, but that’s making their work harder and sometimes impossible to do.”

GRIM INDEED — “Los Angeles Mayor Eric Garcetti warns of mass death, condemns ‘false hope,’ and tells us his city will be on lockdown for another two months — and to ‘be prepared for longer,’” by Business Insider’s Charles Davis.

About The Author: Carla Marinucci

Carla Marinucci, senior writer for POLITICO’s California Playbook, has consistently been named one of California’s leading political writers — and for the last two years has also been honored as the state’s top digital influencer in political reporting. Prior to launching the POLITICO California Playbook in 2015, Marinucci was senior political writer for the Hearst flagship San Francisco Chronicle, and before that political editor for the Hearst-owned San Francisco Examiner. She has covered six presidential elections and seven California gubernatorial elections since 1996, including the historic recall with Arnold Schwarzenegger. In 2013, she was honored by the Society of Professional Journalists with a lifetime achievement award for her coverage of California politics.

About The Author: Jeremy B. White

Jeremy B. White is a senior reporter covering the intersection of politics and policy in California. He’s been watching Sacramento for a decade, most recently as co-author of the California Playbook and, before that, for the Sacramento Bee.

A Maryland native who still believes in the Washington Nationals, lives in Berkeley with his wife and his cat, Ziggy Pawdust.
