Fitch Affirms University Retirement Community at Davis Obligated Group, CA at 'BBB+'

Published: 2024-03-25 06:00:16 ET BAC

Fitch Ratings has affirmed the University Retirement Community at Davis Obligated Group, CA's Issuer Default Rating (IDR) at 'BBB+'.

The obligated group (OG) has $20.1 million in series 2013 revenue refunding bonds directly placed with Bank of America and $42.3 million outstanding on a series 2021 loan with Bank of America that are not rated by Fitch.

The Rating Outlook is Stable.

VIEW ADDITIONAL RATING DETAILS

The 'BBB+' reflects University Retirement Community at Davis' (URC) favorable position as the only full-service retirement community in Yolo County and the strength of The Peninsula Regent's (TPR) condominium model that provides operating stability. The OG consists of URC, TPR and BASS LLC, which owns the land and common areas of TPR. Fitch expects the OG to issue additional debt for an independent living unit (ILU) expansion on a 3.5-acre parcel of land adjacent to the URC campus that was purchased in November 2022.

The Stable Outlook reflects Fitch belief that the OG has debt capacity to fund an ILU expansion project at the current rating level given URC's very strong demand and the expectation of favorable margin generation for the new units. Operating metrics generally remain sound and support the rating.

SECURITY

The series 2013 and 2021 loans are secured by a gross revenue pledge and a first mortgage lien on both the URC and TPR facilities.

KEY RATING DRIVERS

Revenue Defensibility - 'a'

Consistently Solid Revenue Generation

URC's ILU occupancy was robust in FY23, averaging 98.5%. While TPR's ILU occupancy was weak at 76% in FY23, TPR's community's contract arrangement with its members continues to provide steady cash flow as monthly fees must be paid by members until their condominium is sold. Management expects TPR's occupancy to improve over the near term, which should provide additional transfer fees in FY24. The OG collects transfer fees equal to 10% of the condominium seller's purchase price plus 75% of the realized appreciation. A 25% portion of the transfer fee is allocated to PRS, and 75% is allocated to BASS LLC, which is an OG member. Transfer fees totaled approximately $1.2 million in FY22 and more than $900,000 in FY23.

Healthcare service lines, excluding skilled nursing (SNF) at URC, all averaged over 90% occupancy in FY23. SNF occupancy at URC averaged 80% in FY23, but lower occupancy is partly the result of management's limiting of admissions due to staffing challenges. URC SNF maintains good relationships with hospitals and health systems and a five-star overall rating from CMS.

URC is located in Davis, CA and TPR is located in San Mateo, CA. Both service areas exhibit income levels above state and national averages and above average property values. URC has very limited direct competition, as it is the only life plan community located in Yolo County, with the nearest competitor located 20 miles away and maintains a waitlist of nearly 300 members. Although TPR has one main competitor located only a mile away, the local market has historically exhibited adequate demand to support both communities. Management believes that TPR attracts residents who desire a more active lifestyle, which provides natural differentiation between the two communities.

The good service area demographics have enabled both URC and TPR to increase fees regularly. Home values in both areas continue to appreciate, and San Mateo County in particular remains one of the wealthiest towns in the state, which bodes positively for TPR's demand profile. According to Zillow data, entrance fees and condominium prices are below prevailing housing prices in the local market.

Operating Risk - 'a'

Track-Record of Sound Operating Performance, although Margins Compressed in FY23; Manageable Debt Burden

URC offers a non-refundable Type-B contract. URC currently provides unlimited free temporary stays in the health center. Residents must pay the resident healthcare rate after moving permanently to the health center. TPR is a condominium model community where members buy and sell their condominiums with realtors and pay monthly fees until their condo is sold. Residents at TPR pay the same monthly service fee whether they are in an ILU or assisted living unit (ALU).

The OG's strong operating risk assessment primarily reflects URC's sound ILU occupancy and TPR's condominium model, which provides for consistent revenue generation. In FY23, the OG reported an adequate 100.6% operating ratio, a 4% net operating margin (NOM) and a 16% NOM-adjusted despite post-pandemic labor and inflationary pressure as well as significantly higher interest expense resulting from the OG's outstanding debt being fully variable-rate. Net entrance fee receipts were $5.9 million in FY23, after averaging $7.2 million over the prior four years. Prior to FY23, URC recorded even stronger operating metrics, with an operating ratio below 100%, a NOM of 7% or better, and NOM-adjusted of 20% or better.

Capital expenditures have been elevated in recent years as URC has been spending on a main dining room renovation and the creation of two new dining venues. The capital spending ratio averaged a very robust nearly 3x between FY19 and FY23. As a result, the OG's average age of plant remains sound at 10.6 years at FYE23.

Capital-related metrics show the OG with a manageable debt burden and some reliance on entrance fees to cover debt service. The OG produced adequate revenue-only coverage of 0.5x and debt to net available of 7.6x in FY23 even with higher actual annual debt service due to the OG's variable rate debt exposure. The OG's MADS as a percentage of is moderate at 10.3% in FY23. Fitch expects these metrics to remain stable in FY24 through expense control, fee increases and a rebound in staff recruiting in URC's health center that increases census. URC's expected debt issuance will likely depress capital-related metrics over the next few years, but Fitch expects the project, if undertaken and stabilized, to be accretive to the OG's operating risk over the longer-term.

Financial Profile - 'bbb'

Adequate Balance Sheet; OG Maintains Some Debt Capacity to Absorb Potential Expansion Project

Given the OG's 'a' revenue defensibility and 'a' operating risk assessments, Fitch expects in a forward-looking scenario analysis that key leverage metrics to remain consistent with a 'BBB' category rating throughout the current economic and business cycle. As of Dec. 31, 2023, the OG had unrestricted cash and investments of approximately $48 million at unaudited Dec. 31, 2023. This represents more than 75% of total adjusted debt and nearly 360 DCOH.

Fitch's baseline scenario, which is a reasonable forward look of financial performance over the next five years given current economic expectations, shows the OG issuing additional debt in FY25 and improving operating metrics over time as the occupancy in the new ILUs stabilize. Fitch's stress scenario reflects both operating and investment portfolio volatility. Even in a stress case, the OG maintains cash-to-adjusted debt and MADS coverage levels that are sufficient for a 'bbb' financial profile throughout the five-year time horizon. The stress case shows cash to adjusted debt rebounding to be above 50% and MADS coverage above 2.5x in the outer years, which are sound for the current rating. DCOH remains well above 200 days through the base and stress cases, which is neutral to the rating outcome.

Asymmetric Additional Risk Considerations

No asymmetric additional risk considerations informed the rating outcome.

RATING SENSITIVITIES

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Negative Rating Action/Downgrade

Weaker cash to adjusted debt that falls below 50% and is expected to stabilize at that level.

An extended period of weaker operating metrics or cash flow such that the operating ratio remains at or above 100% or NOMA falls to below 15% and/or debt service coverage is below 2.0x.

Factors that Could, Individually or Collectively, Lead to Positive Rating Action/Upgrade

Growth in unrestricted liquidity such that cash to adjusted debt increases is expected to be sustained above 100%, even in a stress case.

PROFILE

URC is a Type B continuing care retirement community (CCRC) located in Davis, CA that consists of 205 ILUs (18 cottages and 187 apartments), 37 ALUs, 14 memory care units, and 37 SNF beds offering only non-refundable contracts. TPR is a membership-based condominium retirement community located in San Mateo, CA that consists of 207 ILUs and 20 ALUs.

In September 2018, TPR formed BASS LLC, which purchased the land and common areas of TPR. The purchase was funded by the combination of a $14 million note from URC to BASS LLC and a $23.5 million loan from Bank of America that was refinanced in 2021. The bank loans from Bank of America are both payable by the new OG consisting of URC, TPR and BASS LLC, which is jointly obligated to the debt.

In FY23 (ended Sept. 30), the OG had total revenue of $49.6 million. URC and TPR are owned and managed by Pacific Retirement Services (PRS). PRS is the manager/operator of more than 35 retirement housing communities and manages communities in Oregon, Washington, California, Texas, Wisconsin, Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, and Oklahoma.
