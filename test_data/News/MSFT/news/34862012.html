Microsoft And Finastra Partner To Make Finance More Digital

Microsoft and Finastra, a London-based fintech with more than 8,000 financial services clients around the globe, have announced a multi-year cloud agreement to facilitate the digitalization of financial services.

Both firms said that the pandemic had demonstrated the need for speed and agility, and for ways to support employees who are working from home.

“The industry has faced a lot of requirements and needs in this unprecedented environment,” said Bill Borden, corporate vice president of worldwide financial services at Microsoft. Banks need to combine agility, speed and the ability to do both in a compliant environment, he said.

The finance industry worked quickly to get the Payroll Protection Act (PPA) funds out to thousands of companies around the country, working through banks and fintechs. Banks had to react quickly to handle the volumes and get those loans out — those are areas Finastra played in, especially in lending apps, to help their customers respond.

“As the pandemic has brought the future forward with regard to digitization, our vision to transform the world of financial services aligns closely with Microsoft’s ambitions, and I believe that together we can help accelerate this digital shift,” said Simon Paris, CEO of Finastra. More than 3,000 banks already rely on both companies for some or all of their technology. Using Microsoft platforms has already enabled Finastra to accelerate its product road maps to bring even more of its solutions to the cloud for its customers, he added.

“Finastra’s open developer platform, built on Microsoft Azure, is leading the way in accelerating collaboration and innovation in financial services. By helping with capabilities such as e-signatory and e-notary, Finastra is able to quickly extend those solutions for more customers,” said Scott Guthrie, executive vice president, Cloud + AI, Microsoft.

Banks are planning on dealing with the pandemic for one to two years, said Eli Rosner, chief product and technology officer at Finastra.

“We say Covid-19 has pushed the fast forward button. Digital transformations planned for three to seven years are happening much faster. Some may be permanent, such as more work from home or dispersed office centers. We see a lot of discussion in the industry on a remote mode for work — we are adapting it ourselves. Banks are thinking of smaller real estate and creating environments where people are working remotely, and also determining which groups come to the office, maybe 1 or 2 times a week.”

He predicts the industry will see hybrid models. Finastra, which built from the Misys platform and acquisitions over the years, has long had a strong

relationship with Microsoft, said Rosner, He said the two companies will collaborate in several areas, including using data for next best offer and risk management. The two companies will also work around strategies on multi-cloud which is a growing concern among regulators who don’t want to see banks reliant on a single cloud provider.

Finastra is talking to a large bank in Australia that is largely an ASW shop and wants information on how Microsoft-based apps can run in a cloud other than Azure. Finastra sees banks mostly interested in clouds provided by the big three— AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google, he added. IBM may be considered for some older applications.

“We found Microsoft to be very configurable in terms of performance in areas of concern to financial services firms, like the need to improve OCR in lending apps.” A year ago Finastra launched FusionFabric.cloud which allows developers access to build on top of Finastra’s core systems.
