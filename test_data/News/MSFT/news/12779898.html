First ARM-Powered Macs Revealed

ByEwan SpenceSenior Contributor
Ewan Spence covers the digital worlds of mobile technology.
Following
Jun 23, 2020, 12:58pm EDT
This article is more than 4 years old.

June 23 update below, post originally published June 22.

This year’s WWDC is a key moment for the Mac platform, with the long-awaited move to ARM-based processors the highlight of the new version of MacOS. Any update will need new machines sporting ARM chips, and the latest leaks highlight the first hardware Apple will release.

June 23 update: Strictly speaking Apple has already released its first ARM powered machine. Developers have access to a new Mac Mini that uses Apple’s A12Z ARM processor. This will allow developers to prepare ARM based apps for later in the year when the commercial MacOS ARM machines are released.Chris Welch reports for The Verge:

"The mini will run on Apple’s A12Z chip — the same one found in the 2020 iPad Pro — and includes 16GB of RAM and a 512GB SSD. It will come preloaded with a beta versions of macOS Big Sur and Xcode to help developers ready their apps for consumer Macs when they launch starting later this year. The DTK also has “a variety of Mac I/O ports.” "

Developers should not that they are not buying the Mac, when the program is over Apple will want their Mini back. At that point, developers will need to head to the Apple Store just like everyone else.

These Macs will have an ARM-based processor are expected to be the MacBook Pro and the iMac. Details come from noted Apple analyst Ming-Chi Kuo, reported by Juli Clover:

"Kuo says that the first Mac models to adopt Arm-based chips will be the 13.3-inch MacBook Pro and an iMac with a redesigned form factor, with Apple planning to launch the new models in the in fourth quarter of 2020 or early in 2021 at the earliest.

"…the Arm-based 13-inch ‌MacBook Pro‌ design will be similar to the current 13-inch ‌MacBook Pro‌, with Apple planning to discontinue the Intel version. As for the ‌iMac‌, it will feature an all-new form factor design and a 24-inch display.”

With Tim Cook announcing support for ARM-based processors at Apple’s virtual WWDC this week, details on the first hardware to feature the technology are welcome. Naturally there is one laptop and one fully desk-bound computer, offering an ARM option for the two main MacOS locations.

What Apple must be careful to do is not upset the sales of the existing MacOS machines. It would be far too easy to Osborne the existing MacBook Pro and iMac line-ups with an early official announcement o the hardware.

There was one ARM machine announced, a test-bed Mac Mini fitted with the A12Z system on chip called the ‘Developer Transition Kit’. Apple will strictly control the distribution of these machines through an application process.

Apple will continue to sell Intel machine, and support these machines with spares and repairs, for many years to come. In fact Apple is expected to update the Intel-based Mac machines later this year. One obvious example is the 16-inch MacBook Pro, currently using Intel’s 9th generation chipset, compared to the tenth generation inside the MacBook Air and the top-line 13-inch MacBook Pro machines.

With 100 percent of Apple’s MacOS machines run with ‘Intel Inside’, Apple needs to maintain the relationship with Intel.

These first-generation ARM machines are more than proof of concept, they are proof of the future, as well as offering an ARM platform for developers to test on. This is the direction that Apple will be moving in. They will no doubt be pitched as ‘the ideal machines for developers who want to join us on the cutting edge of the future.’
