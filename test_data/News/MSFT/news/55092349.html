Surface Duo Redefines The Smartphone Experience

Do you remember June 29, 2007? That is the day that Apple launched the iPhone and revolutionized the cellphone. With the launch of the Surface Duo, the date of September 10, 2020 may be remembered as the day Microsoft fundamentally redefined the smartphone experience.

A Surface Device First

I will leave the unboxing videos and breakdown of tech specs and performance to the plethora of media outlets that will surely be covering the Surface Duo launch. Instead, I want to focus on the Android OS running in the Surface Duo, and the learning curve it takes to truly embrace the dual screen paradigm.

The appeal of the Surface Duo goes beyond the fact that it is a dual-screen folding device that fits in your pocket. To be fair, I haven’t had the opportunity to play with other folding display smartphones—but, frankly, I haven’t had any interest. It just seems like a gimmick—and one that is likely to result in unforeseen and unnecessary issues and repairs. The thing that attracts me to the Microsoft device is that it is a Surface device.

I was impressed with the original Microsoft Surface device, and I have been in love with the entire Surface line since the Surface Pro 2. I appreciate the Surface Book and Surface Laptop, but in my opinion the Surface Pro is the best device available—a seamless and versatile combination of power and portability. Surface Headphones and Surface Earbuds are also great devices.

The impeccable engineering and attention to detail permeates the entire Surface brand, so when I see Surface, I expect a quality device. The Surface Duo does not disappoint. When I saw Microsoft unveil the Surface Duo concept at the Surface event in New York last year, I immediately said that the device might actually be the thing that convinces me to give up my iPhone. I still think that may be true.

I have never been a fan of the Android operating system. I consider the fact that it is running Android to be a flaw I am willing to overlook because it is a Surface device. That is actually still true. After using the Surface Duo for a couple weeks, I am still finding things I miss about iOS and things I don’t like about Android. That said, it is a Surface device and I love the tight integration with the Microsoft ecosystem.

Changing the Game for Mobile Devices

I don’t really like the term “smartphone”. I get it. I understand that it had its origins in the cellphone days and was meant to distinguish between a simple cellphone and a mobile device capable of doing much more than just being a phone. It’s 2020, though. Today, I use my iPhone for a million things a day and rarely—if ever—do I use it as a phone. On the contrary, if anyone calls me I am generally offended, There is little chance I will actually answer the phone, and a very good possibility the number will be blocked. My iPhone is not a “smartphone”. It is a mobile computing device that happens to be able to make and receive phone calls if, for some reason, I decided I want to do that.

The Surface Duo is the same for me. The fact that it is also a phone is secondary—or even tertiary—as a benefit. I won’t lie, it is a different experience. You can fold the Surface Duo inside out—so the displays are on the outside—when you put it in your pocket and that makes it closer to a traditional smartphone experience. If you do get a message or notification, or a call comes in, it is readily viewable without having to open and unfold the device.

I prefer to keep it folded to protect the displays. It also just looks cool. The fingerprint sensor responds almost instantly and the device is generally unlocked by the time I open it up. There are a number of scenarios that just work better with the dual display—researching travel plans in a browser on one display while making notes in OneNote on the second display, or being able to discuss a presentation in Microsoft Teams on one display while making changes to the slides in PowerPoint on the other. You can also easily expand an app so it spans both displays and use it as a small tablet in either portrait or landscape mode.

It takes some getting used to and I am still forming new habits as I go. Of course, the same thing was true when I made the switch from a traditional cellphone to an iPhone and I had to adapt to smartphone that was basically just a glass display, and get used to mobile apps. Now that is all second nature and is the accepted “norm” for a mobile device. Given time, I think the Surface Duo can be equally transformative and may fundamentally change the way we use and interact with our mobile devices.

I would say that the biggest stumbling block for Microsoft and the Surface Duo is price. At $1400, it is not a cheap device. However, price has never gotten in the way of the Apple iPhone. It is a dominant force no matter what the price tag is. Apple has proven that the experience defines the value and drives adoption. Most people will likely take advantage of monthly payment plans from mobile service providers. I am not sure I would spend $1400, but I would be willing to spend $50 a month.

Go and check out the Surface Duo. I think you will be impressed. If you decide to make the leap and get one, though, just make sure you give it a chance and give yourself time to adapt. Once you get familiar with it, I’m confident you won’t want to switch back.
