Overvalued? Apple’s 19.2% Growth Gap

The NASDAQ NDAQ is up 12% in 2020, reports the Wall Street Journal, thanks to a surge in the prices of so-called growth companies.

But some of these stocks could be way over-valued since their growth rates are far slower than the surge in their stock prices.

The company with the biggest growth gap — the difference between its revenue growth and the rise in its stock price is Apple AAPL .

I don’t see investors dumping Apple shares — but with little hope for enough growth to narrow the growth gap, I would look elsewhere.

(I own Adobe shares and have no financial interest in the other securities mentioned in this post).

Calculating Growth Gap/Surplus

What makes stock prices go up and down? My favorite theory is that every quarter a company has a chance to boost its share price by reporting faster than expected growth and raising its growth forecast for the future. A company that beats and raises enjoys a stock pop while a company that misses and/or lowers gets a haircut.

This theory is not perfect — just look at the 13% rise in Adobe ADBE since it delivered its mixed earnings report on June 11.

Yet the beat and raise theory often works. In my mind, this theory has a corollary: if a company does not have a decent chance of running out of money, the faster a company is growing, the more its stock should rise.

If you look at the companies that the Journal reported are driving the NASDAQ, you can test out this theory by subtracting the stock price increase so far in 2020 with their most recently reported revenue growth rates. If the result is negative, I call it a growth gap, if positive, it’s a growth surplus.

Here are the growth gaps/surpluses for five of those companies:

  • Apple -19.2%. (0.5% revenue growth for the most recent quarter less its 19.7% 2020 stock price appreciation as of June 22).
  • Amazon AMZN -16.5%. (26.5% less 43%)
  • Microsoft MSFT -9.2%. (15% less 24.2%)
  • Facebook +4.2%. (18% less 13.8%)
  • Alphabet +6.8%. (13% less 6.2%)

When I take into account the most recent growth forecasts, these numbers tell me three things:

  • Facebook and Alphabet could be bargains because of the growth surpluses.Yet investors may well be discounting them because they expect digital advertising revenue on which they depend to grow more slowly or decline. The Wall Street Journal reported that eMarketer expects Google GOOGL ad revenue to decline 4% in 2020 due to the collapse in the travel industry — while it expects Facebook revenue to grow 5%.
  • Microsoft and Amazon are expected to enjoy decent revenue growth — but investors are more excited about Amazon.Microsoft expects“7.7% growth — the slowest growth since the first quarter of 2017,” according to CNBC. Amazon’s revenue growth is expected to be in the range of 18% to 28%, according to TechCrunch.
  • Apple’s tepid revenue growth forecasts do not justify its 19.2% growth gap.In February Apple said it would not meet its guidance and in April, Apple withdrew its guidance for the quarter ending June 30. Apple’s CFO said he expects iPhone and wearables to decline between the March and June ending quarters with improvements in the iPad and Mac, according to Bloomberg.

Is There A Reason For Apple’s Rise?

While I can see why investors are holding back from Google and Facebook and why they are a bit exuberant about Amazon, explaining Apple’s growth gap defies rationality.

That is until you think about System 1 and System 2 — a concept described in Nobel Prize winning psychologist Daniel Kahneman’s book, Thinking Fast and Slow. System 1 makes decisions based on gut feelings and System 2 weighs costs and benefits to make choices.

My theory is that people who love Apple’s products buy its stock — and they don’t much care whether the company grows, shrinks, and treads water. As long as that is true, I would not bet that its stock will drop to close the growth gap.
