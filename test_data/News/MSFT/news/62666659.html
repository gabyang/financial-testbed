An a capella group has recreated Microsoft's iconic Windows sound effects in real life. The result is a performance that is both impressive and nostalgic, giving a reminder of how different Windows used to sound compared to now. The Windows operating system has drastically evolved throughout the years to a point where it may not look that familiar in the near future.

✕ Remove Ads

Microsoft has not only made major changes to its security and certain functionalities but also to its various aesthetic elements as well. From the constant desktop make-overs to the tile-like Start menu overhaul, the Windows of today look almost nothing like it was decades ago, save for some basic interface conventions. Despite its many changes, there are still some things that many users of past versions will remember, including certain memorable sound effects that are no longer being used in Windows 10, some of which have now been brought back to life vocally.

Related: Microsoft Update Removes Adobe Flash Player From Windows Devices

The a capella group MayTree demonstrated this in a video of its members mimicking a number of these nostalgic Windows chimes. This includes the Windows Vista and Windows XP startup and shutdown themes, along with a handful of iconic notification sounds that are still being used in the most recent Windows iterations. Most of these themes will usually sound mundane to those who have heard them thousands of times before, but the group has managed to make them sound more melodic than they normally are.

✕ Remove Ads

Microsoft & Windows In Music

While robots may be able to dance better than most humans, MayTree's a capella performance shows that humans are also capable of playing machines at their own game. The performance has left some viewers scratching their heads, with one questioning how it's even possible for people to produce such unusual sound effects, like the Recycle Bin's crumpling paper sound effect. In fact, Microsoft's official YouTube account also sang the group's praises, giving their performance a 10-out-of-10 rating in the comments section.

✕ Remove Ads

MayTree's oddly intriguing rendition brought shades of The Chordettes' Mr. Sandman into Microsoft's ubiquitous but prosaic Windows audio themes. However, they're not the only group that's keen on turning Microsoft's bread and butter into a melodic soundscape. Last month, Microsoft itself gave some of its products and features the musical treatment in a Holiday-inspired soundtrack.
