Fred Willard, best known for his work inBest of Show and Anchorman, passed away at the age of 86. Born on September 18, 1933, in Shaker Heights, Ohio, Willard enlisted in the army before starting a career in show business. Willard received a stepping stone into the entertainment industry when he was cast in the 1967 film Teenage Mother.

✕ Remove Ads

Deemed a master of the mockumentary genre, Willard transitioned into a prolific acting career in 1977 after appearing as Jerry Hubbard on the satirical soap opera Mary Hartman, Mary Hartman. Willard wed Mary Lovell in 1968, and the duo was inseparable until she died in 2018. Revered for stellar improvisation skills, smart comedic timing, and creating clueless characters, Willard doled out countless laughs for his audience with his roles in This is Spinal Tap, Waiting for Guffman, and Modern Family. Hailed as a scene-stealer of his projects, Willard frequently collaborated with filmmaker Christopher Guest and appeared in his mocumentary films A Mighty Wind, For Your Consideration, and Best in Show.

✕ Remove Ads

Per THR, Willard passed away on Friday evening, May 15, of natural causes in Los Angeles. Willard is survived by his daughter Hope Mulbarger and her son Freddie, who stated that he passed “very peacefully” and was loved until the very end. As of this writing, service arrangements for Willard will be announced at a later date.

Numerous celebrities, including Judd Apatow, Henry Winkler, and Jesse Tyler Ferguson, have voiced their tributes to Willard in the wake of his passing. Guest’s wife, Jamie Lee Curtis, took to social media to share the tragic news and expressed, “How lucky that we all got to enjoy Fred Willard’s gifts. He is with his missed Mary now. Thanks for the deep belly laughs, Mr. Willard.” Willard consistently aimed to polish his craft and worked steadily into 2020, and will next be seen in the Netflix comedySpace Force as Steve Carell's dad.

✕ Remove Ads

Toting an impressive list of more than 300 appearances in film and television, Willard's formidable career included performances in Everybody Loves Raymond, Mad Aout You, Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me, and Fernwood Tonight. Willard left a permanent mark on show business, not only with his ability to make his audience smile at his appearance, but with his reputation for being a hard-working, talented, and generous actor. Undoubtedly, Fred Willard will be greatly missed.

R.I.P Fred Willard - September 18, 1933-May 15, 2020
