O.J. Simpson’s ‘Tiger King’ hot takes: Missing man is ‘tiger sashimi’ | ’White people ... leave them animals alone!’

  • Updated: Apr. 03, 2020, 1:14 p.m.
  • |Published: Apr. 03, 2020, 1:12 p.m.
By
  • Randy Miller | NJ Advance Media for NJ.com

O.J. Simpsonfeels like he knows a killer when he sees one, and not just when looking in the mirror.

The disgraced Pro Football Hall of Famer binge-watched Netflix’s hit documentary “Tiger King” this week, and his biggest takeaway was believing animal rights activist Carole Baskin’s much-older, millionaire missing husband was fed to tigers at their Big Cat Rescue sanctuary in Tampa.

“There’s not a shred of doubt in my mindthat that lady’s husband is tiger sashimi right now,” Simpson said on Twitter. “I’m just saying.”

“Tiger King" has become a worldwide sensation this past month while people everywhere have been social distancing due to the coronavirus pandemic.

The second of Tiger King’s seven episodes centered on the July 1997 disappearance of Don Lewis, Baskin’s second of three husbands and co-founder of Big Cat Rescue. A decade later, Baskin had Lewis declared legally dead and allegedly destroyed his two wills so that she could get his money.

Simpson watched “Tiger King” at his home in Florida this past Wednesday.

“Listen, I’ve had so many people on my case asking me to watch,” Simpson said. “I watched this show, and oh my God! Is America in this bad a shape? I watched about six episodes of this show and I couldn’t even believe what I was looking at.

“White people, what’s with you and wild animals? Leave them animals alone! This show is crazy. But it’s so crazy that you kind of keep watching.”

A day later, Simpson was back breaking rules, as he opted to pass on following social distancing during the coronavirus pandemic to play a round of golf.

“I actually played pretty well,” Simpson said. “Now it’s back home, back to the couch and back to television.”

So much for Simpson finding time to continue his decades-long search for the real killer of his late wife, Nicole Brown Simpson, and her friend, Ron Goldman. Both were stabbed to death in the Brentwood section of Los Angeles in June 1994.

Unlike Joe Exotic, the main character in “Tiger King”who founded and ran a big cat zoo in Oklahoma, Simpson stopped short of blaming the murder on Baskin.

Exotic, whose real name is Joe Schreibvogel, currently is serving a 22-year federal prison sentence for animal abuse violations and plotting to kill Baskin.

Exotic, the gun-toting, mullet-wearing big cat collector, breeder and zoo keeper, launched his murder-for-hire scheme in November 2017. That was one month after Simpson was paroled from a Nevada prison after serving nine years of a 30-year sentence for criminal conspiracy, kidnapping, assault, robbery and using a deadly weapon for a crime committed at a Las Vegas casino hotel.

Simpson, 72, was charged in 1994 of murdering his wife and Goldman, but in October 1995 was acquitted in the so-called Trial of the Century. In February 1997, he was found responsible in a wrongful death civil suit.

Simpson, by the way, apparently won’t be recommending “Tiger King” to anyone, and he told his 952,000 Twitter followers, “Take care and I hope that you find something better to watch.”
