The Liberator Would've Been A Perfect Band Of Brothers Sequel

The Liberator tells a powerful World War II story that could've been the ideal follow-up to Band of Brothers and The Pacific. Based on Alex Kershaw's eponymous 2012 novel, the four-part Netflix adaptation follows American soldier Felix Sparks and the 157th regiment's 500-mile journey through Europe. Given the scope of the narrative, along with the diversity of the focal military unit, there's more than enough material for a proper 10-episode live-action series, one that would've provided a more detailed account of Sparks' experiences during World War II.

For storytelling purposes, Netflix and series creator Jeb Stuart focus on four main talking points in The Liberator. The first episode introduces "The Thunderbirds" — the 157th regiment with southwestern American roots — and revolves Operation Husky, otherwise known as the Invasion of Sicily. The second episode jumps ahead several months to the devastating Battle of Anzio, in which Sparks emerged as the only survivor of his unit. For episode three, The Liberator focuses on Operation Dragoon in France, and the series finale follows Sparks in Germany as the war comes to a close. After watching the Netflix miniseries, viewers may be reminded of Tom Hanks and Steven Spielberg's outstanding HBO series Band of Brothers and The Pacific, both of which are so incredibly impactful because of the historical authenticity and attention to detail.

The Liberator was reportedly supposed to be a live-action series but eventually turned into a condensed animated production. As a result, the filmmakers naturally had to pick and choose what to focus on thematically, which means that so much contextual information is left out. For example, Band of Brothers begins with Easy Company training in Camp Toccoa, Georgia, which emotionally preps the audience for the subsequent episode about D-Day, or the Invasion of Normandy (Operation Overlord). From there, Band of Brothers follows Easy Company through France and eventually into Germany, covering several key battles along the way. The characters are defined; the motivations are clear. The series is centered on war, of course, but it's the relationship building and character development that establishes the tone for each battle. The Liberator on Netflix covers the backstories of a few characters, but there's so much more to explore.

✕ Remove Ads

Just as The Pacific offers a different look at World War II by focusing on three U.S. soldiers from the 1st Marine Division, The Liberator similarly tells a unique story with the inherent diversity of the 157th regiment. The Netflix series acknowledges that the unit was comprised of Apache, Seminole, Cherokee, Sioux, Choctaw, and Mexican Americans — the problem, however, is that the stories can't be fully explored due to time constraints. Furthermore, the animation offers a unique viewing experience but doesn't necessarily allow the Netflix audience to fully connect with the characters. A more thorough look at the 157th regiment would've made the Battle of Anzio sequence even more impactful.

✕ Remove Ads

The story told in The Liberator is thematically similar to Band of Brothers, The Pacific, and the upcoming Masters of the Air — the latest Hanks/Spielberg World War II miniseries which centers on the Eighth Air Force of the U.S. Army Air Forces. Ideally, The Liberator would've been a 10-part series that spent the first half focusing on training and the Italian campaign, and then shifted to Sparks' experiences in France and Germany through five more episodes. A full live-action adaptation would've allowed for a different angle, both narratively and geographically, as the 157th regiment arrived in Sicily and traveled north, as opposed to troops who landed in France and went south. As for Sparks, The Liberator on Netflix would've ideally dedicated more time to his pre-war experiences with wife Mary, along with his arrival in North Africa before Operation Husky commenced.
