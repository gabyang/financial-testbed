Netflix's Too Hot To Handle was inspired by the classic, risqué-at-the-time season 4 Seinfeld episode "The Contest." Yes, Kramer and Francesca have something in common believe it or not.

✕ Remove Ads

"The Contest" involves a bet, in which the four main characters, Jerry, Elaine, George and Kramer make a bet of who can go the longest without self-gratification. It was a bold idea to put on television in the early 1990s, which makes it one of the more memorable episodes during the show's near decade-long run. "The Contest," which originally aired on November 18, 1992, involves the group throwing $450 in the pot. Jerry, George and Kramer each forked over $100, while Elaine was required to offer up $150 because the men thought it would be easier for her. The rules weren't exactly like Too Hot To Handle's. They were allowed to hook up with other people. Only Jerry had a romantic partner in this episode. A woman who was a virgin had agreed to have sex with Jerry, until he told her about the bet. Then, she stormed off angrily. Kramer was the first to lose, tempted to pleasure himself to the thought of a naked woman across who was visible from Jerry's apartment. Elaine fell next, unable to resist the thought of John F. Kennedy Jr.'s butt. By the end of the episode, Jerry and George had both failed as well.

The Too Hot To Handle cast had a better success rate than the Seinfeld foursome of holding out for the money. The concept of the hit Netflix series was designed by show developer Laura Gibson, who told Entertainment Weekly she was inspired by "The Contest." There were some tweaks of course.Too Hot To Handle scoured the earth for the thirstiest, hottest humans on the planet and tossed them onto one island. They weren't allowed to self-gratify, but they were also forbidden from any lip-locking, heavy petting and hanky panky with one another.

Showrunner Viki Kolar told EW she likened the different prices for each type of indiscretion to a mini bar. She compared the watches that light up green to a fit bit. She said, "It's like, I've done my 10,000 steps a day. It's like your little champion."Even though the show drew upon many different sources to create one fleshed out product, did it work as a cohesive unit?
