Fly Intel: What to watch in Comcast earnings report
Fly Intel
Jessica de Sa-Mota - email
Benchmark sees Tokyo Olympics as a potential boost for Comcast's Peacock

Comcast (CMCSA; CMCSK) is expected to report second quarter results before market open on Thursday, July 28, with a conference call scheduled for 8:30 am ET. What to watch for:

1. OLYMPICS BOOST FOR PEACOCK:Benchmark analyst Matthew Harrigan raised the firm's price target on Comcast to $72 from $70, while keeping a Buy rating on the shares, telling investors that he thinks the Tokyo Olympics should "further acclimate existing and likely many new Peacock users to the superior viewing features" of advertising-based video on demand relative to linear channels. His increased target reflects both a slight upward forecast revision and a higher assumed P/E multiple, Harrigan noted.

Earlier this month, Raymond James analyst Frank Louthan has also raised the firm's price target on Comcast to $65 from $63, keeping an Outperform rating on the shares ahead of second quarter results. Louthan believes there is a continued theme of NBCU upside from Peacock subscriber growth, improved theatrical revenue, and phased theme park re-openings, as well as continued HSD strength. Further, he feels any Title II-related fallout likely won't join the fold until late 2022.

2. MOVIE STREAMING: Beginning in 2022, theatrical releases from Universal will debut on Comcast's new streaming service Peacock no later than four months after their theatrical premiere, The Wall Street Journal's Erich Schwartzel and Joe Flint reported. The movies will be exclusively on Peacock for four months and after Universal will have the opportunity to make them available on a rival streaming service for 10 months, before reverting back to Peacock for another four months.

Meanwhile, Variety's Brent Lang reported that Netflix (NFLX) and Universal Filmed Entertainment Group are extending an agreement that will give the streaming giant exclusive rights in the U.S. to the media company's animated feature films. Under the agreement, Netflix's window to stream upcoming animated films such as "Puss in Boots: The Last Wish" or "The Bad Guys" will begin after a four-month window on Peacock, the author noted.

3. M&A SPECULATION: Shares of Comcast were in the spotlight last month following a report by The Wall Street Journal's Lillian Rizzo. According to the publication, Comcast's CEO Brian Roberts had told people close to him that he doesn't feel a need to seek a merger as his company builds out its streaming service, Peacock. However, he was "scoping out options" and ideas on the table include a potential tie-up with ViacomCBS (VIAC) or an acquisition of Roku (ROKU), one source reportedly told Rizzo.

Commenting on The Wall Street Journal's report, JPMorgan analyst Philip Cusick said he believes Comcast is an "unlikely buyer of anything in the near to medium-term" and that he would take advantage of the any weakness ahead of the likely "strong" second quarter numbers. The analyst continues to like most cable shares with Comcast one of his favorites.
