7 Suspenseful British Dramas To Watch After You Finish ‘Behind Her Eyes’

The six-episode limited Netflix NFLX series Behind Her Eyesquickly jumped to No. 1 in the Top 10 TV shows in the U.S. after its February 17 premiere. The twists and turns were plentiful in this deeply layered supernatural tale based on the bestselling novel of the same name by Sarah Pinborough.

The story follows a dangerously seductive affair involving Louise (Simona Brown), a single mother who has a relationship with her married psychiatrist boss David (Tom Bateman). Things really get complicated when she gets entangled in an inescapable web of secrets, lies and betrayals after befriending his wife Adele (Eve Hewson).

This gripping thriller is truly a nonstop ride with an unexpected twist at the end. So, what can viewers seeking another mystery watch now? Here are seven suspenseful British dramas to binge after Behind Her Eyes.

The Stranger -Hannah John-Kamen stars in this eight-episode series as a mysterious woman who seems to know everyone’s deepest, darkest secrets and uses them to get what she needs no matter the cost. When a man’s wife goes missing soon after she approaches him, an investigation is launched into who this woman really is. Of course, as one question is answered, many more come to the surface. This series is based on Harlan Coben’s novel of the same name.

Criminal: UK (aka Criminal: United Kingdom) - This British police procedural anthology series was created by George Kay and Jim Field Smith and stars David Tennant and Kit Harington. Netflix released the first season of three episodes in September 2019 and the second season of four episodes followed a year later. This is part of the anthology series Criminalwhich had a 12-episode first season with three episodes each set in four countries and filmed in local languages entitled Criminal: France, Criminal: Spain, Criminal: Germany and Criminal: UK.The story is set exclusively within the confines of a police interrogation room where London investigators engage in intense games of psychological cat-and-mouse with their suspects to find the answers they need to solve their cases.

Bodyguard - This six-episode series originally premiered on BBC One in 2018 and quickly became a fan favorite across the pond before making its way stateside. Set in the corridors of power, the story follows David Budd (Richard Madden), a heroic but volatile war veteran now working as a Specialist Protection Officer for the Royalty and Specialist Branch of London’s Metropolitan Police Service. After stopping a terrorist attack, he’s assigned to protectthe ambitious and powerful Home Secretary Julia Montague (Keeley Hawes) and soon finds himself torn between his duty and his beliefs.

White Lines- This 10-episode British-Spanish mystery-thriller was created by Álex Pina and follows the story of a woman searching for the truth about the death of her brother. When the body of a legendary Manchester DJ is found 20 years after his mysterious disappearance from Ibiza, his sister leaves her quiet life behind and returns to the beautiful Spanish island to find out what happened. Her investigation will lead her through a thrilling world of dance clubs, lies and cover-ups. She will be forced to confront the darker sides of her own character in a place where people live life on the edge. The series was released in May of 2020 and canceled by the streamer after just one season.

The One (Coming March 12) -Love and lies spiral in this eight-episode drama when a DNA researcher helps discover a new way to find the perfect partner with the creation of a bold new matchmaking service. Can you imagine how much time and energy singletons the world over could save if the endless swiping of online dating ceased to exist? There is hope for such a reality in this new series based on John Marrs’ book entitled The One: A Novel. It begs the question: What if we could find our perfect partner through DNA matching? The story is set five minutes in the future in a world where a DNA test can find your perfect partner, the one person you’re genetically predisposed to fall passionately in love with. No matter how good your relationship, which one of us can honestly say we haven’t thought about whether there is someone better out there? What if a hair sample is all it takes to find them? The idea is simple, but the implications are explosive.

The Irregulars (Coming March 26) - This eight-episode British series is set in Victorian London and follows a gang of troubled street teens who are manipulated into solving crimes for the sinister Doctor Watson and his mysterious business partner, the elusive Sherlock Holmes. As the crimes take on a horrifying supernatural edge and a dark power emerges, it'll be up to them to come together to save not only London but the entire world. The cast includes Thaddea Graham, Darci Shaw, Jojo Macari, McKell David, Harrison Osterfield, Royce Pierreson and Henry Lloyd-Hughes. The series was written, created and executive produced by Tom Bidwell and executive produced by Drama Republic’s Jude Liknaitzky and Greg Brenman, with producer Rebecca Hodgson. Johnny Allan is the lead director, with Joss Agnew and Weronika Tofilska also directing.

The Serpent(Coming April 2)- This incredible true-crime drama is already a critically-acclaimed sensation in the UK and it deserves all the praise it’s receiving. The eight-episode limited series is inspired by shocking true events and unravels the complicated story of conman and serial killer Charles Sobhraj (Tahar Rahim) and the remarkable attempts to bring him to justice. In the 1970s, this merciless murderer would prey on travelers exploring the "hippie trail" of South Asia. He posed as a gem dealer and traveled with his girlfriend Marie-Andrée Leclerc (Jenna Coleman) across Thailand, Nepal and India in 1975 and 1976, carrying out a spree of crimes before becoming the chief suspects in a series of murders of young Western travelers. When Herman Knippenberg (Billy Howle), a junior diplomat at the Dutch Embassy in Bangkok, unwittingly walks into this intricate web of crime, he and his wife Angela (Ellie Bamber) start their own investigation. Eventually, the pair are joined by police around the world as well as witnesses of Sobhraj’s devious manipulation. What transpires is an extraordinary chain of events that will see Sobhraj become Interpol’s most wanted man, with arrest warrants in multiple continents. Produced by Mammoth Screen, part of ITV Studios, The Serpent is a co-production between BBC One and Netflix. It is written by Richard Warlow and Toby Finlay, directed by Tom Shankland and Hans Herbots, produced by Stephen Smallwood, and executive produced by Warlow, Shankland, Preethi Mavahalli and Damien Timmer for Mammoth Screen. The plot is inspired by a true story, however, all dialogue is fictionalized.
