The Future Of Bollywood: Blending With Streaming Media

In 2019, Statista reported that there were nearly 3,000 multiplexes in India with a 2019 revenue of nearly INR 142 billion ($1.89 billion). These revenues were projected to go up to INR 152 billion in 2020. Meanwhile, in 2018, nearly 2 billion Indians bought movie tickets to watch nearly 1,800 movies.

Everything came to halt on March 25 when the COVID-19 lockdown was announced in India. Now, initial reports by CNN and the Indian Express EXPR estimate box office and related losses in the tune of $300 million. Bollywood needs a strategy.

The future will include creative ways of delivering entertainment content to the Bollywood audience who found an alternative to the theater during the lockdown.

Adoption of Streaming

A large portion of audiences with access to the internet moved to Over-the-Top (OTT) digital streaming services that would channel content to a personal digital device. Based on a survey done by Velocity MR, the New Indian Express reported on May 20: “Amazon Prime and Netflix saw an increase in subscription with 67 per cent and 65 per cent, respectively.” This was just within the period of the lockdown between mid-March to mid-April.

OTT is a relatively new thing to the audience, but the closure of the movie theaters catapulted it to a new position and the audience accepted this alternative. The success of OTT in the period prior to the lockdown was amplified by the lockdown, and a new normal emerged for the audience. The jury is out on whether the audience will ditch OTT when theaters open, and return to a socially distanced movie theater.

There is a good chance that Bollywood will have to develop strategies to meet the needs of the audience who is now accustomed to the diversity in content choice that is available at home.

Direct to Streaming

The first strategy is to offer movies directly via streaming services without waiting for the customary length of time after the theatrical release. One of the most striking example was the much discussed June 12 exclusive Amazon global launch of Gulabo Sitabo, starring the iconic Amitabh Bachchan. Other major names in the industry such as Nawazuddin Siddiqui are also in favor of the simultaneous release or release to OTT only.

Direct to streaming release is being done by other filmmakers, as previously reported by Forbes.com contributor Sweta Kaushal: “Jyothika's Ponmagal Vandhal, Keerthy Suresh's Penguin, Siri Prahlad's Law, Jayasuruya's Sufiyum Sujatayum are other major Indian films that be launched online instead of getting a theatrical release.”

The audience may expect movies to be delivered by OTT even while they are being released in theaters.

Made-for-Streaming Content

The second strategy is the creation of original content primarily for digital streaming. This trend started with shows like Sacred Games (Netflix, 2018) and Mirzapur (Amazon Prime, 2018). These popular shows demonstrated that there is a large audience for a format that serializes the story using shorter episodes compared to the traditional three-hour long Bollywood movies.

The new content will have to replicate what the audience has become accustomed to while maintaining the glamour of Bollywood. Amazon has made a significant stride in this direction, as reported by Forbes, by signing Bollywood star Akshay Kumar for Amazon original content. The key here is rethinking the content and match the expectation of the audience that is showing the preference for the OTT program format.

Theater Enhancement

The third strategy is the development of a unique theater experience with the incorporation of better technology in the theaters such as 3D, MX4D, etc. This is what Hollywood dealt with years ago with the rapid development of TV in the USA. Technologies such as CinemaScope, Dolby sound, 3D were developed to entice the TV audience back to the theater. Now in the USA there are theaters with reclining seats and dinner served at the seat.

Similar changes are happening in the theaters in India where the experience of going to the theater will be technologically dazzling to get the audience back when they are ready to take a break from the OTT viewing. It is important to offer an experience that is unlike what can be achieved with a TV screen in the bedroom or a smartphone on the couch.

As Bollywood addresses the losses of 2020, there is an investment in a blended approach: creating a balanced mix of new and traditional types of content available for a combination of home and theater viewing. Eventually, several different technologies of content delivery will work together to distribute a large spectrum of content to an increasingly discerning audience.
