Colin Kaepernick featured in 6-part series coming to Netflix

  • Updated: Jun. 29, 2020, 2:59 p.m.
  • |Published: Jun. 29, 2020, 11:19 a.m.
By
  • Geoffrey C. Arnold | The Oregonian/OregonLive

Colin Kaepernick is still hoping to join a NFL team, but the free agent quarterback will be busy as he waits for a call from a league executive.

In the meantime, Kaepernick will team with acclaimed director Ava Duvernay to produce a six-part series on Netflix titled “Colin in Black & White,” that will focus on Kaepernick’s high school years.

The series will explore the beginnings of Kaepernick’s activism, the influences and experiences that helped developed the social conscience of a young Kaepernick, eventually leading him to him kneeling during the national anthem during NFL games to protest police brutality in 2016.

He would grow up to play in the Super Bowl and realize you never stop fighting for your dreams.

She would grow up to tell stories that matter to millions.

Kaepernick is an executive producer and will narrate the series, which will have an actor playing him as a youth as a Black kid adopted into a white family.

“Too often we see race and Black stories portrayed through a white lens,” Kaepernick said in a press release. “We seek to give new perspective to the differing realities that Black people face. We explore the racial conflicts I facedas an adopted Black man in a white community, during my high school years. It’s an honor to bring these stories to life in collaboration with Ava for the world to see.”

Kaepernick, 32, played six seasons with the San Francisco 49ers, but has not played in the NFL since 2016, when he began kneeling during the national anthem.

Michael Starrbury will write the series and re-team with Duvernay, after the two collaborated on the Emmy-nominated series “When They See Us” on Netflix. Duvernay also directed the 2014 film “Selma.”
