The London boroughs with the best and worst broadband internet speeds

If your internet speeds are slow, it could be down to where you live

    With millions of Londoners now having to work from home, the internet connection we can boast about - or maybe not in many cases - has become one of our most important assets.

    In the age of the lockdown, we have all had that embarrassing moment on a conference call where the call lags or our internet fails just as we are about complete a crucial and painstakingly long task.

    Unfortunately not all internet is equal. While we are way past the days of dial-up internet and speeds are increasing all the time, the demands that our internet use places on the network also increase.

    Netflix recently confirmed it had to lower streaming quality across Europe due to the amount of bandwidth we were occupying binging Tiger King and Too Hot to Handle as we stay indoors to try and beat the virus.

    If you have moved house frequently in London you might also notice that different areas have different internet packages available. And with more internet connected devices in our homes ranging from phones and laptops to Smart TVs, games consoles and doorbells with cameras, having the right internet connection is more important than ever.

    First some jargon busting. Bandwidth is a way of measuring the maximum amount of data can flow through a connection. The higher the bandwidth, the faster your item will download, although it is not a measure of the "speed" of the internet.

    The speed of your internet is how fast the data can be downloaded, or uploaded, from your computer or device. Bandwidth looks at how much data can be downloaded.

    Internet speed is constantly changing and can be limited by factors like the infrastructure, how far you are from the server, your router or the number of people in your area connected to the internet with the same internet provider.

    Fibre optic broadband is much faster than traditional copper wire connections, but is not yet available across the capital or the rest of the country. It usually also costs more to have fibre-optic broadband.

    Holly Niblett, Head of Digital at comparethemarket, said: “With the UK on lockdown, internet connectivity is more important than ever, whether it be for working from home or streaming movies and TV shows.

    "While it is encouraging that broadband speed has improved in some parts of the country, there is still some way to go.

    “Whilst the government recently pledged to increase broadband infrastructure spending, there are still hundreds of thousands of homes without decent broadband and the vast majority of these are in remote areas.

    "With a significant number of people now working from home, lack of connectivity could be a serious cause for concern.”

    So what is the average broadband speed in your area? Comparethemarket compiled the data for each London borough from 2019.

    Richmond upon Thames can boast having the fastest broadband in London, while Westminster and the City of London are at the lower end. Across London, the average is 66.7 megabits per second (Mbit/s)
