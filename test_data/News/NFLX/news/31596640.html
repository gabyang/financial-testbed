Netflix's The Old Guard centers on a group of immortal warriors living in the shadows of the present day. Here's everything you need to know about them, and the mythology behind their powers. Created Greg Rucka and Leandro Fernández, the story began as a comic book series. Released in 2017, The Old Guard Vol 1: Opening Fire was published by Image Comics and ran for five issues. Well-received by critics and comic book fans alike, Rucka and Fernández have since produced a follow-up, The Old Guard Vol 2: Force Multiplied. Despite some delays due to the coronavirus pandemic, the second five-issue run concluded in mid-July 2020.

The rights to the comic book series were acquired by Skydance Media in March 2017. It was later announced that Netflix would co-financeThe Old Guard and handle the worldwide distribution. Rucka himself returned to write the script for the film. In July 2018, Gina Prince-Bythewood signed on to direct,  becoming the first black woman to helm a major comic book film. As well as starring in the lead role, Charlize Theron also served as a producer. The rest of the cast included KiKi Layne, Harry Melling, and Matthias Schoenaerts.

Released July 10, 2020, The Old Guarddebuted to positive reviews. The film was especially praised for its compelling characters and thrilling action. It has proven equally popular with viewers - occupying the #1 slot on Netflix's Top 10 for a week after its release. Set in modern times, The Old Guard follows five immortals as they find themselves hunted by sinister forces eager to harness their powers for profit. Along the way, many questions emerge regarding the immortals' powers and the history behind them. Though some things were left firmly in the air, there were also some big reveals. Here's a breakdown of everything we know about The Old Guard's mythology and characters.

The Old Guard Mythology & World Explained

✕ Remove Ads

Nile Freeman serves as something of an audience surrogate in The Old Guard. When recruited by Andy, Nile has the usual questions that come with immortality. Unfortunately, Andy herself is largely in the dark and still learning even after millennia wandering the Earth. It's unclear whether they are born immortal or merely acquire it at a certain point. Equally unknown is how it's decided who will be immortal, with the only common trait being an initial desire to use their powers for the good of others. What is known, however, is that superficial wounds and injuries such as broken bones heal almost instantly. Bigger wounds - such as being caught point-blank by a grenade blast - tend to take longer. They can also suffer fatal injuries and seem functionally dead for an extended period. They ultimately always return, however. Unless their powers have faded away, that is...

According to Andy, their immortality is finite. Without rhyme or reason, there will always come a time when their powers are simply gone. One day they will simply get an injury and it won't heal anymore. As a result, there is still a sense of danger, as each battle could still prove to be their last. Also demonstrated via one of the character's backstories, the powers cannot be transferred or directly acquired in some way - as much as some loved ones may crave it or an immortal may want to share it.

✕ Remove Ads

The source of their immortality itself remains shrouded in mystery by the end of The Old Guard. Despite that, the film does convey that there is a higher force at play and an element of destiny to proceedings. As revealed by James Copley (Chiwetel Ejiofor), each person the group has saved over the centuries has gone on to have a profound and positive effect on humanity. The ripples are sometimes direct, or sometimes come about due to descendants of those they save. They range from technological advancements and groundbreaking medical discoveries to merely helping to avert a grand disaster.

Every Immortal In The Old Guard

✕ Remove Ads
  • Andromache of Scythia (a.k.a. Andy) - Played by Theron, Andy is the oldest of the immortals and the squad's leader. Born around 4700 B.C in Central Eurasia, Andy was originally revered as a god. Later, she spent millennia fighting for humanity while simultaneously seeing them at their worst. As a result, Andy is plagued by a crisis of faith regarding her purpose. Regardless, she leads the charge when two of her own are captured and a new immortal emerges. Both situations are made all the more complicated when she experiences a monumental shift in her powers.
  • Nile Freeman - Played by Layne, Nile is the newest immortal to emerge. Initially serving as a US Marine in Afghanistan, Nile's powers emerge after she is fatally stabbed in the neck. As a result of her miraculous survival, she is met with suspicion and distrust from her friends and fellow soldiers. Recruited by Andy, Nile is initially apprehensive about joining the team, not wanting to leave behind her family and everything she knows.
✕ Remove Ads
  • Sebastian Le Livre (a.k.a. Booker)- Played by Schoenaerts, Booker was the youngest immortal before the arrival of Niles. Just over 200 years old, the French native once served as a soldier in Napoleon's army before being "executed" as a deserter. Having had to witness the deaths of his family - including his youngest son to cancer - Booker is already tired of eternal life. Eager to see their immortality used more directly to aid humanity or be taken away altogether, Booker ultimately makes some questionable decisions.
  • Yusuf Al-Kaysani (a.k.a. Joe)- Played by Marwan Kenzari, Joe was a Muslim warrior during the Crusades. Fiercely loyal and devoted, his immortality was discovered on the battlefield when he and Nicky killed each other. When they both resurrected, the pair continued to kill each other over and over again. After an undisclosed time, however, the passion turned from hate to love, and they began a centuries-spanning romance.
✕ Remove Ads
  • Nicoló di Genova (aka. Nicky)- Played Luca Marinelli, Nicky is Joe's enemy-turned-lover from The Crusades. Nicky is deemed the beating heart and most lovingly compassionate of the group. His and Joe's capture by Merrick's forces spurs Andy into action and marks Nile's trial-by-fire introduction to being an immortal warrior.  It also sparks one of the film's most celebrated moments when Joe declares the depth of his love for Nicky.
  • Quynh-Played by Veronica Ngo, Quynh is revealed to be the first immortal that Andy met, and is described as a "pit viper in a fight". Together they traveled and battled for centuries before both being caught in England during the witch trials. Tried and executed repeatedly, Quynh ultimately suffered a fate worse than death when she was sealed inside an iron maiden and thrown into the sea. Though Andy spent decades searching for her, she was unsuccessful. The Old Guard's pre-credits scene, however, reveals Quynh's actual fate and helps to set up the potential and infinitely more personal sequel.
✕ Remove Ads
  • Lykon - Played by Micheal Ward, Lykon is only briefly touched on during The Old Guard. He is fondly remembered by Andy as a companion that traveled with her and Quynh for a time. He is also revealed to have succumbed to wounds sustained in battle centuries before the events of the film. As such, Lykon serves as a visual demonstration that despite being functionally immortal, the heroes' powers will eventually fade and that they can indeed die.

How The Old Guard Movie Compares To The Comic

✕ Remove Ads

With Rucka adapting his own source material, The Old Guardis extremely faithful to the comic book series. Although some of it is delivered by a different character on the screen, much of the dialogue is taken verbatim from the page. This was important to Rucka, especially regarding the declaration of love from Joe to Nicky. In Vulture's interview with Prince-Bythewood, it was even revealed that Rucka had it in his contract that the scene had to occur in the film no matter what. Like the dialogue, some of the story's timeline was rearranged, but is just as faithful. The reveal that the squad's heroics have benefited humanity at large doesn't occur until the second comic book volume. The same goes for the reappearance of Quynh. The decision to bring her return forward was to convey that there was still more story to tell - with Rucka having ideas for a full trilogy.

✕ Remove Ads

Quynh actually marks one of The Old Guard's biggest changes. In the comics, the character was named Noriko and was actually of Japanese descent. When Ngo was cast, however, she pointed out that she was Vietnamese, not Japanese. Not content with merely grouping all Asians as one group, Prince-Bythewood asked Rucka whether that could be accommodated. More than happy to oblige, the character was rewritten to be Vietnamese and Noriko became Quynh. An element of her backstory was also changed. In the comics, she is merely washed overboard during a storm. In the film, her being cast into the sea is more an act of cruelty than a random act of God. In an interview with Polygon, Rucka revealed that the change was Prince-Bythewood's idea and was incorporated to "highlight just how awful people can be." With that idea hanging over several points of the film, Quynh served as the embodiment of it. Equally, it would narratively set up her future agenda of revenge and wanting to punish rather than protect humanity.

✕ Remove Ads

The other biggest change was having Andy lose her powers in the film's third act. According to Prince-Bythewood, this was to take the idea that the next battle could be a character's last to a more visual and visceral level. It also served to dramatically increase the tension, with the other immortals having to actively protect Andy mid-combat. It also served to lead directly into some scenes unique to the film, through which Andy could rediscover the compassionate side of humanity and further regain faith in her purpose to fight on their behalf. As a result of the changes, the eventual clash between Andy and Quynh in future installments won't be entirely black and white but instead an infinitely more nuanced ideological clash. Furthermore, with Rucka conceding that there's more obligation to answer questions and The Old Guardalready surpassing the comic book's revelations,there's every chance that more of the mythology will be explored in the future sequels.
