André Holland developing ‘High Flying Bird’ sequel: Exclusive

  • Published: May. 18, 2020, 9:47 a.m.
By
  • John Hammontree | jhammontree@al.com

André Holland is developing a sequel to his acclaimed film, High Flying Bird, he confirmed in the latest episode of the Reckon Interview, a weekly culture podcast.

The 2019 Netflix film, directed by Steven Soderbergh and filmed entirely by iPhone, follows the efforts of sports agent Ray Burke to help his clients navigate an NBA lockout.

In response to a question about how Burke would approach the NBA shutdown spurred by coronavirus, Holland divulged that he’s in conversations about a sequel.

“In my imagination, I think Ray would, first of all, want to make sure that his clients are good, right. That everybody's taken care of financially. Everybody's fine, you know, families are okay and health wise,” Holland said.

“Now, once that was done, I think that he would probably be using this as an opportunity to try and further level the playing field. For me so much of what the book is about. And what we wanted the movie to be about is this balance of power, labor and management, who gets to be the one that calls the shots.”

André Holland on ‘The Eddy,’ restoring a theater in Bessemer, and how the South shaped his art

This week on the Reckon Interview, we’re talking with Andre Holland, one of the most talented and thoughtful actors in the business. You’ve seen him in movies like 42, High Flying Bird, Selma, and Moonlight, or TV shows like The Knick and Castle Rock.

“And it's funny you bring that up because the call I was on earlier and then have a thing later today to talk about this this very thing. We're working on a sequel to the film. I haven't told anybody that... but working on that. So yeah, I think hopefully, once we get this made, we'll be able, I'll be able to answer that question with a 90-minute movie.”

Holland said that Soderbergh is involved in the discussions but it’s not clear if he will direct the sequel. Soderbergh is an Oscar-winning filmmaker best known for directing the “Ocean’s Eleven" trilogy, “Erin Brockovich” and “Traffic.” He and Holland previously collaborated on the Showtimes series “The Knick.”

“And I think all along you know, very early on…before we even like released High Flying Bird, we, we sort of knew we wanted...there was more to do, more to say. And we just wanted to plant the seed with High Flying Bird, but this next thing will actually be more all-encompassing. And even better, I hope.”
