Netflix, Stan, Binge: The best and worst of Australia’s biggest streaming services

Sunrise rounded out the streaming platforms available in Australia, what they offer and how much they cost.
By Shaun White

The Australian streaming war is heating up, with Binge becoming the latest competitor to join the battle for your eyeballs.

From Netflix and Stan to Amazon Prime Video, Apple TV+and free services like 7plus, we’re now spoilt for choice.

So how do they compare and which subscriptions deserve your hard-earned cash?

Sunrise Consumer Correspondent Shaun White has been investigating.

Netflix

The most well-known of the streaming services, Netflix has a stable of international films, TV series and documentaries as well as their own ‘originals,’ such as Stranger Things, Tiger King and The Crown.

It will cost you $9.99 a month to watch on one screen, $13.99 per month for two screens and $19.99 per month for four screens

Stan

Stan is Australia’s largest streaming service and offers hundreds of films, documentaries and TV series as well as original local productions.

It boasts partnerships with several US networks such as Showtime and MGM, so you can feast your eyes on hit shows like Billions, Homeland and Twin Peaks.

You can get a basic account for $10 a month, pay $14 a month for three screens or pay $17 a month for four screens.

Apple TV+

Apple TV+ launched in November last year and, unlike others, it only streams original Apple shows and movies, such as Morning Wars starring Jennifer Aniston and Oprah’s Book Club.

You get one year free when you buy an eligible Apple device or a subscription for $7.99 a month.

Amazon Prime Video

Most popular in the US, Amazon Prime Video offers a buffet of movies, television and original programming.

Its most well-known titles include The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel, The Man in the High Castle and Jack Ryan.

For people who do most of their shopping online, Amazon Prime is a no-brainer, as you get access with your Amazon account.

It’s the cheapest of the lot, too, sitting at $6.99 per month.

Disney+

Before its release, Disney+ was one of the most anticipated streaming services on the market.

And with a huge number of properties, from Marvel, to Star Wars, The Simpsons and every Disney movie and TV show, it’s not hard to see why.

At $8.99 per month, it’s among the cheapest of the streaming services, and a lot of the content is available in 4K resolution.

Binge

Binge, by Foxtel, is the latest service to join Australia’s streaming war.

One of its drawcards is much-discussed US shows like Succession and Game of Thrones from an agreement with cable channel HBO.

It also features more than 800 movies and exclusive shows from other major international providers such as WarnerBros, NBC and the BBC and.

However it’s library is much smaller than its competitors.

“It pales in comparison to Netflix, Stan or even Amazon Prime, - which have been around for much longer,” Tech Expert Geoff Quattromani told Sunrise.

Binge will set you back $10 a month for one stream and standard definition, $14 a month for two streams in HD content and $18 a month for four simultaneous HD streams.

Free Services

Before parting with your cash, it’s important to remember that there are a number of fantastic free options.

7plus, which offers all of the shows from Seven as well as additional extras, is the fastest-growing of all the broadcast video on-demand services.

Big Brother Australia, which starts next week, will be available to watch as soon as it has aired on TV.

“It’s really worth checking out before you sign up to one of the other services,” Sunrise Tech Expert Val Quinn said.

Streaming Tips

Most streaming platforms will offer a trial of up to one month before you need to start committing to a month-to-month plan

However, some people forget after signing up, meaning they end up getting charged.

If you’ve signed up for multiple trials, it can end up costing you hundreds each year.

“If you’re doing a free trial, note down when its going to finish and make sure that you actually cancel the service,” Val Quinn recommends.
