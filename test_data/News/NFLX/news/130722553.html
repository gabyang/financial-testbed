Election 2024: Where To Put Your Money Ahead of the Vote
Fly Intel
Laurie Pasternack Chan - email
Wedbush discusses the fallout on hardlines retailers should Donald Trump impose tariffs on imports

Election 2024: Where To Put Your Money Ahead of the Vote

In this recurring series, The Fly recaps where the top analysts on Wall Street say to put your money ahead of November's U.S. presidential election. In this edition, BofA puts out its picks for consumer stocks that win under a Trump or Harris presidency, while Wells Fargo discussed the election implications on the media and cable sector..

CONSUMER PICKS:This week, BofA put out its picks for consumer stocks that win under a Trump or Harris presidency. The firm said that while either party will likely enact tariffs, a Trump administration would prioritize trade re-alignment and broad-based tariffs. Higher China tariffs under Trump would be most negative for companies with high China import exposure including Floor & Decor (FND), Target (TGT), Dollar Tree (DLTR), and Williams Sonoma (WSM), though exposure is generally lower than 2018. BofA said tariff impact standouts include Constellation Brands (STZ), as it imports beer from Mexico, Elf Beauty (ELF), as it imports from China, and Brown-Forman (BFB), as it exports whiskey, though the firm thinks this is somewhat manageable. Relaxed regulatory standards are likely supportive of M&A and movement on Cannabis reform, the firm added. Publicly traded companies in the Cannabis space include  Aurora Cannabis (ACB), CV Sciences (CVSI), Canopy Growth (CGC), Cronos Group (CRON), Goodness Growth (GDNSF), Green Thumb Industries (GTBIF), IGC Pharma (IGC), Tilray (TLRY), Trees Corporation (CANN) and Trulieve Cannabis (TCNNF).

Additionally, BofA said lodging C-Corps could benefit from the extension or expansion of favorable corporate tax under Trump or be hurt by their expiry under Harris. A Democratic win could support low-end lodging and leisure consumption through tax breaks or stimulus, impacting Wyndham Hotels (WH), Choice Hotels (CHH), select-service REITs, and Las Vegas and Regional gaming companies, while a Republican victory could raise U.S.-China tensions and is a risk for Macau stocks. Further, Trump's proposed China tariffs would be a headwind for Leisure brands with sourcing exposure like Yeti (YETI), SharkNinja (SN), Mattel (MAT), and Hasbro (HAS), though the firm believes some companies have already taken steps to limit the impact through supply chain diversification.

Select-service REITs include Park Hotels & Resorts (PK), Pebblebrook Hotel Trust (PEB), Apple Hospitality REIT (APLE), and Host Hotels (HST), while Las Vegas and Regional gaming companies include DraftKings (DKNG), Boyd Gaming (BYD), Caesars (CZR), and Penn Entertainment (PENN). Macau stocks include Las Vegas Sands (LVS), MGM Resorts (MGM), Wynn Resorts (WYNN), and Melco Resorts (MLCO).


HARDLINES RETAILERS: Wedbush on Friday examined the implications of a Donald Trump victory and ensuing fallout on hardlines retailers should he impose a promised 10%-20% minimum tariff on all imports from every U.S. trading partner and a 60% or more tariff on imports from China. While some hardlines retail stocks have likely moved in recent days on higher prospects for a Trump victory, another band of movement is likely should prospects further increase and/or Trump win the election. Relative winners in this scenario include CarMax (KMX), Carvana (CVNA), Chewy (CHWY) and Petco (WOOF), while the firm sees relative losers as Best Buy (BBY), Wayfair (W), Williams-Sonoma, Floor & Decor, Dick's Sporting Goods (DKS) and Academy Sports (ASO). consumers. Wedbush believes most hardlines retailers will be able to cope with 10% import tariffs, splitting the costs with suppliers and passing along most cost increases to customers with a modest impact on demand, but thinks mitigation will become more difficult if the tariff rate jumps to 20% as former President Trump has also suggested. Further, a 60% tariff imposed on Chinese goods would be "untenable" for retailers and consumers, likely prompting even more supply chain reorganization to avoid these duties and a material decline in discretionary consumer spending as consumers cope with inflation.

Wedbush said Chewy and Petco have a modest level of import exposure as most hardgoods products come from overseas, but consumables products, which comprise most of their sales, should see limited impact from tariffs and fluctuations in consumer spending. CarMax and Carvana, Wedbush said, are best-positioned in the near- to medium-term with their used model inventory seeing an attractive price advantage relative to new vehicles. Home furnishings retailers are losers in a potential trade war, with Wedbush saying Wayfair, RH (RH), Williams-Sonoma, and Arhaus (ARHS) all have high levels of import exposure while also selling easily deferred products. The firm estimates Wayfair has the highest exposure to imports, creating 450 basis points of gross margin downside risk, but feels the company is capable of passing along vendor price increases while maintaining its take rate and protect margins. Home Depot (HD) and Lowe's (LOW) may also see a relatively modest hit, and Auto parts retailers Advance Auto Parts (AAP), Genuine Parts Company (GPC), AutoZone (AZO) and O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY) have material exposure to tariffs, the firm says. In the near- to medium-term, used auto retailers like CarMax and Carvana would not see a direct cost increase from tariffs while their used model inventory would see an attractive price advantage relative to new vehicles, Wedbush said.


MEDIA AND CABLE:On Friday, Wells Fargo discussed the election in relation to the media and cable sector, with the firm saying a Trump win should mean big positive stock reactions for Broadcast and value names, while a Harris win would mean a mid-single digit EPS impact to Comcast (CMSCA), Disney (DIS), and Netflix (NFLX). Under the last Republican FCC, Broadcast saw numerous transactions, with most of the firm's coverage scaling up including relaxing the Ultra-High Frequency, or UHF, discount, and a new Trump FCC is likely to aggressively deregulate. Tegna (TGNA) is the most obvious target, Wells said, while Sinclair (SBGI) and E.W. Scripps (SSP) could be station sellers. The firm noted that Broadcast rallies on Trump, while Tegna "may be off a bit" on Harris. Additionally, Trump's tax plan restores prior cash tax benefits, including 100% deductibility of bonus depreciation, expensing R&D and deducting more interest, which is most meaningful to stocks with the lowest P/FCF multiples, including AMC Networks (AMCX), Warner Bros. Discover (WBD), and Sirius XM (SIRI).

OTC BIRTH CONTROL:The Biden administration proposed a rule that would provide women with private insurance access to over-the-counter, OTC, birth control pills and contraceptives at no cost. This rule will expand a federal mandate requiring health insurers to cover preventative care services at no cost under the Affordable Care Act, is being proposed by the Departments of Health and Human Services, Labor, and the Treasury, and, if finalized, would come into effect in 2025. Currently, Perrigo's (PRGO) Opill is the only daily birth control pill approved for sale without a prescription by the U.S. FDA, but the proposed rule covers other forms of contraceptives, including emergency contraception, spermicides, birth control sponges, and condoms. The birth control access push comes just two weeks before the presidential election, in which family planning has been a major hot button. The next White House occupant will get to decide whether the proposal goes into effect. Democratic nominee Kamala Harris has made access to abortion and birth control central to her campaign, while Republican rival former President Donald Trump has appointed three of the conservative justices to the Supreme Court who ruled to overturn Roe v Wade in 2022, but has since said abortion laws should be determined by U.S. states, stopping short of proposing a national ban that could imperil his chances with swing voters in the November election. Other major birth control manufacturers include Merck (MRK), Pfizer (PFE), and Church & Dwight (CHD).


BLACKROCK: The outcome of the November election "really doesn't matter" for financial markets, BlackRock (BLK) CEO Larry Fink told a conference hosted by the Securities Industry and Financial Markets Association. "I'm tired of hearing this is the biggest election in your lifetime. The reality is over time it doesn't matter," Fink said, adding that more than half of the firm's $11.5T in assets under management were retirement funds. "Unfortunately, there's too much preoccupation with whether the market's going up or down at any one time, any one quarter," Fink said. "It really doesn't matter." The executive noted, however, "we work with both administrations and are having conversations with both candidates."


TRUMP MEDIA:Trump Media & Technology Group has soared on the stock market over the past month amid rising turmoil internally, The New York Times' Matthew Goldstein reported, citing people with knowledge of the matter. The company has dismissed or pushed out at least three senior managers in recent weeks after several workers anonymously sent a letter to the board accusing CEO Devin Nunes of mismanagement. In the letter, staff said Nunes' "directive" to hire foreign contractors was inconsistent with "the 'America First' principles we stand for" and noted Nunes has not formally addressed staff in two years, adding "this lack of transparency has left employees in the dark about the company's vision, strategy and future." Donald Trump owns about 59% of the company's stock.
