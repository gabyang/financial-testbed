My first brush with public speaking was in Kindergarten on Show-and-Tell day.

Getting in front of everyone was already terrifying, but it was especially brutal because I was presenting my nerdy hobby (Bionicles) to hyper-active, disinterested kids.

What’s worse, my last name starts with a “T” — I was almost always last in line for anything; most kids tuned out.

The result? 10 minutes of pure hell:

Me:“I like Bionicles because they’re creative and fun.”
Kids:*eyes glaze over* *visible yawning* *fidgeting with toes*

Luckily for me, I haven’t had to suffer my entire life as a speaker. In high school, I was part of a track where students focused on public speaking. And I learned that speeches could be incredible.

Being a good speaker could make the difference for your career— all it takes is captivating the right person. After many years of attending and giving speeches, the following are common themes I’ve found.
