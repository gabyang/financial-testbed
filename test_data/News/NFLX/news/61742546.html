Netflix signs deal with film-maker Rapman who survived being shot at as a youngster and whose film Blue Story was blamed for cinema machete riot as streaming giant says it wants to 'be force for good' by increasing diversity

  • Netflix announced it will increase diversity across its programming schedule
  • Move includes the commission of a raft of new shows from British filmmakers
  • It was announced Stormzy's 'cultural strategist' Akua Agyemfra, film maker Rapman and Sex Education writer Bisha K Ali will all produce content for Netflix
  • Streaming service said it would 'amplify' British writing and film-making talent

Film streaming service Netflix has signed a deal with British filmmaker Rapman whose film Blue Story was blamed for a outbreak of violence when it was screened in cinemas.  

London-born film maker Andrew Onwubolu, known as Rapman, has signed a deal with Netflix as part of their comittment to increase diversity and promote British talent.

A programme by Stormzy's 'cultural strategist' Akua Agyemfra is also among the new shows announced, as is a project with Sex Education writer Bisha K Ali.

The steaming service said it intends to increase diversity across its programming schedule as it aims to be a 'force for good'.

Netflix also said it would 'amplify' British writing and film-making talent around the world.

It hopes the content will meet the demands of a 'diverse audience' and attract younger viewers to the subscription service.

Filmmaker and music artist Rapman experienced gang warefare growing up in London and was shot at as a teenager.

His film Blue Story concerns two young men forming a friendship despite living in warring postcodes in London.

London-born film maker Andrew Onwubolu, known as Rapman, has also signed a deal with Netflix for more shows, it emerged as the streaming service announced its commitment to diversity. (Pictured at the 2019 world premiere of Blue Story at The Curzon Mayfair)

It was pulled from Vue cinemas last year after it was blamed for an outbreak of violence after a brawl broke out at Star City cinema in Birmingham when a 'group of people arrived with machetes'.

Five teenagers, including a 13-year-old girl, were arrested.

Rapman, now a pal of billionaire rapper Jay-Z, also produced the award-winning three part musical drama series Shiro's Story on YouTube.

Blue Story (cast pictured) is a tragic tale of a friendship between Timmy and Marco, two young boys from opposing postcodes in London

Netflix's focus on British programming follows the global success of UK shows including The Crown and Sex Education.

Anne Mensah, Netflix's vice-president of original series, told The Guardian the new show commissions are an 'indication of how seriously' the global giant takes the UK.

Mensah, who used to work for the BBC and joined Netflix in 2018, said: 'We've got to get to a place where if somebody is doing something that doesn't take an inclusive approach that should be surprising to you.

'It should be like washing your hands; it should be just what you do.'

The writer behind the highly acclaimed Sex Education, Bisha K Ali, series has also been comissioned to produce more content for Netflix

She teased on Twitter that she is looking forward to announcing the details of her collaboration with Netflix

Akua Agyemfra, Stormzy's 'cultural strategist', will also add to Netflix's raft of new shows, which it hopes will attract younger and more diverse audiences

In her first interview since joining Netflix, Mensah toldThe Guardianshe wanted to attract younger audiences and also identify and overcome the 'systematic barriers' in the film and TV industry. 

She added: 'Because our audience is diverse – why wouldn't you make shows for everyone around the world?

'It's so important to work with brilliant British talent and... it's important to support the industry and, as far as we can, be a force for good.'

Bisha K Ali teased on Twitter her excitement at what she has been 'cooking up'.

She wrote: 'Can't wait 'til (sic) we can fully announce what we've been cooking up together, and not just because I find secrets to be excruciating...'

Mr Bean creator Rowan Atkinson is also set to make a new show which will air on the streaming service.

The streaming service announced last month it would start declaring £1billion-plus UK revenues to the British taxman in January.

The streaming service announced last month it would start declaring £1billion-plus UK revenues to the British taxman in January

Since it launched in 2012, the streaming service has been funnelling profits from the UK through several accounts at the headquarters in the Netherlands.

The move is likely to add pressure on other tech firms to follow suit - other company giants such Amazon and Google are also known to funnel money through offshore tax havens.

Around half of the profits made from subscriptions in the UK, which cost between £5.99 to £11.99 a month, are said to be added to its content budget, which helped the service recently announce it would double its UK programme budget to £1bn.
