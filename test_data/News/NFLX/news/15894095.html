Life in Lockdown - we take a walk along one Grimsby street to see how they're coping

Wallis Court is a tight knit community with lots of different households having their own experiences through the coronavirus lockdown

    Two months into the coronavirus lockdown, it's safe to say that everyone's lives have been different.

    On March 23, Prime Minister Boris Johnson announced the strict measure to slow the spread of the coronavirus.

    We've all had to change the way we live - and we have no idea when things will be back to 'normal' - or if they ever really will be.

    Wallis Court on Scartho Top is a tight-knit community and has a wide variety of different people living there, from NHS workers, to families, to retirees living by themselves - all with different experiences of living in lockdown.

    The retiree

    Susan Davidson, a 70-year-old retiree, lives alone and has been supported a lot by her neighbours.

    She said: "Lockdown has been okay for me. I've been shopping once a week and beyond that I've stayed in and done as I've been told.

    "I've just stayed in the house and done a lot of housework and gardening, mostly.

    "Everyone on the street has been good, to be honest, they've stayed in and even asked me if I need anything, it's been really good.

    "I'm living here by myself now. My mum passed away last month so it's just me around the house.

    "She didn't pass away from coronavirus, she was 93 years old so she was a very old lady. It's definitely different being by myself though.

    "But everyone has been really supportive for me and tried to help me come into less contact with others. The hospital has been really good, they've brought me my medication and everything.

    "When I've driven to the hospital to pick it up too, they told me to just call them and they would come out to my car and bring me it.

    "Between them and my neighbours always checking in and making sure I have everything, it has really helped.

    "But even when I have to go shopping, I head out at about 7am and I'm only out for about half an hour, so I miss everyone and get everything I need. I only go to Morrisons as it's closest.

    "We'll just have to stay like this as long as we have to."

    The young family

    Up next were the Jackson family, with teaching assistant mum, Lisa, 39, staying at home with her children, Liam, 12, and Chloe, 10, and the family's Boxers Bonnie and Bruce, while her husband, Neil, 40, is a key worker with Scottish Power.

    Lisa said: "It's been alright, it's actually got better as it's gone along, really!

    "At first I was a bit worried and wanted to go back to work, but we've all settled in nicely and are enjoying the time we're spending together.

    "The schools have been very supportive for Liam and Chloe, too. They've been keeping them busy with plenty of work while they've been based at home.

    "We are having a lot more family time too, of course, which is nice. Everyone is rushing around a lot usually.

    "We're just taking it day to day, we have the dogs so we have been able to go out with them.

    "But we're all really lucky to have the technology that we do, so everyone can call and video call their friends and family to get through this.

    "I know Liam talks to his friends while he's playing video games too, which helps him stay in touch with them.

    "We're just happy carrying on at the moment. We were cracking a little bit, but getting a bit more freedom to go out more has helped us.

    The NHS worker

    Mandy Farrell, 49, is an assistant practitioner in mammography at the Pink Rose Suite at Diana, Princess of Wales Hospital.

    She lives on Wallis Court with her husband, Anthony, 50, who works off-shore on survey ships, and their dog, Alan.

    Mandy said: "I've been fine through lockdown, I've been working as normal doing Monday to Friday at work.

    "It's like every other day at work, too, you just have to wear more PPE and make sure we're protecting ourselves and the patients.

    "My husband is an off-shore worker, but he's been home while this has happened, so life hasn't been too significantly affected for either of us!

    "But when we have time to ourselves, we've been doing a lot of gardening and baking and tidying. I don't think the house has ever had so much work done to it!

    "My dad and step-mum are in isolation, too, so I've been doing their shopping and helping them out.

    "I think the hardest part for me is not being able to hug my son, who lives nearby. It's so difficult just not being able to be close to people.

    "Thank god for Facetime, I've got to say. It's kept me going, being able to see my friends and speak with them.

    "The street has been really helpful, too, we're all there for each other and keep an eye out for everyone."

    The taxi driver

    Another couple having to stop working through the lockdown were the King family, with Andrew, 57, and Tracey, 56, both taxi drivers that made the decision to stop taking fares through the coronavirus.

    Andrew said: "We haven't been working through the lockdown. We've been taxi drivers for the last 22 years now.

    "There's two reasons why we stopped through all this, one was the financial reason, as demand has decreased with less people going to work and going to school, so we were taking a massive loss in earnings compared to how we'd usually do.

    "The second was that the entire situation has made us quite jittery.

    "The day before we stopped, I did a fare and was picking up a regular from Asda along with his wife, who had just been shopping.

    "I helped him in the front of the car and then his wife got in the back.

    "It was then where I noticed she was moaning, and she was sweating and was clearly showing symptoms. When I asked her about it she said "we need to get food", when she clearly should have been at home.

    "So we decided it wasn't worth it, not for our health or our passengers, so we stopped working.

    "I've been doing housework, I think I've done just about everything the house needed doing to it while I've been off!"

    Andrew also spoke about returning to the job after the bank holiday weekend, but with a much different look to his car.

    He said: "I have tried fitting my car with velcro so I could attach some plastic sheets in the car as a shield from the back seat.

    "I've been working with the council about it and they've recommended doing that to protect us but without needing to fit any hard plastic in the car and put us at risk.

    "For some reason, it doesn't seem to be working and sticking to the lining in the car, so I'm not sure what to do with it at the moment.

    "At the very least, I'm going to have PPE in the car and have plenty of wipes and antibacterial hand wash too.

    "But honestly, one of the hardest things has been sorting PPE for the car. It's only this week that I've been able to get things as it's been sold out for months.

    "We've been using the savings to get by at the moment, but we can claim the self-employment money from the government which will make a big difference."

    The married couple

    The lockdown has not changed much for Hayley Williams, 48, who is a manager at Libertas Home Care in Louth.

    She lives with her husband, Mike, 56, who has recently retired, as is currently enjoying a week off away from work.

    Hayley said: "I'm a frontline worker so I have kept on as normal through the lockdown, really! I work in care, I am a manager at Libertas Home Care in Louth.

    "I'm actually on a well-earned week off now, not that we can do much.

    "My life is very much the same, going to work every day, but the community spirit around here is great, we are all out clapping every Thursday and we had a street party for VE day.

    "Before this, we hadn't done much since everyone first moved in here about 20 years ago, so us being closer with our neighbours is one benefit from all this, at least.

    "It's just been full on at work because of it all, and it is a really scary moment for all the staff at the moment.

    "But it's really shown how we are frontline workers and have spent a lot of this time working with the vulnerable and the elderly, as we do work out in the community.

    "My husband has actually just recently retired, so he's had it lucky, he's just been stuck inside. This week, for me, I'm just taking it easy and recharging the batteries before going back to work."

    The sixth form student

    One person who has been significantly affected by the coronavirus is Jacinta Watson, 18, who should be doing her A-levels at Toll Bar Sixth Form at the moment.

    She lives with her parents, who are two civil servants, and is concerned about the affect of the coronavirus on her education.

    Jacinta said: "I hate it, the lockdown, my family are all working at the moment so I'm just stuck at home.

    "I go to Toll Bar Sixth Form, and we are meant to be doing our A-levels right now which should have decided our final grades and whether or not I'll get into university.

    "I've been spending my time instead doing a lot of puzzles, colouring, and watching a lot of Netflix. I feel like I'm an old grandma!

    "I have also been doing some revision and school work too, but it's quite hard to work from home because you're not always able to concentrate as well as you would at school.

    "We don't have to do any exams anyway, now, our A-level exams have been cancelled so they are just using our predicted grades and current assignments that we've done so far and getting our results on results day as normal.

    "But if you're unhappy with them, you can resit it in October, but, of course, you would be wanting to start university then. I don't even know if we'll be able to start university as normal by then, either.

    "My mum and dad are civil servants so they a re working as normal. They have been telling me they're doing a lot more work than usual because of all this, but they are definitely appreciating the overtime!"

    The working parents

    Julie Gray, 46, has had to manage working from home in her role as head of corporate affairs for Navigo, while also helping her two children with schoolwork as her husband, Chris Wright, 49, works as a dog handler with Humberside Police.

    She said: "It's been really busy because I have been working from home at the moment. We have been doing plenty of Zoom meetings and everything alongside our usual work and it's been busy.

    "I have two kids who have to do their school work, too, so I have been helping them as well.

    "The community has been really good through this, though, we were originally asked to join a bingo game for VE day and we thought "why not" and went along.

    "But it ended up being a really nice day, with music and dancing and everyone enjoying themselves, while social distancing, of course.

    "We have also been out doing the Thursday NHS claps as well, where we've been able to talk to neighbours and see how everyone's been doing.

    "Thankfully we've been blessed with the weather while in lockdown, and we have a garden so we are able to enjoy it. I just feel sorry for all of the people in flats and apartments that can't go and enjoy the nice weather, it must be difficult for them.

    "It's been strange in lockdown, though. The first time we went out on a dog walk I felt really claustrophobic because I was thinking "this is the only time I'm allowed outside of the house today, just an hour long walk."

    "We were worried about going out to the supermarket and doing our shopping too, it just puts us all on edge, which is mad when you realise it's just going to the shops, something that you've done plenty of times before."

    The key workers with young adults at home

    It's been a house of mixed experiences for Deborah Cook's family, with Deborah, 52, and husband, Ian, 54, still working as key workers, and their two sons, Christian, 24, and Luke, 19, stuck at home.

    Deborah works at Diana, Princess of Wales Hospital in the operating department, while her husband works for Land & Water.

    Deborah said: "It's not been too bad for me as I've been able to go to work along with my husband.

    "But we have two young adult sons, too, one who works at the Rose and Crown so he's been furloughed, and the other who goes to North Lindsey College in Scunthorpe.

    "He is doing a bricklaying course and was meant to be doing a practical in March, but they had to cancel it. So they are just going off his mock exam and assessment work to get him a final grade.

    "I work as an operating department practitioner at the hospital, so life hasn't changed too much for me.

    "When we first went into lockdown we had to do a lot of training at work so we could work alongside the intensive care department and the coronavirus patients there.

    "At the hospital, I think the roles have changed there a bit too and you can tell by the way things work there at the moment.

    "My husband works at Land & Water, who go dredging rivers and looking after lakes and so on to stop them from flooding.

    "He goes all over the place with it, as far as Birmingham and Sheffield to Hull recently. The coronavirus stopped him travelling with work for a bit but they have started doing it again.

    "When we've been off, we've done a lot of walking with the dog, but we've also been doing a lot more community things with the neighbours.

    "We had a VE day street party, which was really lovely to see everyone enjoying themselves and spending time with people we wouldn't normally get time to see.

    "We've always been busy at work and in our own lives, but since this, we have spoken to neighbours that we barely had done beforehand.

    "There is an older woman who lives by herself on the street, too, so we have all offered to get things for her and check in on her too. It's good to keep up the community spirit through all of this."

    The vulnerable person

    One person who has found it difficult through the coronavirus lockdown is Susan Warman, 57, who has had to self-isolate due to being a vulnerable person.

    She normally works as a keyworker at a children's nursery, while her husband, Peter, 60, has continued working in his job as a freight railway driver.

    Susan said: "I've found it hard because I've not been able to work as I'm vulnerable. I work in a nursery, so I wasn't able to keep going in because of the risk I'd be putting myself and everyone else in.

    "My husband has had to keep working as a key worker on the rails, so he has been the one going out for shopping and things for the house.

    "My son has also moved back in from Cambridge, he normally works down there for Spotify, but because he can work from home he came back to the house and is staying with us.

    "I've got another son who works over in Cleethorpes as a teacher, so I've not been able to see him much because of him working.

    "But we've been doing what we can to keep going. We actually started playing Monopoly with our son in Cleethorpes via Facetime, which has been really fun.

    "Since the restrictions have lessened I've been walking a lot more too, but I've also kept busy doing courses online for work, as well as gardening and cleaning the house."
