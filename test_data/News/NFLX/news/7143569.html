What is a computer monitor?

A computer monitor displays the information from your computer. While sizes vary, a large monitor is typically 27 inches (measured diagonally) or bigger.

There are many monitors available measuring 30 inches and above. Some top-end gaming monitors push up towards the 50-inch mark.

Why is bigger better? Pros and cons

There are several reasons why bigger is better:

  • Better viewing. A good-quality monitor can offer a much more detailed and user-friendly viewing experience than that 5-year-old model currently on your desk.
  • More work space. An ultra-wide screen allows you to easily look at documents side by side or view large spreadsheets without scrolling. Graphic designers and photographers can benefit from larger as their high resolution and screen space provide accurate display for editing images.
  • Immersive viewing and gaming. With a monitor your PC can double as a home entertainment centre where you can stream movies and TV shows. It also offers a much more immersive and involved experience for gamers.

There are 2 main drawbacks of large computer monitors:

  • Cost. While high-quality monitors are a lot more affordable than you might think, the larger you go, the more you'll need to spend. And when you can pick up a no-frills 24-inch model for less than $200, you might decide that a smaller monitor is all you need.
  • Space. They're a lot slimmer than those old CRT monitors, but a big widescreen monitor can still take up a lot of desk space. Make sure you've got sufficient room before handing over any cash.

What is the best computer monitor for me?

The only way to answer this question is to ask yourself a few other questions. How big a screen do you need? How much desk space do you have to work with? What will you be using the monitor for? How much are you prepared to spend?

The bottom line is that provided you have sufficient space, it's generally worth buying the biggest monitor your budget allows. Once you've spent any amount of time using a large monitor, going back to a smaller screen just doesn't cut it anymore.

Only after you've worked out these specifics will you be able to start comparing products to find the best computer monitor for your needs.

If you're a gamer, check out our detailed comparison of gaming monitors for more info on how to choose the best 4K monitor for your gaming needs.

Types of monitors

One of the main ways to differentiate between big computer monitors is to take a closer look at the panel types they use. There are a few options available:

LCD/LED

The vast majority of modern LCD monitors use LED technology and come in 2 standard types:

  • Twisted nematic (TN) panels are the most common because they're cheap and offer very fast response times. However, they have poor colour accuracy and limited viewing angles.
  • Vertical alignment (VA) panels offer more accurate colours, improved contrast ratios and better viewing angles than TN panels, but their response times are slower.

IPS/PLS

These anagrams respectively stand for in-plane switching and plane-to-line switching. They're very similar types of technology designed to offer better colour accuracy and contrast, a much wider viewing angle and improved image quality. They're the most expensive option and have a slower response time – so they may not be suitable for gamers – but they are generally considered to be the best option.

OLED monitors offering better contrast ratios and faster response times are also starting to emerge. However, as they're not widely available and still prohibitively expensive, OLED is not yet a viable option.

How to compare computer monitors

The first step in choosing a computer monitor is to consider your budget. Entry-level 27-inch monitors start at around $250. From there, prices increase in line with screen size and special features, such as a curved monitor and 4K resolution. Top-spec gaming monitors can cost upwards of $2,000.

Next, compare screen size, image quality, resolution and ease of use before deciding which computer monitor to buy. Make sure you consider the following essential factors:

Screen size

Most monitors sit within the 27- to 34-inch range. There is a small selection of models that offer screens of 40 inches or more. You'll also need to consider the amount of desk space you have before deciding on the right size.

Refresh rate

The refresh rate refers to how often a monitor changes the image on screen, measured in hertz. The average monitor has a refresh rate of 60Hz, which means the display updates 60 times per second. Top-spec gaming monitors can increase this as far as 240Hz.

Contrast ratio

This is the difference between how black and how white a monitor can get. A high contrast ratio is good news, but be aware that there's no uniform way for measuring this across different manufacturers. Instead of taking the specs sheet as gospel, trust your eyes instead.

Resolution

Resolution is the number of pixels on a screen. The higher the pixel count, the better the detail – but make sure you're comparing screens of the same size. 1920x1080, also known as 1080p or full HD, is the minimum you'll need. A higher resolution is recommended for large monitors. 4K (3,840x2,160) is currently the most popular choice.

2560×1440 (1440p or 2K) monitors are available but not as common. A limited number of monitors offer 5K.

Aspect ratio

Aspect ratio is the width of the screen compared to its height. 16:9 is the most common ratio and the best choice for most buyers. 16:10 provides a little more vertical work space for those who might want to look at multiple documents or images at the same time.

Some ultra-widescreen monitors stretch to a ratio of 21:9, offering a smoother and more practical arrangement than a dual-monitor set-up. Ultra-widescreen models are generally more suited to gamers, movie buffs or content creation professionals.

Curved vs flat

Many super-widescreen models are curved in an effort to complement your eyeballs and offer a more natural viewing experience. They're designed to improve viewing angles and make gaming and movies more immersive, affording you a wider display without having to sit too far back.

However, they're more expensive than flat screens. They also stick out from the wall more. Whether you choose a flat or curved monitor really comes down to personal preference.

Frequently asked questions

  • To clean a computer monitor, you will need to follow these steps:

    1. Turn off your computer and unplug the monitor from the power source.
    2. Use a microfiber cloth or soft, lint-free cloth to wipe away any dust or debris on the monitor's surface.
    3. For tougher stains or smudges, dampen the cloth with water and gently wipe the affected area.
    4. Do not use harsh chemicals or cleaning products on your monitor, as they can damage the screen.
    5. For stubborn residue, you can use a small amount of isopropyl alcohol on the cloth and gently wipe the stain.
    6. Once you have cleaned the entire surface, use a dry cloth to remove any excess moisture.

  • There are three main types of computer monitors: cathode ray tube (CRT), liquid crystal display (LCD), and light-emitting diode (LED). CRT monitors were the standard for many years, but LCD and LED monitors have replaced them due to their bulkiness and high energy consumption.

  • The best size monitor for daily use depends on personal preferences and needs. Some people prefer a smaller screen for easier portability, while others prefer a larger one for better visibility and multitasking. Generally, a 22-inch to 27-inch monitor is considered ideal for everyday use.

  • Sitting at least 20 inches away from a 27-inch monitor is recommended for optimal viewing distance. This will allow you to see the entire screen without straining your eyes. However, this can also vary depending on personal preferences and vision abilities. Taking breaks and adjusting your seating position if you experience discomfort or eye strain while using a computer monitor is important.

  • Curved monitors have gained popularity recently for their immersive viewing experience and reduced eye strain. They also offer a wider field of view compared to traditional flat screens. However, they may not suit everyone as some people may find the curved shape uncomfortable or experience distorted images at certain angles.

  • The standard monitor size for office use is typically between 22 inches to 27 inches. This size allows for comfortable viewing and efficient multitasking without taking up too much space on the desk. However, depending on the specific tasks and needs of an office, larger or smaller monitors may also be suitable.

  • Having the top of your monitor at 0–30° below eye level is the optimal height for an ergonomic workstation. This allows you to view the entire screen without having to move your head. To check that your monitor is at the correct height, lean back in your chair and hold a hand out at a right angle toward the screen. It should line up with the centre of your screen.

  • As long as your monitor has an HDMI input or VGA connector and built-in audio, you can use it as a TV.

  • Radiation levels emitted by a computer monitor are tested and regulated to be safe. However, prolonged use can cause eye strain or discomfort.

  • Light emitting diode (LED) computer monitors offer better contrast and colour quality. Most LED models also have a 4K resolution and a better response time, suitable for gaming. However, they are more expensive. Liquid crystal display (LCD) monitors are cheaper with minimal burn-in issues. They are also energy efficient and have lower flicker rates. However, they are thicker and have a slower response time.

  • To avoid glare on your monitor's screen, sit at a right angle at least 3 feet away from a window. Don't place your monitor directly below an overhead lighting fixture. You can also adjust your monitor's brightness level to eliminate glare.

Additional features

  • Response time. The display's response time reflects how quickly a pixel can change from one colour to another and is measured in milliseconds. A slow response time can lead to blurry images, but this is more of a concern for gamers than everyday users.
  • Viewing angle. If only one person at a time will be using your computer, viewing angle isn't a major concern. But if there'll be multiple people looking at the screen at once, for example to watch a Netflix movie, look for a viewing angle of more than 170 degrees.
  • Touchscreen. If you run Windows 10, a touchscreen can be a handy addition to your PC – provided you'll be sitting within easy reach of your new monitor.
  • Ergonomics. Can you easily set up the monitor at your desired viewing height? While entry-level monitors don't have an adjustable height stand, many more expensive models do.
  • Connectors. Make sure the monitor has all the right connector ports to allow you to hook it up to your PC tower and any other devices. Also check how easy all ports are to access when needed.
  • Speakers. Some monitors come with built-in speakers to help save desk space. However, if sound quality is also a priority for you, you'll want to invest in a decent set of speakers.
  • Warranty. Check the length of the manufacturer's warranty and exactly what situations it does and doesn't cover. Most monitors come with a warranty of between 1 and 3 years.
