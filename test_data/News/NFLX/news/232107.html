10 Character Inconsistencies In The Witcher

There is an iconic storytelling trope where a wandering hero rolls into town, learns about some plot or problem afflicting the local inhabitants, and steps up to address the problem. These stories are iconic, and some of the greatest heroes from Conan the Barbarian to the blind samurai Zatoichi embody this archetype. The newest such character to gain mass popularity is Geralt of Rivia, the star of The Witcher series.

These episodic stories are always enjoyable, but one problem with them is such tales often have errors in continuity or consistency of character behavior. With that in mind, here are ten character inconsistencies in The Witcher:

Geralt's Feelings Toward "Monsters"

Geralt's job as a witcher is essentially to hunt and kill monsters for coin. As careers go, it is pretty straightforward, especially compared to the constant toil of medieval serfs or the many duties required of sorcerers in the world.

However, the definition of monsters--and how he handles them--seems to change a lot. In the most famous example of him acting inconsistently, Geralt spared a sylvan he was hired to kill, after which point Jaskier composed the song "Toss A Coin To Your Witcher." He also protected the egg of a dragon he's hired to kill, expresses horror at the killing of a hirikka, and generally seems to think people are the real monsters that need to be handled.

Jaskier's Sexuality

While a lot of fans are shipping Jaskier and Geralt, the bard is an insatiable ladies' man. He continually seduces noblewomen into bed with him, especially married nobles. Jaskier is certainly good looking and as a wandering minstrel he has honed his silver tongue.

He is also a klutzy fool with all the personality of a broken lute. For someone who supposedly has a way with women, Jaskier fails to successfully seduce people and most of the time when he opens his mouth he just makes people mad. So much for his charismatic repertoire.

Geralt's Feelings Toward Jaskier

✕ Remove Ads

Geralt owes his fame to Jaskier's catchy song about him. Despite this, Geralt never asked Jaskier to accompany him. In fact, Geralt has repeatedly asked the bard to sod off.However, their relationship seems to develop into friendship by the time they join up with another group to go dragon hunting.

At the end of this adventure, Geralt yells at Jaskier, breaking the young bard's spirit with unwarranted cruelty. The nature of their friendship--or of Geralt's ire--are never consistent, as it seems Geralt doesn't know how he feels.

Geralt's Feelings About Fame

Geralt claims not to like being famous. He repeatedly shirks off claims of his achievements and avoids the spotlight, even criticizing Jaskier for embellishing upon events to make the witcher look more spectacular than his achievements merit.

✕ Remove Ads

Despite the supposed disdain for this fame, when Jaskier begins singing in a tavern and all the tavern patrons join in, Geralt accepts the coin they begin tossing his way. He also allows himself to be drawn into political machinations where people try to take advantage of him due to his fame. So much for keeping a low profile.

Tissaia's Attitude Toward Yennefer

Tissaia is the first of the leaders of the Mages Academy to figure out that the hunchbacked child Yennefer is actually capable of magic. She recruits Yennefer, buying her for a pittance, but is constantly rude to the girl, insulting her at every chance and calling her "piglet."

✕ Remove Ads

Unexpectedly, Yennefer becomes Tissaia's favorite pupil, being shown secret knowledge and given kindness without explanation. Tensions grow between them later, but this switch from disdain to kindness is out of character for Tissaia.

Yennefer's Sexuality

Yennefer was born a hideously deformed hunchback. She then uses magic to become one of the most beautiful women on the continent, immortal and ever-young, but unable to have kids. She uses her good looks and aptitude with magic to sleep around and create sorcerous orgies around her.

Later, she expresses growing tired of free love and lots of sex. While it makes sense that she would grow tired of loveless physical intimacy after a while, there is no impetus for this change. She goes from having sex with lots of different people (including Geralt) to telling a friend of her's that she's tired of casual hookups.

✕ Remove Ads

How Characters View Witchers

This is not about any singular character but the attitudes of characters within the world. Many people seem to view witchers as untrustworthy or as akin to the monsters they slay. Others seem to view witchers as saviorswho can help in a time of need. Some characters go back and forth on this.

The most obvious examples here are Queen Calanthe of Cintra and her second husband Eist Tuirseach, but they are hardly the only ones. The wizard Stregobor is another such person to react this way, as do numerous characters in the episode "Betrayer Moon."

How Characters Recognize Geralt

This is not so much an inconsistency of something that Geralt does, but rather one regarding how he is perceived and how others react to him.

Throughout his travels, Geralt is instantly recognized as a witcher. His silvery white hair might be a giveaway if not for the fact that at least one witcher has dark hair. In a world without photographs, people somehow recognize him for what he is, even figuring out that he is the legendary Geralt of Rivia.

Yennefer and the Lydian Monarchy

Yennefer was serving the King of Lydia and was travel with the Lydian queen in a carriage when they were attacked. It quickly became clear that the assassin had been hired by the king to kill his wife for failing to provide a male heir.

✕ Remove Ads

Yennefer tried to protect the queen and her child, but this brings up more questions than it answers. If she was serving the king, why work against his assassin? Also, the queen was frankly horrible to Yennefer, so what was the nature of their relationship? Was it the femicidal nature of the attack that caused Yennefer to defend the queen, or was it that she just wanted to save a life? Her actions do not make sense within the context of her obligations to serve the King of Lydia.

Jaskier's Wardrobe

Jaskier is a wandering minstrel. As such, he is not exactly rich as he's basically just a vagabond with a pretty voice. Earlier in the show, his attire reflected his meager earnings. Despite this, he repeatedly seems to find coin to buy new clothes, as his wardrobe is changing all the time.

✕ Remove Ads

Some of these outfits make sense, as he would want to spend a bit of coin to look the part of the bard who travels with Geralt of Rivia, but the troubadour has more clothes throughout the show than some fashion catalogs and changes outfits for no discernible reason while on his travels.
