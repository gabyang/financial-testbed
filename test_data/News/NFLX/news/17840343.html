25 movies about Ireland and Irish people you should watch on St. Patrick’s Day

  • Updated: Mar. 17, 2020, 4:31 p.m.
  • |Published: Mar. 17, 2020, 4:14 p.m.
By
  • Ben Flanagan | bflanagan@al.com

We love Irish cinema, both films born of the country and those that focus on homegrown characters or feature brilliant Irish actors. Celebrate St. Patrick’s Day with these films about Ireland, many of them from filmmakers and actors born there.

[51 hopeful movies that will make you feel good about life, now streaming on Netflix and more]

Angela’s Ashes (1999)

Based on the best selling autobiography by Irish expat Frank McCourt, this adapation follows the experiences of a young boy and his family as they try against all odds to escape the poverty endemic in the slums of pre-war Limerick. Criminally underrated family drama that’ll have you laughing and crying, and wondering why critics dissed it 21 years ago.

Bloody Sunday (2002)

An intense dramatization of the Irish civil rights protest march and subsequent massacre by British troops on Jan. 30, 1972. From director Paul Greengrass, who would later give us other harrowing works like “The Bourne Supremacy” and “United 93.”

The Boxer (1997)

Daniel Day-Lewis plays a man released from prison 14 years after taking the rap for the IRA, as he tries to rebuild his life in his old Belfast neighborhood. Powerful drama from Irish director Jim Sheridan, with Day-Lewis in typical top form.

Brooklyn (2015)

John Crowley’s immigrant love story tugs at the heartstrings and then some thanks to enormously likable performances from Saoirse Ronan and Emory Cohen. She plays a young Irish woman who sets sail for the U.S. in search of work and a brighter future than her homeland can offer in the 1950s. Deathly homesick, her life perks up when she meets a humble Italian-American at a dance, as they begin a lovely courtship soon interrupted by a family emergency that sends her back home, testing their newfound love in this wonderful period drama.

The Boondock Saints (1999)

Fraternal Irish twins ( Sean Patrick Flanery and Norman Reedus) set out to rid Boston of the evil men operating there while being tracked down by an FBI agent (Willem Dafoe). Troy Duffy’s violent cult classic pays homage to several gangster movie predecessors while managing to make its own mark that resonated with young fans who instantly took to it, especially on DVD. Its sequel didn’t make quite as much noise. (Streaming on Tubi and Vudu)

Calvary (2014)

After he is threatened during a confession, a good-natured Irish priest (Brendan Gleeson) must battle the dark forces closing in around him. Irish filmmaker John Michael McDonagh’s thoughtful character drama asks poignant questions about our morals, led by Gleeson’s brilliant performance as a priest struggling with his own.

The Commitments (1991)

Alan Parker’s musical drama follows Jimmy Rabbitte (Robert Arkins), a young music fanatic living in the Dublin Northside who assembles a group of working-class youths to form a soul band named “The Commitments.” An Oscar and BAFTA nominee, it’s considered one of the best Irish films ever made.

Darby O’Gill and the Little People (1959)

A wily old codger matches wits with the king of the leprechauns and helps play matchmaker for his daughter and the strapping lad who has replaced him as caretaker. Starring Albert Sharpe, Janet Munro and a young Sean Connery (pre-James Bond!). (Streaming on Disney+)

Far and Away (1992)

Tom Cruise plays a young man who leaves Ireland with his landlord’s daughter (Nicole Kidman) after some trouble with her father and they dream of owning land at the big giveaway in late 19th century Oklahoma. Ron Howard’s earnest historical drama features sweeping visuals and terrific chemistry from the leads (a real-life couple at the time), despite what you might think of their Irish accents. (Streaming on Hulu)

Hunger (2008)

Irish republican Bobby Sands leads the inmates of a Northern Irish prison in a hunger strike. The feature film debut of writer/director Steve McQueen (“12 Years a Slave”) also marked a breakout moment for actor Michael Fassbender, who’d collaborate with the director on his next two films. Hard to watch for a few reasons, but ultimately very rewarding. (Streaming on Tubi)

The Crying Game (1992)

A British soldier is kidnapped by IRA terrorists. He befriends one of his captors, who is drawn into the soldier’s world. And that only scratches the surface of Neil Jordan’s controversial thriller that features a twist for the ages. Standout performances from Stephen Rea, Jaye Davidson and Forest Whitaker propelled this to multiple Oscar nominations in 1992. (Streaming on Netflix)

In America (2002)

A family of Irish immigrants adjust to life on the mean streets of Hell’s Kitchen while also grieving the death of a child. Paddy Considine, Samantha Morton and Djimon Hounsou is this acclaimed drama, also from director Jim Sheridan. (Streaming on Hulu and Sling TV)

In Bruges (2008)

Guilt-stricken after a job gone wrong, a hitman and his partner await orders from their ruthless boss in Bruges, Belgium, the last place in the world he wants to be. Colin Farrell, Brendan Gleeson and Ralph Fiennes give brilliant performances in director Martin McDonagh’s hilarious and thoughtful (and profane) comedy. (Streaming on Hulu)

In the Name of the Father (1993)

A Irishman’s coerced confession to an IRA bombing he did not commit results in the imprisonment of his father as well. An English lawyer fights to free them. Daniel Day-Lewis and director Jim Sheridan re-teamed for this compelling prison and courtroom drama also featuring exceptional acting from Emma Thompson and Pete Postlethwaite. (Streaming on Hulu)

Leap Year (2010)

Amy Adams woman wants to travel to Ireland to propose marriage to her boyfriend (Adam Scott) on Leap Day because, according to Irish tradition, a man who receives a marriage proposal on a leap day must accept it. Things don’t go as planned. Likable romantic comedy thanks to Adams and the man she meets on her journey, played by Matthew Goode. (Streaming on Hulu)

Michael Collins (1996)

Neil Jordan’s historical biopic of Irish revolutionary Michael Collins, the man who led a guerrilla war against the U.K., helped negotiate the creation of the Irish Free State, and led the National Army during the Irish Civil War. Features an all-star cast led by Liam Neeson as Collins and featuring Julia Roberts, Aidan Quinn, Alan Rickman and Stephen Rea. (Streaming on Vudu)

My Left Foot (1989)

Daniel Day-Lewis earned his first Oscar for playing Christy Brown, an Irishman born with cerebral palsy who learns to paint and write with his only controllable limb, his left foot. Brenda Fricker also won an Oscar for her supporting work, while the film also earned nominations for best picture and director (Jim Sheridan). (Streaming on HBO)

Once (2007)

John Carney’s modern-day musical about a busker and an immigrant and their eventful week in Dublin, as they write, rehearse and record songs that tell their love story. Come for the wonderful original songs (including the Oscar-winner “Falling Slowly”), but stay for the winning chemistry of leads and real-life musicians Glen Hansard and Markéta Irglová. (

The Quiet Man (1952)

A retired American boxer returns to the village of his birth in Ireland, where he finds love. John Wayne and Maureen O’Hara star in John Ford’s scenic drama featuring beautiful landscapes and one of the best on-screen fistfights of all time.

The Secret of the Kells (2009)

A young boy in a remote medieval outpost under siege from barbarian raids is beckoned to adventure when a celebrated master illuminator arrives with an ancient book, brimming with secret wisdom and powers. Tomm Moore and Nora Twomey’s animated adventure stunned fans with an Oscar nomination for animated feature in 2009.

Sing Street (2016)

A boy growing up in Dublin during the 1980s escapes his strained family life by starting a band to impress the mysterious girl he likes. John Carney’s delightful coming-of-age musical features a handful of irresistible songs that has you rooting for the young band right away. Many wonder why this failed to earn any Oscar recognition in the original song category, but that doesn’t mean you should enjoy them any less. (Streaming on Tubi and Vudu)

Waking Ned Divine (1998)

When a lottery winner dies of shock, his fellow townsfolk attempt to claim the money. Kirk Jones’ comedy won over critics in 1998 thanks to performances from Ian Bannen, David Kelly and Fionnula Flanagan.

The Wind that Shakes the Barley (2006)

Against the backdrop of the Irish War of Independence, two brothers fight a guerrilla war against British forces. A surprise winner a of the Palme d’Or at the Cannes Film Festival, just another example of Ken Loach’s outstanding career.
