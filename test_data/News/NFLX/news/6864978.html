Summer signings show Sunderland manager Jack Ross really does love a captain

Are the Black Cats trying to build a fabled 'team of 11 captains'? Probably not, but their strong dressing room should make it easier to play youngsters

    What do Jack Baldwin, Grant Leadbitter, Glenn Loovens, Max Power, Conor McLaughlin, Jordan Willis and George Dobson have in common?

    All five are former captains manager Jack Ross has brought to Sunderland in the last 12 months.

    The Black Cats may have lost their on-field leader – not their captain, their actual leader – when they shook hands on a deal to let Lee Cattermole go earlier this month, but it should only allow the authority of an already strong group to grow.

    It could all be a little intimidating for the 24-year-old George Honeyman if he lets it, but the Wearsiders’ official club captain is clever enough to use it to his advantage. The home-grown player is not every Sunderland supporter’s cup of tea to put it mildly, but Ross is a big fan. He has already confirmed Honeyman will retain the armband he was given this time last year for the coming season.

    If he is sensible about it, the man from Prudhoe will make good use of the support network around him.

    He is nowhere near as vocal or in-your-face as Cattermole, Power or Leadbitter – one of the accusations his critics level against him – but such leaders are becoming increasingly rare in modern football. With so much of that around him, he can concentrate on doing his job in different but still important ways rather than worrying about trying to be something he is not.

    Baldwin was captain of Peterborough United, Leadbitter skippered Middlesbrough, Loovens had the job at Sheffield Wednesday and Power was younger than Honeyman when he led Tranmere Rovers.

    The arrival of Willis from Coventry City only adds to the list. He was at the front of the queue when the Sky Blues twice walked out at Wembley in 2017.

    On top of that, Aiden McGeady filled in as skipper on occasions when Honeyman was missing last season and isa demanding and unforgiving player who has experienced how things are done at some of the highest levels of the game.

    The club has also signed former Walsall captain George Dobson. Maybe Ross has got his eyes on the clichéd “team of 11 captains”.

    Time Line

    Sunderland's captains in the squad

    1. Jack Baldwin

      Central defender Baldwin was captain of Peterborough United for his final season at London Road, before the move to Sunderland.

      An intelligent man who was due to go to university when a career in football presented itself, the 26-year-old lost the confidence of manager Jack Ross after his part in the 5-4 defeat to Coventry City, and has not played a competitive game since.

    2. Lynden Gooch

      Not an obvious choice for this list, perhaps, but having been with the club for around 13 years, the American says: “I may not be the captain but I still need to lead in my own way and help my team.”

    3. Grant Leadbitter

      A real quiet man off the field, as soon as he was back in a Sunderland shirt, Leadbitter was bossing his team-mates around on the field. The former Middlesbrough captain wore the armband in the second half of the Black Cats’ opening pre-season friendly, at South Shields.

    4. Glenn Loovens

      The former Netherlands international captained Sheffield Wednesday and Ross has occasionally referred to the influence he has off the field but sadly, at 35 years-old and at the back of a long queue of centre-backs, that might be where it stays.

    5. Aiden McGeady

      The former Celtic, Spartak Moscow and Everton winger is not afraid to speak his mind, as he showed when eviscerating then-manager Chris Coleman on the Netflix documentary Sunderland ‘Til I Die. Ross is a huge fan and handed him the armband on occasions last season.

      Talk of mutual interest in his playing Major League Soccer cast doubt about his future, but now the rumours are more about a new contract on Wearside.

    6. Jon McLaughlin

      If Leadbitter is shy and retiring off the field, McLaughlin is one of the great talkers. An experienced goalkeeper of Championship quality.

    7. Max Power

      The Merseysider captained every Tranmere Rovers team all the way up to the first XI before he left, aged 22, and has stood in at times for Sunderland too.

    8. Ruben Sammut

      Unlikely to captain Sunderland any time soon - certainly not outside of the Football League Trophy - having been signed with the under-23 rather than the senior squad immediately in mind, but centre-back Sammut has captained Chelsea at all age groups.

    9. Jordan Willis

      Captain of Coventry City, leading them twice at Wembley in the 2016-17 season, the centre-back has now moved to the Stadium of Light.

    10. Conor McLaughlin

      Before moving to Championship club Millwall, the Northern Ireland international was captain at Fleetwood Town.

    11. George Dobson

      Despite being just 21, Dobson was made captain of Walsall last season having shown many leadership qualities that impressed his then Saddlers manager.

    Baldwin and Loovens in particular might struggle for game-time next season, particularly now centre-back Willis has been signed to play in their position, but leadership extends way beyond what happens on the pitch.

    The successful managers tend to have dressing rooms which regulate themselves and as a young leader himself – Ross only recently turned 43 – with a young coaching staff, he has had the courage, the nous and the humility to not be afraid to have opinionated players in his squad, or to ignore them.

    Ross is not a ranter or a raver in the technical area, so it is important people are doing that on the field for them. He prefers to try to take a colder, more analytical approach. His assistant James Fowler is more aggressive on the touchline but often watches parts of the game from the stands – usually at the request of Ross, occasionally the officials.

    A manager can lay down the law all he likes, but it can only take him so far.

    The example set by the likes of Honeyman, Baldwin, McGeady, Leadbitter, Loovens, Power and Willis, and by senior professionals and internationals such as Jon McLaughlin, Conor McLauglin and – if he stays – Bryan Oviedo can be every bit as influential. Seeing his Academy of Light contemporary Honeyman wearing the armband has encouraged Lynden Gooch to think he also has to do that.

    It is obvious Ross is trying to build a dressing room of strong characters, but as with everything else, it is about balance.

    A team of grizzled leaders is all well and good, but this squad needs a shot of youthful exuberance too from the likes of Denver Hume, Elliot Embleton, Luke O’Nien, Bali Mumba, Ethan Robson and Benji Kimpioka.

    Having the characters and role models Sunderland do at the Academy of Light should make it easier for Ross to sprinkle a few younger talents into the squad, adding something in flair, personality and potential their more senior counterparts might not have.
