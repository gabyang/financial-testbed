Homecoming Season 2: 10 Ways Alex Changes Throughout The Season

During Homecoming season one, the show became known for the dark, menacing atmosphere created by the combination of careful direction, smart, creative cinematography, and the constantly creeping, emotive score. What is particularly notable about the show is the quality and depth of its characters. The leads of this show are not predictable.

The audience doesn't even find out who they truly are until half-way through the series---and this is true for both season one, and two. But even once the full identity of a character is completely unveiled, there is no clean tying up of loose ends, no absolute closure. The creators of Homecominglike their characters to be just as messy and complicated as people in real life, and the new leading character Alex, played by Janelle Monaé, is no exception.

Begins As A Flat Character

Alex is completely unreadable at the start of the show. She has just emerged from a boat in the middle of a lake, and, unhelpfully (although helpful for the plot) drops her phone in the water.

✕ Remove Ads

The next couple of episodes see Alex mostly passive and vacant, offering little for viewers to go on. She, like the audience, is trying to figure out who she is.

Asserts Self-Confidence At The Hospital

The first strong move she makes that contributes to the further building of her character is when Alex checks into the local hospital in order to try to sort out what has happened to her.

The doctor concludes that Alex is probably a drug addict, and Alex immediately rejects this notion. Something in her knows that this isn't who she is and that she's being over-simplified for the convenience of the doctors.

Proves Herself Resourceful At The Bar

✕ Remove Ads

Evidence of Alex's intelligence andinvestigativenature emerges when she and the man from the hospital who briefly seems to be helping her (spoiler, he lets her down) is sat in a bar, attempting to re-trace Alex's steps.

The waiter proves unhelpful, but Alex considers that there must be a receipt from when he claims she dined there yesterday. She takes it upon herself to wade through the mess of old receipts, exemplifying her determination and resourcefulness.

Violent Towards Craig

Memory-addled Alex feverishly searches through Geist for evidence of who she is.

Craig, who viewers know to be annoying and persistent from his role in season one, comes off as threatening to Alex who then displays her capacity for violence by sneaking up behind him and tasing him.

✕ Remove Ads

Reveals Herself To Be Audrey's Girlfriend

The most memorable shift in Alex's character comes not from anything actually, technically changing in who she is, but in a sudden flip of the script in relation to what the audience--and, indeed, what Alex herself knows--about her character.

The better part of the first half of the season features Alex stalking Audrey as if she is her enemy, just for her to turn out to be her beloved girlfriend.

Goes From Social Hero To Con Artist

The first time viewers get a glimpse into who Alex was before she lost her memory, the impression is that she was a powerful, inspiring person who helped to lift women out of terrible situations. Only part of this is true, however.

Alex is certainly powerful, but she uses her intelligence and capability to make money by exploiting the vulnerability of women--and eventually others-- who trust her.

Becomes An Aspiring Mother

Alex's characters spend much of her time involved with the vulnerability of others--at least, before her memory loss--but there is a key factor of her character pre-memory loss which reveals her own vulnerability, and that is when Alex brings up the fact that she wants children and even seems to be at the mercy of Audrey when it comes to the decision of whether they can have them.

Shows Compassion For Walter

✕ Remove Ads

Alex majorly just usesWalter, tricking him, wasting his time and nearly causing him to forget even more of his memories, but there is an instance where she casually admits some compassion toward him.

It's surprising, as one would think that in order to carry out what she does she would need to convincer herself he was an unworthy person anyway, but this isn't the case. She is a person who can feel for others, yet also hurt them in the same instance.

Adopts Disgust For Herself And Audrey

When Alex finds out the truth about who she is, and what she and Audrey have been working to do, she is horrified with them both. She can hardly believe who she is, and it's clear she wishes it weren't true.

She wants to believe that Audrey is the enemy and that she is lying to her--because that would be easier than believing that her situation is of her own making.

Maintains Some Loyalty To Audrey

Whether it's due some compassion existing at the core of who Alex is, or otherwise a persevering sense of obligation toward Audrey somehow carried over from her former self, Alex ends the series sitting by Audrey's side, waiting to help her when she rises from the effects of having taken the Geist drug.
