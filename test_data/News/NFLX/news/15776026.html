Netflix’s live-action series Cowboy Bebop series won’t premiere until 2021. The project is based on the Japanese animated program directed by Shinichirō Watanabe. Consisting of 26 episodes, the show chronicles the lives of a bounty hunter crew in the year 2071 as they travel in a spaceship called the Bebop. Although it dabbled in a variety of genres throughout its run, the influences of science fiction, western, and noir films are the most pronounced. Back in November 2018, Netflix announced plans for a live-action adaptation of Cowboy Bebop.

The series is set to star John Cho as Spike Spiegel, the leader of the group of intergalactic bounty hunters. Progress has been made on the live-action iteration of Cowboy Bebop, with several important casting announcements and a planned run of 10 episodes. Watanabe will serve as a consultant on the show, with Christopher Yost writing the first episode and serving as an executive producer. Yost had previously co-written two movies in the Thor trilogy, Dark World and Ragnarok, with the latter film possibly serving as an example of what Netflix’s Cowboy Bebop might look when it hits the streaming service. However, that won’t happen for a little while longer.

Production was halted on Cowboy Bebopin October 2019 when Cho suffered an injury while filming. Occurring during the final take of a scene that was routine, and considered a freak accident, the incident required Cho to undergo surgery on his knee. Additionally, it had been reported that the actor needed extensive rehabilitation. Because of this, and because of the effect COVID-19 has had on the entertainment industry, i09 notes that audiences shouldn’t expect to see Cowboy Bebop until at least 2021.

The update came as part of an extensive interview with Javier Grillo-Marxuach, a writer on Cowboy Bebop. Grillo-Marxuach also expressed doubts on prior assertions by executive producer Marty Adelstein that three episodes had been finished prior to Cho’s injury. New Zealand, where the live-action series films, recently approved some Hollywood productions to proceed. However, Cho may still need time to fully recover before filming can recommence.

✕ Remove Ads

For now, in a bit of good news, Grillo-Marxuach mentions that writers are already working on a second season of Cowboy Bebop. It’s clear that the creative forces behind the project understand the importance of presenting a successful and well-received adaptation. The original Cowboy Bebop has a significant and surprisingly varied legacy, influencing director Rian Johnson on his debut feature Brick. The series has even been held up as a vital gateway for better understanding the world of anime as a whole. Whenever it does debut, the live-action adaptation has a lot to live up to.
