Love Wedding Repeat Review: Netflix's Sliding Doors Redux is a Miss

In recent years, Netflix has become a destination for romantic comedies in an era when few such films are released in theaters, and its latest is Love Wedding Repeat. With a premise similar to the 1998 Gwyneth Paltrow movie Sliding Doors, Love Wedding Repeat follows different timelines of a wedding and all the varied ways it could go wrong or right based on chance. The feature-length directing debut for Death at a Funeral writer Dean Craig, Love Wedding Repeat continues the streamer's mixed track record of success with the genre over the last few years and, unfortunately, not in a good way. Netflix's latest rom-com, Love Wedding Repeat, is too cringey to be any fun and fails to deliver on its premise of multiple alternate timelines.

✕ Remove Ads

Love Wedding Repeat focuses on Jack (Sam Claflin), who's stuck at the English table alongside his ex-girlfriend Amanda (Freida Pinto) at his sister Hayley's (Eleanor Tomlinson) Roman wedding and tasked with giving a sleeping sedative to Hayley's ex-boyfriend Marc (Jack Farthing). However, when a group of children rearrange the place settings of the table, there are at least eight possible outcomes depending on who at the table drinks the sedative. Though Love Wedding Repeat sets the stage for the movie to explore any number of these eight timelines, Craig's film only briefly shows six of them in a montage, choosing to focus instead on two main sequence of events. The first is what would happen if the maid/man of honor Bryan (Joel Fry) were to drink the sedative and the other is if Jack did, ostensibly showcasing the worst timeline and the best.

Of course, for one of these timelines to occur, Jack has to drink the sleeping sedative directly from the glass he himself poured it into, somehow without noticing it's the same glass and table setting he roofied just moments before. This alone stretches the bounds of believability, but falls right in line with all of Love Wedding Repeat, which sees all of its characters making terrible decisions for no discernible reason other than to create comedic situations. But the trouble with Craig's script and direction is that it forces the viewer to point and laugh at the characters in a rather mean way, to the detriment of developing those characters, giving audiences little reason to actually care about Jack and his almost-romance with Dina (Olivia Munn). There are moments of heart and catharsis in Love WeddingRepeat, though they largely arise between everyone except the two romantic leads, and these moments are crammed into the third act so that the wait is barely worth it.

✕ Remove Ads

For their parts, the cast does a good job of delivering the kind of humor Craig seems to be aiming for, which largely relies on secondhand embarrassment and making the characters look like fools. Those who find that brand of humor difficult to watch will have trouble with Love Wedding Repeat as there are few other kinds of comedy, while others with a taste similar to Craig may find the movie rather enjoyable. Still, the cast is serviceable enough, with Claflin playing the charmingly bumbling Jack and Munn the effortlessly cool Dina. But with Love Wedding Repeat bouncing around to the various dramas playing out at Hayley's wedding, the movie gives viewers little time to actually learn too much about any character. As a result, the characters who are meant to be comedic relief - Aisling Bea's Rebecca and Allan Mustafa's Chaz - become the most memorable simply for their ridiculousness.

✕ Remove Ads

Love Wedding Repeat never quite delivers on its premise of exploring multiple timelines, instead only being relegated to two - and the montage of the other six possibilities only serves to tease the viewers about what could have been. Instead, its two timelines are rife with dramatic and comedic beats, but little character development so that the moments of catharsis in the third act ring hollow. The cast is fine enough, but even they cannot elevate Craig's script beyond what it is. There's little chemistry between Claflin and Munn as the romantic leads, though they do have some fun moments with a few of the other characters. Ultimately, Love Wedding Repeat is more comedy than romance, but if viewers don't find Craig's sense of humor to be funny, then the movie has very little going for it.

✕ Remove Ads

In terms of Netflix's romantic comedies, Love Wedding Repeat may become someone's new favorite, but it doesn't seem destined to be the kind of breakout hit the streamer has had with movies like To All the Boys I've Loved Before and Set It Up. It won't appeal to a wide variety of viewers like those films did; Love Wedding Repeat isn't for just any fan of rom-coms. Those interested in cringe humor and romantic comedies may find something to like in Netflix's latest, but if neither of those elements, nor the Sliding Doors-style premise, sounds appealing, then there's little reason to check this one out. Unfortunately, Love Wedding Repeat is one of Netflix's missable movies.
