Pass the popcorn: The top movies to watch these holidays

ByCraig Mathieson
December 11, 2020 — 11.30am
, register or subscribe to save articles for later.

Save articles for later

Add articles to your saved list and come back to them any time.

In a year of extremes such as 2020, the movies often served as our collective shorthand. We referenced the past 12 months via pandemic thrillers, political procedurals, black satires and uplifting dramas; real life definitely had a very imaginative screenwriter. Now we’ve got to December it’s time to make the most of the next five weeks by watching the summer movies that take us through Boxing Day and school holidays to the start of Hollywood’s award season in January. Cinemas are coming back to life and the streaming services have their exclusive titles cued up – just find the films that matter to you in our preview.

DOM AND ADRIAN: 2020

In a sentence: World domination goes wayward for Campbell Parade’s entitled man-child duo Dom (Christiaan Van Vuuren) and Adrian (Nicholas Boshier) when the coronavirus puts an end to their DJ career and they have to pivot to self-preservation.

What to watch for: How durable is the Bondi Hipsters concept? The comic satirists have featured in YouTube videos and ABC sitcoms, but this annus horribilis comedy, directed by Van Vuuren, will require a fresh perspective. Stan, December 13

SUPERINTELLIGENCE

In a sentence: A rogue artificial intelligence weighing up humanity’s future gives an average American woman (Melissa McCarthy) three days to influence its decision, while she gets a second chance with her former boyfriend (Bobby Cannavale).

What to watch for: Melissa McCarthy’s comic genius trying to elevate another comedy directed by her husband and frequent collaborator, Ben Falcone (The Boss, Life of the Party). Atlanta’s Brian Tyree Henry, in the best friend role, might just steal the movie. Cinemas, December 17

DREAMLAND

Advertisement

In a sentence: There are echoes of Bonnie and Clyde in this story of a Great Depression-era Texan teen (Finn Cole) whose failing family farm becomes the refuge of a famous but wounded bank robber (Margot Robbie).

What to watch for: Margot Robbie’s performance – the Australian star has had a succession of prominent roles since her breakthrough, Martin Scorsese’s The Wolf of Wall Street, but here she’s the headline act in this independent feature that has considerable ambitions. Cinemas, December 17

MA RAINEY’S BLACK BOTTOM

In a sentence:Producer Denzel Washington put together this adaptation of August Wilson’s 1982 play about a group of black musicians in a 1920s Chicago recording studio awaiting their boss, blues belter Ma Rainey (Viola Davis), who is fighting to control her career.

What to watch for: Chadwick Boseman as the band’s ambitious trumpeter, Levee. The Black Panther star was just 43 years old when he died in August of complications due to colon cancer. His posthumous performance, in a film about creative life, is already hotly favoured for a Best Actor Academy Award. Netflix, December 18

THE MIDNIGHT SKY

In a sentence: As an unknown cataclysmic event spreads across the planet, an exiled astronomer watching the stars from the Arctic Circle (George Clooney) tries to warn a group of astronauts (Felicity Jones, David Oyelowo) not to return to Earth.

What to watch for: George Clooney the filmmaker catching up with George Clooney the movie star. As accomplished a throwback Hollywood figure as he is, Clooney’s own movies haven’t measured up. Here, he has an acclaimed novel, Lily Brooks-Dalton’s Good Morning, Midnight, to work with. Netflix, December 23

SOUL

In a sentence: Shifted from a cinema release to Disney’s streaming service, the new computer animated Pixar feature tells the story of a music teacher, Joe (Jamie Foxx), whose separated soul tries to get back to Earth in the company of an unimpressed new spirit, 22 (Tina Fey).

What to watch for: Co-writer and director Pete Docter matching the melodic to the metaphysical in an ambitious jazz-infused concept. But it’s not like he hasn’t previously hit a high degree of difficulty – his last Pixar film was 2015’s emotion-juggling classic Inside Out. Disney+, December 25

THE CROODS: A NEW AGE

In a sentence: The 2013 animated hit about a Neanderthal family (voiced by Nicolas Cage and Emma Stone) and their adventure with a homo-sapien, Guy (Ryan Reynolds), gets a sequel that finds them looking for a safe home as evolution’s gap becomes an issue.

What to watch for: Basically, can this be better than cursory? Animated blockbusters that bring families to cinemas always get a sequel, whether they need one or not. Cinemas, December 26

HOW TO BE A GOOD WIFE

In a sentence: Set in 1967 France, this comedy mocks the era’s conservative social standards via the head of a finishing school (Juliette Binoche) for immaculate and dutiful young housewives who reconsiders her beliefs after encountering a former suitor.

What to watch for: Binoche, one of the icons of French cinema, returning to deft romantic-comedy after a run of typically eclectic roles in a film scheduled for the annual French language feel-good slot. Cinemas, December 26

NOMADLAND

In a sentence: Having lost her financial security and community roots in the Global Financial Crisis, a retiree (Frances McDormand) decides to become a van-living nomad traveling across America’s western states.

What to watch for: The assent of writer-director Chloe Zhao – her 2017 contemporary western The Rider was an arthouse sensation that revealed a wrenching sensibility. She shot Nomadland while prepping the forthcoming Angelina Jolie Marvel fantasy Eternals. Cinemas, December 26

WONDER WOMAN 1984

In a sentence: The adventures of demigoddess and Amazon warrior Diana Prince (Gal Gadot) move from World War I to the Cold War, as she faces new foes that include comic Kristen Wiig discovering her dark side.

What to watch for: It’s a bona fide blockbuster! Director Patty Jenkins returns – as somehow does Chris Pine as Dana’s love interest Steve Trevor – and the film will be trying to top the original’s mix of female empowerment, culture-clash comedy and comic-book wonder. Cinemas, December 26

THE DRY

In a sentence: When a tragic murder-suicide shakes an already drought-stricken rural town, a police officer (Eric Bana) who grew up there and was childhood best friends with one of the dead is reluctantly summoned back to search for answers.

What to watch for: An accomplished local thriller, directed by Robert Connolly (Balibo, Paper Planes) and adapted from Jane Harper’s best-selling novel, bringing audiences to a distinctly Australian telling of a gripping story. Cinemas, January 1

MONSTER HUNTER

In a sentence: In this video game adaptation from the prolific Paul W.S. Anderson (Resident Evil, Death Race), a US Special Forces team led by an indomitable officer (Milla Jovovich) find themselves in a parallel world filled with fantastical and deadly creatures.

What to watch for: The continued rule of Jovovich as the queen of B-movie action franchises. Having already kicked, sliced, and slashed her way through six Resident Evil instalments, she gets a new foil here in Thai martial arts daredevil Tony Jaa (Furious 7). Cinemas, January 1

PIECES OF A WOMAN

In a sentence: After the home birth of a Boston couple (Vanessa Kirby and Shia LaBeouf) goes wrong, the wife must come to grips over the course of a year with the transformation of her life even as the midwife faces criminal charges.

What to watch for: British star Kirby turned heads as the original Princess Margaret in The Crown and then revved up Mission: Impossible and Hobbs & Shaw, but this demanding and intimate performance won her Best Actress at September’s Venice Film Festival. Netflix, January 7

PROMISING YOUNG WOMAN

In a sentence: A stylised and blackly comic thriller about a young American woman (Carey Mulligan) whose life was ruined and who is now taking revenge against those she blames and the system that enables them.

What to watch for: The public conversation that surrounds this ferocious but unexpected examination of sexual assault culture, which has an electrifying Mulligan embodying the ideas of writer-director Emerald Fennell, who mastered the telling and twisted with television’s Killing Eve. Cinemas, January 7

AMMONITE

In a sentence: British filmmaker Frances Lee follows up 2017’s compelling gay romance God’s Own Country with a 19th century drama about a foundering palaeontologist (Kate Winslet) who discovers love with a privileged young wife (Saoirse Ronan) she must employ.

What to watch for: Two of the finest actors from different generations giving intertwined performances for a director who bends the elemental landscape and desire’s inexplicable grasp into hold-your-breath moments (and probably multiple nominations). Cinemas, January 14

ONE NIGHT IN MIAMI

In a sentence: In this fictionalised account, after he wins the heavyweight boxing title in 1964, Muhammad Ali (Eli Goree) meets with minister and activist Malcolm X (Kingsley Ben-Adir), football star Jim Brown (Aldis Hodge), and soul singer Sam Cooke (Leslie Odom Jr).

What to watch for: Acclaimed actor Regina King (If Beale Street Could Talk, Watchmen) making her directorial debut with a stage-bound concept that must depict titanic public figures yet also give a sense of them as individuals. That’s a demanding balance. Amazon, January 15

CHRISTMAS CRACKERS

In recent years the Christmas-themed film or special has become an end-of-year institution. Whether you find them silly, satisfying or spiritual, here’s this year’s streaming highlights.

The Christmas Chronicles 2: Kurt Russell and Goldie Hawn play Mr and Mrs Claus as visiting children are charmed at the North Pole and a rogue elf tries to sabotage the big day. Netflix

Dolly Parton’s Christmas on the Square: This wholesome with a wink musical confirms a major 2020 theme: Dolly Parton is a divine being. She supplies the songs and plays an angel who helps save a town being evicted by Christine Baranski’s Grinch-y mogul. Netflix

Mariah Carey’s Magic Christmas Special: The diva’s All I Want for Christmas Is You remains a modern standard, and this goofy Yuletide adventure furthers her role as a fabulously gowned deliverer of Christmas tunes. Apple TV+

A Sunburnt Christmas: Bondi Hipster Christiaan Van Vuuren also directs this modern Australian fable about two children on a drought-stricken farm who mistakes an escaped con in a Santa suit (Snowtown’s Daniel Henshall) for the real thing. Stan
