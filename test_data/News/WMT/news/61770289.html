In Self-Driving, Amazon Invests While Walmart Partners

Amazo AMZN n and Walmart WMT , two of the largest retailers in the world, each have a presence in the autonomous vehicle industry. Their approaches are different, however. Amazon has purchased equity stakes in several different companies in the space, and is building its own efforts internally. Walmart, by contrast, is focusing on partnerships with multiple self-driving delivery services. Each approach has benefits and costs, and perhaps reflects each company’s competencies and self-conception.

Amazon

The Seattle technology giant has made so many forays into robotics that just keeping track of them all is a challenge.

Zoox

In 2020, Amazon purchased self-driving startup Zoox for $1.2 billion. Just this week, Zoox unveiled its new vehicle. Zoox is focusing on ridesharing, although the vehicles could also transport goods.

Rivian

Amazon has invested hundreds of millions of dollars in Rivian, an electric car manufacturer set to release its first vehicle, the R1T, in 2021. Amazon has also placed an order for 10,000 Rivian electric delivery vans. Although Rivian is known best as an electric vehicle manufacturer, it is also building an advance driver assistance system called Driver+ that will come standard on the R1T.

Aurora Innovation

Amazon has also invested millions of dollars in Aurora Innovations, a leading self-driving vehicle startup founded by former Waymo and Tesla TSLA executives.

Amazon Web Services

Amazon Web Services offers a suite of products and tools specifically targeted at autonomous vehicle developers. Customers include Toyota Research Institute, Lyft LYFT , and TuSimple.

Amazon Robotics

Amazon Robotics was born out of the 2012 acquisition of Kiva Systems. In the US alone, 200,000 of these robots now work in Amazon warehouses.

Amazon Scout

Amazon launched a slow-moving sidewalk delivery robot called Scout in early 2019. The robot is currently delivering customer packages in four locations around the United States.

Amazon Prime Air Drones

In the summer of 2019, Amazon projected, “its package delivery drone that it says will start shipping packages to consumers ‘in a matter of months.’” That timeline appears to have delayed. Amazon currently describes Prime Air as, “a future delivery system.”

Walmart

In contrast to Amazon, instead of purchasing or investing in autonomous vehicles, Walmart has announced several partnerships with self-driving companies.

Gatik

Walmart has been working with Gatik, a self-driving truck company, since 2019. They just announced a plan to expand that partnership in 2021, and remove the safety operator from the vehicle.

Cruise

Walmart and Cruise announced in late 2020 the intention to pilot deliveries using Cruise’s self-driving vehicles.

Waymo

In 2018, Walmart paid Waymo to bring customers to stores in the Phoenix metro area, using Waymo self-driving vehicles. The customers ordered online ahead of time, and purchases were brought to the vehicles.

Udelv

In 2019, Walmart began testing in Arizona using a UDelv self-driving van, specially equipped for grocery delivery.

Ford

In 2018, Walmart partnered with Ford to, “explore how customers will want to use self-driving cars when it comes to their grocery shopping.” The pilot took place in Miami, and integrated with Postmates, the regional delivery partner for Walmart in that area.

Nuro

In 2019, Walmart and Nuro began a pilot partnership to delivery groceries with Nuro’s custom-designed self-driving delivery vehicles.

Flytrex

In fall 2020, Walmart and Flytrex announced a partnership in Fayetteville, North Carolina, to deliver groceries by automated drone.

Investing And Partnering

These two approaches—investment for Amazon, and partnership for Walmart—reflect the companies’ core competencies. Amazon, which has a strong foundation in technology, seems eager to own the core components of its robotics stack. Walmart, which has a strong foundation in brick-and-mortar retail, seems interested in identifying the best technology vendors and partnering with those companies.

Investment provides more direct control over technology development and timelines, whereas partnerships allow Walmart the flexibility to work with multiple autonomous vendors and adjust its approach over time.
