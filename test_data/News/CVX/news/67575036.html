Dow Jones Today Eyes Record, Tech Stocks Bounce As Yellen Clarifies; Big 5, Under Armour Lead Retail Rally

Licensing
  • ALAN R. ELLIOTT
  • 10:00 AM ET 05/05/2021

Stocks opened higher Wednesday, as tech stocks bounced back from Tuesday's harsh losses. Retailers flexed early muscle, with Big 5, ODP and Under Armour sharply higher. Facebook shares rose after upholding the site's blackout against former President Donald Trump. And Chevron led the Dow Jones today, as oil prices rose toward a 30-month high.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average added almost 0.2%. Thee S&P 500 clocked a 0.3% advance. The Nasdaq Composite pared its early gain to 0.4% on the stock market today, as video game developer Activision Blizzard (ATVI) jumped more than 5% on earnings news to lead the Nasdaq 100.

T-Mobile U.S. (TMUS) also traded high on the Nasdaq, swinging up 2.4% after reporting its revenue surged more than expected in its first quarter.

At the top of the S&P 500, Caesars Entertainment (CZR) vaulted more than 8% higher, after reporting an earnings miss, but offered strengthening views on Las Vegas occupancy. The gain lifted Caesar shares to just below a 106.30 buy point in an eight-week cup base.

IBD Leaderboard stock Nvidia (NVDA) notched an early 2.9% gain. Baird initiated coverage of the stock with an outperform rating and an 800 price target late Tuesday. Nvidia shares are about 7% below a615 buy point in a cup base.

General Motors (GM) rallied 3.5%, after crushing first-quarter earnings expectations, but falling short on the revenue line.

Among metals and mining plays, Cleveland Cliffs (CLF) rose 1.8% to trade high on the S&P 500. Freeport McMoRan (FCX) climbed 1.3%, suggesting shares would move out of a buy range above a double-bottom base buy point at 37.71.

Dow Jones Today: Chevron, Apple Lead

Chevron (CVX) headed the Dow Jones today with a 2.6% gain. Apple (AAPL) bounced 1.3% after taking a 3.5% tumble on Tuesday. On Wednesday, Apple enters the third day in its court cases brought by Epic Games, accusing the iPhone maker of monopolistic practices in the way in which it charges for app sales.

A Wednesday research note from Bernstein projected modest financial impact from the proceedings, even if the judge rules against Apple. Bernstein also projected minimal impact going forward for the company from future regulatory concerns.

Apple stock on Tuesday dropped back to test support at its 50-day moving average. The move set aside, for now, any anticipation of a breakout above a cup-with-handle base buy point at 135.63. It also shows why jumping into a stock at early, aggressive buy points — such as Apple's bounce on Monday off its 21-day line — can be risky.

Facebook Rises After Trump Decision

Facebook (FB) shares rose 0.4%, moving to snap a three-day losing streak, after the company announced it would uphold for now its suspension of the Facebook and Instagram accounts of former President Trump. The company's independent panel ruled the current ban against Trump was justified, and gave the company six months to determine whether or not to make the ban permanent.

In a statement, the company said, ""the Board found that, in maintaining an unfounded narrative of electoral fraud and persistent calls to action, Mr. Trump created an environment where a serious risk of violence was possible."

In addition, it said "at the time of Mr. Trump's posts, there was a clear, immediate risk of harm and his words of support for those involved in the riots legitimized their violent actions."

Facebook shares are near the top of a buy range, above a cup base buy point at 304.77.

Rising Retailers: Big 5, Under Armour

Big 5 Sporting Goods (BGFV) sailed more than 16% higher after reporting a fourth-straight quarter of triple-digit or better earnings gains. The premarket move suggests shares will open at their highest level since July 2013.

ODP (ODP), the updated Office Depot, swept 12% higher in early action after posting a surprise profit in its first quarter.

Under Armour (UA) rallied almost 8% as three analysts upgraded the stock following its first-quarter report on Tuesday. Pivotal Research kept its buy rating on the stock and upped its price target to 34, which was 72% above Tuesday's closing price.

Under Armour shares are less than 3% below a 20.41 buy point in what IBD MarketSmith analysis plots as a six-week flat base.

The Yield Whisperer

An off-the-cuff comment made by Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen on Tuesday triggered heavy tech-sector selling.

"It may be that interest rates will have to rise somewhat to make sure that our economy doesn't overheat," Yellen told The Atlantic magazine's Future Economy Summit. "Even though the additional spending is relatively small relative to the size of the economy, it could cause some very modest increases in interest rates."

Yellen, a former Federal Reserve Chairman, provided more detail on her view later in the day, sending stock futures higher.

"I don't think there's going to be an inflationary problem, but if there is, the Fed can be counted on to address it," Yellen said at The Wall Street Journal's CEO Council Summit.

Economic News: 742,000 New Jobs In April

Economic news began its ramp-up to Friday's closely watched April payrolls report with a positive piece of jobs data from ADP. The payroll manager's National Employment showed U.S. nonfarm private sector employment soaring by 742,000 jobs in April.

That was a gain of almost 43% over March's 517,000 new hires, though short of economic projections for 785,000 jobs gained. Small, midsize and large employers were unusually evenly yoked in the month's performance, right around the 250,000 new employees mark. Service-sector jobs accounted for more than 85% of the new additions.

Bitcoin Rebounds, Ethereum Tops $3,000

Bitcoin prices slipped 2%, to below $56,000, according to CoinDesk. The cryptocurrency had run up to a new high above $64,800 on April 14, ahead of the Coinbase Global (COIN) IPO. Bitcoin dropped about 3% in April, but remains up about 90% since Dec. 31, after starting the year just above $29,000.

Competing cryptocurrency Ethereum slipped 3%, but held above $3,300. Ethereum rose above the $3,000 level for the first time late Sunday.

Vital Signs: Oil, Copper, Bond Yields, Bitcoin

West Texas Intermediate oil continued its climb, up 1.2%, to above $66 a barrel. WTI oil is now at its highest price since March 8, when it reached 67.98 — its highest level since October 2018.

Copper prices flattened at around $4.52 a pound, after briefly pushing past a February 2011 peak to notch a new record high above $4.55. Copper prices have climbed more than 29% since the start of the year.

Stock Market ETF Strategy And How To Invest In The Current Uptrend

Bond yields edged higher, with the 10-year yield rising above 1.61%, after settling just above 1.59% on Tuesday, according to CBOE data. That marked the 10-year yield's first close below support at its 50-day moving average since September. Yields skirted pre-pandemic levels late in March, climbing to almost 1.76% — the highest level since January 2020.

A More Cautious Strategy

The Dow Jones today has a three-month rally under its belt, and is trading just off record highs while resting neatly above its 21-day exponential moving average. The S&P 500 pulled back Tuesday to touch its 21-day line for the first time since March, following its strongest month since November.

After Tuesday's sharp drop, it's important to remember that the Nasdaq Composite is sitting on a six-month advance. Tuesday's tumble put the index on track toward its first weekly decline in seven weeks. The Nasdaq beat the S&P 500's advance in April, 5.4% vs. 5.2%, and double the Dow's 2.7% return for the month.

However, growth stock investors know it has been tough to grapple with the recent market. And Tuesday's sharp pullback underscored the pressure under which the market's uptrend operates.

Tuesday's Big Picture article notes that the Nasdaq's pullback signals to investors to become much more picky in buying stocks. Avoid adding to existing holdings and take profits where feasible. Trading on margin, in particular, should be avoided. Above all, be sure to sell any stock that falls 7% or 8% below the purchase price. Stocks faltering at new buy points could be sold even with a 3% loss.

Dow Jones Today: Winners, Losers

At the end of Tuesday's session, Apple, Salesforce.com (CRM) and Microsoft (MSFT) sat at the bottom of the Dow. Apple stock dropped 3.5% in its biggest loss since January.

Apple's basing effort is on hold for now, as the stock tests support at its 50-day line. Salesforce has broken down below support, and has work to do before it could offer a valid buy point.

Microsoft has given up all of its gains from an April 5 breakout. That's a sell signal, although the stock is still holding just above the 246.23 buy point, as well as its 50-day moving average.

Tuesday's winners among the Dow industrials were specialty chemicals maker Dow (DOW) and heavy equipment leader Caterpillar (CAT), up more than 2% apiece for the day. Dow stock now has a 20% gain for the year, and is less than 2% from a 67.37 buy point in an eight-week flat base.

Caterpillar has climbed 28% since Dec. 31. It ended Tuesday less than 2% below a flat-base buy point at 237.88.
