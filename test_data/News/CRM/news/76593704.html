Stock Market Rally Hammered On Hawkish Fed, Omicron Fears; Salesforce, Snowflake, DocuSign, Xpeng In Focus: Weekly Review

Licensing
  • IBD STAFF
  • 04:43 PM ET 12/03/2021

The stock market rally came under heavy pressure, with the Dow Jones, S&P 500 and Nasdaq breaking key support levels, despite rallies on Monday and Thursday. Fed chief Jerome Powell took a more-hawkish tone, signaling that he's open to a faster bond taper. Omicron Covid fears also weighed on the market. OPEC+ decided to keep boosting output slowly as planned, despite the recent tumble in crude oil prices. Snowflake (SNOW) surged on earnings, but several other software makers dived, including Salesforce.com (CRM), DocuSign (DOCU) and Zscaler (ZS). Marvell Technology (MRVL) skyrocketed on earnings. China EV sales were strong but Xpeng (XPEV) and Li Auto (LI) plunged amid delisting fears.

Stock Market Rally Under Pressure

With the major indexes tumbling through key moving averages, leading stocks showing poor action and market breadth only getting worse, the stock market came under severe pressure. Hawkish Fed comments and omicron fears weighed on markets. Many software stocks plunged or crashed, extending a sector sell-off. Crude oil prices slashed weekly losses but are still down sharply since Thanksgiving.

Fed Chief Powell Takes Hawkish Turn

Federal Reserve chief Jerome Powell sounded like a hawk this week for the first time since late 2018. Appearing before Congress, Powell explicitly dropped his prior characterization of inflation pressures as "transitory." Saying that "the threat of persistently higher inflation has grown," Powell said that the Fed would consider wrapping up asset purchases a few months earlier. Yet Powell also made clear that his hawkish tone didn't yet incorporate the emerging risk presented by the omicron variant, which could have major implications for Fed policy. Still, Powell indicated that the mission of the Fed's big policy shift in 2020, to stoke inflation above 2% to make up for prior shortfalls, had been achieved. He also made clear that he thinks the labor market will be worse off, not better, if elevated inflation persists.

Jobs Report Shows Tight Market

November nonfarm payrolls showed a tepid gain of 210,000 jobs. But the household survey showed the unemployment rate diving to 4.2% from 4.6%, as the ranks of the employed swelled by over 1.1 million.
Both surveys show the demand for new workers is still high, with little slack. The household survey showed that the labor force swelled by nearly 600,000 but the ranks of the unemployed shrank by 542,000. Still, November's participation rise might be the start of a trend that could signal a less inflationary outlook.

Coronavirus

Vaccine and treatment companies pledged last week to test whether their products will target the new variant called omicron. Pfizer (PFE) partner BioNTech (BNTX) said it needs two weeks to test the current vaccine against the new variant and another six to reconfigure the vaccine, if necessary. Moderna (MRNA) is planning to test four potential approaches to taking down omicron. Johnson & Johnson is also planning to run its vaccine against omicron in testing. Adagio Therapeutics (ADGI) skyrocketed on Monday after the company said it believes that its experimental antibody will still respond to omicron cases of Covid. Moderna and BioNTech, after extending Nov. 26 spikes on Monday, fell for the week.

On the treatment side, Regeneron Pharmaceuticals (REGN) said omicron could undermine the antibodies in its cocktail. Eli Lilly (LLY) said it needed to do more testing to determine the variant's impact on its antibodies. Pfizer believes its experimental antiviral pill will still work against omicron, while Merck (MRK) said it needs to run additional testing. But FDA advisors narrowly endorsed the latter, a pill called molnupiravir in partnership with Ridgeback Biotherapeutics. In a 13-10 vote, the advisors said the benefits of the pill outweigh its risks for certain Covid patients. Pfizer's pill is believed to be more effective.

The FDA is pushing to speed up reviews of omicron-related tweaks to Covid vaccines and drugs.

BYD, China EV Makers Report Strong Sales

China EV giant BYD (BYDDF) reported 91,219 new energy vehicles sold in November, up 241% vs. a year earlier. That includes 46,137 EVs, up 153%, while plug-in hybrids shot up 500% to 43,984. That's the sixth straight month that BYD has increased EV/hybrid sales by roughly 10,000. Nio (NIO) delivered 10,878 vehicles in November, 106% above a year earlier and up sharply from October's 3,667. Nio expects to deliver 23,500-25,500 EVs in the fourth quarter, little changed vs. Q3. Xpeng (XPEV) delivered 15,613 EVs, up 270% vs. a year earlier and 10,138 in October. It appears on track to beat its recent Q4 delivery target of 34,500-36,500. Li Auto (LI) delivered 13,485 Li One hybrid SUVs in November, up 190% vs. a year earlier and a big increase from 7,649 in October. Li expects Q4 deliveries of 30,000-32,000. Earlier, Li Auto reported mixed third-quarter results, with a 6-cents-a-share loss, below expectations, while sales shot up 210% in local currency to $1.21 billion, beating views.

Despite the strong sales, shares of all four EV makers retreated, with Xpeng, Li Auto and Nio plunging late in the week amid China delisting fears.

OPEC To Keep Raising Output

OPEC+ announced Thursday that it will continue with plans to increase production by 400,000 barrels per day in January, despite the emergence of the omicron strain of the coronavirus and its uncertain impact on energy markets. But in an unusual move OPEC+ didn't call the meeting to close and in its official communique said the meeting remains open "pending further developments of the pandemic." The group said it would "make immediate adjustments as required." Crude oil prices, after plunging 13% on Nov. 26, extended losses last week.

Marvell, Ambarella Soar On Results

Marvell Technology (MRVL) reported 72% EPS growth and a 61% revenue gain to $1.211 billion in Q3, beating views. Shares of the data center chipmaker surged to a record high. Ambarella (AMBA) delivered 533% EPS growth with sales up 64% to $92.2 million, the fourth straight quarter of accelerating growth for both. The computer vision chipmaker also guided higher for the current quarter but noted some component shortages are a factor. AMBA stock skyrocketed to a new high but did pare gains. GlobalFoundaries (GFS) headed for its first weekly drop in three weeks, despite reporting a surprise profit and a 56% third-quarter revenue gain. The contract chip manufacturer also guided higher for the current quarter.

Business Software Stocks Are Big Movers

Salesforce.com (CRM) reported Q3 earnings fell 27% per share while revenue climbed 27% to $6.86 billion including the recently acquired Slack Technologies. Both beat views. Current remaining performance obligations, or CRPO bookings, rose 23% to $18.8 billion, edging by estimates of $18.71 billion. In addition, Salesforce projected CRPO growth of 19% in the January quarter, below estimates of 21%. Management cited weakness at its MuleSoft unit, acquired in 2018. Salesforce promoted Bret Taylor to act as co-CEO alongside current CEO Marc Benioff. Shares plunged.

Snowflake (SNOW) after reporting a narrower-than-expected Q3 loss while revenue jumped 110% from a year earlier to $334.4 million, topping estimates of 305.6 million. The software firm forecast current-quarter product revenue of $345 million to $350 million, above estimates of $316 million. SNOW jumped on earnings but fell for the week.

DocuSign (DOCU) reported 164% EPS growth, easily beating, while revenue rose 42% to $545.5 million, also topping views. But the company forecast revenue of $560 million for the current quarter vs. estimates of $575.3 million. DOCU stock crashed Friday.

Cybersecurity Software

Zscaler (ZS) reported flat Q3 EPS while revenue rose 62% to $230.5 million, both beating. Billings swelled 71% to $247.7 million, crushing views. Zscaler guided up for current-quarter revenue but sees EPS just below views amid higher investments in its sales force. Shares initially rose but then reversed sharply lower.

Okta (OKTA) reported a smaller-than-expected Q3 loss while revenue rose 61% to $350.7 million, including the acquisition of Auth0. Billings climbed 54% to $386.7 million, 3% above the official consensus but disappointing some analysts.

CrowdStrike (CRWD) reported Q3 EPS jumped 125% as revenue rose 63% to $380.1 million, including acquisitions. Both topped. Annual recurring revenue increased 67% to $1.51 billion from a year earlier, slightly beating. CrowdStrike guided slightly higher on January-quarter revenue.

Data Software Earnings

Box (BOX) and Elastic (ESTC) beat views and guided higher. While Box stock jumped, Elastic skidded. Splunk (SPLK) posted a mixed earnings report and a muted fourth-quarter outlook. It reported preliminary results on Nov. 15. Splunk has struggled with a cloud transition and the rise of formidable, next-gen competitors. SPLK stock fell toward 52-week lows.

Dollar General Goes 'Five Below'

Discount chain Dollar General (DG) gave a more upbeat full-year outlook that still came up short of Wall Street expectations. Third-quarter same-store sales missed views, even as EPS and total revenue beat. The chain will accelerate openings of its new, higher-priced Popshelf store format, which sells products largely priced at $5 or less. The move came shortly after Dollar Tree (DLTR) said it would soon begin selling many products for $1.25. Five Below (FIVE), a teen and tween-centric off-price retailer, also beat Q3 views. Five Below rose on earnings but fell for the week. Dollar General slid.

News In Brief

Allbirds (BIRD), a sustainable sneaker maker and recent IPO, reported mixed third-quarter earnings and a full-year sales outlook that missed expectations.

Ulta Beauty (ULTA) reported Q3 EPS jumped 140% with sales up 29% to $2 billion, both beating. Same-store sales rose 25.8%. The beauty retailer raised its full-year earnings and sales outlook.

Twitter (TWTR) co-founder Jack Dorsey relinquished his role as chief executive. The company appointed Chief Technology Officer Parag Agrawal as CEO. Dorsey said, simply, that it was time for him to move on. He remains CEO of digital payments leader Square (SQ). Meanwhile, Square changed its name to Block, to reflect its emphasis on blockchain and cryptocurrencies.

Veeva Systems (VEEV) beat third-quarter expectations with 24% adjusted EPS growth with sales up 26% to $476.1 million shares tumbled on weak revenue guidance for fiscal 2023.
