Department of Foreign Affairs splashed €133K on business class flights, €35K on patio and €20K on designer rugs

The department spent close to €5.36 million on what was described as “major maintenance” at embassies, residences, headquarters, and other offices

    A new patio costing €35,000, engraved harp logos for embassy buildings at €1,250 apiece, designer rugs worth €6,512, and €133,000 in business class flights were among the items billed to the taxpayer by the Department of Foreign Affairs last year.

    The rent bill at the Ambassadorial residence in Tokyo also shot up to €49,237 for the month of December – the most expensive of all the homes leased by the department – according to a database of spending released under FOI.

    It details more than €68 million in Department of Foreign Affairs spending on items costing in excess of €5,000 from 2019.

    The department spent close to €5.36 million on what was described as “major maintenance” at embassies, residences, headquarters, and other offices.

    This included €35,000 on a patio for their new residence in Washington DC. The bill was made up of a “flag-stone dry set option” costing just over €21,000 and an LED lighting system for €3,134.

    The department said this project involved “improvements to the outdoor event area for use for engagements with key interlocutors in Washington”.

    Costs continued to mount up on the replacement of the roof of the Embassy in London where more than GB£1.9 million has been spent on a restoration project.

    The “Roof Repair Project” at the Belgravia property has involved rectifying structural defects and the removal of asbestos.

    “Significant additional variations have been required to facilitate the completion of this project such that a water-tight, safe and complaint project could be delivered,” said the department.

    Other invoices released under FOI detail spending of €15,375 on 20 “escutcheons” to adorn the outside of embassies around the world.

    These consist of golden harps on a blue background with each of them costing €1,250 each.

    The department also paid out €20,882 for five rugs for the Irish Embassy in London.

    Purchased from Ceadogán Rugmakers, the most expensive of them – items entitled Mainie Jellett No 4 and Mainie Jellett No 3 – cost €6,512 each.

    The rugs were part of €119,136 that was spent on “items of artistic value” in 2019, according to the database.

    A total of €5.19 million in spending was listed for residences for ambassadors and other senior diplomatic staff right across the globe.

    The highest bill by far remains the €566,975 – or €47,246 a month – that was paid out for the Ambassador’s residence in the Japanese capital Tokyo.

    The monthly bill there rose to €49,237 in December due to rising value of the yen.

    The Department is currently developing a new “Ireland House” so that they will no longer have to rent in Japan.

    Other costly residential bills include around €17,000 a month for a property in Singapore and €12,715 per month in the capital of Finland, Helsinki.

    From the new diplomatic missions that opened last year, the department is also paying out €6,314 a month for a residence in Santiago in Chile.

    In the state entertainment category, the department spent €76,583 on services from Optimum Chauffeur Drive and another €14,630 on VIP air services at Dublin Airport.

    New vehicle costs were listed in the database at just over €593,000.

    That included an invoice for €41,867 from July of last year.

    More than €375,000 was spent on what are known as detention and disturbance allowances.

    The detention allowance is paid to diplomatic staff when they move abroad and are seeking accommodation while the disturbance allowance is paid when they come back to Ireland.

    More than €292,000 was paid out on electronic subscriptions for services including the Financial Times, Factiva, and the Economist Intelligence Unit.

    Just over €256,000 in expenses to honorary consuls are also listed as are “legal expenses”, excluding settlement costs, of €338,000.

    A total of €6.19 million was spent on passport booklets, €5.16 million on postage and other courier costs, with a further €1.287 million on removal and storage costs.

    One of the largest bills was the €14.15 million spent on renting chancery buildings.

    That included €263,825 for the embassy in the German capital Berlin, €350,400 in Hong Kong, and €954,000 in chancery rental costs in London.

    A spokeswoman for the Department of Foreign Affairs said they had nothing to add to the information that was released.
