Brexit candidate Michelle Dewberry makes passionate pitch to Hull's frustrated voters

Michelle Dewberry hopes to win in Hull West and Hessle - and send a shockwave through Westminster

  • Share

    Michelle Dewberry believes Brexit Party representation is the one way Hull can gain leverage in parliament.

    The former Apprentice star finished fourth behind candidates from Westminster’s big three parties when she stood in Hull West and Hessle as an independent in 2017.

    Two years on she has allied herself with Nigel Farage’s Brexit Party and is back looking to win votes in the same constituency.

    But Ms Dewberry has highlighted the fragmented political climate as a trump card for her new party against their more established opposition.

    “I feel very strongly that if you look at the country it is very divided," she said.

    "So it’s not realistic that either of the two main parties will return a thumping majority to the House of Commons.

    “I think that will be reflected in the election. And what I feel strongly about is if this city votes Labour or Tory this city will then be just another vote amongst hundreds of others in the Westminster bubble.

    “If there is a majority it will be a very tight majority and then what you’ll have is have a handful of seats outside of the opposition who really do have a lot of power.”

    'What is in it for Hull?'

    At the 2017 election the Conservative Party failed to secure majority in Westminster despite being the biggest party and were forced to enter into a confidence and supply agreement with the DUP.

    Mis Dewberry is convinced that if Hull return a Brexit Party candidate it would be a unique opportunity for the city to be heard in Westminster.

    “The example we have is with the DUP in the previous parliament. They had a lot of leverage and sway and they actually managed to get £1billion in terms of support for their area.

    “I truly believe that if you vote for the Brexit Party we are outside of the two main parties which will give us leverage in parliament."

    She added: “Whichever party is leading will need support to get things across the line and if anyone calls on us as Brexit Party MPs my question to them would be ‘what is in it for Hull?’

    “I truly believe it is the only opportunity for Hull to have leverage in Westminster and properly get the voice of Hull heard because you won’t be one of many you’ll be one of the few that will truly hold the power in whatever government is returned.”

    'Someone somewhere needs to stand up'

    After her experience campaigning last time round she said she was hesitant to put herself forward again.

    “It was a very tough decision for me because I had such a tough experience in 2017," she said.

    "So when an election was called I wasn’t there with my flip board and pen canvassing in Hull, I was contemplating whether or not it was the right thing to do.

    “It was a tough decision for me but I made it because I feel so strongly about the principle of democracy and because I feel that the Labour candidates have let us down.

    “I feel that someone somewhere needs to stand up. And I just don’t believe that people from this area will vote in a Tory - I just don’t.

    “I think that if you’re a frustrated Brexit voter or someone concerned about Labour’s socialist agenda and living in Hull there really wasn’t an option."

    An independent thinker

    The Brexit Party was founded earlier this year in anticipation of the European elections and with an uncompromising stance on the UK’s exit from the EU.

    Ms Dewberry said she decided to stand as a candidate with new political movement because she felt the party’s beliefs were compatible with her own and did not want to split the working class Brexit vote.

    Even so she said she has retained her autonomy and distinguishes herself against traditional Westminster politicians.

    She said: “I’m an independent voice, I am campaigning under the banner of the Brexit Party which I actually see as a movement across this country of people who are frustrated with the status quo and I share their sentiment in that regard.

    “But I know having spoken to Nigel Farage that I have an opportunity to be an independent thinker in this scenario.

    "And as we’ve seen time and time again with politicians in party’s they lose that opportunity to have independent thought because you become cog in a big party machine and I don’t want that.

    “I’ve never had ambitions to be a politician, I never went to my local politics debating society, I’ve never worked in political office.

    "I’ve purely stepped up because I passionately believe what is happening with Brexit is wrong.”
