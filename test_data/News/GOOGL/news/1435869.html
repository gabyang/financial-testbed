Wokingham council leaders wanted to "get rid of" popular kebab van at heart of drugs and urination complaint

Kenan Mursaloglu was given just six days notice to relocate BBQ King

getreading
  • Bookmark
  • Share
News
By
Ollie SirrellLocal Democracy Reporter
  • 16:00, 9 JUN 2020
  • Updated17:21, 9 JUN 2020
  • Bookmark

    • The owner of a kebab van stripped of permission to operate in Wokingham hopes the decision will be reversed, after it emerged he was only given six days notice to pack up his business.

    Kenan Mursaloglu's BBQ King van was based at the car park of Wokingham Youth and Community Centre, but he was told in November he would have to move following a complaint about a customer smoking cannabis and urinating in the car park.

    Wokingham Borough Council claimed the business was 'incompatible' with the location, and removed Mr Mursaloglu's permission to sell kebabs and burgers there.

    Find out how you can get the latest news from BerkshireLive delivered straight to your inbox

    Mr Mursaloglu appealed the refusal and his business has been allowed to trade just outside the centre while he waits for a final decision.

    But the way in which Wokingham Borough Council went about removing BBQ King’s trading consent has concerned Colin George from Wokingham Business Association.

    Emails released via a Freedom of Information request showed:

    • The council gave Mr Mursaloglu only six days notice he needed to find a new site
    • The leader, deputy leader and director of children’s services did not support BBQ King’s location
    • Officers were hoping for a “quick win” in removing Mr Mursaloglu, but they conceded “the kebab man is digging in”
    • One councillor wrote the council's decision not to renew BBQ King’s licence “seems rather strange” and “no explanation seems to have been given”

    The council's corporate leadership team (which includes the chief executive and senior officers) “instructed [officers] to get rid of this van from the youth club car park”.

    Reacting to these emails, Mr George said: “Wokingham Business Association has tried from the start, to mediate a solution, for Wokingham Borough Council’s (WBC) desire to remove BBQ King kebab van from the Wokingham Community and Youth Centre car park.

    “The council only gave the kebab van six days’ notice to quit the car park and the sequence of emails shows the lengths that WBC have gone to remove this trader from his legitimate pitch.

    “The trader has always been open to the suggestion of relocating near Wokingham town centre, but […] no negotiation has been forthcoming or tolerated by WBC, and any requests by Wokingham Business Association to mediate have been rejected so far.”

    Cllr Wayne Smith, executive member for planning and enforcement, said: “The council cannot comment in detail at this time as this is part of an active licencing appeal.

    "However, while we recognise this is a difficult situation we do not feel this community centre used by people with special needs and vulnerable people is an appropriate site for a burger van.

    “We have given the business six months to look for an alternative location and have been communicating with Mr Mursaloglu throughout.”

    BBQ King will learn its fate at a licensing panel meeting on Thursday, June 11, where councillors will decide whether to revoke the council's refusal of Mr Mursaloglu’s licence.
