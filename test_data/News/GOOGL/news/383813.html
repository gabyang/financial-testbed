Southeastern Conference hires a lobbying firm in college athletics fight

By THEODORIC MEYER 

02/13/2020 03:00 PM EST

Presented by

With David Beavers and Daniel Lippman

PROGRAMMING NOTE: POLITICO Influence will not be published on Monday, Feb. 17. We’ll be back on our normal schedule on Tuesday, Feb. 18.

SEC ADDS LOBBYING FIREPOWER IN COLLEGE ATHLETICS FIGHT:The Southeastern Conference— better known as the SEC — has hired a lobbying firm as Congress considers whether to allow college athletes to make money using their “name, image and likeness.” Hunter Bates, a former chief of staff to Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell; Arshi Siddiqui, a former aide to House Speaker Nancy Pelosi; and three other Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld lobbyists started lobbying last month on legislation “promoting the welfare of student-athletes,” according to a disclosure filing.

— The Senate Commerce Committee’s manufacturing, trade and consumer protection subcommittee held a hearing on Tuesday on whether to allow college athletes to be paid. TheNational Collegiate Athletic Association and its allies have pressed Congress to weigh in on the issue to avoid a patchwork of laws passed by different states, with the help of several lobbying firms. The NCAA retains Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreckand also has two in-house lobbyists, according to disclosure filings.

— The Atlantic Coast Conference, meanwhile, retains DLA Piper to lobby on the matter, as well as Tom Korologos as a subcontractor to the firm. And the Big 12 Conferenceretains former Rep. Kenny Hulshof(R-Mo.) of Kit Bond Strategies. Rep. Mark Walker (R-N.C.), who introduced a bill last year that would ensure student athletes could be paid for use of their names, likenesses and images, told The Associated Press that “there’s no question” the NCAA’s lobbying has been effective.

Good afternoon, and welcome to PI. Send me your best lobbying tips (which you actually have a military and legal obligation to do): [email protected]. You can also follow me on Twitter: @theodoricmeyer.

MORE NEW BUSINESS: Forbes Tate Partnershas signed seven new lobbying clients, the most notable of which are probably the pharmaceutical companiesEli Lilly andGilead Sciences. Former Rep.Ed Royce (R-Calif.), who left Congress last year, Marc Lampkin and three other Brownstein Hyatt Farber Schreck lobbyists have registered to lobby on unfair trade practices for the Manitowoc Company, a Milwaukee crane manufacturer, according to a disclosure filing. AndCitizens for Responsible Energy Solutions has hiredCogent Strategiesin a shake-up of its Washington representation. The nonprofit has hired Akin Gump Strauss Hauer & Feld, Domer Consultingand SC Partners in recent months and parted ways with Ballard Partners and Flywheel Government Solutions.

MEET ORACLE’S MAN IN WASHINGTON: The Wall Street Journal‘s James Grimaldi, Brody Mullins andJohn McKinnon have an insightful profile of Kenneth Glueck, Oracle‘s top Washington lobbyist, who managed to get himself on President Donald Trump‘s “transition team even though he was a Democrat during the Obama administration before changing his affiliation to Republican. Friends and detractors alike acknowledge Mr. Glueck is among the most skilled and aggressive at persuading policy makers to rein in other tech firms. ‘You know a lot of lobbyists in town who are relationship lobbyists,’ said former AT&T Inc. top lobbyist Bob Quinn. ‘Ken is way more substantive [and] hands-on than most.’”

— “Others say Oracle should focus more on competing in the marketplace. Oracle is ‘the poster child for much of what’s wrong with tech advocacy in the U.S.,’ saidTim Wu, a Columbia University law professor and a former Federal Trade Commission official who has been an advocate for stronger antitrust enforcement. “It amounts to using government as a weapon to delay, annoy and extract value from other entities.’ ‘Oracle spends substantially less on lobbying than Google, Amazon, Microsoft or Facebook,’ retorts Mr. Glueck. ‘So we clearly walk in the footsteps of giants.’”

— Larry Ellison, Oracle‘s billionaire co-founder, meanwhile, will host a fundraiser for PresidentDonald Trump next week at his estate in Rancho Mirage, Calif.,The Desert Sun’s Sam Metz reports. Donors willing to part with $250,000 can have their photos taken with Trump, go golfing with him and participate in a roundtable discussion. The money goes to Trump’s campaign as well as the Republican National Committeeand state parties.

ANOTHER PLEDGE FOR DEMOCRATS: National Nurses United, a steadfast backer of Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) and his single-payer health care plan, “urged political candidates Wednesday night to reject donations from a powerful coalition of insurance and pharmaceutical companies and hospitals that oppose ‘Medicare For All,’”POLITICO’sAlice Miranda Ollsteinreports. The union “called on candidates up and down the ballot — from the remaining presidential hopefuls to members of Congress to local city council members — to sign their ‘Patients Over Profits Pledge’ and reject any donations from any members of the Partnership for America’s Health Care Future,” which opposes proposals advanced by Joe Biden, Amy Klobuchar and other more moderate Democrats for a public option as well as Medicare for All.

— The union “will direct its network of members and allies to pressure candidates and incumbents to sign the pledge to refuse donations greater than $200 from the PACs, executives, corporations and lobbyists who are part of the Partnership. The pledge does not bar donations from rank-and-file workers at member companies, which include many of the country’s major hospital networks and insurers.”

ALUMINUM ASSOCIATION PICKS A NEW CHIEF EXECUTIVE: TheAluminum Associationhas hiredTom Dobbins as its next president and chief executive. He’ll start next month. He was previously the head of the American Composites Manufacturers Association. Heidrick & Strugglesadvised the firm on the search.

‘PEOPLE ARE FRUSTRATED’: POLITICO’s Maggie Severns reports on the frustrations of black donors with the Democratic field. “‘Black donors are sophisticated and they want to see candidates engage on a full-scale level. Right now, if I’m being transparent, I don’t think any of the candidates running are up for the challenge,’ saidQuentin James, founder of The Collective PAC, which raises money for black candidates. ‘People are frustrated. I don’t know how else to communicate that.’”

A NEW VENTURE FOR GULA: The Republican fundraiser Mike Gula has started a new firm with Stacie Monroe, a longtime PAC hand.AMP — an acronym for Advocate, Mobilize, PACs — will handle fundraising, compliance and grassroots work for PACs. Gula will continue to run the GOP fundraising firm Gula Graham and the fly-in logistics firmPrime Advocacyas well.

Report Roundup

—Hope Hicks is returning to the White House after serving asFox Corp.‘s chief communications officer, The New York Times’ Maggie Haberman reports.

— Hamilton Place Strategies has hired Michael Michaud as a director and has promoted Stratton Kirtonto managing director, Stephanie Walstromto senior director and Julie Sarneto director. Michaud previously was assistant vice president at boutique investment bank Keefe, Bruyette & Woods.

— Global Counsel, a London-based consulting firm, has opened a Washington office and hired Erin Caddellto run it. Caddell previously was director of research at Capstone.

— Steve Gonzalez has been promoted to senior vice president of government, military and veterans relations at Career Education Colleges and Universities.

Chris Pappas (D-N.H.), at BGR Group’s offices on Wednesday evening, according to a PI tipster: Corey Lewandowski; Reince Priebusof Michael Best & Friedrich; former Rep. Sean Duffy(R-Wis.), Dan MurphyandJoe Laiof BGR Group; Keith Nahigian and Ken Nahigian ofNahigian Strategies; Meghan Bauer of the Federal Housing Finance Agency; Pam Patenaude,former deputy HUD secretary; Phil Cox of GuidePost Strategies; Steven Cheung, a former White House spokesperson now consulting for Trump’s reelection campaign; Boris Epshteyn; and Marty Obst of MO Strategies.
