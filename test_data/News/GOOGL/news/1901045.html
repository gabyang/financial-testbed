25 beer gardens in Wales we really need to visit after lockdown

You can look forward to enjoying a cold beer at these places in the coming months

    If there are two things you're going to find almost everywhere you go in Wales, it's brilliant pubs and stunning scenery.

    Unfortunately, while the sun's shining and we are experiencing one of the hottest Aprils for a long time, we are unable to visit our favourite beer gardens right now as they are closed to the public due to the coronavirus lockdown restrictions.

    But that doesn't mean that we cannot make a wish list of those we will be heading to in the future.

    Here are some of the best places - in no particular order - to enjoy the great Welsh outdoors in the months to come with a pint or two.

    1. The Plymouth Arms, St Fagans

    Expect rural charm and rustic character inside the pub and a pretty beer garden outside.

    The menus are full of hearty favourites and country pub classics, including steak and mushroom pie and beer-battered fish and chips.

    2. The Druidstone, Broad Haven

    If the stunning view over St Bride’s Bay isn't enough, The Druidstone offers a range of tipples as well as top-notch food.

    The pub itself enjoys a fine reputation having been in the Good Food Guide consistently since 1974. Food is homemade and locally sourced where possible, including Welsh lamb and fresh fish.

    The terrace provides the perfect spot to kick back and take in the glorious Pembrokeshire scenery.

    Its website states it's currently planning to reopen on May 7.

    3. The Ferry Inn, St Dogmaels

    Situated in the picturesque village of St Dogmaels in Pembrokeshire, the Ferry Inn sits on the bank of the River Teifi.

    But it's not just the balconies and large decked areas outside which make this a great place to enjoy a tipple outside - there is a jetty for customers who plan to arrive by boat.

    4. The Albany, Cardiff

    The Albany's garden is a bit of a hidden gem and the last thing you might expect as you make your way through the pub in a residential part of Cardiff.

    The pub describes it as a "hidden garden which is truly delightful" - and, to be fair, it is.

    5. Rose and Crown, Nottage

    A traditional stone-built inn, in an idyllic setting in this beautiful area of Porthcawl.

    Enjoy a pint on the village green in good weather.

    6. Tafarn Sinc, Rosebush

    In the heart of the Preseli Hills and Pembrokeshire Coast National Park, you'll find the community-owned Tafarn Sinc in the small rural hamlet of Rosebush.

    It's known as Tafarn Sinc because of the materials used in its construction and, having featured prominently in the historic and social life of the area, it still oozes authenticity and traditional old world charm.

    7. The Worm's Head Hotel, Rhossili

    The view from this beer garden in Rhossili has got to be a contender for one of the best spots in the world to savour a tipple.

    To the left you can see Worm's Head point, which becomes an island when the tide comes in. And to the right, the breathtaking view is completed by a long stretch of golden, sandy beach.

    The beer garden is simple with picnic tables, but this place is all about the view.

    8. Ty Coch Inn, Porthdinllaen

    Nestled in the beautiful fishing village of Porthdinllaen, the beer garden is just a stone's throw from the sandy beach and has plenty of space to enjoy the stunning views.

    Once named Wales' best pub according to a WalesOnline poll, owner Stuart Webley puts the pub's success down to "location, location, location" and "a slightly eccentric landlord".

    Mr Webley said the "good food, good beer, beachside location, friendly staff and friendly locals" are all part of the establishment's success.

    9. The Ship Inn, Tresaith

    The pub sits neatly in the sheltered sandy bay named after the River Saith which cascades as a waterfall over the cliffs to the beach.

    Inside you will find traditional brick features and low wooden beams.

    The heated terrace is a good size, and the perfect place to enjoy summer days or evenings overlooking Tresaith beach.

    10. The Old Sailors, Newport (Pembrokeshire)

    Formerly called the Sailors Safety because of the light it once kept burning as a guide to ships, this pub is a hidden gem nestled behind Pwllgwaelod beach.

    The setting allows you to enjoy views over the beach as well as superb summer sunsets in the spacious beer garden, and the pub serves up local fresh fish and seafood on the daily menu.

    11. King's Head Inn, Llangennith

    It's just a short journey to the shore from the King's Head, so you'll find plenty of beach-goers stopping off for a drink here.

    You'll find up to seven real ales on offer usually, nearly all produced at The Gower Brewery and there is seating at the front of the pub looking across to the oldest church in Gower.

    12. Snowdonia Parc Brewpub

    You can choose from real ales which are brewed on the premises from Snowdonia water.

    In a mountain setting on the banks of the River Gwyrfai, the beer garden has pretty views - well it is in Snowdonia!

    It's also next to the station for the Welsh Highland Railway with its narrow gauge steam hauled locomotives, so you can watch the trains while enjoying lunch or a drink in one of the beer gardens.

    13. Pen-y-Bryn, Colwyn Bay

    Inside there are oak floors, open fires and bookcases. Outside you’ll find a stunning garden and terrace with panoramic views out over the sea and the Great Orme.

    14. Plough & Harrow, Monknash

    The Plough and Harrow has barely changed in the many hundreds of years that it's been standing with most of its original features remaining, including the ceiling support beams hauled from the beach from wrecked ships.

    It sells itself as a proper pub with real ale and real food, and has the beer garden to match.

    Surrounded by beautiful countryside in the heart of the Vale of Glamorgan, there are rows of picnic tables allowing you to kick back and enjoy the sunny weather.

    15. Oxwich Bay Hotel, Gower

    Enjoy sweeping views of Oxwich Bay as you take a tipple at the hotel's beer garden, which is within 10 metres of the beach.

    The hotel itself is set in eight acres of private grounds at the western end of the beautiful beach, and there’s a good choice of beers and wines and food on offer.

    16. Ty Mawr, Cardiff

    Ty Mawr is a traditional pub which has a rural feel in its location in Morganstown on the outskirts of Cardiff. There are great views of the surrounding countryside.

    17. Dyffryn Arms, Bryncoch

    Nestled in the beautiful countryside near Brynoch in the Neath Valley, the establishment aims to balance cosy old country pub style charm with clean, modern features.

    The beer garden is a great spot to enjoy a drink in the sun.

    18. St Julians Inn, Newport

    Sitting alongside the River Usk, there is a balcony and loads of outdoor tables spread around the gardens and lawn which make for a lovely spot to while away hours drinking in the sunshine.

    19. The Dolaucothi Arms, Pumsaint

    A pretty Grade II listed coach house, The Dolaucothi Arms is tucked away in the Carmarthenshire countryside.

    Hearty lunches and dinners are served up in the dining room and they say walkers and cyclists (mud and all) are welcome in the flagstoned bar.

    But you're here to read about the alfresco situation, which, as you can see, is set in the countryside with plenty of picnic tables on offer.

    20. Britannia Inn, Llanmadoc

    Enjoy the stunning views over the Loughor Estuary with beer gardens to the front and back of the building, and there's even something for the little ones to enjoy with a rabbit hutch and aviary with a variety of budgies, cockatiels, quails and a parrot.

    There is a daily menu in the pub and restaurant, which uses fresh, local ingredients.

    21. Cross Keys, Swansea

    The pub itself is said to date back to the 1300s and is situated in Swansea city centre.

    The beer garden is a popular spot on sunny days but it is used all year round so it's complete with outdoor heaters its fully lit.

    22. The Corn Mill, Llangollen

    Set alongside the River Dee, this provides the perfect setting to enjoy the sunshine. Steeped in history, The Corn Mill has been grinding flour at this site for at least 700 years - owing its foundation to the Cistercian monks of Valle Crucis - and it reopened as a pub and restaurant in June 2000.

    The jumble of old beams is the first thing that visitors notice, as well as the water wheel turning slowly behind the bar.

    The decks outside are perfectly positioned, built directly over the mill race and the rapids, and on the opposite bank of the river, steam trains arrive and leave from the restored station.

    23. Groes Inn, Conwy

    The hotel has 14 individually designed rooms but it also describes itself as "all about the food".

    You'll find real ales on tap at the bar and you can take a seat at the front of the pub where there is seating and parasols. It reopens in May but they say it's a "fantastic" redevelopment in progress.

    24. Fox & Hounds, Llancarfan

    The venue has seven rooms and a restaurant, plus a large area outdoors.

    25. The White Eagle, Rhoscolyn

    Named in the Michelin Guide to eating out in pubs 2016, expect seasonal menus and a good range of drinks as well as fantastic views.

    Their resident Wrexham Lager is available on tap throughout the year and recurring guest ales include favourites from Conwy Brewery and Weetwood Ales, such as Eastgate and Southern Cross.
