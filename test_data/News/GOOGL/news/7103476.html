Blind pensioner left alone in Paris after being ordered off flight

Eric Smylie was removed from plane and left to fend for himself after row

    A blind and ailing pensioner says he was ordered off an EasyJet flight in Paris to fend for himself when airline staff accused him and his carer of ‘having drink taken’.

    Eric Smylie, 78, was with his guide Davey Pogue, when their trip turned into a disaster leaving them both stranded alone and without medication.

    Now Eric, from Lisburn, fears he will never get over the trauma he suffered.

    He explained: “I’m blind, diabetic and I have COPD, so being left in Paris on my own has been a terrifying experience. I keep reliving the panic attacks.

    “I’m still shaking about it, I’ve lost my appetite, my confidence has gone and I feel nervous and afraid. My wife Meta who’s very ill didn’t know if I was dead or alive. Neither of us will get over this.”

    Racing fans Eric and Davey, 64, had flown with easyJet to attend the Prix de l’Arc de Triomphe on October 4. They were due to fly home with the same airline into Belfast International Airport on Monday, October 7.

    And Eric, who is registered blind, had booked special assistance to get him to the Belfast-bound plane at busy Paris-Charles De Gaulle but it never materialised.

    But instead of enjoying a flight home, Eric and Davey say they were:

    • Accused of ‘having drink taken’
    • Removed from the plane by easyJet stewards, and
    • Handed over to French police who separated them.

    Davey was assessed by a French doctor who treated him for low blood sugars, before he was taken to a police cell where he was offered dinner. Meanwhile, Eric was left alone to negotiate a room at a hotel.

    Eric said: “Davey was assisting me to the plane but when we got to the doorway one of the staff told Davey we couldn’t come on board because we’d drink taken.

    “Davey said we’d had three drinks and he was very annoyed and challenged them. The whole thing blew up. But the air stewardess said we had to go. There was a whole row. Davey was very upset because he felt responsible to get me home to Meta safely.

    “We were not drunk. We’d had three of pints of beer in the airport. Some other passengers from Northern Ireland stood up to try to help us and said it was obvious we weren’t drunk.

    “Then the easyJet staff told us we had to go and they’d called the police. So we got booted off the flight and handed over to French police.

    “I was shocked beyond belief. I found out later Davey was put in police cells and I ended up in the Holiday Inn Express. I was terrified. I couldn’t understand anyone or see anything.”

    Diabetic Eric left the hotel at 6.20am but had not been able to charge his phone to allow his wife to get a call through to him because he could not find his charger. To make matters worse, he could not find his insulin.

    Eric said: “I lay in the room and wept. Poor Meta was at home and didn’t know if I was alive or dead. All she knew was that I was missing and so was Davey. She kept trying to phone me but my phone battery was dead. When Davey got his phone back he had 52 missed calls.”

    The friends, unable to contact each other by phone, made their way separately to Paris-Charles de Gaulle airport to try to find their way back to Belfast.

    Eric was helped to the easyJet desk and told Belfast Live he broke down in tears when he was told easyJet had no Belfast flights that day.

    He said: “I was so frightened. People helped me and eventually I got an Air France flight to Dublin for £290 and when I got to Dublin Airport someone took me to the bus stop to get to Belfast. I was in tears.”

    That’s when Carryduff woman Carine Duke stepped in to help.

    Eric said: “Carine found me at the bus stop. She and another girl called Lisa, from Armagh, were so kind. Carine arranged a taxi for me to get me home. When I finally got there on Tuesday I almost collapsed and so did Meta.

    “This was one of the worst experiences of my life. Only for the kindness of strangers I’d never have got home. Why did easyJet do this? How is that to treat a blind and sick pensioner?”

    Davey got home via Manchester all the while not knowing where Eric was.

    He said: “It’s going to take time to get over this but I’m very worried for Eric. He’s not well at all. We’d gone away to give him a break and he ended up terrified and I ended up in a police cell.

    “The airline staff accused us of having ‘drink taken’ and I told them that we’d had three pints in the airport. I’m entitled to have a drink with Eric when I’m on my holidays. We were not drunk.

    “I was furious and there was a big row because they just wouldn’t let us on board. The woman said we’d been swaying. I told her she’d sway too if she was holding the arm of a blind man who is not too firm on his feet, while carrying our bags on a walkway to a plane.

    “We were handed to police and I was seen by a French doctor who said my blood sugars were low and he gave me a solution to drink to bring them back up to normal. I stayed in a cell for a few hours and they offered me a dinner in tin foil which I refused. Then they said to go. I couldn’t find Eric. It was a disaster - and very frightening for him.

    “I feel terrible. I should have been able to look after Eric better. I just cannot understand how easyJet can act like that. They labelled us drunk without knowing us or taking Eric’s physical ability into consideration. It was a disgrace.”

    Businesswoman Carina Duke who helped Eric get home from Dublin said: “I could see how upset and shaken he was and I was totally outraged and disgusted when he explained what had happened to him. How can it be acceptable to treat a blind and obviously frail pensioner like this?”

    A spokesperson for easyJet said: “We are very sorry that this incident has caused distress for Mr Smylie and Mr Pogue and we are investigating to fully understand what happened and see if any lessons can be learned.

    “Mandatory safety reports which were filed at the time report that the passengers were disruptive and aggressive towards our crew which is why the police were called to intervene and the passengers were not permitted to travel.

    “We have contacted the French police to understand how the two men came to be separated after they were escorted from the aircraft.

    “Unfortunately our customers did not contact us to let us know of their situation, so we didn’t offer assistance at the time.

    “The safety and wellbeing of all our passengers and crew is our highest priority.”
