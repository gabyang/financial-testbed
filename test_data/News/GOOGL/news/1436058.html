City nursery schools and early years providers threatened with closure as lockdown continues

Some Birmingham schools have lost £50,000 this term, Education Secretary Gavin Williamson was told

News
Jonathan Walker Political Editor
15:12, 09 Jun 2020Updated 15:12, 09 Jun 2020
Education Secretary says not all primary school children will go back to school before summer break

Birmingham nursery schools and early years providers could be forced to close as the lockdown continues, Education Secretary Gavin Williamson has been warned.

He was urged to protect education facilities for young children - and told there would be nowhere to send pupils to once the current health crisis ends, if he failed to act.

‌

It came as Mr Williamson confirmed that some primary pupils won't be allowed to return to classrooms until after the summer holidays.

Speaking in the House of Commons, Birmingham MP Steve McCabe (Lab Selly Oak) said: "Nurseries and early years centres in my constituency tell me they are facing losses of up to £50,000 this term alone.

"If the government doesn't act soon, there won't be any nurseries left to send children to. When does the Secretary of State hope to come forward with a realistic plan to protect essential nurseries and early years provision?"

‌

Mr Williamson said the Government was subsidising nurseries and early years providers, through grants, the furlough scheme and rate relief. He said the government was in talks with the sector about the support they need to continue to be able to succeed.

The Government said in May that its "ambition" was for every primary school child to return to school for at least a month before the summer holidays.

‌

But Mr Williamson confirmed in the House of Commons that the Government had now concluded this would not be possible - and some primary pupils will not be allowed to return until the summer holidays end in September.

It comes after school leaders, teachers and governors urged ministers to reconsider plans for a full return to primary school – as they said it would be impossible amid capacity issues, staff shortages and social distancing.

Children in nursery, Reception, Year 1 and Year 6 in England began returning to primary school last week after the Government eased lockdown measures.

‌

But some schools said they did not have enough space on site to admit all pupils in the eligible year groups, while adhering to Government guidance to limit class sizes to 15 and encourage fewer interactions.

Figures released by the Department for Education (DfE) on Tuesday showed that just over half (52%) of primary schools in England had reopened to more children on Thursday last week.

‌

But addressing MPs, Mr Williamson said the number of schools that have begun reopening their doors to more pupils has risen in recent days.

He said the Government would like to see schools who “have the capacity” bring back more pupils where possible before the summer break.

‌

The annoucement that some primary pupils will have to wait months to return to school prompted fresh fears that those in more deprived areas will fall further behind.

Conservative MP Robert Halfon, the Education Select Committee chairman, told MPs: “We know that around 700,000 disadvantaged children are not doing school homework and 700,000 don’t have proper access to computers for the internet. So, what is the Government doing to help these disadvantaged children learn again and avoid an epidemic of educational poverty?

“Can we have a long-term plan for education for a catch-up premium to look after these left-behind children and will he also reconsider ensuring that these children get free school meals over the summer given the financial anxieties their families have to face during the current pandemic?”

‌

Mr Williamson replied: “100,000 of those laptops have already been distributed to children who are most vulnerable and most disadvantaged, and we took the decision to make sure they were prioritised over and above schools – those children who have social workers.”

He added that “we’re on schedule to distribute the full 230,000 of those computers over the coming month as on schedule”.

‌

Mr Williamson told MPs that “we do need a long-term plan” and that “that is what we are doing."
