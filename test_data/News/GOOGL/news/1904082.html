Debenhams tight lipped on North Wales stores with concerns rates hit could threaten their futures

The department chain - which is battling for its future - faces rates bills in Wales but not England

    Calls have been made to give Debenhams a business rates break as bosses remain tight lipped about the future of three stores in the region.

    Seven stores in other parts of the UK have been earmarked for closure since the high street giant entered administration for the second time in the space of a year.

    Deals have been reached with landlords at 120 stores to protect the long-term interests of the high street giant - but the chain has admitted a "handful" of more stores will shut.

    The company says it expects to have in excess of 100 sites at the end of the process - with 142 stores currently in their portfolio - but is tight lipped about where the deals with landlords have been reached.

    There are fears that two stores in North Wales could be put at a disadvantage by plans by Welsh Government to not offer all retail properties in Wales a rates holiday.

    In England all retail, leisure and hospitality properties have had their rates halted for a year.

    But in Wales properties with a rateable value of £500,000 and above will not get a break in rates. The money saved has gone to a fund for smaller businesses.

    This impacts the Debenhams stores in Llandudno and Wrexham - which will pay around £500K and £250K in rates respectively, potentially a factor in deciding their futures.

    The store in Bangor does not reach the £500K threshold.

    Debenhams - which currently has all its stores closed due to coronavirus - has been asked to comment on whether it is appealing for discretionary support.

    Aberconwy AM Janet Finch-Saunders wants Welsh Government to think again about imposing rates on large properties, like Debenhams stores.

    She said: “As I stated last month, the Welsh Government has made a mistake with its U-turn on 100% relief.

    “The decision not to provide 100% relief to big retail, leisure, and hospitality businesses with a rateable value of £500,000 and above could see some business struggling as I know from casework that a high rateable value does not necessarily mean that an enterprise is making significant profit.

    “If it is the case that Debenhams here in Llandudno or any other business in Aberconwy has been negatively affected by the Welsh Government’s withdrawal of the offer of business rate relief, I would encourage them to contact me so to help add pressure to my campaign to see the support actually provided as originally intended."

    She added: "It is a sad reality that COVID-19 is putting the future of many well known high street stores at risk.

    “It is essential that the Welsh Government does everything it can to help businesses bridge this COVID-19 crisis. We need them to survive so that people have jobs to return to and the economy has a real chance of bouncing back once the lack-down is relaxed."

    She added: “They should all be assured that I am striving to see grants made available to businesses with rateable values of more than £51,000 also."

    A Welsh Government spokesperson said: "We decided to limit the Non-Domestic Rates (NDR) Relief for the hospitality, retail and leisure sector announced to exclude the small proportion of properties with a rateable value of over £500,000.

    "This affects fewer than 200 properties across Wales but releases more than £100 million towards our Economic Resilience Fund – enough to support more than 2,000 businesses with grants of £50,000. The Economic Resilience Fund will support businesses of all sizes, but especially micro, small and medium sized, in responding to the pandemic and received 6,000 applications within the first 24 hours of opening."
