Travellers who set up without permission in South Gloucestershire could be moved to a site in Bristol

    It could take up to seven years to find a new transit site where illegal traveller encampments in South Gloucestershire can be moved on to.

    There are currently no temporary stopping places for gypsy or travelling families in the district, making it harder for the police and council to tackle the problem, according to a report.

    But because it will take so long to identify a suitable location, council officers are set to hold talks with their Bristol counterparts to use the city’s transit site in Lawrence Weston on a formal basis.

    At present, travellers in South Gloucestershire who set up camp without a landowner’s permission are moved there on an ad hoc, case-by-case basis.

    Consultants are currently assessing the level of need and possible sites after a scrutiny commission taskforce set up in 2018 recommended a review of how the local authority deals with unauthorised encampments.

    A report to commission members on March 4, said: “This review was undertaken as any encampment on public or private land without a landowner’s consent is considered unlawful.

    “However, the council’s ability to address these instances (working with the police) is more limited than it otherwise might be, as there are no transit sites and no negotiated/temporary stopping places on which gypsies and travellers are permitted to stop within South Gloucestershire.”

    It said the number of illegal camps had been falling for years, from 72 in 2014/15 to 21 last year, of which nine were on private land and 12 on public sites, usually council-owned greens and open spaces.

    But the report said: “There are a number of locations where unauthorised encampments often occur, which include the areas around Siston, Webbs Heath, Chesley Hill, Yate and Alveston commons and also playing fields and sports grounds within the Kingswood area.”

    It said the reasons for the decline included practical measures to discourage illegal occupation, such as physical barriers, and an increased use of existing permanent gypsy/traveller sites, including the district’s 39 pitches at Patchway and Winterbourne, where more families have stayed with relatives while passing through the area.

    South Gloucestershire Council officers have also worked informally with the Bristol local authority to move families to the short-term caravan site, in Kings Weston Lane, which has 20 pitches and a three-month stay limit.

    But the report said this arrangement was unlikely to be an option for larger encampments because it could cause “friction” with those already there and it might not have the capacity to cope with them.

    It said a transit site in South Gloucestershire could “enable police to use their powers more effectively – any group that refuses to leave would be committing a criminal offence”, while a temporary stopping place, which has more basic amenities and is available only during peak demand, would also help.

    The council has rejected a number of sites suggested by the travelling community as unsuitable and is instead reviewing land it owns.

    “Owing to the potential complexities, officers consider that the provision of a transit site/temporary stopping place in South Gloucestershire would be likely to be brought forward within the next five to seven years,” the report said.

    “With this in mind, officers consider that there may be value in revisiting discussions with Bristol City Council to investigate whether potential exists for a formal agreement around use of the transit site on Kings Weston Lane in the short term.”

    It said government advice was for local authorities to work with neighbouring councils to ensure “areas of the country are not left without transit provision, leaving other communities at risk of unauthorised encampments”.

    South Gloucestershire Council leader Toby Savage told members: “Historically when we have reviewed our need for gypsy and traveller provision we have done that as part of a wider Local Plan.

    “First and foremost it’s a planning consideration that needs to be considered among the other planning priorities we’ve got in South Gloucestershire.

    “But there is also a very specific aim to not want to draw undue attention onto a vulnerable group in society that’s subject to significant levels of discrimiation.

    “That is the approach that has taken place historically and I see no reason to depart from that.”

    A council spokesperson said: “The cost of managing traveller and gypsy encampments includes staff resources, site administration, repairs and maintenance, court and external process server’s/bailiff’s costs, as well as Streetcare or contractors clean-up and reinstatement costs and potentially managing horses.

    “The incidents of unauthorised encampments can vary significantly from year to year, which impacts how much we are required to spend.

    “Where encampments are on council-owned land we must bear those costs.

    “Where we are requested to assist in moving travellers from third-party land we are able to re-charge for those services.”
