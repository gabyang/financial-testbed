Can You Use Buffer & Hootsuite to Post to Parler & Twitter at the Same Time?

The recent rebirth of Parler has Twitter users wondering if it's possible to post on both platforms at the same time, through services like Hootsuite and Buffer.

Twitter and Parler are similar platforms in that they specialize in micro-blogging. Rather than focusing on a page and a profile, they're mostly about the individual posts people make, and the comments springing from those. Parler can be considered as an unofficial spin-off of Twitter, in some ways. It functions like Twitter but with the added goal of being free-speech-friendly in ways Parler users claim Twitter is not. Prioritizing free speech is mostly interpreted as "not going to censor conservative opinions" and, as a result, many right-wing social media users have flocked to Parler following Twitter finally enforcing its policies on some posts from Donald Trump.

Since many of Parler's new fans are also Twitter users, they're wondering if it's possible to have posts on Twitter show up on Parler and vice versa. The answer is yes, but only if it's done manually because currently, that can't be accomplished through the most common means. Services like Hootsuite and Buffer, which allow people to automatically cross-post content, are not currently compatible with Parler. The popular suggestion from most people who use both Parler and Twitter is still to simply write the post somewhere, then use copy and paste.

It's Uncertain if Companies Will Partner with Parler

✕ Remove Ads

Despite its recent influx of users, Parler still has less than 1% the user base Twitter does, and the service has only existed for about two years. That means there are still kinks to work out and important features, like reliable embedding, that Twitter expatriates will have to live without if they make the switch permanently. These issues are understandable for any new social media platform, but Parler also runs a strong risk of other potential problems that could scare of companies like Hootsuite.

Most notable among these is the way it courts right-wing conspiracy theorists. Parler isn't the first (or fifth) social media platform to crop up, waving the "free speech" flag to attract conservative voices. Platforms like Gab marketed themselves the exact same way, leading to them gaining a reputation as an extremist, right-wing safe haven, and subsequently being banned on Google and Apple's storefronts. Considering the number of high-profile, conservative figures we've seen expressing interest in Parler, it's understandable that some people fear the same fate awaits this platform. It remains to be seen what kinds of posts will qualify for moderation on Parler, but if the company doesn't handle instances of hate speech well enough, don't expect other services to partner with it.
