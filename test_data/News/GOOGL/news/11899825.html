The closed Kent pubs we wish for one last drink in

These pubs shut their doors forever and left regulars with fond memories

    The ringing bell for last orders can make any customer upset, but for some pubs, the bell sadly never rang again.

    Regulars have been left with nostalgic memories of public houses been and gone in Kent.

    Many have shut their doors over recent years for a number of different reasons.

    Business rates continue to sky-rocket as locals struggle to pay the rent in a troublesome retail market.

    The 13 things 90s kids just can't do in Dover anymore

    The smoking ban which was implemented nationally in 2007 was also initially blamed for the demise of pubs.

    Not only this, the rising cost of a pint is said to have forced many people to buy their drink from an off licence or the supermarket - drinking it at home.

    Moving aside from all the challenges current pubs and bars face, we have rounded up some of the well known establishments across the county that are no longer with us.

    We have split them up into areas for your ease.

    How many of these do you remember?

    Folkestone

    The Leas Club

    Location:The Leas, Folkestone

    Factfile:  This stunning venue was home to a u-shaped balcony inside, equipped with pool tables, a stage, a bar and a dancefloor.

    Having been a bowling alley and a tearoom since its existence in 1902 - the building closed its doors as a popular night spot back in 2008.

    Developers Churchgate bought it, applied to build flats on top of the Grade II listed building, and then failed to do so.

    Planning permission for the premises has now lapsed, and the building remains empty and derelict for now.

    Many revellers will remember drinking until closing time, then walking down to the front where the Priz would welcome you in until gone 3am.

    The Princess Royal Pub

    Location:South Street, Folkestone

    Factfile:   The pub was deemed an unsafe structure more than two years ago, and plans were put in place to demolish it and flatten the area behind Gillespie's.

    The site has always been occupied by a pub since 1845 and was originally called the Engine Inn.

    A structural survey revealed that the building was found to have dry and wet rot on all floors, which included the joinery, collapsed rain water drains and asbestos.

    The building still stands.

    The Cherry Pickers

    Location:Ashley Avenue, Cheriton

    Factfile:   The pub was left derelict for years before it was destroyed in a fire back in May 2014.

    Despite attempts to save it over the years, the old boozer was flattened at Christmas 2017 and now the site is being redeveloped to provide nine homes.

    Hoardings have been erected and the area will be transformed into a small housing estate called Emory Place.

    The building looks set to finish later this year.

    The Morehall

    Location:Cheriton High Street

    Factfile:  The pub was one of Cheriton's main boozers throughout the 1970s, 80s and 90s.

    But the tired interior was given a spruce up in 2016, when an out-of-town developer came in and bought the building at auction.

    The structure remains, but has since been divided into flats.

    Despite being on the market for nearly two years some flats took longer than others to sell.

    The pub had its five minutes of fame when Homes under the Hammer featured it in an episode.

    The White Lion

    Location:Cheriton Road, Cheriton

    Factfile:  This pub closed back in 2006 after a shooting in the May of that year.

    It has sat derelict ever since, and despite scaffolding being erected more than a year ago, the pub remains empty.

    Movement has been seen on site, but news as to what its future holds is not yet known.

    Plans were refused for it to become shared accommodation for ex-servicemen in 2017 by Folkestone and Hythe District Council's planning committee.

    The Frenchman

    Location:Christ Church Road

    Factfile:  The Frenchman was a big part of Folkestone's night scene, with its closure only being announced last year.

    It was always popular with punters who were able to enjoy a pint, watch Sky Sports and listen to live music.

    In May 2017 the pub – which first opened its doors in 1857 – suddenly closed for the final time, much to the dismay of the community.

    Family chain  Ramblinns  has since taken it over, renaming it the  Radnor Arms  and giving it a modern and trendy overhaul.

    The pub now serves an array of dishes.

    Stones Bar

    Location:Rendezvous Street, Folkestone

    Factfile: This bar is a part of Folkestone's more recent history, being a part of the bar and club scene dating from 2009 to 2011.

    Of course Stones Bar is just one of the names it underwent during its times.

    It was commonly known beforehand as Mustang Sally's.

    Popular restaurant Luben's Pizza has since taken it over.

    The Earl Grey

    Location:Old High Street, Folkestone

    Factfile:  This popular boozer provided people heading to the night scene in Tontine Street and Marine Parade (the Priz) with a few pre-clubbing tipples.

    Renowned for its regulars, the club closed its doors within the last decade.

    The old Shepherd Neame-owned building was renovated and vacated between 2008 and 2009.

    The site is now home to the Big Boys Fine Burger Co restaurant in the ever-popular Old High Street.

    Castle Inn

    Location:Foord Road, Folkestone

    Factfile: The pub remains largely derelict to this day, with old posters advertising a circus show at nearby Radnor Park on the windows.

    It is understood the pub closed in 2012 and it is not known what the future holds for the premises.

    Piper's

    Location:Tontine Street, Folkestone

    Factfile: Back in its heyday Piper's was also known as Mick's Flamenco Bar in the late Sixties.

    But change was in the air back in 2010 when approval was granted to get rid of the bar for it to be replaced with offices to rent.

    Now, in its place is a multi-storey of modern space for businesses, with Josh De Haan's company View London being the anchor tenant.

    The quirky layout has a slide connecting two of its floors.

    Tofts

    Location:Grace Hill, Folkestone

    Factfile: Not everyone will remember Tofts, after it reportedly closed its doors in the 1980s.

    According to a pub archive, the official name was Tofts Jazz Club which had more than 6,000 members and moved from Bouverie Road West (where Saga is now) to Grace Hill in 1964.

    Dover-Kent  says the ground floor of the premises was occupied by other bars in its time, such as Tom Brown's, Cartoon, Toffs, Toby House and Heroes.

    The club is said to have attracted stars such as Eric Clapton and the Four Tops.

    While the club started out as something a little bit different, where people would actually take part in jazz lessons, this spot is somewhat nostalgic for dozens of adults who grew up in the Sixties and 70s.

    The Railway Bell

    Location:Dover Road, Folkestone

    Factfile: Most people who once drank here will know it now no longer exists.

    Sitting in its place is a Tesco Express store, which includes a car park at the front.

    The former Railway Bell was also popular with football fans, but when it originally shut was plagued by flytippers who used the land at the rear to dump unwanted items.

    The Richmond Tavern

    Location:Margaret Street, Folkestone

    Factfile: This pub has been boarded up for around four to five years and remains derelict to this present day.

    Previous articles from the Folkestone Herald suggests it was an instrumental part of the community feel in the Folkestone East area, with meat raffles on Sundays.

    One of the pub's good deeds resulted in enough raffle money being raised to buy a guide dog for a blind man in Folkestone back in 1995, and another 20 by 1998.

    It used to have outdoor seating, live music and real ale.

    Dover

    The Elephant and Hind

    Location:Market Square, Dover

    The Elephant and Hind was a drinking establishment that came about through the merger of two other neighbouring Market Square pubs – the Walmer Castle and the Duchess of Kent.

    The Walmer Castle was welcoming customers way back in 1729, although research indicates it was known before then as the Hare and Hounds..

    Like its neighbour, the Walmer Castle, it used to open around four in the morning and this continued at least until 1900. It was here that the horse-drawn coaches left each morning to take travellers to St Margaret's and beyond.

    It was in 1962 that permission was given for the two neighbouring pubs to be re-fronted and to merge under the name of Elephant and Hind, a name later changed to Bar Ellie and then the Port of Call.

    The Burger Bros took over the venue last year, but the building is now vacant after the restaurant encountered problems with its upkeep.

    Prince Regent

    Location:Market Lane, Dover

    The popular Prince Regent in Market Lane run by the Moody family for years.

    Long before they came on the scene the Prince Regent was serving drinks since back in 1826. Many will remember how popular it was with the Navy boys, including the Dutch and Norwegians, during Dover's Hellfire Corner days in the Second World War.

    It could get a bit noisy, but never as bad as the riot in May 1853, when there was fighting on all floors of the pub.

    By 1900 life was quieter and the magistrates allowed opening at five in the morning. The Prince Regent closed for a few months in the early days of 1940 but was re-opened a year later under the Moody management. Market Lane was re-aligned in 1952 and now provides the access to the Discovery Centre.

    Antwerp Hotel

    Location :  Market Square, Dover

    Another Market Square drinking establishment long gone was the Antwerp Hotel, where horses were kept in its stables to haul coaches to Canterbury. History tells that Dover jurats and councillors often met up in The Antwerp after formal discussions in the old Guildhall that stood in the Square until 1861.

    The Arlington

    Location :  Snargate Street, Dover

    Built in 1721, The Arlington claimed to be "the smallest pub in Kent."

    Formerly the Perseverance and the Avenue, Paul Barrett bought the property and following alterations, reopened it as a free house in October 1981.

    Further alterations and extensions followed in 1983.

    It closed in 2003 to reopen as a restaurant and then in 2014 it became a micro-pub, which has since closed.

    King Edward

    Location:Goschen Road, Dover

    A perfectly positioned boozer for those enjoying a pub crawl through the Hamlets in the 1980s and 1990s.

    It sadly closed its doors in 2010.

    The premises is now operating as a Londis, but still retains the pub signs and name at present.

    The Engineer

    Location :  60 and 121, Folkestone Road, Dover

    This pub formerly on the corner of Folkestone Road and Clarendon street was a popular haunt back in the day.

    Many a pint was sunk here before heading further into town.

    In 1999 the pub made headlines for the wrong reasons when police officers were called to prevent an outbreak of violence as a group of around 40 asylum seekers laid siege to the boozer.

    Officers with dogs sealed off the road while the crowd was calmed and four people were arrested.

    The landlord of the pub, who was hosting a party to celebrate his first anniversary in charge of the business, later said he had not been aware of the trouble brewing outside until police had burst in.

    The Westbury

    Where :  1 Westbury Road, Dover

    Many who grew up along the road had their very first pint in this pub.

    It was a big venue with three separate bars and a room upstairs - as well as a decent darts team.

    It has now closed and has remained boarded up and empty since 2007.

    Alma

    Location :  Hostel, Folkestone Road, Dover

    The Alma was a one time Charrington outlet, but was serv8ing Thomson and Sons by 1974.

    The pub gained a Grade II listing in December 1973.

    The building later became a free house and re-opened as Renaissance.

    However, after that closed the Alma Express Café opened, which sadly closed within four weeks of opening.

    Prince of Orange

    Location :  8 New Street (Turn-again Lane)

    Mentioned in Pigot's Directory 1840 under "Taverns and Public Houses", The Prince of Orange presumably honoured the prince, who was reported off Dover, with five hundred ships and transports, on 3 November 1688.

    His landing was much further West but the result was significant.

    The ancient property, in what was once Turn-again Lane, was rebuilt in 1901.

    It closed in 2007 and a planning application was made to change the use of the pub into four one-bedroom flats, with a first-floor extension above a flat roof area

    Orange Tree

    Location :  357 Folkestone Road, Dover

    Local tradition has it that the Orange Tree was originally two cottages, in the front gardens of which grew two trees closely resembling the orange, hence the name.

    It was one time a Whitbread outlet and the bars had been improved by David Hanney in 1978.

    The pub had been closed on several occasions, but after again opening in 2005, advertising that it was "probably the best pub in Dover" the pub finally closed in 2006.

    The Ancient Druids

    Location :  11 Stembrook Street, Dover

    The Ancient Druids was previously known as the Prince of Orange.

    It closed in 1940 when the licensee Frank Ashbee was killed by German shelling on his way to the pub.

    It was demolished in 1952 and most recently replaced with a Co-op foodstore

    Mogul House

    Location : 5-6 Chapel Place

    The Mogul opened in 1858 and closed in 1986 after a fire.

    The pub was rebuilt, reopened the following year and once again named the Mogul

    It closed its doors for good in 2007 and was turned into housing

    The Grapes

    Location :  Winchelsea Street, Dover

    The Grapes in Winchelsea Street, off Folkestone Road, was once one of two pubs in Dover with that name.

    The former Shepherd Neame boozer was closed in 1997 and is now a four bedroom house.

    Duke of Wellington

    Location : L ondon Road, Dover

    The Duke of Wellington was situated at 201 London Road.

    It closed in 1971 and was converted into housing.

    The Criterion

    Location :  8 Last Lane, Dover

    The Criterion was at 8 Last Lane and was previously known as The American Stores, the Who’d A Thought It and The Oxford Music Hall.

    It closed in 1968 and was demolished in 1970

    The Imperial Crown

    Location : 48 Tower Street

    This pub first opened in 1861 and was sadly closed over a decade later in 1989.

    It was at one time run by brewers Whitbread and was well used under landlords the Goddens.

    By 2007 it had been transformed into a private residence.

    Ramsgate

    The Admiral Fox

    Location:Grange Road

    The pub has been boarded up for more than a decade, after it closed its doors in 2004.

    This is despite the fact the building is Grade II listed, and has been since February 1988.

    The building went before planners at Thanet District Council back in 2008 where Listed Building Consent and plans for three flats and a two-storey house were put forward.

    Despite permission for this being granted in May of that year, work has not commenced.

    New proposals will need to be put forward again to the council before any work starts due to a period of three years lapsing, meaning that permission has expired.

    The George and Dragon

    Location:Cavendish Street

    Known for its huge logo on the side of the premises, the George and Dragon has been closed for years.

    According to Dover Kent Archives, the pub called its orders for the final time in 2009.

    The building is now understood to be a residential property.

    Duke of York

    Location:Addington Street

    Factfile: This boozer closed its doors nearly 40 years ago in the early 1980s but was officially listed as a Grade II in 1988.

    This property is also said to be in private residential use.

    Granville Bars

    Location:Victoria Parade

    This pub was part of a former hotel – The Granville Hotel – which opened its doors in 1869 and enjoyed almost a century of success until its closure in 1946.

    The bar continued to operate until around 1991.

    The hotel has since been converted into 48 self-contained flats but remains listed since October 1973.

    The Paragon Inn

    Location:Addington Street

    Another boozer of decades ago which is now a residential property.

    It is thought it was in its prime in the 1950s where a picture has showed people of all ages gathered outside of it.

    It closed in the late 1970s.

    Rising Sun

    Location:Effingham Street

    The Rising Sun's building dates back to the 18th and the 19th century.

    It is now an Indian restaurant called Flavours by Kumar.

    The Westcliff Tavern

    Location:Townley Street

    Another Ramsgate pub turned into a residential address is The Westgate Tavern which closed its doors for the final time in the 1970s.

    The boozer was once owned by brewers Shepherd Neame and had a "spacious car park" up until the year 2000 when it was too converted into private residence.

    Trafalgar Hotel

    Location:Royal Road, Ramsgate

    Factfile: The Trafalgar used to be at 48 Royal Road but is said to have closed in the early 1970s and was converted into a house ever since.

    Tunbridge Wells

    Bristol Arms

    Location:Calverley Road, Tunbridge Wells

    The Bristol Arms was located in Calverley Road, just outside the town centre.

    It reopened as the Guinea Butt and just last year was taken over and rebranded as gastro pub the Guinea.

    Crystal Palace

    Location :  Camden Road, Tunbridge Wells

    The Crystal Palace pub in Camden Road closed in 2014 and has remained boarded up ever since.

    Its reputation was in tatters before the closure because of earlier violence at the pub.

    Landlord Matthew Liles was jailed in 2014 after he glassed a man in the face.

    People living nearby told Kent Live after the pub closed drug takers have been getting into the site, including during the last period of snow, and could clearly be seen injecting drugs.

    But there are plans by owner and independent brewer Harvey's and Son (Lewes) to  redevelop the former pub and garden  which is on the busy corner with Victoria Road.

    Glenn Moore of G M Moore and Associates said the pub would be replaced with a three-storey building with three underground parking spaces.

    It would be retail for A1 or A2 use, so either a shop or financial and professional services along with two two-bed flats and a one-bed flat.

    A second building would be constructed in the rear garden, creating a one-bed flat on the ground floor and a two-bed flat above.

    He said access would be via an existing right of way off Victoria Road and the flats would be for the private sector.

    Downs Tavern

    Location:Culverden Down

    The Downs Tavern in Culverden Down had been selling alcohol since the 1900s.

    It reopened in 2011 as the Trading Post.

    The Greyhound

    Location:Upper Grosvenor Road, Tunbridge Wells

    The Greyhound pub, which was in Upper Grosvenor Road, caught fire in June 2003, destroying stock and personal contents inside.

    At the time, investigators said six drums of petrol had been ignited in the pub.

    The fire was treated as arson but no-one was ever brought to justice.

    Over the years the boarded-up pub became one of the town’s most notorious eyesores and at one point became an unofficial car lot.

    It is now houses and flats.

    Hole In The Wall

    Location:High Street, Tunbridge Wells

    The Hole In The Wall in the High Street was a popular watering hole in the town from the 1960s to the 1980s.

    It was a tobacco shop with an exclusive bar at the back, which at first was not signposted outside.

    It was very cliquey and exclusive, usually men only, but in later years opened further to the public.

    The pub closed in the late 1980s and is much missed.

    The Kelsey Arms

    Location:St Johns Road, Tunbridge Wells

    The Kelsey Arms in St Johns Road was a popular pub with locals living in the area and was a 19th century town house pub.

    Darts and pool leagues were a big feature back in its heyday.

    They used to serve popular Sunday roasts.

    But it closed in 2012 and is now a busy Tesco Express.

    Litten Tree

    Location:Grosvenor Road

    This pub closed in 2005.

    The site, on the corner of Rock Villa Road, was a Courts furniture store for many years before its controversial conversion into a 400-capacity super pub at Christmas 2000.

    Despite opposition from local residents concerned about noise and late-night disturbance - although the threatened lap dancers failed to materialise - the pub was a popular venue until landlord Surrey Free Inns called in the administrators.

    It stood empty until September 2008, when it opened as a British Heart Foundation Furniture and Electrical shop.

    Lord Cornwallis

    The Lord Cornwallis was located in Eridge Road. It has now been knocked down.

    Major Yorke

    Location:Langdon Road, Tunbridge Wells

    The Major Yorke pub - formerly the Brokers Arms - reopened in 2002 and was in Major York's Road opposite the Spa Hotel.

    The 16th century coaching inn, which is famous for its traditional ales, was under ownership of the Spa Hotel.

    It was named after Major Martin Yorke who bought the Spa hotel in 1772, after serving under Lord Clive of India and making his fortune with the East India Company.

    The Roebuck

    Location:Camden Road

    It is now the Tunbridge Wells Mosque which opened in 1995.

    Ironically, this building was formerly the headquarters of the local branch of the National Front.

    Windmill Tavern

    Location:North Street, Tunbridge Wells

    The Windmill Tavern was located in North Street but is now a private house.
