What every Blackpool primary school plans for reopening to more pupils

Blackpool Council has left the reopening decision to individual schools and each one has taken a different approach

    As primary schools across England are given the go-ahead to reopen from today (June 1), most of those in Blackpool have chosen to delay inviting more pupils to attend.

    The vast majority of schools which have shared their plans for opening their doors again are choosing to wait until next week or later to welcome pupils other than those who are vulnerable, or children of key workers.

    The government had asked primary schools to welcome back "children in nursery, reception, year 1 and year 6, alongside priority groups" today, but many local authorities told their schools not to readmit any more pupils.

    One of these was Lancashire County Council, whose director of public health, Dr. Sakthi Karunanithi, warned of the “significant risk” that would involve.

    Blackpool, however, left the decision making to individual schools.

    A spokesperson for Blackpool Council previously said, "As a local authority we have and will continue to support the stance that extending the school offer can only be done when individual schools feel that it is safe to do so."

    "We are setting no specific target date for this, each school will expand its offer when appropriate for their situation.

    "Most schools are already open and are looking at slowly bringing in additional numbers again rather than reopening."

    Research by LancsLive has found very few schools are choosing to reopen their doors to a larger number of pupils this week, however that could be compounded by the fact that some are currently in half term.

    Blackpool’s director of public health Dr Arif Rajpura said the council was committed to helping schools make the right decision for themselves as well as for families and children.

    Dr Rajpura said, "The opening of schools is important in supporting children’s welfare and education, and also allowing those who are caring for children to resume their normal working lives, and we greatly appreciate the work and dedication of our schools staff.

    "There is clear evidence that the risk to children from COVID-19 infection is lower than in the general population.

    "It also now appears that children under the age of 12 are no more infectious than adults and might actually be less infectious.

    He acknowledged any lifting of restrictions was "likely to introduce some element of risk".

    But Dr Rajpura said: "The risks with this particular proposal are low, particularly if the working practices within the school are changed and positive cases are promptly identified and contacts self-isolated in the appropriate way."

    Support will also be available through an outbreak management hub in collaboration with Public Health England to respond to cases that might occur within a school.

    The vast majority of schools in Blackpool are detailed here. If they are not here, it is because they have not released details publicly and did not respond to request for comment.
