Belfast flooding targeted by man on a mission to make city prettier

Maintenance man's quiet progress in replacing paving with green grass

    He's a man on a mission to make Belfast a city free from floods - and a whole lot prettier.

    Malachy Brennan understands how this town is prone to being swamped thanks to years of low lying ground and bogs that once absorbed rainfall being smothered in concrete.

    But he has made it his purpose in life to slowly but surely replace paving stones with green grass that not only improves districts but absorbs the downpours that can lead to flooded homes.

    The veteran of the Housing Executive’s Grounds Maintenance department, Malachy has been beavering away for years to make a real difference. So far, 15 sites across the city have been ‘brightened and softened’ by the removal of hard surfacing and 1980s style raised brick planters.

    Paving and brick work have been removed and replaced by grassed and shrub planted areas - some containing trees - in an effort to “slow the advance of surface water into the Belfast basin”.

    Malachy says: “Much of Belfast lies below sea level and the area around the Westlink, for example, lies on the Belfast flood plain. In years gone by, this bog land would have helped slowly absorb rain water and provide drainage overflow from local rivers.

    “We have a city of hills, with regular rainfall and this supported the development of Belfast as an industrial city, as the water from rivers and water courses powered many of our textile mills.

    “Housing generally sprang up around the mills in inner city Belfast. Over time, new housing areas were developed on green land, which again, had previously acted as a sponge for rainfall - the city increasingly became a concrete jungle.

    “As roads and hard surfaces replaced this soft land, flooding hot-spots developed in places like lower east Belfast, around the Lagan and surrounding the Bog Meadows .

    “I’m sure many remember when the new Westlink opened in 2008, the underpass flooded - there are three rivers in that area and the rush of water into the basin led - literally - to a perfect storm.”

    Improvements have taken place since then and Malachy’s Grounds Maintenance team have cast a keen eye over housing estates that could benefit from hard roads or brickwork being converted into grassland and the ground level planting of shrubs.

    He says: “We’ve already completed the installation of grass in 15 spots across Belfast, including areas like Sandy Row and the Falls Road, Hartwell Place in the New Lodge area and Constance Street and Tamery Pass off the Albertbridge Road .

    “Tenants tell us their areas are made to look much more aesthetically pleasing, that there is improved lighting and - in the case of Sandy Row - better access to their properties.

    “But I’m sure few realise the broader, positive impact being made in terms of flood relief.”

    Sandy Row Resident’s Association Chairperson, Margaret Gibson, said: “This work has really brightened up our area and we’re thankful to the Housing Executive for being so responsive.

    “Residents have become very protective of our new green spaces and we encourage everyone - particularly visitors and also local dog owners - to respect the area.”

    For more information, click online at www.nihe.gov.uk .
