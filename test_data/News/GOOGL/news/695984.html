What is the future of downtown Cleveland in the coronavirus age? This Week in the CLE

  • Published: May. 08, 2020, 9:00 a.m.
By
  • Laura Johnston, cleveland.com

CLEVELAND, Ohio -- How can downtown Cleveland thrive in the coronavirus era, without the office workers and big sports and entertainment options that have propelled its success.

Downtown Cleveland Alliance President and CEO Joe Marinucci joins This Week in the CLE to talk about the future, during and after the pandemic.

Listen online here.

Editor Chris Quinn normally hosts our daily half-hour coronavirus news podcast, with help from politics editor Jane Kahoun and me. We answer many of the questions you’ve sent through our text message platform. But today we’re trying something new, with our first-ever This Week in the CLE remote guest.

The Downtown Cleveland Alliance is dedicated to improving the pedestrian experience of people who work, live and play in downtown Cleveland, and attracting more investment there. On Thursday, Marinucci talked about how the city center might change without professional sports or theater and with more people working from home.

If the coronavirus ends the seasons for the Indians, the Browns, and the Cleveland Orchestra, scuttles concerts, leaves Playhouse Square dark and keeps Rock & Roll Hall of Fame closed, the economic impact could exceed $1 billion dollars. What will the new normal look like?

Marinucci talks about the trend of office buildings being converted to living spaces, and what could change in retail as the downtown population nears 25,000.

What about bars and restaurants, which depend on crowds from office workers, entertainment and professional sports?

What if the Indians play in an empty Progressive Field? Is there a way fans could watch safely from downtown restaurants? Could streets be closed off toallow restaurants to expand into the road?

If fireworks are canceled, are there alternative events we could dream up for July 4?

Does the coronavirus change the need for downtown parking? Could we add more bike lanes or playgrounds? Could we close Public Square to buses, to make it an outdoor gathering space for downtown residents?

Marinucci says there are ideas about adding bike lanes to Superior Avenue and transforming Ontario Street into a more walkable, accessible thoroughfare through downtown, maybe eliminating on-street parking for the time-being.

For some of the other questions, we’ll have to wait and see.
