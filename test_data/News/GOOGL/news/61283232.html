This Black Health Tech Founder Shines After Major Funding From Unilever And Google

Racial disparities within healthcare are not only well-known, but well-documented. And if 2020 and the corona virus pandemic taught us nothing else, it’s that these disparities aren’t going anywhere anytime soon. Black and Latino people have been disproportionately affected by the COVID-19 pandemic in a widespread manner that spans the country, throughout hundreds of counties in urban, suburban and rural areas, and across all age groups. That is of course, unless Kimberly Wilson has a say about it.

Wilson founded HUED, a healthcare technology startup, in 2018 to improve patient care experiences for Black and Latino populations. The platform does such, by connecting these patients with culturally competent providers to receive the incredible care that they need, and deserve.

Why is this necessary? One major factor is that unconscious biases held by health care providers are considered to be partially to blame for racial disparities in health. In 2005, the Institute of Medicine — now referred to as the National Academy of Medicine (NAM)—released a report documenting that the poverty in which Black people disproportionately live cannot account for the fact that Black people are sicker and have shorter life spans than their white complements. NAM found that “racial and ethnic minorities receive lower-quality health care than white people—even when insurance status, income, age, and severity of conditions are comparable.”

“HUED's mission is rooted in our goal to decrease healthcare disparities for vulnerable, underrepresented and mistreated populations, ” said Wilson. “Black people (and those within the Latinx community) simply are not receiving the same quality of health care that our white counterparts receive. One of the ways we accomplish this is simple: make it easier for Black and Latino patients to find culturally competent healthcare providers who understand their needs.”

Wilson understood this as a Black woman trying to navigate the healthcare system herself and resolved to do something about it. Though, her journey navigating the healthcare and technology industries has not been an easy one. Just as disparities exist within healthcare, the same bias exists within the multi-billion dollar tech industry, which has proven not so welcoming to people of color — specifically Black women. Of all VC funding over the past decade, Latinx women-led startups have raised only 0.32 percent while Black women have raised only .0006 percent. But that didn’t stop her from staying the course.

Thankfully, there were a few companies that saw Wilson for the visionary she is. Announced today, HUED and Unilever (Vaseline brand) have partnered in an effort to eliminate the long-standing bias against and exclusion of people of color in skin health and dermatology. The first of its kind partnership, with Academy Award-winning Regina King onboard as a campaign spokesperson, was created to meet the skin health needs for people of color by providing trusted resources and education to ensure people of all skin complexions get the care they deserve.

Ultimately, under Wilson’s leadership and the forward-thinking approach of Vaseline, they’ve come together to ignite change and galvanize the medical community to close the gap in care.

“We’re working tirelessly to build up the resources we’re able to provide for communities of color which is why this partnership with Vaseline is so critical,” says Wilson. “To build on these efforts, we've been collaborating with Vaseline to create an intuitive search tool for patients to find local dermatologists experienced in treating skin of color.”

As if a cosign from one of the largest consumer health brands wasn’t enough, HUED also received funding from Google, just last month. The $5M Google for Startups Black Founders Fund provided non-dilutive cash awards to 76 Black led startups.

“Google saw the value in HUED before anyone else did,” she revealed. “Participating in Google For Startups and American Underground’s Black Founder Exchange was already life altering for me as a first time founder. The non-dilutive funding from Google’s Black Founder Fund was just icing on the cake.”

Wilson notes that 2021 will unveil HUED’s updated technology and newer product offerings that make quality care accessible for all. “Trust and believe, we’re not stopping there,” she added. “As much as we want to just create another app or online tech community, there’s a 360 approach to seeking care. We recognize that there’s some cultural reprogramming that has to happen, and HUED’s product seeks to gamify patient care to increase incentives for communities of color to seek preventive health measures.”
