How To Remove Roadblocks And Release More Mobile Apps

Bottom Line: Enterprises are wasting valuable time by relying on manual approaches to upload mobile apps to public and private app stores when a more automated approach for updating existing apps and uploading new ones can be done in seconds. 

In A Post COVID-19 World, Mobility Rules

The hard reset every business is going through is showing how prescient a mobile-first strategy is. Proving their worth every day during this pandemic, mobile apps are making collaboration and business continuity possible. Manufacturers, including Core Technology Molding, are using mobile-based ERP apps to keep medical products and supplies production on schedule, getting healthcare professionals the supplies they need to treat and heal COVID-19 patients. In a post-COVID-19 world, mobile apps are going to save millions of lives and thousands of businesses.  

Keeping existing mobile apps current and launching new ones on app stores can be tedious, time-consuming manual work, however. Compounding the time sink is the inherent lack of collaboration that lengthy, and often error-prone, email strings create. It’s encouraging to see cybersecurity companies with deep expertise in deploying mobile apps at scale acquiring the knowledge to take on this challenge. Enterprises especially need to remove the roadblocks their customers face in getting much-needed mobile apps out to their distributed workforces. That’s why MobileIron’s acquisition of incapptic Connect is noteworthy. The bottom line is manually managing mobile apps makes it nearly impossible to manage them over their lifecycles.

Mobile App Owners Need More Power To Publish

With the renewed intensity to improve existing mobile apps and launch new ones, organizations are moving to more automated approaches to mobile app distribution. They’re removing roadblocks to releasing mobile apps by abandoning manual approaches and choosing to automate app distribution. Best of all, departments who are mobile app owners now get the power to publish any time they are ready. incapptic Connect’s architecture is designed to remove the many roadblocks every business faces in attempting to update their existing apps and publish new ones:

incapptic Connect’s Mission: Remove Mobile App Publishing Roadblocks

I recently had a chance to interview Dr. Rafael Kobylinski, founder, and CEO of incapptic Connect. Before starting incapptic Connect, he was the Manager, Systems Engineers Germany/Austria for Apple for seven years. While at Apple, he worked with enterprises on all aspects of their enterprise computing, device, and mobility strategies. While there, he realized the existing manual approaches to securely distributing apps could be improved. Here’s my short interview with Rafael:

Louis:Of the several mobile security platform vendors in the market, what is unique about your relationship with MobileIron that made the acquisition the natural progression of your partnership?

Rafael: The initial version of our product actually did not include any UEM integrations. While we were getting traction in the market, one large MobileIron customer told us that while our product was great, they were looking to achieve full self-service capability and eliminate the manual steps. To achieve this functionality, they were willing to introduce us to the great people at MobileIron. Hence, MobileIron was the first UEM integration we did, and the UEM vendor that we had the longest and increasingly stronger relationship with over the years.

Louis:On average, how many mobile apps do a typical enterprise create and load into an App Store or Google Play in a given year?

Rafael:The numbers differ significantly. While some organizations have only a few apps on the Apple App Store and Google Play, we have customers that have hundreds. That said, we have observed a tremendous growth in apps targeting an organization’s own employees. These apps typically do not appear on the Apple App Store or Google Play, but are rather distributed via systems like MobileIron UEM securely and directly to managed devices and the employees who need them.

Louis: Which shared customers of MobileIron and incapptic Connect are live today?

Rafael:One of the early adopters of our technology was CLAAS, a global agricultural machinery manufacturer that introduced our solution to deploy their mobile apps faster, safer and more cost-efficiently to their MobileIron UEM. Read the full case study here.

Louis:How did your experiences at Apple contribute to your founding incapptic Connect?

Rafael:Working at Apple gave me the unique opportunity to witness and contribute to the disruption of enterprise IT by the influx of a new generation of mobile devices like Mac laptops and the iPhone. I had observed enterprise IT departments, incumbent system integrators, and established software vendors struggle with adjusting to the new normal and sensed the opportunity. Without having a detailed business plan, I quit and started my journey towards incapptic Connect.

Louis:When an organization uploads their app into an App Store or on Google Play, does incapptic Connect also perform any quality or Cyclic Redundancy Check on the app’s code to see if it complies with App Store standards?

Rafael:Some of our customers claim 30% of their submissions to the Apple App Store and Google Play are rejected for reasons that our software can detect. Besides checking for the rejection reasons that Apple and Google have documented officially, we have added checks for other issues that we have discovered cause submission problems over the years while our customers have been using our product.

Louis:Is it possible to batch load several different UEM-based applications simultaneously using incapptic Connect?

Rafael:We provide a very simple to use REST API that can be used to upload new versions to our system automatically. Our customers use this REST API to build end-to-end integrations with upstream development toolchains of various types, e.g. Jenkins, GitLab, or Atlassian Bamboo.

Louis:Can you explain how your technology in incapptic Connect is language-agnostic so multiple language-based applications can be uploaded at the same time?

Rafael: Early on, we made a deliberate decision to not restrict the development environment. Based on our experience with large enterprise customers, we observed it’s mute to try to dictate a specific tool chain or environment enterprise wide. Rather, we decided to allow the lowest common denominator, a binary file, IPK for iOS or APK for Android. Hence, we don’t care if a certain department or third-party supplier uses native tools or cross-platform frameworks like PhoneGap, Xamarin, React Native, or some other framework of their choice. It just works.

Conclusion

Thanks to mobile-first app development and continual updates, medical supplies and much-needed testing kits are getting produced around the world because manufacturers can keep producing them. First responders and healthcare providers are using mobile devices to collaborate in real-time and do all they can to save lives. Every business struggling through the hard reset every economy on the planet is going through has a continuity plan thanks to mobile apps. Staying connected with employees, customers, and suppliers is all possible because mobile apps are available 24/7 and work. Removing the roadblocks to launching more mobile apps couldn’t have come at a better time.
