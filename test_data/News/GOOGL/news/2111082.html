Yorkshire pub shut down by police after 'shameless' owners serve punters during coronavirus lockdown

A vape shop in Manor, Sheffield, was also forced to close after breaking the rules

    A Sheffield pub has been slapped with a notice ordering it to shut down after breaking strict coronavirus lockdown rules by "shamelessly" continuing to serve customers.

    The Staffordshire Arms pub in Burngreave received a visit from police last Friday night after members of the public reported concerns that the owner was still serving people on his premises.

    Sheffield City Council investigated the evidence and issued a prohibition notice which was served to the pub's owner by South Yorkshire Police.

    On the same day, the owners of the vape shop Prince of Vapez in Manor, Sheffield, were ordered to close after also continuing to operate in breach of Government rules.

    The council’s health and safety officers visited the premises in Prince of Wales Road and served a notice to the owner requiring the business to close. The owner was also warned that failure to comply would lead to prosecution.

    Sheffield City Council and South Yorkshire Police are able to use new enforcement powers to close businesses that continue to operating outside of Government guidance under the new Health Protection (Coronavirus Restrictions) (England) Regulations 2020 Act.

    It was announced by the Government last week that under this new legislation local authorities and the police can close premises that are flouting the closure ban.

    Councillor Mark Jones, cabinet member for environment, streetscene and climate change at Sheffield City Council said: “It’s been encouraging to see that most Sheffielders are adhering to the social distancing guidelines and staying at home unless absolutely necessary.

    “I know this is a really difficult time for many residents and businesses, but the Government has been very clear – stay at home. And there’s an essential reason for this, to save lives.

    “We simply cannot allow business owners to ignore the guidance, it's our duty to make sure they listen. We now have the power to enforce this and we are. There is support available to help businesses to get through this pandemic. So I urge them to remain closed, for their own safety as well as everyone else.”

    John O’Malley, liquor licensing manager for South Yorkshire Police, said he hopes the owner of The Staffordshire Arms' "shameless" idea to carry on serving post-lockdown will show that such actions will not be tolerated.

    He said: “The majority of licensed premises in Sheffield, and South Yorkshire more widely, have been fantastic during what is a really difficult and uncertain time.

    "I hope that this closure order, on a premises that has shamelessly gone against the legislation, sends a message that we are taking this seriously and we are taking action.

    “It is also important that licensed premises understand that there could be longer term consequences linked to any breaches of the legislation, so it is wholly in their interests to comply. I’d like to thank all of those landlords who have done so, with great patience and professionalism, and I’d encourage the public to report any breaches they see or hear.”

    Other reports have been made to the council and South Yorkshire Police and these are being investigated. Anyone found to be operating in contravention of the new rules will face enforcement action.

    Greg Fell, director of public health in Sheffield, said: “It’s concerning to hear that some businesses are still not observing social distancing and other Public Health England guidance.

    "We will take action because we have to. Every interaction outside the home is a potential virus transmission point and we must reduce those points of contact in order to reduce the spread of this virus.

    “I will keep saying it, stay at home, save lives. It’s the most important thing you can do now to prevent this illness overwhelming our health services and to protect yourselves and your loved ones.”
