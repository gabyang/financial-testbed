Grand Opera House Miss Saigon youth talent wow as rehearsals get underway

The teenagers will be taking on Miss Saigon School Edition as part of the annual Grand Opera House Summer Youth Project - the first of two productions this summer

    Star of screen and stage Anthony Boyle once tread the boards of the Grand Opera House as part of its first summer youth project.

    Now a host of other aspiring stars are hoping they too will follow in his footsteps as they prepare to wow audiences this summer.

    This July, 80 talented youngsters will be staging Miss Saigon School Edition at the iconic Belfast theatre and in August 140 young people will take on Bugsy Malone.

    It is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity for the children, who are aged from 10-18, to work with professionals on the same stage as some of the idols.

    This week rehearsals got underway for Miss Saigon which runs from July 18-21, so Belfast Live were invited along to see how the stars of the future were getting on.

    This is the ninth year of the summer youth project and across that time, 1,700 young people have come through the doors with most taking on roles on stage, alongside a small number of youth musicians and youth technicians.

    "Over the past couple of years we have really upped the game in terms of production values, we want the young people taking part to feel as if they are part of a big, major musical," said Ian Wilson, chief executive of the Grand Opera House .

    "Therefore the productions in the last couple of years have all been fully staged, there's a full set, full lighting rig, lighting designers, sound designers, costumes designers and the whole gambit to make this the biggest summer youth project of its kind in Northern Ireland."

    The summer youth project is also a chance for the Grand Opera House technical and creative team to shine as the theatre is a receiving house and rarely get the opportunity to produce their own shows.

    Getting the rights for Miss Saigon School Edition this year was also a huge opportunity for the theatre, which is preparing to go dark after this year's panto for some restoration work.

    "The chance to get Miss Saigon is quite rare for us given the size of the Grand Opera House, the rights are usually locked off which means we can't perform it," said Ian.

    "So we were given the unique opportunity to perform it and that has its own challenges because the Grand Opera House stage is the biggest and most iconic stage in Northern Ireland so to take on one of the biggest and most successful musicals of all time and to translate that onto our stage then gives us the challenge to upscale and make sure the production values match the expectations of not only the young people taking part but also the audience as well."

    Planning for this show started last August and in February this year, auditions were held to find the young people to take part and over 400 put themselves forward for the two productions.

    "It took us three days of auditions to find these young people, then on top of that we have to plan for the set, the lighting, the sound and of course unique to Miss Saigon is that moment when the helicopter lands on stage," said Ian.

    "You can cheat in as much as you can use lighting to have the effect of the helicopter landing, but given that this is the Grand Opera House and given this is the biggest youth summer project of its kind in Northern Ireland, we then decided to make our own helicopter."

    The cast have around two weeks to bring everything together before taking to the stage so the pressure is on, although director Tony Finnegan and choreographer Rebecca Leonard are more than confident the young people are up to the task.

    Two days in Tony said the rehearsals were going "fantastic" and they he was "very confident that we have the right people".

    "A lot of kids do want to do this one because you don't normally get an opportunity to do Miss Saigon in such a beautiful theatre," he said.

    "We have a great production team, we throw everything at them in terms of professionals, the only thing that's amateur are the kids and they are the top kids we could find in Northern Ireland so they have a brilliant opportunity to work with people they wouldn't normally get to work with and I am talking stage crew, lighting people, sound people, all the top guys."

    Talking about what audiences can expect, he added: "I can understand that when they hear youth production that they think it's not going to be of a professional standard but it really is because of two things. One is we surround them with professionalism and because we get choosing the top talent, people who are possibly going to go into the business and we have caught them at the right time.

    "They are fantastic and they give you such energy, the standard of the Grand Opera House youth productions is very, very high and you should come to see it because you are going to see such energy."

    Rebecca, who has been with the project since it started, said one of the most rewarding things for her was seeing people progress through the chorus to leading roles and the team spirit on show.

    "This year we have quite a few new people as well which is great to see them join in and the dynamics change," she said.

    "I get to see hundreds of young people on this stage every summer and see how they progress and learn and make friends and that is the best bit about this whole thing."

    She added: "There is something very special about the Grand Opera House, not only because of its history but the sheer size of audiences who come to watch these shows, it is something I never had the opportunity to do when I was 10 or 11 so it is an amazing experience.

    "They come here and rehearse for two weeks, we do rehearsals for about 10 days and then we are into running shows so it is a very fast pace rehearsal time. They are treated like professionals, they are expected to learn a lot very quickly, we work very fast and they rise to the challenge and we hopefully every year put on a really good show so it is an amazing experience in so many aspects for them."

    You can catch Miss Saigon at the Grand Opera House from July 18 to 21 and Bugsy Malone from August 1-3.
