    Google Maps update borrows a useful feature from Apple Maps - and we can't wait

    GOOGLE has started to test a new feature within Google Maps that seems to be inspired by the latest upgrade to Apple Maps.

    By Aaron Brown, Express Affiliate Development Editor with 10 years of experience writing about the latest developments in consumer technology, product reviews, and buying advice
    08:26, Sat, Jul 11, 2020 | UPDATED: 08:26, Sat, Jul 11, 2020
    Link copied Bookmark

    Google Chrome explain how users can view extension data

    Google Maps is an incredibly useful navigation tool – whether you're looking to walk, take public transport, drive, or bike – that is being slowly improved and streamlined by the Mountain View-based company all the time. And for the latest upgrade, it seems Google looked to Apple for inspiration.

    Google is testing the ability to show the location of traffic lights on its maps. Traffic lights will be displayed within the main app as well as when following turn-by-turn navigation. Apple introduced this feature for its redesigned mapping service last year.

    The new feature was part of a massive overhaul of Apple Maps, which brought more detailed maps than Google Maps across the United States. When iOS 14 rolls out to iPhone owners worldwide this autumn, it will bring these highly precise maps to users in the UK and Canada for the first time.

    Being able to spot traffic lights on the map ahead of time is a useful feature. Of course, it not only means drivers are less likely to be caught off-guard by traffic lights at an intersection, but it also means drivers and cyclists are able to choose their route based on the number of lights in their path.

    Unfortunately, Google isn't yet able to show the current status of the lights.

    So, you won't be able to see whether the light is about to change – or has recently turned red. However, as 5G mobile networks become more common, it's possible that smart cities – with a network of connected traffic lights, zebra crossings, and more – could tie into mapping solutions like Google Maps and Apple Maps in future.

    Google seems to be gradually testing the system with users in the United States. Android-obsessed blog Droid-Life has reported seeing traffic lights pop-up in West Des Moines.
