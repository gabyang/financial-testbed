Police inadvertently found 'sophisticated' cannabis farm after being told someone was being attacked

Endri Velaj, 28, who was found at a house in Crumpsall, has been jailed and faces deportation

    Police inadvertently discovered a 'sophisticated' cannabis farm after being told a man was being attacked by a balaclava clad gang.

    When officers arrived at Holland Road in Crumpsall, there was no sign of the four alleged attackers or the victim.

    But when they conducted door to door enquiries they found a cannabis farm in a semi detached home.

    No-one was present, but in the joining house they found another cannabis farm, where 28-year-old Endri Velaj was in an upstairs bedroom on March 4.

    A total of 110 plants were found, which police estimated could be worth anything from £2,000 to £41,200, depending on the strength of the drug and whether it was sold in bulk or in street deals.

    Velaj is now set to be deported back to his native Albania, after he was jailed for 12 months at Manchester Crown Court.

    The court was told that Velaj had been promised legitimate work in the UK, but it never materialised.

    He did not dare to refuse the request to 'supervise' the cannabis farm, the court heard, as he was worried about his family in Albania.

    Velaj's lawyer Michael Balmer said £465 in cash which police found at the property had been given to the defendant by his 'gangmasters' for food and supplies.

    The cash was supposed to last two months, Mr Balmer said.

    He said that the bypassing of the electricity supply had not been done by Velaj, and that there were cannabis plants installed in the property before the defendant arrived.

    The court was told that Velaj may have had a defence to the charge of producing cannabis, if he was found to have been a victim of 'modern slavery'.

    But Velaj refused to give his consent for further investigations to be conducted, 'for fear of reprisals'.

    He now wants to return to Albania 'as soon as possible', the court heard.

    Judge Anthony Cross QC said that he was locking Velaj up on the basis that he 'volunteered' to supervise the cannabis farm.

    "Whilst I don't accept that you were the victim of modern slavery, I do accept that you were more vulnerable than others to threats of violence," the judge said.

    Judge Cross said it was a 'sophisticated operation' and a 'significant' amount of cannabis was being grown.

    Because he was sentenced to 12 months in jail, Velaj is automatically liable for deportation after serving his sentence. He admitted an offence of producing cannabis.
