Sit Fund Excels With Mix Of Home Runs And Singles

Licensing
  • PAUL KATZEFF
  • 01:40 PM ET 11/05/2021

If Roger Sit, lead manager of one of the best mutual funds, were skipper of a baseball team, it would be a "Moneyball" club. Its lineup would feature low-cost journeymen, not just costly home run sluggers.

And that's how and his co-manager brother Ron run their growth stock portfolio, $191.5 million Sit Large Cap Growth Fund (SNIGX). Roger Sit said, "We try to hit singles and doubles. If we happen to hit a lot of triples or home runs in a year, more power to us. But the last thing we want is to swing for the fences and strike out. We take a slow-but-steady approach instead."

That means holding stock in "companies that report strong, visible, high-quality growth," he said. That's how the Sit portfolio got to be one of the best mutual funds.

Best Mutual Funds: Looking For Tailwinds

Most stocks like that are benefiting from a long-term secular trend. Many are leaders in technology, services and software, Sit says. Those all-stars have helped the portfolio become one of the best growth stock funds.

But about 20% of the fund's work aims at discovering companies that benefit from some cycle. The success may not be long-term. But it's driving winners in perhaps the next three to five years, Sit says.

They include names that they see as cyclically sensitive, like JPMorgan Chase (JPM). "They have different drivers that help grow their top line in a crosscurrent environment," Sit said. "They're not only a traditional bank, but they have capital markets exposure. They have investment banking and mergers-and-acquisitions."

Among industrials, the Sits like Parker-Hannifin (PH). The company makes motion and control devices and systems. "It participates in the pickup in economic activity as we get past the supply chain issue," Sit said.

He added, "We own Linde (LIN), a producer of industrial gasses, because we anticipate a pickup in economic activity as we get past Covid-19." Linde was named No. 2 last week on IBD's Best 100 ESG Companies of 2021.

The fund also owns Applied Materials (AMAT). The company supplies equipment, services and software for manufacturing computer chips. "Amid the chip shortage, Intel (INTC) is ordering more semiconductor capital equipment," Sit said.

And the fund holds FedEx (FDX). "We bought FedEx as it got beaten up," Sit said. "It will get its act together. And there's plenty of demand for sending parcels all over the world."

Lineup For One Of The Best Mutual Funds

Those singles and doubles hitters helped lift this lineup into the ranks of the best mutual funds. Compare them to a batting champ like Nvidia (NVDA), another holding. Nvidia's average annual return is 126% over the two years through Oct. 31, according to Morningstar Direct. Those journeymen's yearly average gains range from JPMorgan's 19% to Applied Materials' 60%.

Still, most are outperforming the S&P 500's 25% yearly pace. "Those companies are participating in the economic improvement," Sit said. "They are coming into the favorable part of their business cycles."

Large Cap Growth Fund's All-Stars

Nvidia, Google-parent Alphabet (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT) are among Sit Large Cap Growth holdings that are riding long-term secular waves. Sit and his team spend about 80% of their time researching all-stars like those.

They are key drivers in the fund's push to remain among the best growth stock funds.

How Nvidia Helps Sit's Portfolio Rank Among Best Mutual Funds

How did Nvidia help Sit Large Cap Growth land among the best mutual funds? Nvidia makes high-speed computer chips. Those are vital to everything from graphics to gaming, artificial intelligence (AI), data centers, 5G networks and autonomous driving.

Many program developers learn their skills on Nvidia's CUDA platform. They stick with Nvidia chips after commencing their careers.

AI alone presents myriad opportunities for Nvidia chips, Sit says. It should be an area for huge growth. Facebook's (FB) pending name-change to AI-related "Meta" is evidence of that. Nvidia chips will likely be heavily used in AI processes by Facebook and others, Sit says. He added, "And think of all the other industries getting involved in AI like health care, financial services and retail. So there will be lots of opportunities for a market leader like Nvidia."

Nvidia is building sales not only in the U.S. but elsewhere as well. "They're selling everything they make," Sit said. "This is scalable. So they should have strong earnings growth."

Alphabet: Google And What Else?

Alphabet plays a star role in this portfolio's drive to repeat as one of the best mutual funds. Much of that success stems from Google. The search engine draws advertising revenue. Is the absence of big, or any, profits from other business lines a worry for Sit?

Alphabet's cloud services business is growing as it competes with Amazon.com's (AMZN) Amazon Web Services, he says. Alphabet's YouTube is also growing. Its Waymo self-driving car technology business is not yet profitable, Sit says. He doesn't think Google Cloud Platform is yet either. But Alphabet overall is incubating those lines. "If you want to bet on a front-runner with a great current product that is remaking itself for the future, this is the company," Sit said.

Microsoft Incubates Potential Future Leaders

Like Alphabet, Microsoft is an incubator of promising future businesses, Sit says. "You've got to give them credit for that too," Sit said. "They're diving into the metaverse, AI and the cloud. The cloud is where you can get increased productivity and cost effectiveness. So this is another company with several levers for future revenue growth. And that can give you more predictability in earnings. So it's a company people want to own."

By The Numbers: One Of The Best Mutual Funds

This growth stock fund became an IBD Best Mutual Fund Awards winner by topping the S&P 500 in 2020 and in the three, five and 10 years ended Dec. 31.

This year through October, the fund's 34.57% return topped the S&P 500. The fund's large-cap growth rivals tracked by Morningstar Direct were up 33.04% on average.

Sit's father, Eugene, founded fund parent Sit Investment Associates in 1981.
