Coronavirus updates: Prime Minister returns to work as UK lockdown continues

North Staffordshire medic living in caravan to protect family, a local sportswear firm is making scrubs, a little girl in Silverdale is spreading smiles with rainbow rocks. More local and national updates as they come in throughout the day

Matt SimpsonSenior Live Reporter
  • Updated13:10, 27 APR 2020
  • Bookmark

    • Prime Minister Boris Johnson has arrived back in Downing Street after recuperating from Covid-19 at his country home Chequers.

    He is due to resume work today and will chair the morning meeting of the Government's coronavirus 'war cabinet'.

    Over the weekend the number of lab-confirmed cases of the virus in the UK rose to 152,840 - while the number of people who have died in UK hospitals with Covid-19 has risen to 20,732.

    The number of unconfirmed cases will be far higher - and people have also died in care homes, private residences and other locations.

    Locally there are now 395 confirmed cases in Stoke-on-Trent, 1,560 in Staffordshire and 757 in Cheshire East.

    As of Sunday afternoon's update 168 people had died at Royal Stoke University Hospital and Stafford County Hospital after testing positive for the virus - along with one at Harplands Hospital and two at Burslem's Haywood Hospital.

    The number of deaths at Mid Cheshire NHS Trust -which includes Crewe's Leighton Hospital - is now 103, while 64 deaths have been recorded at East Cheshire NHS Trust - which runs hospitals in Macclesfield and Congleton.

    13:10

    Some Sage participants to be named

    Downing Street confirmed some of the Sage participants will be named “in the coming days” while further advice documents from the group will be published this week.

    “It’s right that only the names of those who wish to disclose their participation will be published,” the Prime Minister’s official spokesman said.

    “This is to safeguard the personal security for the individuals and to protect them from lobbying which may hinder their ability to give impartial advice.”

    He added that the Sage advice documents and evidence are expected to be published some time this week.

    Questions from the public to be put to government

    Members of the public will be given the chance to question ministers at the daily Downing Street coronavirus press conferences.

    Questions will be chosen by an independent polling organisation, a statement on the Gov.uk website said.

    “The Government is not involved in choosing questions,” the statement said.

    Under the plan, one question a day from a member of the public will be put to ministers.

    Should people be quarantined at UK airports?

    Asked about reports of people arriving at UK airports being quarantined for two weeks, Mr Argar said: “It’s a possibility but it’s not something we’re in a position to say we’re going to do at this point.”

    He explained: “The scientific advice is it can be useful if you are trying to contain a virus, but the virus is already in this country and therefore the advice from the chief scientific adviser and chief medical officer thus far has been this would – in scientific and clinical terms – have a minimal effect in actually slowing the spread of the virus.”

    08:22

    People should expect 'dramatic increase' in testing

    Health minister Edward Argar said people should expect “dramatic increases” in the number of people being tested for coronavirus.

    Asked if the Government would hit the 100,000 tests figure by the end of the month he told the BBC: “We’ve seen in recent days an exponential increase in the number of tests being made available and we’ve increased dramatically the eligibility for them.”

    He added: “I suspect you will see in the next few days very dramatic increases.”

    He also said numbers had been higher than usual this weekend, adding: “This weekend, when normally at a weekend we see a slight dip in the numbers actually this weekend we’ve seen more tests done than on previous weekends.

    “The modelling for these tests and for the roll-out is it won’t be a smooth increase, it will be a dramatic day-by-day exponential increase which is what we are beginning to see.”

    Half of all InterContinentinal Hotels closed

    Around half of all the hotels run by InterContinental are closed in Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Australia, the company revealed on Monday morning as coronavirus sweeps across several continents.

    The hotel giant said that 10% of US hotels are also closed, as it announced that the company had taken £600 million under a Bank of England loan scheme to help larger businesses through the crisis.

    Intercontinental said that it has also committed to keeping 400 million US dollars (£322 million) in assets that are easy to sell off to convert into cash until June 30 next year.

    Trades Union Congress calls for shifts to be staggered

    Frances O’Grady, general secretary of the Trades Union Congress (TUC), said she wants to see shifts staggered and social distancing rules continue when the lockdown is eased by the Government.

    She told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “Everyone wants people to get back to work safely so we can get the economy back on its feet.

    “But workers have to know and be confident that their health and safety is being put first. Otherwise we are going to see the virus spread again and be back to square one.

    “We are calling for tough new measures – we think there should be a proper risk assessment on the virus in every workplace, that we need practical changes like social distancing, staggered shifts, safe transport.

    “And we need those risk assessments signed-off by unions or health and safety inspectors, with tough enforcement and the resources to do that tough enforcement.”

    Ms O’Grady said the issue should be at the “top of the Prime Minister’s in-tray” now that he has returned to work.
