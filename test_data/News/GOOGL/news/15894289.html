Mel Morris reveals 'brutal' impact of crisis on Derby County, Nottingham Forest and rivals

The football season has been suspended for more than two months as a result of the coronavirus pandemic

    Mel Morris - chairman and owner of Nottingham Forest's arch rivals Derby County - has revealed the "brutal" impact of the coronavirus crisis on Championship clubs.

    The football season has been suspended for more than two months in the wake of the Covid-19 pandemic.

    Forest and their Championship counterparts could return to training from next week ahead of a potential return to action.

    However, that would be behind closed doors for safety reasons, which will have far-reaching financial implications for clubs beyond what has already been experienced up to know.

    Morris has reassured Derby fans that the club can survive through all of this, though admitted challenging times lay ahead for the Rams and others.

    "In 1884, there was a Derby County and I guarantee in 100 years from now there will still be a Derby County," Morris told the club's official website.

    "This is a devastating time for our club, but also for lots of others, but overall no matter what the challenges are I honestly believe the top flight clubs will definitely survive this and come out the other side of it, but it will be challenging.

    "If you look at the way the football pyramid operates, if you look at the Premier League many of the clubs there their gate receipts are maybe 10% to 15% of their revenues.

    "So for them, as long as they continue to get their media incomes they are in a reasonably good state even playing without supporters in the stadium.

    "In the Championship, those numbers are far more brutal.

    "In the Championship, many clubs are operating where the actual gate receipts represent anywhere between 40% to 80% of the revenues for the club. And that is devastating when those things are impacted.

    "As you go down the pyramid the challenges get tougher and tougher.

    "But to answer the question, I think there will be a Derby County even 500 or 600 years from now."
