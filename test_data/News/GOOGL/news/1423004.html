UK Housing Market Interest Revives Strongly But Surveyors Report Fall In House Prices

By Tim Clayton
Published: Jun 11, 2020 at 18:00
Updated: Jun 11, 2020 at 18:35
UK Economy UK House Prices

The re-opening of the housing market in England has boosted activity, but a greater proportion of surveyors reported a decline in prices.

The RICS housing index declined to -32 for May from -22 the previous month indicating that a net balance of 32% of surveyors experiences lower house prices during the month. This was weaker than consensus forecasts of -24 and the lowest reading since late 2010.

On a near-term view, respondents expect prices to decline further with 43% expecting prices to decline. Looking 12 months ahead, a balance of 16% of agents are expecting prices to fall.

There was a re-opening of the market in England during the month, although the market remained closed in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Image: rics balance

A balance of 35% estate agents said newly-agreed sales were down. There was certainly greater interest from prospective buyers, although a balance of 5% of estate agents reports lower enquiries for May compared with 94% for April.

Even where there was an increase in enquiries, doubts whether this interest would translate into sales given uncertainty over employment and tighter credit standards.

Overall, near term sales expectations turned broadly neutral in May at -4% from -58% previously, and twelve-month sales expectations are now slightly positive.

Simon Rubinsohn, Rics chief economist, commented: “Following the reopening of the housing market in England, pre-Covid sales that were in the pipeline are now largely going through. This is encouraging but it remains to be seen how sustained this improvement will prove.

“Much will inevitably depend on the macro environment and, in particular, the resilience of the jobs market as the furlough scheme unwinds. For the time being respondents to the survey see the trend in transactions being broadly flat.”

There were expectations that the coronavirus outbreak would trigger a shift in underlying patterns of behaviour and housing demand. The vast majority of surveyors and estate agents (81%) expected homes with gardens or balconies will be in greater demand over the next two years. Properties near green spaces are expected to be in demand and 68% reckon that properties with greater private and less communal space will become more desirable.

In contrast, properties in crowded urban areas are expected to be less in demand. 78% expected a decline in demand for tower blocks with 58% expecting a drop in demand for housing in highly urban areas.

There were no significant expectations of change in relation to proximity to transport hubs.

Image: rics expectations

Looking at the rental market RICS commented; “In the lettings market, tenant demand was down over the month and landlord instructions continued to fall sharply, extending a trend that long predates the impact of the COVID-19 outbreak.”

Google Trends data compounded signs that demand has rebounded, with online searches for the three most popular housing websites — Rightmove, Zoopla and Onthemarket — last month recovering to 76 per cent of January’s level.

There should be a further rebound in activity for June.

According to Samuel Tombs, chief UK economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics; “May’s Rics survey provides more evidence that housing market activity recovered quickly after the government permitted home-seekers to view properties in person and surveyors to inspect properties again.” Nevertheless he was concerned that housing demand will “peter out later this year, once some of the pent-up demand has faded”.

There will be particular risks once government employment support measures are scaled back.

Latest data from Zoopla, reported that demand for housing is now 54% higher than at the start of March, as pent-up demand returns to the property market while new agreed sales are just 12% below levels in early March.

Richard Donnell, Director of Research & Insight, commented: “The rebound in housing demand over the last month is not solely explained by a return of pent-up demand. COVID has brought a whole new group of would-be buyers into the housing market.”

Trends in global risk appetite have dominated Sterling moves with downbeat Fed commentary triggering a less confident tone. Sterling/dollar has stabilised around 1.2700 after highs around 1.2815 and lows near 1.2650 in volatile trading.
