A combination of quarantine, multiple streaming outlets, and decades of excellent horror means there is a lot of ground for horror fans to cover.

Horror as a genre predates the advent of film, popularized in written and oral fiction. However, its relationship with cinema is certainly intertwined. For about as long as there have been movies, there have been horror movies, meaning there are over a century of horror films out there for fans of the genre to digest.

Related: Every Movie & TV Show Now Streaming For Free

Streaming has changed the way audiences consume media. Netflix introduced its streaming services in 2007, kicking off the trend that made movies and television readily available to home audiences. Now, there are a plethora of platforms to find content on, from Hulu to Disney+. The rash of quarantines caused by COVID-19 has completely restructured the film release schedule, meaning moviegoers have been turning to streaming platforms almost exclusively for any content, both old and new. Here are the best horror movies from each decade to stream at home.

The House of the Devil: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1890s

✕ Remove Ads

Georges Méliès, considered by many to be the father of cinema, released Le Manoir Du Diable in 1896. As such, it is the first horror movie ever, an integral part of the genre’s history. Although its tone is far more light-hearted than contemporary horror fans might expect, its use of traditional special effects is impressive for its time. For many years, the movie was lost until a single copy was discovered in a New Zealand junk shop in 1988. If anything, Le Manoir Du Diableis worth watching as a relic of cinema. Being so old, it is now in the public domain, making it quite easy to access. Although a silent film, many of the online copies include a musical track, adding to the movie’s overall atmosphere.

Le Manoir Du Diable can be streamed on Youtube, or on the Ngā Taonga Sound & Vision website.

✕ Remove Ads

The Merry Frolics Of Satan: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1900s

Another film by Méliès, Les Quat'Cents Farces Du Diable is one of the most visually disturbing movies to come out of the decade. Although Spanish director Segundo de Chomón released many iconic horror films at the time, it is the combination of beautiful color tinting and genuinely terrifying skeletal horse practical effects that define Méliès’ 1906 masterpiece. A fast-paced short film, its hellish visuals are evocative of the whirlwind tunnel scene from 1971’s Willy Wonka And The Chocolate Factorythat has scarred kids for generations.

Les Quat'Cents Farces Du Diable can be streamed on Youtube, or on Internet Archive.

Frankenstein: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1910s

Horror in the early 20th century often adapted some of literature’s darker tales: Jekyll and Hyde, folk tales. The 1910 adaptation of Frankenstein is iconic, faithful to the book in its theming (if not in its ending). Its use of interesting practical effects reinforces the story’s primary question: who is the real monster? With Thomas Edison involved in the film’s production, it makes a name for itself as a slice of history. The Library Of Congress did a recent preservation of the film’s reel, so the copies available online are crisp and highly watchable.

Frankenstein can be streamed on Youtube or on the Library Of Congress website.

✕ Remove Ads

Nosferatu: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1920s

Nosferatu,as a piece of German expressionism, leans into the use of shadow and light to create disturbing imagery. A full-length feature film, it was originally released in 1922 in Germany as an adaptation of Dracula. However, Bram Stoker’s heirs sued for copyright infringement, so it didn’t make it to the United States until the 1929. The movie swaps the word vampire for nosferatu and Count Dracula for Count Orluk, but some online versions changed the title cards to reflect Stoker’s original language. Although an adaptation, Nosferatuis so iconic, it has managed to establish its own independent legacy.

The Mummy: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1930s

The legacy of 1932’s The Mummy is undeniable. It is the birthplace of one of Universal’s most iconic movie monsters, a character that has been used time and time again throughout the decades. The first spoken film on this list, the movie relies on interesting character acting and a creeping dread. Although the oldest, it remains one of the best adaptations of the mummy story to date, with a slow measured approach to horror.

The Mummy can be streamed and purchased through YouTube, iTunes, Google Play, Vudu, or Amazon Prime.

✕ Remove Ads

The Wolf Man: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1940s

With Lon Chaney Jr as the lead and Bela Lugosi in a supporting role, The Wolf Man has serious movie monster talent behind it. It came out in 1941, a spooky portrayal of the werewolf story. Although it is preceded by a silent 1920s movie of the same name, it is this iteration that can really be considered the film that launched the werewolf genre, making everything from An American Werewolf In London to Teen Wolf possible.

The Wolf Man can be streamed or purchased on Vudu, iTunes, Google Play, YouTube, or Amazon Prime.

The Fly: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1950s

The Fly includes some of the most disturbing imagery to come from 1950s horror. Hitting theatres in 1958, it takes the phobia of insects and amplifies it, creating a giant insect piece of sci-fi horror that is genuinely disturbing. The plot might border on the ridiculous, but the film also has a genuine sense of fun, with classic horror that occasionally dips into the unnerving.

The Fly can be streamed or purchased from YouTube, iTunes, Google Play, Vudu, or Amazon Prime.

Rosemary’s Baby: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1960s

✕ Remove Ads

It is difficult to narrow down one “best horror movie” of the 1960s as the decade features some of the most ground-breaking movies in the genre: Alfred Hitchcock’s Psycho, and Night of The Living Dead. However, Rosemary’s Baby has a universality to it that makes it as disturbing today as it was when it came out in 1968. Described as “the best horror movie without any horror in it” by its own producer, the movie places audience sympathies alongside Mia Farrow’s highly convincing Rosemary. It focuses on universal fears: contagion, invasion, something being wrong with our babies.

Rosemary’s Baby can be purchased or streamed from YouTube, Google Play, Vudu, Amazon Prime, iTunes, or Netflix.

The Exorcist: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1970s

Once again, the 1970s are a difficult decade to narrow down. The slasher movie was in full swing with films like The Texas Chainsaw Massacre and Halloween hitting the silver screen. Alien, Carrie, and The Omen are also horror classics that launched over the course of the decade. However, The Exorcist’s place in pop culture and excellent combination of directing, acting and writing earns it the top spot of its decade. It is a genuinely scary movie that terrified audiences when it came out, and can still terrify audiences today. Its focus on demonic possession and exorcism is chilling while Linda Blair’s portrayal of Regan is extremely convincing.

The Exorcist can be streamed or purchased through YouTube, Google Play, Vudu, Amazon Prime, iTunes, or Cinemax.

The Shining: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1980s

Although Stephen King was not happy with Stanley Kubrick’s adaptation of his book, there is no denying that this 1980 film is a part of horror legacy. The Shining leans into the psychological horror of the book, but it is Kubrick’s meticulous directing that adds so many extra levels of disturbing. His set design and impossible architecture make the movie simultaneously dizzying and breath-taking. Jack Nicholson does a fantastic job portraying Jack’s descent into madness. Overall, it is must-watch for horror and non-horror fans alike.

The Shining can be streamed or purchased on YouTube, Google Play, Vudu, iTunes, or Amazon Prime.

The Sixth Sense: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 1990s

Many genre-defining horror movies came out in the 1990s. Scream proved the fear potential of well-crafted parody. The Blair Witch Project launched the found footage genre. The Silence Of The Lambs consistently makes it into top movies of all time listings. However, 1999’s The Sixth Sense serves as a perfect specimen of 1990s horror. It combines a creepy premise with strong acting, and plot twists that were shocking in their time and are shocking today (if spoilers can be avoided). It is, in essence, a contemporary ghost story, capturing the kind of tale whispered at campfires and sleepovers in a Hollywood style.

✕ Remove Ads

The Sixth Sense can be streamed or purchased from YouTube, Google Play, iTunes, Amazon Prime, or Vudu.

The Descent: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 2000s

When one thinks of 2000s horror, many supernatural films come to mind: The Others, Paranormal Activity, 28 Days Later. It was also the decade that saw the rise of the torture porn genre through Saw. However, it is Neil Marshall’s 2005 flick The Descent that brings together many of the fears represented in these, and amplifies them. Best described as “spelunking gone wrong”, this claustrophobic supernatural film is the definition of the word disturbing. It is worth noting that The Descent has two alternate endings. Hereditary: Where To Stream The Best Horror Movie From The 2010s

Hereditary displays an extremely measured approach to horror. Ari Aster’s horror debut, it combines astonishing cinematography with talented acting. Hereditary contains some of the most shocking twists in the decade but doesn’t rely on shock factor to move its story forward. Its quiet creeping dread means the movie stays in audiences’ minds long after the credits roll. With slow camera work and corner-of-your-eye horror that is deliciously unsettling, Hereditary makes such good work of foreshadowing, its resolution feels extremely satisfying, despite the fear it instills.
