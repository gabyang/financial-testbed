Google Just Gave Your Chromebook Another Gaming Boost

Your cheap Chromebook is getting another gaming boost, Google has announced.

Similar to Apple’s new(ish) focus on high-end mobile gaming, the search company rolled out a new premium gaming category on the Google Play Store, which houses games that are optimised to play on Chromebooks. 

Google says games like Game Dev Tycoon and Bridge Constructor Portal have made the initial list. Browsing through the new section also shows there is a healthy selection of titles, including favourites like Monument Valley 2 and Don’t Starve: Pocket Edition.

Google has also created a “works with Chromebook” certification for controllers, which obviously includes the Stadia controller. Two other wireless devices from Logitech, the F710 and F310, have also made the list.

ForbesMillions Of Chromebooks To Get Another Serious Feature UpgradeBy Jay McGregor

This new Play Store update represents one of many upgrades for the humble Chromebook in 2020. Google’s game streaming service, Stadia, not only bought AAA gaming to underpowered Chrome OS laptops, it is also free for three months with Chromebook purchases. Nvidia announced in August that its long-standing game streaming service GeForce Now is also compatible with Chromebooks. I’d be happy to bet that Xbox Game Pass Ultimate makes its way to Chrome OS at some point in the new future too.

There’s also the new partnership between Google and Parallels that will bring native Windows apps to Chrome OS. Enterprise Chromebook users will be able to run Windows apps locally on Chromebooks without an internet connection or rebooting. Users looking for something extra from their Chromebook can also dive into Linux apps, which offer a range of serious desktop-like programs. 

The Chromebook of September 2019 is very different from the Chromebook of September 2020. Google is promising more updates “in the coming months” that make the lightweight laptops “better for work and play”. It will be interesting to see what else the search company can squeeze out of ChromeOS.
