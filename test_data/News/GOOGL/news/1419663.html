'INCREDIBLY MOVING'

Novichok attack survivor Charlie Rowley reveals he broke down in tears watching The Salisbury Poisonings

  • Sarah Arnold
  • Published: 21:33, 13 Jun 2020
  • Updated: 21:33, 13 Jun 2020

    SALISBURY poisoning victim Charlie Rowley has revealed he broke down in tears as he watched a new TV drama about the nerve agent attack.

    He was overcome with emotion after seeing the depiction of girlfriend Dawn Sturgess, 45, who died after he unwittingly gave her a perfume bottle containing the deadly chemical Novichok.

    Grieving Charlie, 49, who has ­suffered strokes, meningitis and flashbacks since being infected too, said: “I have watched all three episodes. It was incredibly moving and I had a few tears.

    “It shows the Dawn I loved, a lovely, caring and generous person. For our relationship to end so abruptly in such a way was an unnatural thing.

    “I’ll be watching tomorrow night with my brother as I don’t want to view it alone.”

    The Salisbury Poisonings, which starts this evening on BBC1, stars Johnny Harris as Charlie — and the two men have formed a close bond.

    Charlie, who invited the This Is England ’90 actor to his house to talk about his ordeal, said: “I never expected to find a friend in such terrible circumstances.

    “When he first came round I thought he’s a really decent guy. He was very dedicated. He’s never played a real-life person so he took it upon himself to learn as much about me as he could.

    ‘There’s no justice’

    “We went through the whole thing. He had seen all the interviews and coverage and he even wanted to pick up on my mannerisms. I’ve made a friend. We regularly speak on the phone.”

    Ex-Russian double-agent Sergei Skripal and his daughter Yulia were found slumped and close to death on a park bench in the city after being poisoned in March 2018.

    Alleged Russian agents named as Alexander Petrov and Ruslan Boshirov have been charged with their attempted murder and that of Detective Sergeant Nick Bailey, poisoned after going to the Skripals’ home. The men fled to Russia and are still wanted. DS Bailey has since returned to work. 

    The deadly liquid was believed to have been left on the Skripals’ front door.

    Jobless Charlie chanced upon a bottle of counterfeit Nina Ricci perfume in a charity shop bin around four months later and gave it to his partner Dawn. 

    She died eight days later while Charlie was scrambled to intensive care after Novichok was found in his body.

    Today, in this exclusive interview, Charlie insists for the first time that the bottle he found was SEALED — meaning it could not have been used on the Skripals.

    He said: “It was a boxed perfume and it looked sealed, unbroken.

    “So I took it and unfortunately gave it to Dawn. It’s very unfair what ­happened to her. I carried a lot of guilt for a long time. How did that bottle end up in that place such a long time after the first ­incident?

    “It’s almost like it was put there to be found again. I think it was put there on purpose. The time frame is just too wrong for that to turn up in a sealed box three months after the initial ­poisoning. But as for who or why, that’s the biggest mystery of all.”

    The Skripals survived and are thought to be living under new identities in New Zealand. Charlie suffers debilitating flashbacks. He was put in an induced coma in hospital and during that time suffered a series of strokes, so has been unable to work.

    He said: “I get out of breath very easily. We still don’t know what damage it has done but it’s affected my memory and eyesight. For a year and a half I couldn’t move my left arm properly, and I can’t go too far now.”

    Charlie is pleased that the drama, which stars MyAnna Buring as Dawn, shows his partner in a good light.

    He said: “It will show people she was an honest, decent woman. Some of the stories that came out in the beginning weren’t fair on her, her children and her parents. I’m glad that’s going to be set right.”

    And he hopes the drama will aid his quest for justice.

    In a search for answers, Charlie and his brother Matthew went to the ­Russian Embassy in London to meet then ambassador Alexander Yakovenko. 

    He said “I wanted to know, did a Russian do it? I didn’t get any answers. I got a whole load of stories.”

    Charlie now lives in Warminster, Wilts. He had to abandon all his  belongings from his address in Salisbury in case they were contaminated— and he has now learned his old flat is being knocked down. He said: “I’ve just been told it is going to be demolished and turned into houses. It’s the last tangible thing of what happened. It’s such a sad story.”

    Charlie added: “The person who did this should be punished. There’s still no justice for Dawn and those who loved her.
