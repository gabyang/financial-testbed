Galaxy S20 Defeats Hands-On Data Hackers, Samsung Says

Is it game over for your data if a hacker actually has hold of your phone? If that phone is a Galaxy S20, Samsung says no.

My name is Davey Winder, and I'm a Samsung-holic. There, I've said it. I have owned a Samsung flagship device pretty much ever since shrugging off my iPhone habit a decade ago. If you find it surprising that someone in the cybersecurity business prefers Android to iOS, you are probably not alone. I can already hear the cries of wait a minute didn't you just publish a report about a "perfect 10" critical vulnerability that affects every Samsung Galaxy sold from late 2014 onward? Well, yes, I did. But the kind of zero-click vulnerability concerned, uncovered by the Google Project Zero team, is remarkably similar in exploitability to another that it also found in iOS devices.

Where Apple has the upper hand is in the ability to have full control over the iOS ecosystem so that security updates are rolled out more efficiently and effectively to every device. The Android ecosystem is a fractured one, and when or if you get even a critical update can depend on not only the make and model of device you own but also the phone network to which it is connected.

Cutting to the chase then, there's no such thing as a 100% secure smartphone; even the specially adapted devices used by very high-value individuals cannot truly claim that much. All that anyone can do is make a device that is as secure as possible, and Samsung reckons the Galaxy S20 range is evidence of this in action.

The Galaxy S20 dedicated security chip

As I revealed in an article dated March 1, the Galaxy S20 features an all-new secure element security solution with a dedicated security chip. This brings the concept of standalone and isolated sensitive data storage to Samsung smartphones for the first time. Samsung itself has now revealed more detail about how this secure processor can protect such data on a Galaxy S20 from advanced hardware attacks where the attacker has your smartphone in their possession.

Explaining that when a hacker has your hardware in their hands, then more advanced attacks can be executed against the device to extract data. These include physical attacks that manipulate components, fault attacks that seek to provoke hardware errors than can be exploited, and side-channel attacks that may analyze heat or electromagnetic emissions to breach the security solutions in place.

This hack-attack triumvirate is the reason why you will have heard so many people, me included, repeating the "once they have physical access to your device, it's game over" mantra.

To counter this, and prove us all wrong, Samsung insists that the Galaxy S20's secure processor can defeat such attack methodologies. This is a dedicated security chip in addition to the existing layers of security already provided in Galaxy devices, such as the Knox mobile security platform. Think of it as a dedicated and tamper-resistant strongbox for confidential and cryptographic data.

Protection against hackers with your hardware in their hands

"In addition to the continuous scrambling and encrypting of confidential data, it employs a physical shield to guard against physical attacks." Samsung said, "The component can also detect invalid voltage or temperature changes and is equipped with security algorithms to thwart side-channel attacks."

Starting with the lock screen, Samsung said that it is "almost impossible" (remember, there is no such thing as 100% secure anything) for attackers to reset the error counter to bypass failed unlock attempts using a PIN, pattern or password. But the secure processor does a lot more than that: it processes blockchain private keys and supports something called Strongbox Keymaster to enable hardware protection for those private keys as well as sensitive data on third-party apps.

OK, so this kind of dedicated security chip is old news to Pixel owners, Google has the Titan M there, and Apple has the T2 chip-powered enclave in iPhones. But that matters not, what matters is that this brings proper storage separation for PINs, passwords, crypto-currency credentials and credit card payment tokens. The S3K250AF-based Secure Element security factor is a big move forward for Samsung in terms of the flagship Galaxy range, and one that I welcome. That the May 2020 security update that patches a critical vulnerability has still to reach my Galaxy Note 10+ 5G is far less welcome.
