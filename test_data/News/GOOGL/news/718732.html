Pub regulars reveal disgust after ‘evil’ landlady stole their hard earned money

Victims of Sally Johnstone, who stole more than £70,000 from the Christmas savings fund of the Kings Arms in Cheltenham, have spoken out

    Pub regulars have spoken of their anger towards the woman who stole tens of thousands of pounds that belonged to them.

    One said Sally Johnstone was “evil and devious” for having stolen over £70,000 from more than 50 people who put money into the Kings Arms’ Christmas savings fund.

    Johnstone has admitted taking the money, which was collected at the pub in Gloucester Road, Cheltenham, between 2017 and 2019, when she was its landlady.

    She was bailed until June 9 after pleading guilty at Gloucester Crown Court last week, with Judge Ian Lawrie QC warning her that she faced going to prison.

    Here, we feature some of the people whose lives were badly affected by Johnstone’s crime.

    The family of five who lost nearly £2,500

    Darren Green’s family lost nearly £2,500 due to Sally Johnstone’s dishonesty.

    The 51-year-old, of St George’s Road, Cheltenham, said he and three other people in his household - his wife, son and mother - had been affected.

    They had been saving through the club to go away to Benidorm for a Christmas break.

    So when the family, who live just a few hundred yards from the pub, discovered their money had gone, they were devastated.

    Darren said: “It was absolutely disgusting. We were sickened by someone we thought we could trust. It was absolutely unbelievable, not a nice feeling at all.”

    He said it was bad enough for him and his wife but they could at least earn more money to replace that which they had lost.

    But he said that was not the case for his mother as she was a pensioner.

    And he said it had been tough for his 22-year-old son who lost his contribution, having been badgered by his father to use the club as a way of saving for the holiday.

    Darren said: “It’s put a lot of bad feeling in him and he’s not like that at all.”

    Happily for the family, they still went on the holiday - with Darren saying they didn’t want to lose any more money and so they managed to find enough to go to Spain.

    He said: “We did it but it meant other money for other things had gone.”

    On a positive note, he said the pub’s regulars had stayed friends and he was sure they would continue to socialise there when the coronavirus restrictions allowed.

    “Everyone mixed in after she (Johnstone) had gone. If any good has come out of it, it’s that people are closer knit now.

    “Everyone speaks in there. We’re all one big family.

    “There aren’t many community pubs left but that’s certainly one.”

    The mum who couldn’t take her disabled daughter on holiday

    Anne Waring did not mince her words when saying what she thought of the woman who robbed her of the chance to take her disabled daughter on holiday.

    The 81-year-old, of Rowanfield, Cheltenham, lost £780 that she planned to use to take her physically and mentally disabled daughter Sandra for a holiday in Northumberland, where her sister lives.

    The pensioner said losing the money, which accumulated by her putting £20 a week into the fund, had been a big blow.

    “Sandra doesn’t understand why we haven’t been able to go on holiday. She doesn't understand at all about money,” she said.

    Regarding Johnstone, she said: “She was so two-faced. She was always very friendly but she was sitting there and taking our money when she was being friendly.

    “She would always ask if I would be in on Friday to pay my money in - and all the time she knew she wasn’t putting the money in.

    “It’s not as though she didn’t know her customers, she was local to the pub. She was there for three Christmases, at least.”

    Mrs Waring, whose husband Alan died seven years ago and who has been visiting the pub for five or six years, added: “Evil and devious. That’s what she was.

    “At first, nobody believed she had done it. It was hard to believe that she did it.

    “I do feel very let down by her.”

    She hopes to take her 55-year-old daughter, who lives in a residential home in Down Hatherley, away this Christmas. Saving some money by going out less during the lockdown had helped her financially, she said.

    “Fingers were pointed at me” - the man who collected the money at the pub

    Mark Finch collected the money from savers at the club and said it was a very awkward time for him when he discovered Johnstone had not banked it as she was supposed to.

    The 54-year-old, who lives in Springbank, said he kept asking Johnstone for the balance of the account.

    He said that when she wouldn’t tell him, he went to the bank himself and discovered there was £11 in the account when there should have been nearly £40,000.

    He said: “I was pretty upset as you can imagine. Everyone put their trust in me as much as her. We were all screwed over by her.

    “I had a couple of fingers pointed at me at one stage.”

    He said the spirit among the pub’s regulars was good, despite what had happened.

    He added: “She (Johnstone) was a friend to all of us. We’re a very local pub.

    “We all know each other. We all get on.

    “It’s unbelievable what happened but the pub is recovering. We’re still friends.”

    The widower who was saving to go to Australia to see his daughter

    Bill Hutchinson was saving through the pub’s fund to generate spending money for a trip to see his daughter Nichola in Australia.

    The 81-year-old, who lives in Cheltenham, said he lost about £480 when Johnstone stole the Christmas club money.

    The trip in December last year ended up being cancelled, although not because of the money he lost.

    His daughter is now due to visit him, as he is recovering after a bout of COVID-19 that put him in hospital for several days and nearly killed him.

    Mr Hutchinson, whose wife Marlene died 13 years ago, said Johnstone should go to prison.

    “I think she should go down for a long while. Some people put a lot more money in the kitty than I did.

    “It was not nice. It’s a pub I go down to most Friday nights for the company and there’s good people down there,” he said.
