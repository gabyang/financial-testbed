London Underground: 7 Essex towns that should be on the Tube map

The huge transport network continues to expand every year

    Is there anything that screams "London" more than the capital's iconic Underground system? Well, maybe the red buses.

    The ever-expanding transport network has been a vital part of the city for more than 100 years and you can spot its style a mile off.

    Not only is it a vital means of public transport, it plays an integral part in transforming places into fully-fledged towns.

    A direct link to the capital makes a location instantly desirable for commuters - and, given the coronavirus pandemic, plenty of people are looking to move further away from central London.

    You might be under the impression the Tube map doesn't change very often and that there's no chance places in Essex will be on there anytime soon.

    But, it wasn't that long ago that Shenfield or Brentwood weren't on the map - and look at them now.

    It seems inevitable the links to the capital are going to expand further and further - and that's great news for our country.

    We've highlighted the seven most likely towns and villages that will appear on the London Underground in the near future - let us know what you make of our suggestions in the comments section here.

    Chipping Ongar

    This one is cheating a little bit, as Chipping Ongar was once on the Tube map. Ongar was on the Central line between 1949 and 1994 - when the station closed.

    It's still the end of the physical train track line for the central line, and steam trains still offer a heritage line between Epping and Ongar - as Epping is now the end of the Central line.

    The station was originally shut after services to the line were scaled back throughout the 1980s and 1990s as a result of cost-cutting measures.

    But as London continues to expand, it's likely more housing will be built in what is currently countryside near Chipping Ongar. The station is well equipped to re-open, as it's still connected to the active Central line.

    North Weald

    Like Ongar, North Weald also used to be an active London Underground station. Similarly, it was operational on the Central line between 1949 and 1994. It closed for the same reason Ongar did - to save money.

    It's also a stop on the Epping to Ongar Heritage Railway, so still sees regular travel. If Ongar is made a Tube station again, it would make sense to reopen North Weald too, to serve those living in North Weald Bassett and Thornwood.

    Both stations would also provide an easier way for Londoners to reach the Secret Nuclear Bunker at Kelvedon Hatch - meaning the attraction would likely benefit from this travel route.

    Ingatestone

    When the Crossrail project began - now called the Elizabeth Line - it was clear that there would be some new places on the Tube line.

    Since initial services were running on the line - appearing on the map as TfL Rail - both Shenfield and Brentwood appeared on the Tube map for the first time.

    Although the coronavirus crisis has delayed work on the project, soon both Brentwood and Shenfield will be fully-fledged stations on the map, with regular services directly to central London and beyond.

    Once the Elizabeth Line has been running for while, it's not unlikely that TfL will look to expand it. And in Essex, the logical next step is Ingatestone.

    It's the next station on the Brentwood/Shenfield line, and has been called a great place for London commuters by the Sunday Times who named it among their best places to live in the country earlier this year.

    So it wouldn't be a stretch to include Ingatestone as a new stop on the Elizabeth Line.

    South Ockendon

    Heading to south Essex, the District line already extends into the county. Places like Upminster, Barking and Dagenham are all served by the line - places generally considered to have an Essex identity.

    The next logical step would be to extend the District line. Currently, Barking and Upminster are both served by the c2c Rail line from Shoeburyness to London Fenchurch Street.

    The next stop - splitting off toward Tilbury - is Ockendon, in Thurrock, Essex. This station serves South Ockendon - which has a larger population than both Upminster and Epping - places which both have tube stations.

    To turn the c2c Rail station into a Tube station could be the next step in renovating this area - and would also make South Ockendon a more suitable commuter town.

    Rainham

    Staying on the c2c Rail line - Rainham would be a logical area to appear on the map.

    Although sometimes confused for the similarly named town in Kent, Rainham is a town in the London borough of Havering, nearer to Dagenham than South Ockendon. Although the town is situated in Greater London, it is also a short distance to neighbouring Purfleet and Aveley, in the Essex borough of Thurrock.

    This section of the c2c line runs from West Ham to Dagenham Dock and then to Rainham.

    As both of the areas served by the previous stations on this line are served by the London Underground - it would make sense for Rainham to become another station.

    If both Rainham and South Ockendon become new stations, then the District line would require a new split, creating two new terminuses in Essex.

    This is not unheard of on the Underground, as the Northern Line finishes at both High Barnet and Mill Hill East.

    Purfleet

    Staying in Thurrock, Purfleet-on-Thames is set for a huge regeneration over the next decade.

    Purfleet is the next station following on from Rainham, and recently they've announced more details about the huge project underway to regenerate the area.

    A million-square-foot media village - complete with TV studios - will be built on the banks of the Thames, as well as a plethora of new homes and potentially a new train station.

    With Purfleet-on-Thames expanding to become a creative hub, it would make sense for it to have good connections to London.

    With train improvements already planned, it would make a lot of sense to expand Tube services on the District line to Purfleet-on-Thames as well.

    Harlow

    Unlike other towns mentioned, Harlow wouldn't be on the London Underground map, but on the London Overground map. Currently, services end at Cheshunt.

    But if needs must, it wouldn't be a stretch to connect Harlow through Hoddesdon, in Hertfordshire. Harlow is a densely populated town where many people already travel into the city by train.

    It would make sense to expand the underground to serve those in Harlow - in a similar way that the Metropolitan line serves Watford in Hertfordshire.

    Harlow could also be used as a stop on the way to Stansted Airport, if TfL wants to link the airport in the same way they have Heathrow Airport.
