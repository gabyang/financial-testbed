Johnson & Johnson Ends Bladder Cancer Study But Still Backs TAR-200

Published: 2024-10-08 09:51:17 ET JNJ

By Colin Kellaher

Johnson & Johnson is ending one of several studies of a proposed bladder cancer therapy, but the pharmaceutical giant said it remains confident in the drug's prospects.

J&J said it stopped a Phase 3 study of TAR-200 in patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer who aren't receiving radical cystectomy after the therapy didn't show superiority versus chemoradiation at prespecified interim analysis.

The New Brunswick, N.J., company is studying the safety and efficacy of TAR-200 in several Phase 2 and Phase 3 studies in patients with muscle-invasive and non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer.

J&J last month recently reported favorable results from a Phase 2 study of TAR-200 plus cetrelimab in patients with muscle-invasive bladder cancer who are ineligible or refuse neoadjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy and scheduled for radical cystectomy.

TAR-200 is part of the Taris platform that J&J acquired when it bought biotechnology company Taris Biomedical in late 2019.

The company said it remains confident in the platform having non-risk-adjusted peak year operational sales potential topping $5 billion.
