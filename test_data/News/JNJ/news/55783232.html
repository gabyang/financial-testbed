Coronavirus updates from the Bay Area: June 10-15, 2021

ByChronicle StaffUpdated June 27, 2021 9:55 p.m.

The Chronicle began covering the coronavirus crisis before the first cases were reported in the Bay Area and a pandemic was declared in 2020. We reorganized the newsroom to dedicate nearly every resource to stories focused on the health and economic disasters. Each day, into the second year of the pandemic, we have published live updates to reflect the most critical local, national and global updates on COVID-19. This news is free of charge to help keep our community safe and informed.

Updates from Tuesday, June 15:

Arizona governor bans vaccine requirements, masks for students: Gov. Doug Ducey signed an executive order Tuesday stating that students at Arizona’s public universities and community colleges cannot be mandated to receive a COVID-19 vaccine, forced to show proof of inoculation or wear masks “in order to participate in learning.” They also cannot be required to be tested for the coronavirus. “The vaccine works, and we encourage Arizonans to take it,” Ducey said in a statement. “But it is a choice and we need to keep it that way.” He added, “Public education is a public right, and taxpayers are paying for it. We need to make our public universities available for students to return to learning.”

Two provinces are blocking cellphone service for unvaccinated residents, and some government employees who haven’t gotten a shot are seeing their pay suspended, The New York Times reported.

American Express will let employees work from home two days a week: American Express will allow most employees to work from home for up to two days a week permanently, Reuters reports. Most of the U.S. and U.K. staff of the credit card issuer will have the choice to work remotely on Mondays and Fridays starting October, AmEx Chief Executive Officer Stephen Squeri said in an internal memo. AmEx will begin bringing back employees to the office starting Sept. 13, with an aim to fully adopt the hybrid model in the week of Oct. 4.

CDC labels delta a ‘variant of concern’:The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has upgraded the delta coronavirus variant (B.1.617.2, first discovered in India) as a “variant of concern.” That means “there is evidence of an increase in transmissibility, more severe disease, significant reduction in neutralization by antibodies generated during previous infection or vaccination, reduced effectiveness of treatments or vaccines, or diagnostic detection failures.” Experts believe the variant caused the devastating surge in coronavirus infections in India over recent months. United Kingdom Health Secretary Matt Hancock recently said the variant accounts for 91% of new cases seen in the U.K. and is replacing the alpha (B.1.1.7) variant as the dominant version there. It has now been reported in at least 60 countries including the U.S. The CDC previously considered the Delta variant a “variant of interest.”

U.S. death toll tops 600,000: COVID-19 elevated its deadly toll Tuesday — to more than 600,000 American lives claimed since the start of the pandemic — men, women and children of all ages and races — according to tracking by Johns Hopkins University. Even as vaccinations have sent infections plunging across the country, new coronavirus strains are emerging and many Americans are refusing vaccination. Globally, more than 3.8 million have died from COVID-19 related causes.

More evidence suggests COVID-19 was in U.S. by Christmas 2019: A new analysis of blood samples from 24,000 Americans taken early last year is the latest and largest study to suggest that the new coronavirus popped up in the U.S. in December 2019 — weeks before cases were first recognized by health officials, the Associated Press reports. Federal health officials are increasingly accepting a timeline in which small numbers of COVID-19 infections may have occurred in the U.S. before the world ever became aware of a dangerous new virus erupting in China. “The studies are pretty consistent,” said Natalie Thornburg of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. “There was probably very rare and sporadic cases here earlier than we were aware of. But it was not widespread and didn’t become widespread until late February.”

Nearly 900 got expired doses at Times Square site:Nearly 900 people received expired COVID-19 vaccine doses at a vaccination site in Times Square this month, the Associated Press reports. The 899 individuals who received doses of the Pfizer vaccine at the former NFL Experience building in Times Square between June 5 and June 10 should schedule another Pfizer shot as soon as possible, the New York City Health Department said. ATC Vaccination Services, the company that administered the shots under contract to the city, said in a statement, “We apologize for the inconvenience to those receiving the vaccine batch in question and want people first and foremost to know that we have been advised that there is no danger from the vaccine they received.”

That means, a county satement said, “no more physical distancing, no more capacity limits on businesses, no more county tiers, and relaxed mask guidance.” Workplaces will have to follow Cal/OSHA standards, including on masking, the county said. 2:20 p.m. Delta variant more potent than Alpha strain: People infected with COVID-19’s Delta variant, first detected in India, have double the risk of ending upin a hospital compared to the Alpha variant that emerged in England, a large study in Scotland, where Delta is dominant, has found. Researchers said Monday that Pfizer and AstraZeneca vaccines protect against Delta, with Pfizer’s product offering better protection The findings were published in a research letter in the Lancet.

1:52 p.m. Marin County has no COVID patients in hospitals: For the first time in more than a year, on the eve of California’s reopening, Marin County hospitals have zero COVID-19 patients requiring hospitalization, county officials announced Monday. The last time Marin did not have a single patient hospitalized for COVID-19 was May 3, 2020, just prior to a major surge of cases statewide. Citing the county’s high rate of vaccinations, the county health officer, Dr. Matt Willis, said “The pattern couldn’t be clearer that vaccinations protect us.”

1:30 p.m. England’s “freedom day” postponed:Prime Minister Boris Johnson on Monday postponed by four weeks the nation’s so-called“freedom day” of lifting restrictions after a spike in cases of the highly transmissable Delta variant that was first identified in Inda. Restaurants and pubs in England, while open, will still have to observe social distancing rules indoors, limiting capacity, and nightclubs and theaters will remain firmly closed.

The restrictions go beyond California health department guidelines, which as of Tuesday call for masking only on public transit, in K-12 schools, child care and health care settings and long-term care facilities, correctional facilities, and homeless, emergency and cooling shelters.

Visitors ages 2 and up who are not fully vaccinated will still be required to wear a face covering, except when dining. The number was still shy of the 2.6 million travelers logged by TSA on the same day in 2019, before the pandemic hit. Last year, with the pandemic curtailing air travel, just over 544,000 passed through the airports.

But S.F. is the norm for major U.S. metropolitan areas, with people migrating out of city centers into less crowded suburban surroundings. 9:39 a.m. U.K. set to delay lifting of COVID restrictions: Spread of a new coronavirus variant had British Prime Minister Boris Johnson set to announce Monday a delay of up to four weeks on the lifting of COVID-19 restrictions, the New York Times reports. Johnson was expected to cite rising cases of the Delta variant, first detected in India, as a roadblock to removing the remaining curbs on June 21 as planned.

9:22 a.m. Some 350,000 shots later, mass vax site closing down: Santa Clara County’s mass vaccination site at Levi’s Stadium will shutter on June 24 in the face of declining demand and after administering more than 350,000 shots against COVID-19, county officials announced Monday. “The move comes amidst an optimistic COVID-19 outlook in the county with cases slowing to less than 2 per day for every 100,000 people,’ and nearly 80% of county residents over 12 having received at least one vaccine dose, a county statement said. “Levi’s Stadium is among the highest capacity vaccination sites in California and vaccinated almost 12,000 people in a single day at its peak capacity in mid-April.”

9:13 a.m. Latest pandemic victim is Girl Scout cookies:The Girl Scouts have 15 million boxes of unsold cookies this year thanks to the coronavirus pandemic that impeded their usual in-person sales. The Girl Scouts normally sell around 200 million boxes of cookies per year, or around $800 million worth. The Scouts came up short despite innovative selling methods like drive-thru booths and contact-free delivery, the Associated Press reports.

7:09 a.m. Novavax COVID vaccine posts 90% effectiveness in trial: The U.S. company Novavax said Monday that its COVID-19 vaccine was90% effective at preventing infections, and 100% effective in protecting against moderate or severe symptoms. The efficacy rates are similar to those reported by Pfizer and Moderna, and higher than that of Johson & Johnson’s COVID vaccine. The Novavax results follow a trial involving just under 30,000 participants and could presage a fourth U.S. vaccine.

6:55 p.m. Republicans still lead strongly in unvaxxed category: Nearly 30% of Republicans say they are not planning to be vaccinated against COVID-19, compared to 5% of Democrats, according to a CBS News-YouGov poll released Sunday. The June 8-10 poll using a nationally representative sample of Americans found 52% of Republicans are at least partially vaccinated, compared to 77% of Democrats. Another 6% of Republicans plan to get the shots, and 13% are undecided, with 29% saying they won’t get vaccinated. Among Democrats, 18% said either that they plan to get shots or are undecided. Nearly 80% of Democrats said employers should mandate vaccination, compared with 39 % of Republicans.

5:30 p.m. S.F. expert warns vax resisters that variant “should ring the alarm and spur action”:The so-called delta coronavirus variant,a more infectious strain that first was identified in India and now dominates in the U.K., poses the prospect of a scary fall resurgence of COVID, mainly among unvaccinated Americans,Dr. Bob Wachter, UCSF chair of medicine, said Sunday. “I’ll now bet we’ll see significant (incl. many hospitalizations/deaths) surges this fall in low-vaccine populations due to combo of seasonality, delta’s nastiness, & ‘back to normal’ behavior,” he said in aseries of tweetsexplaining the Delta variant’s transmissability andits greater strength against vaccines.“We’ve been lulled by the amazing efficacy of the vaccines & by fact that prior variants haven’t been all that nasty ... Delta should ring the alarm & spur action.” He called for authorities to step up research on booster shots and more quickly approve vaccines for children. “For me, Delta adds to my resolve to keep mask on indoors when unvaxxed/unmasked people may be around,” he also said.“If you’re unvaxxed, get your shots! You may be reassured by a low a local case rate, but don’t be: it may be due to summer,” and the delta strain’s low prevalance so far in the U.S. “Both will change,” he warned. The campus this year was the region’s only one to hold a fully live graduation ceremony with all the undergraduates seated in one place at one time. Because of the pandemic, in 2020 all graduations in the region were virtual, including Stanford’s. This year brought an assortment of virtual and hybrid commencements. 3:13 p.m. Haiti has yet to administer 1 dose of vaccine: Haiti was spared the worst of the pandemic last year, but the coronavirus has returned to the hemisphere’s poorest nation with a fury. The New York Times reports the average number of new daily cases has more than doubled in the past two weeks to 153, fairly low for a population of about 11.5 million, but a presumed undercount due to limited testing and unrreported cases. Haiti’s government has yet to administer a single dose of any vaccine.

2:02 p.m. Judge tosses out employees’ suit over hospital vax requirement: A federal judge threw out a lawsuit filed by employees of a Houston hospital system over its requirement that all staff be vaccinated against COVID-19, the Associated Press reported. The Houston Methodist Hospital system suspended 178 employees without pay last week because they refused to get vaccinated. Some of them sued seeking to overturn the requirement. In a scathing ruling Saturday, U.S. District Judge Lynn Hughes of Houston deemed the lawsuit’s contention that the vaccines are “experimental and dangerous” to be false and otherwise irrelevant.

1:54 p.m. Kindergarten prepares for a surge:School districts across the United States are hiring additional teachers in anticipation of what will be one of the largest kindergarten classes ever as enrollment rebounds following the coronavirus pandemic, the Associated Press reports. Educators are also bracing for many students to be less prepared than usual due to lower preschool attendance rates. Research has found that the number of 4-year-olds enrolled in preschool — which in most states is not required — fell from 71% before the pandemic to 54% during the pandemic, with poor children much less likely to attend in person.

1:43 p.m. States where infections are not dropping also have low vax rate: With new COVID-19 cases declining across most of the country, almost all states bucking that trend have lower-than-average vaccination rates. Experts warn that relief from the pandemic could be fleeting in regions where few people get inoculated, the Associated Press reports. Case totals nationally have declined in a week from a seven-day average of nearly 21,000 on May 29 to 14,315 on Saturday, according to data from Johns Hopkins University. Some states are seeing increased immunity because of high rates of natural spread of the disease.

10 a.m. Positive test rate hits all-time low in U.S.:The share of coronavirus tests in the U.S. coming back positive has declined to its lowest point since the pandemic began, according to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention data. An average of more than 677,000 COVID-19 tests a day were processed during the week ended June 3, down from the peak of more than 2 million during the fall and winter, the Wall Street Journal reported. Of those, about 2% were positive, compared with 13% during the winter surge. The positive test rate is now at its lowest since March 2020, when Johns Hopkins University began compiling coronavirus data. The positive rate was highest early in the pandemic, when testing resources were limited and the focus was on people exhibiting symptoms.

8:15 a.m. Zuckerberg gives work-from-home option to more employees: Facebook CEO Mark Zuckerberg plans to spend at least half of next year working from home, and he is also giving more Facebook employees the option of working remotely. “I’ve found that working remotely has given me more space for long-term thinking and helped me spend more time with my family, which has made me happier and more productive at work,” the social media company’s co-founder wrote Wednesday in a memo obtained by multiple media outlets. Facebook told employees in a separate memo that “anyone whose role can be done remotely can request remote work.” Zuckerberg, 37, plans to avoid the office for at least six months in 2022. He said he instead plans time at his estate in Hawaii, where, according to a pair of videos posted to his Facebook page, he has been practicing his weaponry skills. Read the full story.

8 a.m. G-7 leaders promise 1 billion vaccine doses for world: British Prime Minister Boris Johnson on Sunday said the Group of Seven wealthy nations will pledge over 1 billion coronavirus vaccine doses for poorer nations. Speaking as a G-7 leaders’ summit concluded in southwest England, Johnson said the doses would be provided both directly and through the international COVAX program, the Associated Press reported. The World Health Organization has said 11 billion doses are needed to vaccinate at least 70% of the world’s population and end the pandemic.

7:55 a.m. Reopening dangers lie ahead for California:As California prepares to fully reopen Tuesday, it’s hard to imagine need for a renewed statewide stay-at-home order, experts say. Generally high vaccination rates, combined with the roughly 20% of people who have immunity from previous coronavirus infection, mean most parts of the state have enough immune protection to prevent another surge. But there are still obstacles ahead, including some that could set back the state’s recovery and prompt renewed local restrictions or targeted closures. California will be vulnerable to outbreaks in unvaccinated communities, such as recent clusters reported in Marin County. Public health officials also will be watching for cases of serious COVID-19 illness among young children who can’t be vaccinated.
