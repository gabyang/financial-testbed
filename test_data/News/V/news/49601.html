Priti Patel ditches polo industry visa deal: Home Secretary axes arrangement that allowed 500 polo players into Britain despite fears it was being exploited by illegal immigrants

  • Last week temporary special allowance of visas for polo industry was revealed
  • The Queen feared the 2017 polo season would be ruined by the clampdown
  • But Ms Patel has now called time on the arrangement and will bring it to a close

By HARRY COLE FOR THE MAIL ON SUNDAY

Home Secretary Priti Patel (pictured) is to end a controversial arrangement that allows 500 polo players and their entourages to compete in Britain every year

Home Secretary Priti Patel is to end a controversial arrangement that allows 500 polo players and their entourages to compete in Britain every year, The Mail on Sunday has learnt.

Last week this newspaper revealed the temporary special allowance of visas for the polo industry had been set up in 2017 in the wake of Buckingham Palace's considerable interest in the matter.

The previous Government had tried to restrict the number of polo players and expert horse grooms coming to the UK amid concerns the system was being exploited by low-skilled migrants.

But the move was watered down amid a backlash from the industry and because Ministers believed the Queen feared the 2017 polo season would be ruined by the clampdown.

One source said Her Majesty became 'very animated' over the plans, while a former official said Ministers went 'above and beyond' normal practice to address the Palace's concerns.

The 'temporary' measure was then rolled over for three years.

But Ms Patel has now called time on the arrangement and will bring it to a close on April 1, risking a fresh row with the Palace.

Players and expert horse grooms will be able to apply for the special visas up until that point, meaning this year's polo season is unlikely to be affected. But the 2021 season, from March to October, will not be able to rely on foreign workers.

Home Office insiders hope the measure will encourage British clubs to train more domestic horse grooms and stable staff, with visas restricted to top-flight players amid concerns they were being used as a backdoor into Britain.

The country's 200 polo clubs rely on an influx of seasonal workers, mostly from Argentina and Australia, but immigration officials claimed many of the more than 800 foreigners who entered the country on polo visas – each of whom was sponsored by a club – ended up working as farm labourers, rather than in the teams' stables.

The Queen and The Duke of Edinburgh during the polo at the Guards Polo Club, in Windsor Great Park, Egham, Surrey

The Home Office says an announcement will be made in due course, but one insider said that 'time was up' for the special visas.

Sources in the polo industry confirmed they had received guidance from the Home Office that the visa arrangements were coming to an end.

One revealed a letter said the measure had been temporary and was designed to allow the sport time to recruit and train enough British players and grooms.
