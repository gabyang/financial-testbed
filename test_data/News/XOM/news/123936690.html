Canadian Court Quashes Federal Government's Single-Use Plastics Ban

Published: 2023-11-16 19:17:02 ET XOM

By Paul Vieira

OTTAWA--The Federal Court of Canada ruled Thursday that the Liberal government's ban on some single-use plastic products was unconstitutional and unreasonable.

Canada listed some single-use plastics--like straws, stir sticks, bags and takeout containers--as toxic in May, 2022. The government said this was part of its plan to meet its target of zero plastic waste by 2030, and reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Canadian Prime Minister Justin Trudeau championed the initiative at a campaign stop during his successful 2019 re-election bid.

Companies such as chemical maker Dow and Exxon subsidiary Imperial Oil, lobby groups like the American Chemistry Council, and two Canadian provinces challenged the ban, arguing in part that the government lacked meaningful evidence that plastics are toxic. In a judgment, Justice Angela Furlanetto said the prohibition was "unreasonable and unconstitutional," adding officials "could not have been satisfied" from evidence it had available that all plastic is toxic.

"Even if the statement that all plastic-manufactured items have the potential to become plastic pollution is taken on its face, the evidence available to [cabinet] did not support the finding that all [plastic items] are toxic," she wrote. Justice Furlanetto added the cabinet "acted outside their authority" in listing a broad category of single-use plastic as toxic "in an unqualified matter."

A spokeswoman for Canadian Environment Minister Steven Guilbeault said officials are reviewing the decision and "are strongly considering an appeal." The judgment orders the ban be retroactively quashed and declared unlawful, as of April 23, 2021.
