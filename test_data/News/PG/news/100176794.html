'Incredible beat' in Disney's park business wows Wall Street analysts

Published Thu, Feb 10 20228:38 AM ESTUpdated Thu, Feb 10 20229:33 AM EST
Hannah Miao@hannahmiao_
WATCH LIVE
Analysts across Wall Street sang the praises of Disney after a quarterly print showing a stunning recovery in its parks business. "When we reflect about the massive surprise that Disney delivered in F1Q 2022, we are primarily focused on the incredible beat in Parks profits that came from the most amazing set of drivers that we have ever seen," MoffettNathanson's Michael Nathanson said in a note Thursday. The media and entertainment giant beat expectations on the top and bottom lines in its fiscal first-quarter report Wednesday after the bell. However, what really stole the show was Disney's parks, experiences and consumer-products division. Disney's PECP segment notched $7.2 billion in revenue during the quarter, double the $3.6 billion it generated in the prior-year quarter. The division saw operating results jump to $2.5 billion compared with a loss of $100 million in the same period last year. From the September quarter to the December quarter, domestic park revenues rose $1.33 billion while domestic park profits added $1.31 billion — nearly identical. "In other words, in a period of rising inflation, the domestic park business added zero incremental costs as revenues surged," Nathanson said. Even more striking is that Disney's blowout parks results came as the Covid pandemic continues to weigh on attendance. "Disney is achieving this success despite operating below capacity and without the benefit of international tourism, thanks to over 40% growth in per capita spending relative to 2019," Benjamin Swinburne of Morgan Stanley said in a Thursday note. The rollout of tickets upgrades Genie+ and Lightning Lane helped boost average per capita ticket revenue, Disney reported. Customers also spent more on hotel accommodations, food and beverage and merchandise, the company said. "Per caps are very strong and DIS says its guest spend on room rates, F & B (food and beverage) and merch, rather than ticket pricing, are driving up revenues," Wells Fargo's Steven Cahall said in a note Thursday. Bullish analysts see even more upside ahead as tourism returns. "We maintain that Disney should see Parks margins exceed pre-pandemic levels in F23 as Disney continues to enhance the guest experience with new attractions and improved operations," JPMorgan's Philip Cusick said in a Thursday note. Take a look at what every major Wall Street analyst said about Disney's earnings report. JPMorgan — Overweight rating, $200 price target "Even more notable were strong Parks results, which we had expected to be solid after Comcast/Universal results in January, but they were even better on very high margins due to improving yields from dynamic pricing on admissions, as well as significantly higher per caps, and the popularity of new products ... We maintain that Disney should see Parks margins exceed pre-pandemic levels in F23 as Disney continues to enhance the guest experience with new attractions and improved operations." Goldman Sachs — Buy rating, $205 price target "The material beats to Parks revenue and operating income supports our outlook for structurally improved economics post-pandemic. This is because DIS's record results in this segment were achieved despite capacity constraints in the US and continued COVID-related pressures in International. We have increased our near- and longer-term forecasts for Parks revenue and OI, as we expect that DIS will sustain per caps above pre-Covid levels driving higher post-pandemic margins." Bank of America — Buy rating, $191 price target "A big premise of our bull thesis was predicated on the recovery in Theme Parks and inherent operating leverage in the model and FY1Q results is a key validator of that view. Near-term catalysts include: (1) continued theme park improvement/recovery and (2) DTC rollouts in new geographies along with increased content output in 2HFY22 and potential price increases." Morgan Stanley — Overweight rating, $170 price target "F1Q results were broadly ahead of our above consensus expectations, with Parks revenue and OI the clear standout while Disney Plus net adds also outperformed. Following F1Q results and new disclosure on streaming, we reiterate our OW ... The Parks performance highlighted strong consumer demand for Disney's experiential offerings and the potential operating leverage in that business." Wells Fargo — Overweight rating, $196 price target "The Parks story is now back with domestic parks OI of $1,555mm in F1Q22 vs $1,572mm in F1Q20. Domestic Parks revenue was just -3% below the comparable pre-pandemic quarter, yet we think attendance is still ~25% lower, occupied room nights were ~20% lower and cruise revs were likely small. Per caps are very strong and DIS says its guest spend on room rates, F & B and merch, rather than ticket pricing, are driving up revenues." Citi — Buy rating, $210 price target "Disney reported F1Q22 revenues +4% ahead of the Street, and a 63% beat at the segment operating income level. Both DMED and DPEP outperformed, with Domestic Parks notably delivering a record quarter. Disney+ subs ended F1Q22 at 130 million, reflecting 12 million net adds in the quarter. This was significantly ahead of consensus at 7 million. We expect the shares to trade materially higher on the back of this print." UBS — Buy rating, $205 price target "Parks meanwhile had its 2nd best quarter for profits in history, driven by record financials at domestic parks. We expect further upside as international parks get back up to speed and recent initiatives improve the LT cost structure/sustain higher per cap spend." Barclays — Neutral rating, $168 price target "Strong performance at parks and streaming were the highlights of the quarter. However, investor concerns around streaming may not abate outside of the near-term tactical relief around the quarter." Credit Suisse — Outperform rating, $218 price target "Parks results impressed, well ahead of our above-Street estimates ... Management's efforts to improve monetization and margins were clear, and suggest a path for the Parks to earn substantially more than pre-pandemic levels, as attendance continues to rebound and new high margin assets like three new cruise ships are added. We continue to see the Parks alone worth over $100/share." Atlantic Equities — Neutral rating, $167 price target "Parks performance driven by double-digit QoQ domestic attendance growth (although still below pre-pandemic levels) with domestic per capita spending up 40%+ YoY. Spending was driven by on site spend and hence management feels it is not pricing out potential visitors. Furthermore, international visitation has yet to return while capacity has been artificially constrained. This all implies long-term upside in this business." BMO Capital Markets — Market perform rating, $175 price target "DIS stabilized the short-term narrative by outperforming on its most important metrics, especially Disney+ subs in the face of increased investor skepticism of the streaming model of late. Parks achieved its second largest revenue and operating income quarter in history benefiting from strong attendance and recent investments. Notwithstanding the after-hours bounce, we think shares are likely to remain range-bound as investors continue to seek a clearer path to how DIS/ESPN's live sports business will shift to streaming." Deutsche Bank — Buy rating, $191 price target "The domestic Theme Parks are emerging from the pandemic stronger than ever. Results in Disney's DPEP segment significantly exceeded our forecast for revenue and OI, driven by strong domestic growth in per cap spending combined with the continued progress toward attendance recovery." Guggenheim — Neutral rating, $172 price target "Disney rebounded from a disappointing F4Q by beating 1Q where it mattered: streaming subscribers, parks trends and consolidated profitability. ... Per-capita park spending growth of 40%, combined with only modest cost increases drove parks operating margin to 33.9%, compared to a 33.3% pre-Covid high." Needham — Hold rating "Parks results were outstanding in 1Q22, with revs doubling YoY and operating income margins of 34%, the highest we can remember (and DIS believes there is more upside from here)." RBC Capital Markets — Outperform rating, $210 price target "Domestic parks posted record financials despite the omicron surge and attendance remaining below 2019. Results reflected a meaningful increase in per cap spend (up over 40% vs. 1Q-FY19) due to a more favorable guest and ticket mix from strategic managing of attendance, higher food/beverage/merch spend, and contributions from the recently launched Genie+ and Lightning Lane, with guest adoption of these services tracking well ahead of management's expectations." MoffettNathanson — Neutral rating, $165 price target "When we reflect about the massive surprise that Disney delivered in F1Q 2022, we are primarily focused on the incredible beat in Parks profits that came from the most amazing set of drivers that we have ever seen. Consider this: from the September quarter (F4Q21) to the December quarter (F1Q22), domestic park revenues increased by +$1.33 billion while domestic park profits increased by a nearly identical +$1.31 billion. In other words, in a period of rising inflation, the domestic park business added zero incremental costs as revenues surged. As the company noted in its 10-Q, the recovery was driven by a stunning rebound in both volume and price." Evercore ISI — Outperform rating, $190 price target "Parks showed significant strength – with SOI above pre-Covid levels, while capacity is still below pre-Covid and international attendance hasn't returned. Underlying trends continue to remain healthy as we look to the rest of FY22, we think the parks momentum should continue, while the streaming side of the business should have a positive catalyst path." —CNBC's Michael Bloom contributed reporting.
