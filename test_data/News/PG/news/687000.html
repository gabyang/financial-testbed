Trump’s Newest Billionaire Donors: A Governor, A GOP Megadonor And An Alleged #MeToo Perpetrator

Michela Tindera
Former Staff
May 7, 2020,12:10pm EDT
Updated Sep 21, 2020, 08:20am EDT
This article is more than 4 years old.
  • Share to Facebook
  • Share to Twitter
  • Share to Linkedin

Donald Trump’s presidential campaign received contributions from 12 new donors who are billionaires, or married to one, in January, February and March, according to the latest Federal Election Commission filings. The group includes several notable names.

First, there’s West Virginia GovernorJim Justice, a former Democrat whorecently told Forbesthat the president’s coronavirus critics “ought to have their ass kicked.”

Then there’s Lori Milken, wife ofMichael Milken, who pleaded guilty to securities fraud in 1990. Trump pardoned him in February.

Las Vegas mogulSteve Wynnalso donated. He served as the finance chair of the Republican National Committee until 2018, when he stepped down amid sexual misconduct allegations, which he has denied. He also later stepped down from his position as CEO and chairman of Wynn Resorts.

Fellow casino kingSheldon Adelsonpitched in, along with his wife Miriam, who Trump awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in November 2018. That move prompted former government ethics chief Walter Shaub to declare on Twitter, “Every bit of government is for sale in the Trump administration. Every last bit.”

Here are Trump’s 12 newest billionaire donors, ordered by amount given. To see a list of Trump’s earlier donorsgohere.

Sheldon & Miriam Adelson

Net worth:$31.2 billion *

Source of wealth:Casinos

Contributions:$1.16 million **

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?Yes

The GOP megadonors were not early Trump backers in the 2016 election, but they eventually got behind the Republican nominee, donating $492,600 in August 2016. They alsogave $5 millionto Trump’s 2017 inaugural committee, said to be the largest donation to an inaugural committee in history. Sheldon Adelson, a staunch supporter of pro-Israel causes, was recently photographed at the White House for a press conference with Israel Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in January.

Ira & Ingeborg Rennert

Net worth:$3 billion

Source of wealth:Investments

Contributions:$900,000

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

On March 12 Rennert and his wife each gave $450,000 to Trump Victory, which appears to be the largest individual contributions that the couple has ever made in their names to a federal political campaign or committee, according to FEC records. Rennert founded industrial empire Renco Group, which holds stakes in a number of companies, including military truckmaker AM General, lead and zinc producer Doe Run Company and U.S. Magnesium Corp. He might be best known as the owner of the gaudiest house in the Hamptons, a palace currently valued by local assessors at $262 million.

Steve Wynn

Net worth:$3.1 billion

Source of wealth:Casinos, hotels

Contributions:$468,500

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

After stepping down as finance chair of the Republican National Committee, Wynn donated $250,000 to the RNC the next year. Committees supporting two prominent Trump defenders, Senator Mitch McConnell of Kentucky and Representative Elise Stefanik of New York, have also received recent contributions from him, FEC filings show.

Jim Justice

Net worth:$1.2 billion

Source of wealth:Coal

Contributions:$100,000

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

The West Virginia Governor Jim Justice did not contribute to Trump’s 2016 campaign. Back then, he was a Democrat. But during Trump’s first year in office, Justice decided to join the GOP. “The Democrats walked away from me,” he said during a Trump campaign rally, announcing his plan to switch parties.

Billy Joe “Red” McCombs

Net worth:$1.5 billion

Source of wealth:Real estate, oil, cars, sports

Contributions:$25,000

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

Over a long life, 92-year-old McCombs has invested in car dealerships, radio station giant Clear Channel, as well as the San Antonio Spurs, Minnesota Vikings and Denver Nuggets. Before donating to Trump, filings show McCombs had not given to a presidential candidate since 2008, when he put $57,000 toward the McCain-Palin Victory committee.

Lori Milken, wife of Michael Milken

Spouse’s net worth:$3.7 billion

Source of wealth:Investments

Contributions:$12,500

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

Lori Milken is married to “junk bond king” Michael Milken, who does not make his own political contributions, a spokesperson for Milken toldForbes, adding, “As the head of the non-profit, non-partisan Milken Institute, he remains strictly non-partisan.” Lori Milken’s contribution came with a ticket to attend lunch with First Lady Melania Trump. However, she never attended the lunch—it was cancelled due to the coronavirus pandemic, Milken’s spokesperson said.

Donald Friese

Net worth:$1.1 billion

Source of wealth:Manufacturing

Contributions:$2,800

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

Friese's fortune comes from C.R. Laurence, a company that makes products for the glass industry.

Clayton Mathile

Net worth:$2.3 billion

Source of wealth:Pet food

Contributions:$1,300

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No

An early employee at pet food maker Iams, Mathile bought the business in 1982 and then sold it to Procter & Gamble for $2.3 billion in 1999. He gave $52,500 to a committee supporting Mitt Romney in 2012.

Jimmy John Liautaud

Net worth: $1.7 billion

Source of wealth: Jimmy John’s sandwich chain

Contributions: $924

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?No


Alexander Rovt

Net worth:$1.3 billion

Source of wealth: Fertilizer, real estate

Contributions: $500

Gave to 2016 Trump campaign?Yes


*ForbesReal-Time net worth as of May 29, 2020.

** Contribution amounts represent total money given to the Donald J. Trump for President presidential campaign committee and joint fundraising committees Trump Make America Great Again Committee and Trump Victory from January 20, 2017 through March 31, 2020, according to Federal Election Commission filings. Contributions exceeding the $5,600 contribution limit to the president’s campaign committee go to the Republican National Committee or supporting state-level RNC groups.Forbesdid not look at super PAC contributions for this analysis.

May 8, 2020: This article was updated to include additional information about Lori Milken’s contribution.

May 29, 2020: This article was updated to include two additional donors whose names were included on a Trump campaign form filed with the FEC on May 20, 2020.
