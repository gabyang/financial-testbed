Galaxy S21 Vs Pixel 5: Is Samsung Or Google's Phone The Better Buy?

The Samsung Galaxy S21 has debuted as the most affordable phone in the S21 lineup, putting it closer to the Google Pixel 5 than some of the more expensive flagship smartphones on the market. The S21 features a starting price of $799 in the U.S., which is $200 less than the launch price of the Galaxy S20. At its current price, the standard Galaxy S21 is only $100 more than the Google Pixel 5.

✕ Remove Ads

There are quite a few differences between the Galaxy S21 and the Pixel 5. With the Galaxy S21, Samsung has made a few comprises over the "Plus" model to bring down the price of the phone, but so has Google with the Pixel 5. Historically, the Pixel devices have always been a top-end range, but the Pixel 5 launched in the U.S. at $699, $100 less than the Pixel 4. Additionally, the standard Galaxy S phones have always been similar to their "Plus" counterparts with some differences, but build quality has always remained the same on both devices. However, both Google and Samsung have followed a similar approach with their latest releases, compromising some features to offer a more accessible price.

First off, the two phones are pretty similarly sized, with the Pixel 5 sporting a 6-inch display and the standard S21 a 6.2-inch display. Another noticeable but minor difference on the front of the two phones is the position of the camera cutout on the screen, with the Pixel 5's in the top left corner and the S21's remaining central. Moreover, the Pixel 5 features a glass back, while the S21 has a ‘glasstic’ back panel, which is a glass-like plastic. But beyond build quality and design, the display, chipset, and cameras of these two devices have some more significant differences.

Galaxy S21 vs Pixel 5: Specs Comparison

✕ Remove Ads

The Galaxy S21 has an FHD+ (2,400 x 1,080 pixels) AMOLED display with a 120Hz adaptive refresh rate. The Pixel 5 has a similar resolution, but loses out on the high refresh rate, with a 60Hz panel OLED panel. Battery size and charging speed are nearly similar on both devices, but the Galaxy S21’s retail box doesn’t include a charger. The Pixel 5 boasts a cleaner interface with its stock Android operating system, while Samsung's One UI 3.0 version features more customizations.

Performance is the one area where the Galaxy S21 easily outshines the Pixel 5. The S21’s Snapdragon 888 is a flagship-grade system on a chip (SoC) built on the latest 5nm architecture, while the Pixel 5’s Snapdragon 765G is a mid-range chip based on the older 7nm process. Not only is the Snapdragon 888 faster, but it also offers significant gains in graphical and AI performance, as well as a better standard of power efficiency as compared to the 765G.

✕ Remove Ads

For optics, the Pixel 5 has a dual-camera setup, comprising of a 12.2-megapixel primary sensor and a 16-megapixel ultrawide shooter. The Galaxy S21 comes with a 12-megapixel primary camera, a 12-megapixel ultrawide lens, and a 64-megapixel telephoto camera. Not only does the Galaxy S21 feature more cameras, but it also has newer camera hardware compared to the Pixel 5’s older sensor, which was first seen on the Pixel 2 back in 2017. But what the Pixel lacks in hardware, it makes up for in software. Google’s camera software is what makes Pixel phones among the best in the world for still photography. However, Samsung’s Galaxy S phones are no slouches in the photo department and can go toe-to-toe against the Pixel 5’s camera in most scenarios.

In the battle between the Samsung Galaxy S21 and Google Pixel 5, there’s only one clear winner and that’s the S21. Google's software does make a good case, but the Galaxy S21 offers far better value. It may cost a little more, but the Galaxy S21’s superior chip and display hold more weight than the marginally better software and camera processing on the Pixel 5.
