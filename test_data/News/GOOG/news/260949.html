LEGISLATURE extends recess — NEWSOM vows TESTING progress — PRISON releases blocked — FEMA helps on HOMELESS

By JEREMY B. WHITE, CARLA MARINUCCI, GRAPH MASSARA and AARON LEATHLEY 

04/06/2020 09:15 AM EDT

Presented by Phenomena Global

THE BUZZ —Sacramento has a new targeted return date, but uncertainty and frustration continue to pervade the Capitol.

In the weeks since the Legislature adjourned early, a scheduled April 13 reconveninghad seemed increasingly untenable. With the state under an indefinite stay-at-home order — already extended until the end of May in the Bay — and hospitalizations crescendoing to a peak that’s still expected to be weeks away, virtually no one in the world of California politics believed the Capitol would be repopulated with lawmakers in April.

Leadership made it official on Friday, pushing back their hoped-for return date to May and citing guidance from Gov. Gavin Newsom and health officials who have urged continued vigilance. A joint statement released late Friday belied some intra-house tension that had bubbled up earlier in the day; after Senate President Pro Tem Toni Atkins affirmed lawmakers wouldn’t be back in April, Speaker Anthony Rendon noted in a statement, “I haven’t had that conversation directly with the Pro Tem, and I certainly would before commenting to the press.” The Senate has continued to amend bills; the Assembly has not.

And that late Friday confirmation didn’t sit well with some staffers who felt blindsided. A larger complaint hanging over that: We’re in an incredibly fluid and unpredictable situation. Committee chairs and chief consultants are trying to figure out how much lawmakers will need to attenuate their bill loads, and when we might see votes. There was a feeling that leadership took too long to recognize the inevitability of a delay — and even that May date seems unrealistic to many folks, who heard Newsom predict that infections will hit their apex in May.

One thing that does seem uncertain: Ambitious January budget proposals will wither away.Newsom has already said this year will see a keep-the-lights-on budget, and he affirmed this week that a widely watched push to insure older undocumented immigrants is likely off for the year, in noting that reeling local governments have implored the state to “not impose upon them any mandate or dictate on high unless we are prepared to backfill.”

FIRST IN PLAYBOOK: Opponents of a “split roll” ballot measure to revise the landmark Prop. 13 property tax measure are citing a new study, “Taxing Commercial and Industrial Property at Full Market Value: An Economic Impact Assessment,” that suggests the proposed changes could have dire effects on the CA economy. The study, to be released Monday by the nonprofit California Tax Foundation, was completed before the Covid-19 crisis by Berkeley Research Group LLC, a global consulting firm that includes former Legislative Analyst William Hamm and former California Department of Finance Director Tom Campbell.

Among the predictions:California will lose “as many as 120,000 private sector jobs” from firms going out of business or moving out of California. It also suggests the measure would have a “disproportionate impact” on women-and minority-owned firms; and while “strengthening the incentive for ‘economic zoning,’ it may exacerbate California’s housing shortage and affordability problems,” the study found.

Schools and Communities First — the labor and education coalition in support of the measure, which calls for reassessment of commercial and industrial properties valued over $3 million — just turned in 1.7 million signatures in support of the measure, and counters it could raise $12 billion a year for California education and local community initiatives.

About The Author: Jeremy B. White

Jeremy B. White is a senior reporter covering the intersection of politics and policy in California. He’s been watching Sacramento for a decade, most recently as co-author of the California Playbook and, before that, for the Sacramento Bee.

Carla Marinucci, senior writer for POLITICO’s California Playbook, has consistently been named one of California’s leading political writers — and for the last two years has also been honored as the state’s top digital influencer in political reporting. Prior to launching the POLITICO California Playbook in 2015, Marinucci was senior political writer for the Hearst flagship San Francisco Chronicle, and before that political editor for the Hearst-owned San Francisco Examiner.
