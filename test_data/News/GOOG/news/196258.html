Coronavirus Misinformation Videos On YouTube: What To Watch Out For

There’s plenty of information on coronavirus available online although not all of it is helpful. This is particularly true when it comes to YouTube videos and that’s a problem that's unlikely to change anytime soon. In the meantime, here are some of the things to watch out for to avoid falling for coronavirus misinformation on YouTube.

✕ Remove Ads

Access to coronavirus information has become one of the main focuses of many tech companies. Whether it’s in terms of information portals like Google Search or social media sites, many big tech companies have been doing what they can to reduce exposure to misinformation or misleading content. In YouTube’s case, the service is now relying more heavily on algorithms to flag bad content and remove it before it reaches more viewers. However, that’s not always a fool-proof system and not only will good videos be falsely flagged and removed, but bad ones will find ways to circumvent the automated checks.

✕ Remove Ads

Although YouTube has said that it expects a higher number of legitimate videos to get incorrectly removed during the coronavirus pandemic, a recent report by the Tech Transparency Project highlights how there are many videos still circulating on the platform. Evidently, some of these will still be taken down (if they have not already been), but all of them might not be if they do not clearly violate YouTube’s guidelines and policies. Therefore, it is sometimes a matter of reading between the lines.

How To Coronavirus Avoid Misinformation On YouTube

As a platform, YouTube offers the best and worst of media. You can find legitimate and useful content quickly, but you can also be exposed to misinformation and misleading content. Conspiracy-based videos have proved particularly popular on the platform in the past and this has resulted in Google and YouTube taking a harder line on that style of content. However, not everything is always labeled (or understood by an algorithm) as conspiratorial in nature.

✕ Remove Ads

First off, like most news media, headlines are often a giveaway. If a video suggests that by watching you might find out the “cure” or the “answer” then there’s a good chance the video is lacking in substance. Right now, there are very few coronavirus answers and even fewer cures. Another aspect to be aware of is that in a bid to avoid YouTube’s automatic regulation, many videos won’t talk so blatantly in terms of cures and answers. Instead, they may talk in more subtle terms such as “tips” and “wellness.” While there are plenty of good quality and authority tips and wellness videos on YouTube, it is important to be a bit skeptical of coronavirus-related videos and especially if they do not come from an authority source.

✕ Remove Ads

Another aspect to be aware of is the actual avoidance of referencing "coronavirus" or "COVID-19" in general. If videos (either in the content or the title) try to skirt around using the word then that may be a warning sign. As YouTube is cracking down and demonetizing many videos that directly reference the pandemic, those who go out of their way to avoid direct references might be created more to generate revenue than be informative. Even with these tips in mind, things are still not that straightforward as there will be plenty of good and bad examples of videos for each of the tips mentioned here. Which again brings it back to reading between the lines. One of the best tips is to ask yourself what is the purpose of the video? Is it trying to convince you of something? Sell you something? Or just be helpful and informative on coronavirus?
