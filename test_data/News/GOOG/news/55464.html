Warwickshire's best burger according to Google - here's our verdict

Hundreds of positive Google reviews has thrust Libertine Burger in Leamington Spa to top-dog status

    A picture speaks a thousands words and this one screamed 'eat me', 500 times over. Libertine Burger has been on my mind ever since its array of mind-boggling offerings popped up on my Instagram feed.

    Pools of glistening, melting cheese rolling down juicy, tender beef patties encased in a golden toasted sesame-topped brioche bun, with a merest suggestion of salad providing more colours for the eyes to fall in love with.

    And many, many people have done just that. Its Leamington Spa branch has received 413 Google reviews to achieve an incredible 4.7 (out of 5) star rating. Its more-recently opened Rugby offering scores slightly better at 4.8 (albeit from just 49 reviews).

    This popularity projects Libertine Burger Leamington Spa to top of the Google searches for 'Warwickshire's best burger'. This reviewer now had a reason to head south of the county with a hungry belly and memories of those Instagram posts still fresh on my mind.

    This could get messy

    First impressions were good. Located on Warwick Street just off the Parade that runs through the heart of Leamington Spa, the set-up and decor blurs the lines between takeaway and regular restaurant. A large company logo has echoes of a big-name burger chain and the open-plan, back-of-house kitchen further strengthens the familiarity with a fast-food burger joint.

    But the warm welcome we received coming through the door and the Super Mario themed decor screamed of independent restaurant charm. And the kitchen roll present on each table was an encouraging sign; this could get messy.

    The menu, which I'd long since analysed online, sits before you in an uncomplicated, A5 format. It wasn't an easy decision, but the No.4 was my winner. This features a 150g signature patty, American cheese, lettuce, ketchup, French mustard, pickles and the addition of crispy smoky bacon that differentiates it from the No.1.

    My partner, a pescetarian, went for the No.111 (trademark vegan) "beyond meat patty, plant based sesame brioche bun, ketchup, French's mustard, lettuce and pickles".

    In less than 10 minutes our order was before us, complete with our sides - which I will discuss later.

    Not blown away

    My No.4, which I could've bolstered with an extra beef patty for just £3, was a decent size. Not quite a full 'two hander' but not a million miles away. The combination of sauces and textures made for a classic burger experience. But not one that blew me away.

    Is this really twice as good as, for example, a McDonald's Big Tasty if we're basing it on price point alone? It seems a cheap comparison to make, but when your number one commodity is burgers, you're instantly placing yourself in a crowded market with the likes of Five Guys, Burger King, Byron Burger, Wendys and Handmade Burger Co - to name but a few at various levels of the industry.

    Maybe it was my mistake upon ordering, but this experience didn't get messy. A retrospective glance at the menu shows an array of sauces and extras I could've added to the star of my meal. But there was no repeat of the sloppy, joyous affair that I've experience at Original Patty Men in Birmingham - no matter what I order (and I've been a fair few times!).

    The burger itself was of decently quality, cooked to medium, if not a little over for my liking but not enough to complain, and combined nicely with the crispy bacon and combination of sauces and cheeses. Was it my 'best in Warwickshire'? The jury is out.

    Matter of taste

    Across the table, my co-diner was having a similar pleasant but unexceptional time of it. She has laboured through many a dry, unimaginative vegetarian option as this unabashed carnivore chomped through multiple-meat burgers. This wasn't in that category. It's incredible how much flavour Libertine Burger pack into its No.111.

    As somebody who opted for pescetarianism on a matter of taste preference, rather than an ethical decision, she found it to be too much like meat, an experience she's happy to consign to history. Having sampled her burger myself, I can see where she's coming from. The texture is Spam-like, the after taste akin to smoky bacon.

    Similarly to me, she conceded perhaps her choice wasn't the strongest. But the menu is small, where no meal option is overly left field. It's rare we dine out together and come to the same conclusion; good, but not great.

    Super sides

    Our sides were house fries, dusted in sea salt, deep fried pickles and coleslaw - costing a combined £8. The pickles were my favourite thing about this meal. The golden batter perfectly encased the hearty portions of pickle. The coleslaw and fries were generous and tasty.

    What else can I tell you? There's an old-school Nintendo games console hidden away by the toilets with controllers ready for you to play. A nice touch that extends the theme of the decor. The open plan kitchen is clean, clinical and seeing staff busily working away is a pleasant distraction. As was the music. Ian Brown and Kasabian tunes initially seemed a little loud but it allowed for conversations to ensue without overhearing the other customers, who were sat less than an arm's reach away on the next table.

    The selection of drinks is fantastic, from some awesome beers, a decent selection of wines and a soft drinks list that includes some big names, and quirkier offerings too. My cherry 'Soda Folk, State of Colorado' drink was a nice accompaniment to the meal, arriving in a can with a straw.

    If you enjoyed reading this, try these stories next

    A brief fact-finding chat with a waiter informed me that many visitors to the restaurant have arrived with pictures (some from Libertine Burger's Instagram account, some, quite audaciously are not) and have asked for them to be recreated.

    Maybe that was my mistake, not going in armed with the first image that put the establishment on my radar, or maybe it was just simply setting the bar too high from those images, that the reality of the experience struggled to meet.
