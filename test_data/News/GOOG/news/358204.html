    Chelsea's Stamford Bridge ranked as worst Premier League stadium experience on matchdays

    Chelsea stadium Stamford Bridge has the unwanted accolade of being ranked bottom for matchday experience in the Premier League by a recent study.

    By Ben Pringle
    15:20, Sun, Mar 22, 2020 | UPDATED: 15:23, Sun, Mar 22, 2020
    Link copied Bookmark

    Chelsea: John Terry shoots cone off Drogba's head in training

    Chelseamanager Frank Lampard may have generated a sense of optimism around the club with his bright young squad heading in the right direction this season, but fans still have plenty to complain about off the pitch. A new study has ranked Stamford Bridge as the worst stadium in the Premier League for matchday experience, giving supporters reason to feel blue.

    Opened in 1877 for use by the London Athletic Club, Stamford Bridge became home to Chelsea after the club was founded in 1905.

    The stadium underwent renovation work in the 1990s and now boasts a capacity of 41,837 – although this only makes it the ninth-biggest ground in the Premier League.

    Chelsea do have plans to drastically expand Stamford Bridge but these appear to have been put on hold as owner Roman Abramovich dials back his investments in football.

    And that’s bad news for Blues fans and visiting supporters, who are getting something of a rough deal when Chelsea play at home.

    Chelsea's Stamford Bridge stadium has been ranked as the worst Premier League matchday experience (Image: GETTY)

    New research by Online Casinos has ranked every top-flight stadium on a range of metrics to find out the best and worst grounds, and it’s The Bridge which comes out rock-bottom.

    Chelsea’s fortress achieved a measly index score of just 3.64 out of 10, compared to table-toppers Wolves whose Molineux home posted 7.73.

    This is because Stamford Bridge has the worst overall TripAdvisor reviews as well as the priciest pints and pies.

    Burnley’s Turf Moor has the cheapest pie at just £3 compared to Chelsea’s eye-watering £4.60, while Clarets fans also have the cheapest pint at £4 compared to the Blues’ £5.30.

    Chelsea also have the third most-expensive season ticket, only trailing behind fellow Londoners Tottenham and Arsenal, although a redeeming feature is the proximity to the nearest station at Fulham Broadway.

    Manchester United fans have easiest access with Old Trafford boasting its own station, and the Red Devils come in a respectable 13th in the overall table with an index score of 5.58.

    The study takes into account the Google rating for each stadium as well as the number of ‘Excellent’ and ‘Very Good’ reviews in relation to overall reviews submitted on TripAdvisor.

    The cost of a standard stadium tour for an adult is also factored into the overall scores, with the majority of pricing information gathered from club websites or in answer to direct queries.

    Unfortunately for Chelsea fans, Stamford Bridge is easily worst of the lot for a matchday experience while West Ham’s London Stadium comes in second bottom.

    The King Power Stadium is third worst for Leicester while at the other end of the scale Norwich’s Carrow Road offers competition to Molineux for top spot.

    Surprisingly, the Etihad Stadium boasts the third best matchday experience ahead of Bramall Lane and the new Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

    Liverpool’s Anfield home is seventh on the list, just behind Newcastle’s iconic St James’ Park while Arsenal are comfortably midtable with the Emirates sat 12th.
