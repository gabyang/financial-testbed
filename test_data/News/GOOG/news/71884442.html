Stock Market Rally Hits Record High As Google, Microsoft, Tesla Jump; Facebook Is Now Meta: Weekly Review

Licensing
  • IBD STAFF
  • 11:50 AM ET 10/29/2021

The stock market rally hit fresh record highs, with the major indexes running solidly higher despite a couple week closes. Tesla (TSLA) surged on a Hertz deal, hitting a $1 trillion valuation, with several other EV stocks racing higher. Google parent Alphabet (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT) jumped on strong earnings, while Apple (AAPL) and Amazon.com (AMZN) fell on disappointing results. Facebook (FB) fell on weak revenue and guidance, then changed its corporate name to Meta Platforms, to emphasize a new metaverse focus.

Stock Market Rally Hits Highs

The Dow Jones, S&P 500 and eventually the Nasdaq hit record highs, fueled by Tesla (TSLA), Alphabet (GOOGL) and Microsoft (MSFT). Earnings overall are strong, though Apple (AAPL), Amazon.com (AMZN) and Facebook (FB) were among the losers.

Treasury yields fell sharply. Crude oil prices hit multiyear highs, but then backed off.

Google Soars On Earnings

Google-parent Alphabet (GOOGL) reported Q3 EPS jumped 71% while gross revenue swelled 41% to 65.12 billion, both easily beating. Internet search and other revenue rose 44% to $37.93 billion vs. estimates of $36.41 billion. Google said YouTube ad revenue rose 43% to $7.2 billion while cloud-computing revenue grew 45% to $4.99 billion, both slightly missing. But Google cloud nearly halved its operating loss to $644 million. Shares surged to a new high.

Tesla Soars To $1 Trillion Valuation

Tesla (TSLA) soared this week, with the company achieving a trillion-dollar valuation after announcing a mega deal with car rental giant Hertz (HTZZ). Tesla will provide 100,000 vehicles to Hertz by the end of 2022, with Hertz likely benefiting from a 30% EV subsidy for commercial vehicles. Hertz secured an exclusive agreement to sell EVs to Uber (UBER), saying its overall Tesla buy could hit 200,000. Starting in early November, customers will be able to rent a Tesla Model 3 at Hertz airport and other locations in major U.S. markets and select cities in Europe. Hertz is also contracting with Carvana (CVNA) to sell its older rental cars.

Apple Misses Sales Target

Apple (AAPL) reported a 70% EPS gain, meeting fiscal Q4 views as sales climbed 29% to $83.4 billion, falling short. Apple said supply chain constraints cut its revenue by $6 billion in the period and could have a bigger impact in the December quarter. IPhone sales increased 47% to $38.9 billion. Services sales grew 26% to $18.3 billion. Apple's iPad sales rose 21% to $8.3 billion and its Wearables, Home and Accessories sales advanced 12% to $8.8 billion. The Mac computer business was a laggard, growing less than 2% to $9.2 billion. Apple stock fell Friday on earnings.

Microsoft Gets Cloud Lift

Microsoft (MSFT) reported a 25% EPS gain as fiscal Q1 sales grew 22% to $45.3 billion. Microsoft Cloud generated $20.7 billion in revenue, up 36%. Azure cloud infrastructure sales rose 50%. Microsoft guided higher for current-quarter revenue.

Chipmakers Beat And Raise

A bunch of chipmakers delivered beat-and-raise September-quarter earnings reports. They included Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), MaxLinear (MXL), Silicon Labs (SLAB), STMicroelectronics (STM) and Wolfspeed (WOLF). However, several semiconductor sector firms posted mixed quarterly reports included Allegro MicroSystems (ALGM), Entegris (ENTG) and Texas Instruments (TXN).

Facebook, Twitter Face Ad Headwinds

Facebook (FB) and Twitter (TWTR) revealed the advertising revenue fallout from privacy changes made by Apple to the iPhone operating system. Twitter had its biggest drop in six months as its Q4 revenue outlook fell below expectations. Facebook third-quarter revenue missed as did its fourth-quarter outlook. Facebook stock fell 5%. Facebook changed its name to Meta Platforms.

Mastercard Earnings Top, Visa Guidance Disappoints

Mastercard (MA) EPS grew 48%, with Q3 revenue climbing 30% to $5 billion, both beating, thanks to healthy domestic spending and solid growth in cross-border spending. Meanwhile, Visa (V) posted a 45% EPS gain as sales swelled 29% to $6.6 billion. But Visa sees 2022 revenue growth below analyst targets for 20%. Both stocks fell sharply.

Casinos Deliver Big Earnings

BYD Gaming (BYD) reported 242% EPS growth as casinos rebound from pandemic shutdowns. Revenue rise29% to $843.1 million, also topping views. Monarch Casino & Resort (MCRI) more than doubled EPS from the year-ago quarter to $1.15 per share in Q3, on an 86% surge in sales to $111.63 million, both above estimates. MCRI stock spiked higher, while BYD stock fell 2%.

Big Oil Earnings Beat

Exxon Mobil (XOM) and Chevron (CVX) reported strong Q3 earnings and revenue that beat analyst expectations. Despite rising oil prices, Chevron said that it will keep spending discipline after slashing capital expenditures this year. Exxon, which announced a $10 billion buyback, said it sees future annual capital investments of $20 billion to $25 billion.

Drug Earnings

Bristol Myers Squibb (BMY) and Merck (MRK) topped forecasts. Bristol EPS grew 23% to $2 and sales increased 10% to $11.62 billion. Merck reported adjusted EPS rising 28% as sales climbed 20% to $13.15 billion. But Bristol fell on looming competition for its cancer drug, Revlimid, while Merck surged on bullish expectations for its Covid pill, molnupiravir. Eli Lilly (LLY) and Novartis (NVS) were mixed. Lilly earnings rose 38%, lagging views. But revenue rose 18% to $6.77 billion, beating. Novartis missed on sales, up 6%, while EPS grew 13%, beating. Generic drug giant Teva Pharmaceutical (TEVA) and Alnylam Pharmaceuticals (ALNY) missed on the top and bottom line.

Software Earnings

Atlassian (TEAM) said it earned 46 cents a share, up 53% from a year earlier, in its first fiscal quarter. Analysts had projected profit of 40 cents per share. Revenue rose 34% to $614 million, ahead of views for $585 million in sales. For the December quarter, Atlassian said it expects total revenue in a range of $630 million to $645 million versus estimates of $621 million. Also, Atlassian announced that James Beer will retire as chief financial officer at the end of June 2022.

ServiceNow (NOW) reported Q3 EPS grew 28%, comfortably beating, while revenue rose 31% to $1.51 billion, narrowly topping. The business software firm forecast Q4 subscription revenue of $1.515 billion to $1.52 billion, just above estimates of $1.51 billion. NOW stock rose on earnings, back into a buy zone.

Shopify (SHOP) reported Q3 earnings fell 26%, revenue rose 46% while gross merchandise volume climbed 35%, but all missed forecasts. But SHOP stock, which trended lower for a few months, jumped on earnings. Shopify sets up e-commerce websites for small businesses, and partners with others to handle digital payments and shipping

Twilio (TWLO) earnings fell 75% while revenue jumped 65% to $740.2 million, both beating. But organic sales growth slowed. Meanwhile, the communications software maker guided low on Q4 EPS and said COO George Hu is leaving.

Ford Bullish On Future

For Q3, General Motors (GM) disclosed a 65% EPS dive as revenue slid 25%. Ford (F) earnings fell 22% as revenue fell 5%. Both beat earning views while missing on revenue. A worsening chip shortage hit the auto giants' production afresh last quarter. GM's full-year guidance suggested a weak Q4. But Ford hiked full-year EPS guidance, citing improving chip supplies, and will resume paying a quarterly dividend. Ford stock broke out past a 16.55 buy point to a seven-year high. GM stock sold off.

BYD Co. (BYDDF), the Warren Buffett-backed EV and battery giant, reported a Q3 revenue gain but lower profit. Shares fell sharply on earnings after trending higher.

Amazon Dives On Q3 Miss

The e-commerce and cloud-computing giant reported a 51% EPS decline while sales growth slowed to 15%, both missing. Q4 sales guidance also was light. Amazon (AMZN) is dealing with decelerating sales growth as consumers go back to physical stores and as the company faces supply chain challenges.

Shipping Firms Truck Higher

UPS (UPS) hiked 2021 financial targets after easily beating earnings and revenue estimates for the third quarter. The shipping giant increased EPS 19% with sales up 9% to $23.114 billion, on strength across business segments. UPS jumped, closing in on a buy point. Amid trucking firms, Saia (SAIA) delivered 83% EPS growth and 28% sales growth, while Old Dominion Freight Line ODFL came in with 44% and 32%, respectively. Both rose to new highs.

Defense Firms Short On Sales

Lockheed Martin (LMT), Northrop Grumman (NOC), Raytheon Technologies (RTX), and General Dynamics (GD) reported Q3 revenue that fell short of Wall Street expectations. Raytheon CEO Greg Hayes said that the U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan has a $75 million impact on the company's sales. Lockheed lowered its full-year sales outlook on the withdrawal and a drop in F-35 production. LMT stock and Northrop sold off, while Raytheon and General Dynamics fell modestly.

Boeing (BA) reported a larger-than-expected Q3 loss and recorded charges on its 787 Dreamliner and Starliner programs. The Dow Jones aviation giant expects Dreamliner's low production rate and rework on the aircraft to cost $1 billion in total. Boeing expects to receive regulatory approval for its 737 Max from Beijing by year-end with deliveries to Chinese customers resuming in Q1.

Market Data Firms Solid

Financial-information providers S&P Global (SPGI) and MSCI (MSCI) reported third-quarter earnings that beat estimates. Revenue in S&P's large debt-rating business rose 14%. The firms reported as markets rebound from the coronavirus pandemic, deal-making drives greater need for financial analytics, and investing becomes more complex. SPGI stock broke out while MSCI is setting up.

McDonald's Beats, Starbucks Misses

McDonald's (MCD) reported third-quarter results that beat expectations. Analysts cited the Crispy Chicken Sandwich, strength in digital ordering and its loyalty program. Starbucks (SBUX) narrowly beat EPS views, but sales missed, as fresh coronavirus lockdowns in China weighed. The results arrived as restaurants face difficulties attracting workers.

News In Brief

O'Reilly Automotive (ORLY), an auto-parts retailer, reported third-quarter results that beat estimates. The company also raised its full-year outlook.

PayPal (PYPL), responding to reports it will buy Pinterest (PINS), said in a terse statement that it is not "pursuing an acquisition at this time." PayPal stock had retreated amid the reports. But after a brief bounce Monday, PYPL stock kept falling. PINS stock plunged.

Enphase Energy (ENPH) skyrocketed after reporting Q3 EPS doubled and revenue rose 97%, beating views, lifting Q4 guidance as well. The results lifted a basket of solar stocks. Enphase also discussed its new generation of product offerings and addressed lingering fears of supply chain disruptions.

Edwards Lifesciences (EW) reported adjusted EPS rose 4%, meeting views. Revenue grew 15% to $1.31 billion, just missing.

Cadence Design Systems (CDNS) reported a 14% EPS gain with revenue up 13% to $751 million in Q3. The electronic design software maker guided higher.

AbbVie (ABBV) reported adjusted EPS rose 18% to $3.33 with sales up 11% to $14.34 billion. The medical products giant also raised its full-year EPS outlook.
