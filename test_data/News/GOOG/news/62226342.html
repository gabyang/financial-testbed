Lancashire pubs for sale as new landlords sought after lockdown

    With the majority of lockdown restrictions lifted for the vast majority of Lancashire, the economy is starting to tick again across the red rose county.

    From March 23 to July 4, pubs and bars were forced to close in line with national coronavirus guidelines to curb the spread of the disease.

    Since July 4, the hospitality sector has been getting back to work, helped out through Government schemes including August's Eat Out to Help Out.

    But there are some venues across Lancashire that are looking new people at the helm as they enter the post-lockdown world.

    From Lancaster and Morecambe to Preston, West Lancashire, and Rossendale, there are plenty of opportunities on the market for budding landlords looking to make it through pint pulling.

    LancsLive has brought some of the most noticeable pubs available on the market right now. The list is by no means extensive, but merely an insight into what you can get your hands on:

    Elletson Arms and Pilling Chippy, Pilling - £75,000

    The Elletson Arms pub is on the market for £75,000 - which also gets you the neighbouring fish and chip shop.

    On the market through Blackpool-based Kays Peake Properties Ltd, the pub in the heart of the Wyre borough is on the main road to Lancaster at the junction of Garstang road, opposite Pilling's local newsagents.

    The pub was refurbished less than three years ago, with Pilling Chippy built in 2019.

    There is a large car park and beer garden, with 50 covers in the main bar and restaurant area.

    A new lease is negotiable with the landlord with the pub free of ties. The pub is a lock up business but the landlord owns the upper floors of the pub and is open to offering a flat for accommodation through separate negotiations.

    For more details click here.

    The Britannia, Lancaster - £285,000+

    The Britannia pub on the corner of Ullswater Road and Moor Gate in Lancaster is looking for a new freehold owner.

    Minimum offers of £285,000 are being sought.

    The two-storey Georgian watering hole has a lounge and dining area seating 60 people with a central wooden bar and exposed wooden flooring.

    There is a games room, open fireplace, pool table, and mounted televisions.

    Owner's accommodation can be found on the first floor, made up of a landing, lounge, kitchen, four double bedrooms and a bathroom.

    The current turnover is in growth with a weekly figure of around £5,000 and an additional £1,200 per week derived from the first floor letting accommodation.

    More information can be found here.

    The Railway, Parbold - £650,000

    Situated next to the railway line in Parbold, The Railway pub is looking for a new owner.

    Costing £650,000 for the freehold to the watering hole, comes with a 75 capacity beer garden and parking for 20 cars.

    The pub's two snug areas inside have capacity to seat around 30 people, with an open plan bar area occupying the central part of the pub.

    The open plan area has room for 60 punters. There is also a cellar area which can be utilised as an outside bar.

    The private residence comes with three bedrooms, a loung area, office, kitchen and dining area, and bathroom.

    For more information click here.

    Baffito's, Preston - £700,000

    The former Baffito's restaurant at Preston Docks is on the market for £700,000.

    Available at long-leasehold, the former restaurant and bar has 80 covers in its dining restaurant and 60 covers in its lounge and bar area. There is also a private dining room with space for 24 people.

    Baffito's, in Navigation Way, Preston, closed in October 2019 amid reports of anti-social behaviour.

    The eatery, which opened 2013, was a family restaurant which also featured a sports bar with bare-brickwork interiors and a large patio.

    For more information see here.

    Hadlows, Garstang - £750,000+

    Available for offers over £750,000, Hadlows to the north of Garstang is available for freehold.

    Based to the immediate north of Cabus on the A6 Lancaster New Road, the public house has a lounger and dining restaurant with space for 140 customers.

    There is a very large patio area and lawned beer gardens overlooking the Wyre countryside.

    A barn on the 1.168 acres of land that comes with the pub also has planning permission for a one-bedroom manager's accommodation.

    Three single-story outbuildings also have permission to be transformed into a farm shop and ice cream parlour.

    The pub is five kilometres to the north of junction 31 of the M6 and has parking for 50 cars.

    More information on the pub can be found here.

    Nowhere, Morecambe - offers invited

    Located in the central Morecambe just minutes from the coastline, Nowhere lounge bar is currently available for freehold.

    The two-storey, mid terraced bar has an extension to the rear with capacity for 220 people.

    It has been running for the last decade, with there now an opportunity to refurbish and take advantage of the disused function room on the first floor to increase business revenue.

    Private accommodation comes with the bar in the form of five bedrooms, one of which is currently used as an office. There is also a lounge and dining area and bathroom.

    Trading hours are 10pm to 4am, Friday and Saturday, and 10pm to 2am on Sundays.

    For more information see here.

    Applejax, Chorley - £315,000

    The former Applejax nightclub in Chorley is on the market for £315,000.

    Available at freehold, the once popular night time destination is three storeys in height.

    The property is well located on the A581 Park Road at its intersection with Back Mount street directly opposite St Laurence Church and is within walking distance of the town centre retail shopping precinct and Chorley railway station.

    For more information click here.

    The Red Lion, Rawtenstall - £59,995

    Located to the east of Rawtenstall town centre, The Red Lion is on the market for just under £60,000.

    The well-established pub in New Church Road, Rossendale, has been in business for more than two centuries, and has an excellent rapport with locals.

    It hosts a number of charity events, including a popular annual bake off. The pub is especially busy for football matches displayed on the TV's in the lounge area.

    The Red Lion is available for leasehold. It comes free of ties for wines, spirits and soft drinks.

    The upstairs of the pub is a three-bedroom flat with a kitchen, bathroom, and lounge, but has scope to be converted into dining areas if plans are laid out to expand the pub's capacity.

    The access to upstairs is currently behind the bar but there is space to move the bar and have the upstairs access open to customers.

    The Red Lion has parking for six cars as well as street parking. There is a well designed and maintained beer garden to the left of the watering hole.
