    EU hopes for coronavirus measures dashed as huge row erupts

    A BITTER row between advocates of two rival European smartphone apps aimed at tracing people at risk from COVID-19 is jeopardising EU efforts to get to grips with the coronavirus pandemic.

    By Ciaran McGrath, Senior News Reporter
    22:29, Tue, Apr 21, 2020 | UPDATED: 22:49, Tue, Apr 21, 2020
    Link copied Bookmark

    Matt Hancock announces launch of NHS contact tracing app

    Scientists from more than 25 countries signed an open letter yesterday warning the Pan-European Privacy-Preserving Proximity Tracing (PEPP-PT), a project spearheaded by Germany, potentially cleared for surveillance of members of the public. Instead, they are backing a decentralised contact tracing protocol called DP-3T, pioneered by Swiss researchers, which is aligned with a technology alliance between Apple and Google. The letter, signed by a total of 300 scientists, states: "We are concerned that some 'solutions' to the crisis may, via mission creep, result in systems which would allow unprecedented surveillance of society at large."

    The wrangle centres on whether sensitive data will be kept safely on devices, or alternatively, would be stored on a central server in such a way which could permit somebody accessing it for nefarious purposes to build a picture of where and when users had met other people.

    The letter said: "Solutions which allow reconstructing invasive information about the population should be rejected without further discussion."

    Contact tracing apps are widely used in Asia, but there are worries a similar approach in Europe would violate privacy laws.

    Criticism of PEPP-PT has been steadily building, with Marcel Salathe, a researcher at Ecole Polytechnique Federale de Lausanne tweeting on Friday: “I am personally disassociating from PEPP-PT.

    Contact tracing apps are widely used in Asia, but there are worries a similar approach in Europe would violate privacy laws.

    Speaking on Thursday, European Commissioner for Internal Market Thierry Breton offering such technologies his qualified backing.

    He said: “Contact tracing apps to limit the spread of coronavirus can be useful, especially as part of Member States' exit strategies.

    "However, strong privacy safeguards are a pre-requisite for the uptake of these apps, and therefore their usefulness.

    "While we should be innovative and make the best use of technology in fighting the pandemic, we will not compromise on our values and privacy requirements.”

    In a separate development, the French government today announced National Assembly members would be permitted to vote on plans to launch a contact tracing app.
