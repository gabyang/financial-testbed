March 30th, 2022 15:49
#SocialStocks: NAAG urges Snapchat and TikTok to add parental controls
Market Mover
Andrew Perez - email
Apple and Meta disclose user info to hackers, Meta orchestrated campaign against TikTok, and other notable stories from this week

Welcome to "#SocialStocks," The Fly's weekly recap of Wall Street's reactions to social media stock news.

WHOOPS: Apple (AAPL) and Meta Platforms (FB) gave customer information to hackers who masqueraded as law enforcement, Bloomberg's William Turton reported, citing three people with knowledge of the matter. The two tech giants provided basic subscriber data, including addresses, phone numbers, and IP addresses, in mid-2021 in response to the fake "emergency data requests," the author said, noting that typically such requests are only provided with a search warrant or subpoena signed by a judge. In this case, such emergency requests do not need a court order. 

TORONTO ENGINEERING HUB:In a company blog post yesterday, Meta's head of engineering and remote presence Maher Saba said, "Today, we're announcing the creation of a new engineering hub in Canada based in Toronto, alongside increased hiring across Canada. This is expected to create up to 2,500 new jobs over the next several years, with recruiting already underway. The majority of roles are engineering-focused and expected to span across building extended reality experiences and Meta technologies. We're also establishing the first Canadian WhatsApp, Messenger and Remote Presence engineering teams and growing our Canadian Reality Labs and AI Research teams...Today, we're also announcing an additional $510,000 in unrestricted grants to 17 Canadian research labs working on research that will advance innovations needed to build the metaverse. Unrestricted grants provide maximum flexibility for labs to pursue their mission, and once published, their research becomes publicly accessible to drive further innovation across the industry. "

THE HEAT OF BATTLE:Meta paid Republican consulting firm Targeted Victory to orchestrate a campaign against TikTok in the U.S., including placing op-eps in regional news outlets, The Washington Post's Taylor Lorenz and Drew Harwell reported. Dream would be to get stories with headlines like 'From dances to danger,'" one campaign director said.

PARENTAL CONTROL:The National Association of Attorneys General, or NAAG, urged TikTok and Snapchat (SNAP) to give parents the ability to monitor their children's social media usage and protect their children from online threats using parental control apps. In a letter to TikTok and Snapchat, a bipartisan coalition of 44 state and territory attorneys general expressed concern that the companies are not taking appropriate steps to allow parents to protect their kids on your platforms and asked them to conform to widespread industry practice by giving parents increased ability to protect their vulnerable children. "Parental control apps can alert parents or schools to messages and posts on your platforms that have the potential to be harmful and dangerous. Apps can also alert parents if their child manifests a desire for self-harm or suicide. On other platforms where these apps are allowed to operate appropriately parents have received notifications of millions of instances of severe bullying and hundreds of thousands of self-harm situations, showing that these apps have the potential to save lives and prevent harm to our youth," read the letter.

SOCIAL MEDIAMOVEMENTS: "We're testing a new feature to help people discover social movements happening in the world and on Instagram, as well as ways to take action. We're rolling this out first to a handful of hashtags that are focused on popular and long-standing movements on Instagram and in the world, like #BlackLivesMatter, #womensrights and#climatecrisis. We're making it easier to raise awareness of these causes by sharing the hashtag page via DM or creating a fundraiser. We're beginning to test a new feature that helps people find and support social movements on Instagram. People often come to Instagram to make their voices heard, elevate causes and bring communities together. They're eager to support causes they hear about on Instagram and are always looking for new ways to take action - these updates will make it easier to do so. With this new feature, you'll be able to support, fundraise and spread the word about the social causes centered directly through hashtags. Hashtags have long been a place where people discover new causes to support on Instagram, and now when you search for specific hashtags associated with certain movements, you'll have the option to support them. "Support" shows more information about the movement, "Spread the Word" lets you share it with your friends via DM, and "Create a Fundraiser" lets you start a fundraiser on behalf of the movement," said Instagram, in a blog posting.

CHALLENGING TRENDS:Morgan Stanley analyst Brian Nowak downgraded Pinterest (PINS) to Equal Weight from Overweight with a price target of $30, down from $53. The company's user and time spent trends are challenging while the company is shifting existing time spent toward lower-monetizing creator videos, the analyst told investors in a research note. Nowak further states that the larger platforms with more engineers, ad sales people and existing advertisers like Facebook and Google (GOOGL) will be advantaged in their ability to monetize this format over smaller players like Pinterest.

CREATOR FUND EXPANSION:In a company blog post, Pinterest's creator inclusion lead Zeny Shifferaw said, "Creating a more inclusive space online means increasing our investment to support the work of underrepresented creators. Starting today, we're announcing an expanded Creator Fund program, with more opportunities for creators to participate and get hands-on support...New for 2022, we'll announce a new Fund cycle every quarter, giving even more creators access to financial resources and training. Each cycle will focus on a different content area, for more personalized training and community building. This first 2022 cycle is focused on Fashion and Beauty, inviting Fashion and Beauty creators in the US to apply. Future cycles will include topics like Food, Lifestyle and Wellness....Participation in the Creator Funds opens up potential brand partnership opportunities for you, and gives you access to creator-focused conferences. And because a financial leg up matters, you'll receive $25,000 in the form of a cash grant, ad credits and an equipment stipend. You'll also get a first look at new Pinterest products...The fund is based in the US for now. But we're excited to expand the program to include underrepresented creators in Brazil and the UK later this year. It's happening! Be sure to check back for updates and new opportunities over the coming months."

A NEW PROJECT: Tesla's (TSLA) Elon Musk said he is giving "serious thought" to building a new social media platform in a tweet on Saturday. On Friday, Musk tweeted: "Given that Twitter (TWTR) serves as the de facto public town square, failing to adhere to free speech principles fundamentally undermines democracy. What should be done?"

ANITRUST LATEST:Lawmakers in Europe have reached an agreement on the main points of the Digital Markets Act, a new digital competition law focused on the world's biggest tech companies, The Wall Street Journal's Kim Mackrael and Sam Schechner reported. The legislation seeks to impose new obligations and prohibitions on digital giants the EU calls "gatekeepers" in order to limit the ability of tech companies to take advantage of their presence in digital markets. Provisions would allow developers to make their apps available to iPhone users without going through Apple's App Store and could limit how sites such as Google and Amazon (AMZN) can rank their own products and services ahead of those offered by smaller competitors in search results, according to the report. 

ANALYST COMMENTARY:Exane BNP Paribas analyst Stefan Slowinski initiated coverage of Zoom Video (ZM) with a Neutral rating and $110 price target.

Loop Capital analyst Alan Gould lowered the firm's price target on Twitter (TWTR) to $51 from $65 to reflect an updated valuation framework across the group of peer companies but maintained a Buy rating on the shares. The company's early turnaround efforts have largely addressed core platform and tech infrastructure issues, reversed user engagement trends, and set the table for meaningful growth, the analyst tells investors in a research note. Gould adds that this has in turn set the table for meaningful growth in direct response advertising while creating a favorable set-up for new Twitter leadership.

Gould lowered the firm's price target on Snap to $65 from $68 but reiterated a Buy rating on the shares. Snap is the quickest growing of the large public internet companies, but it still monetizes at a much lower rate than its primary competitors which provides substantial revenue opportunity, the analyst tells investors in a research note. Gould adds that Snap is yet to monetize its numerous new businesses - including Spotlight, AR lenses and SNAP Map - which should help sustain its growth rate.

Loop Capital analyst Rob Sanderson lowered the firm's price target on Pinterest to $34 from $50 to reflect his updated valuation framework across the group of peer companies but kept a Buy rating on the shares. While user growth trends that have challenged the stock may not improve until late-2022, there is a "strong pipeline" for an already robust monetization story as shopping becomes a larger driver of activity on the network, the analyst tells investors in a research note. Sanderson further states that the easing of supply chain issues should help CPG advertisers, which is a "key vertical" for Pinterest.

Lastly, UBS analyst Lloyd Walmsley maintained his Buy rating and $280 price target on Meta Platforms, stating that the Instagram newsfeed changes along with Reels monetization should drive a "meaningful bump" to time spent on the platform overall and ultimately lead to higher monetization at scale. Walmsley adds that the push of Reels, prior to monetization, could see a 6% revenue headwind in the first half of the year, but as the company scales up monetization, it could turn into an 8%-15% tailwind, driving a "meaningful inflection" in ad revenue.
