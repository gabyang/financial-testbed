PELOSI vs. MCCARTHY on MAIL BALLOTS — NEWSOM extends HOTEL help — LEGISLATURE sets CORONAVIRUS hearings — LGBTQ icon LYON dies

By JEREMY B. WHITE, CARLA MARINUCCI, GRAPH MASSARA and AARON LEATHLEY 

04/10/2020 09:24 AM EDT

Presented by Phenomena Global

THE BUZZ: It’s not just who we vote for. How we vote for them has become a burning issue as the pandemic overlaps with an election year, requiring a balance of civic participation and public health — and California’s in the thick of it.

Voting by mail is now commonplace in California,where a majority of the state’s population live in counties that (with the exception of Los Angeles) mail ballots to all voters. LA elections officials are exploring all-mail balloting in November after intolerably long in-person waits in March led LA supervisors and Secretary of State Alex Padilla to advocate for universal remote votes. We’ll get a taste of that in two upcoming all-mail primaries to fill a state Senate and a U.S. House seat; last night, Newsom extended the all-mail mandate to three more local elections.

Padilla has championed a charge for nationwide vote-by-mail and hasaddressed that argument directly at Trump. On a call Thursday with Sen. Amy Klobuchar,Sen. Chris Coonsand other secretaries of state, Padilla said there is “no excuse: vote-by-mail ballots must be the standard across the country.” California Congress members called for the sameafter Wisconsin voters were compelled to pack together in person this week; Sen. Dianne Feinstein called that primary “a wake-up call that Congress must act.”

IN SACRAMENTO,whenever lawmakers return they’ll consider newly announced legislation from Assemblyman Marc Bermanthat would mail a ballot to every single registered voter in California for the November election. Berman cited Wisconsin’s “debacle” as a cautionary tale: “Californians should not have to risk their health, and possibly their lives,” to vote, he said.

IN D.C., Californian leaders have been clashing over this issue on the national stage.HouseSpeaker Nancy Pelosi is pushing for mail voting money in a new stimulus package, which her Republican counterpartKevin McCarthydenounced yesterday as “disgusting” leverage play. Pelosi retorted that Republicans who have warned vote-by-mail could hurt them, as President Trump openly mused, should “have more confidence in what the Republican party stands for.

BUENOS DIAS, good Friday morning. Gov. Gavin Newsom shared an encouraging development Thursday: ICU admittances, a key gauge of the virus’s arc, dropped by 1.4 percent day-over-day. The governor cautioned against over-interpreting a single data point, but it’s certainly welcome news.

QUOTE OF THE DAY: “Under normal circumstances, the Legislature would have had more time to deliberate an expenditure of this magnitude and would have been allowed to thoroughly vet the details of the contract before proceeding.” State Senate Budget chair Holly Mitchell demands more details from the Newsom administration on that $495 million mask outlay — language leadership echoed in setting oversight hearings.

GAVINLAND

HOW’S HE DOING?Californians give overwhelmingly high ratingsto how their state government is responding to the Covid-19 crisis — and are far less satisfied with the federal response, according to a survey by Democratic pollster David Binder.

The poll of 1,800 likely California voters was taken online from April 2-5 by Binder,and didn’t specifically name Newsom or Trump. But it showed a whopping 74 percent of the respondents believe California government is doing a “good” or “excellent” job. That’s a sharp contrast to the just 40 percent of the respondents who believed the federal government’s performance rates that high.

A third of likely voters said the crisis has impacted them or their family “a great deal.” Two thirds were “very concerned” or “extremely concerned” that it will have a negative impact in the future, and more than half said that they believed the public is “not taking the virus seriously enough.”

IN MEMORIAM

GROUNDBREAKING LESBIAN ACTIVIST PHYLLIS LYON:Lesbian activist Phyllis Lyon, who made national news in 2008 when she and her partner, Del Martin, became the first same-sex couple to be legally married in California under then-San Francisco mayor Gavin Newsom, has died at the age of 95. The effort to legalize same-sex marriages was challenged and overturned by the California Supreme Court before it was eventually upheld by the U.S. Supreme Court.

The couple were lifelong — and legendary — activists in the LGBTQ movement, both nationally and in the Bay Area. They pushed to decriminalize homosexuality in the late ’50s and ’60s, and co-founded the Daughters of Bilitis, the first political and social organization for lesbians in the U.S. They later were instrumental in activism in San Francisco’s first gay and lesbian political organization, the Alice B. Toklas Democratic Club. Martin died in 2008, just weeks after their wedding, at the age of 87.

Toni Atkins, the first openly lesbian Senate leader:Lyon and Martin “were both critical individuals in my early years as a college student seeking positive role models for me as a lesbian and as a young feminist.”

About The Author: Jeremy B. White

Jeremy B. White is a senior reporter covering the intersection of politics and policy in California. He’s been watching Sacramento for a decade, most recently as co-author of the California Playbook and, before that, for the Sacramento Bee.

About The Author: Carla Marinucci

Carla Marinucci, senior writer for POLITICO’s California Playbook, has consistently been named one of California’s leading political writers — and for the last two years has also been honored as the state’s top digital influencer in political reporting. Prior to launching the POLITICO California Playbook in 2015, Marinucci was senior political writer for the Hearst flagship San Francisco Chronicle, and before that political editor for the Hearst-owned San Francisco Examiner.
