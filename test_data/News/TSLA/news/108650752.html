MUSK TO RUFFLE SOME FEATHERS

Published: 2022-04-06 02:01:33 ET TSLA

ELON Musk will join the board of Twitter in a move that could significantly change the direction of the Silicon Valley media giant.

The announcement came a day after a regulatory filing revealed the Tesla chief had taken a near 10 per cent stake in Twitter, news which gave the firm's share price a more than 20 per cent boost.

Twitter CEO Parag Agrawal said yesterday that Musk - who has more than 80m followers on the platform and has used it in the past to make significant market announcements as well as once asking his followers whether to dump stock - was a "passionate believer and intense critic of the service".

Agrawal, who took the job in November 2021, said that is "exactly what we need on Twitter, and in the boardroom, to make us stronger in the long term".

Musk's first act in response to the announcement was to ask his followers if the platform needed an edit button.

His activity on the platform has also drawn the ire of regulators, with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) charging Musk with making "false and misleading" statements on the platform in 2018 when he was reportedly considering taking the electric vehicle maker private.

Musk eventually agreed to a settlement with the SEC which the watchdog in turn alleged he ignored when he tweeted "Tesla stock price is too high imo" in 2020.

The Tesla boss has often criticised the platform for its approach to free speech.

The firm is under fire from regulators and governments across the world for a perceived failure to crack down on hate speech on the platform, though Musk has warned against censorship of what he called the "de facto public square".

Analysts suggested Musk's ascension to the board was a sign of changing winds at the big tech firm.

Susannah Streeter, senior investment analyst at Hargreaves Lansdown, said "although his involvement has already sparked a share surge with expectation that it could lead to higher levels of participation on the platform, over the longer term Twitter investors will want to see that high levels of governance are adhered to, otherwise the independence of Twitter could be questioned, and the risk is that users may start to drift away".

Questions will also be asked about Musk's workload.

In January Musk said Tesla would shelve plans for a more affordable electric vehicle, saying "we have... too much on our plate, frankly".

Tesla shares were down three per cent yesterday.
