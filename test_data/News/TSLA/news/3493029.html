    These car tax changes will offer 'significant personal savings' for owners of these models

    CAR TAX changes introduced this April will ensure owners of electric vehicles can make "significant" savings which could shift people's attitudes on the technology, according to an expert.

    By Luke Chillingsworth, Cars Reporter
    11:55, Thu, Apr 2, 2020 | UPDATED: 15:29, Thu, Apr 2, 2020
    Link copied Bookmark

    Budget 2020: Sunak reveals plan for cheaper low emission cars

    Car tax changes for 2020 will see costs fall for owners of electric vehicles and those looking to purchase fully-electric cars. Updates to car tax rules will see massive tax breaks for owners through changes to VED rates and benefit-in-kind charges.

    Related articles

    • Electric cars to drop by 10 percent after tax changes
    • Chancellor announced car tax reforms to boost electric cars

    READ MORE

    • Motorists could pay over £1,000 for cars due to this tax change

    The updates will mean electric car owners will now no longer pay any tax whatsoever after the remaining £320 charge for owning an expensive vehicle was scrapped. 

    The changes mean motorists can now save up to £1,600 across six years of ownership if their vehicle is priced above £40,000. 

    However, updates to benefit-in-kind rates will see tax for electric cars purchased through salary sacrifice finance schemes dropped. 

    The updates, set to come in on 6 April, will see benefit-in-kind charges dropped from 16 percent to zero percent which will cause overall costs to plummet.

    Dan Martin, CEO of Elmtronics said: “Announced in the latest budget, Electric Vehicles are subject to zero percent Benefit in Kind rate which will have a significant influence over the decisions of company car drivers as to what vehicle they select for their next car. 

    “When people know they can make significant personal savings, many people's attitude towards EV's will undoubtedly change in favour of the technology. 

    “Saving money can have a real impact on both head & heart when making decisions.”

    Benefit-in-kind rates will slowly be increased but they are only set to hit one percent in 2021 and two percent in 2022.

    Chancellor Rishi Sunak has then frozen benefit-in-kind rates at 2022 levels for the next two tax periods to further encourage uptake of vehicles.

    The updates will see prices slashed on some of the most popular electric vehicles on the market if they are purchased under a company car scheme.

    Estimates have predicted Tesla could sell their Model 3 for 42 percent cheaper under the new agreement while savings are also set for the city runarounds such as the Nissan LEAF.

    Some experts have described the changes as one of the most important announcements for the UK and a game changer.

    READ MORE

    • Electric car industry set for major finance boost

    Dan Martin believes the updates will lead to a faster uptake of electric vehicles over the next 12-24 months as cost savings are highlighted to customers.

    Mr Martin also said the changes were a positive move and could lead towards extra sustainability.

    Speaking to Express.co.uk, he said: “Over the next 12- 24 months I believe there will be a far faster uptake from drivers wanting to move over to electric leaving their Diesel alternatives behind.

    “The challenge we may be facing is the supply vs the demand as manufacturers struggle to cope with the amount of orders, particularly post Covid 19.

    “Overall this is a positive move by the government with steps toward a cleaner environment and a more sustainable future.”
