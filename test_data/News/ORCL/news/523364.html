Job gains since the Great Recession erased

By REBECCA RAINEY and ELEANOR MUELLER 

04/17/2020 10:00 AM EDT

With Daniel Lippman

Editor’s Note: Morning Shift is a free version of POLITICO Pro Employment & Immigration’s morning newsletter, which is delivered to our subscribers each morning at 6 a.m. The POLITICO Pro platform combines the news you need with tools you can use to take action on the day’s biggest stories.Act on the news with POLITICO Pro.

Quick Fix

— The coronavirus pandemic has wiped away almost all the job gains since the Great Recession, according to new Labor Department data.

— President Donald Trump has a plan to reopen the economy, but much of it will be up to the states.

—The Payroll Protection Program, created to prevent layoffs amid the pandemic, is out of money.

Driving the Day

UNEMPLOYMENT CLAIMS TOP 22M OVER 4 WEEKS:Americans filed 5.2 million jobless claims in the week that ended April 11, pushing the total for the past four weeks above 22 million, your host reports. That erasesnearly as many jobs as were created during the decadelong recovery from the Great Recession of 2007-2009. Economists say unemployment may now exceed 15 percent, which would be the highest since the Great Depression of the 1930s.

The silver lining:As of March 28, about half of unemployment claims were approved. By the week ending April 4 that had risen to between 60 and 69 percent, which is closer to the percentage approved when the economy isn’t going haywire. In 2018, the last year for which data was available, two-thirds of UI applications were approved. (Keep in mind that it can take states up to 2-3 weeks to process a claim.)

On the other hand: Independent contractors and gig workers are running into dead ends, delays and bureaucracy while they try to collect unemployment benefits for which they’re newly eligible, as states struggle to set up new systems and figure out how much to pay out, your host reports with POLITICO’s Megan Cassella.

Groups representing ride-hailing drivers in California and New York,where new state laws classify many app-based workers not as independent contractors but as employees eligible for traditional jobless benefits, complain that gig companies won’t share payroll information with state unemployment agencies. That prevents the states from calculating what these workers’ weekly benefits should be.

Economy

TRUMP TAPS LAWMAKERS IN REOPENING ECONOMY:The White House has named members of Congress from both parties to its task force on how and when to reopen the economy, POLITICO’s Marianne LeVine, Sarah Ferris and Melanie Zanona report.

Butting heads:“The task force invitations come as Trump and some congressional Republicans are pushing for a swift reopening,” they add. “Democrats are urging a more gradual approach … and are seeking to dramatically expand testing as part of a first step.”

“More than a dozen statesand the District of Columbia have already bucked the White House,” POLITICO’s Caitlin Oprysko reports, “extending shelter-in-place mandates in place either indefinitely or past that deadline. Seventeen other states and the U.S. Virgin Islands have orders that are set to lift the same day or the day after the White House deadline, though those could be extended in the coming weeks.”

THE GREAT REOPENING?:Trump released his guidelines for a gradual reopening of the economy Thursday, but how to implement them will be left up to state leaders, POLITICO’s Gabby Orr, Jeremy B. White and Nancy Cook report.

The initial goalwill be to bring people under age 65 and without preexisting health conditions back into the workforce as soon as possible. The guidelines called for states to move in three phases once they have a downward trajectory of Covid-19 cases over a 14-day period and a robust testing program in place for health care workers.

Business Relief

SMALL BUSINESS PAYROLL PROGRAM RUNS DRY: The small business rescue fund set up by Congress to keep workers on the payroll exhausted its $350 billion in funding Thursday morning, POLITICO’s Zachary Warmbrodt, Burgess Everett and Heather Caygle report. “Without additional funding, banks and the Small Business Administration will be unable to approve more of the Paycheck Protection Program loans designed to avert layoffs during the coronavirus pandemic, which can be forgiven if businesses agree to maintain their payrolls.”

Lawmakers have been gridlocked for two weeks over Republicans’ request for an additional $250 billion, because Democrats want equal aid for hospitals and local governments. In a phone call Thursday, Trump told senators on his new economic task force that he wants to work with Democrats to get a deal, they report. But others on Capitol Hill fear the impasse could stretch into next month.

White House

MEANWHILE, ON TRUMP’S ‘GREAT AMERICAN ECONOMIC REVIVAL’ TEAM: One of the major companies that have representatives in the tech section of Trump’s “Great American Economic Revival Industry Groups” was alleged, by Trump’s own Labor Department, to have underpaid women and minorities. The White House announced on Tuesday that Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison and Oracle CEO Safra Catz are part of the industry groups, and Catz was also on an industry leader call with Trump on Wednesday.

Last year, the Labor Department argued in court that Oracle underpaid women and minorities by $400 million. Oracle denies the allegations and has counter-sued the department, which started the case in the waning days of the Obama administration. A number of liberal groups and officials, including seven Democratic state attorney generals, the American Civil Liberties Union and the Communications Workers of America, filed an amicus brief earlier this month supporting DOL’s position against Oracle.

Safety

TESTING FOR AMAZON WORKERS?:Amazon CEO Jeff Bezos said Thursday the company is working to develop coronavirus testing capability with the goal of providing regular testing for all its workers, POLITICO’s Cristiano Lima reports.

WORKERS FILE THOUSANDS OF COMPLAINTS WITH OSHA OVER VIRUS RISKS:Thousands of complaints have been filed with OSHA detailing workers’ concerns about being forced to work in conditions that could expose them to the coronavirus, The Washington Post’s Peter Whoriskey, Jeff Stein and Nate Jones report.

The complaintscome from hospitals, construction companies, grocery stores, pharmacies, and shipping companies, they write. Health care workers described working in close quarters, were “given ‘plastic ponchos’ and masks made out of paper towels” and reported a “lack of hand sanitizer or soap in bathrooms.”

In total, OSHA released to the Post more than 3,000 complaints related to the coronavirus filed from January through early April. But the the actual number of complaints is “likely far higher, because that count does not include the complaints from the roughly 20 states that collect the complaints on their own.”
